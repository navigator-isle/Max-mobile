**Polar Code — International Code for Ships Operating in Polar Waters**

**What is the Polar Code**
- IMO code for safety and pollution prevention of ships in Arctic and Antarctic waters
- Mandatory under SOLAS Ch XIV (Part I — safety) and MARPOL Annexes I, II, IV, V (Part II — environment)
- Goal-based: each chapter has a goal, functional requirements and regulations
- Certificate: Polar Ship Certificate (PSC — Category A, B or C) · Polar Water Operational Manual (PWOM) on board

**History**
- Before: IMO Guidelines for ships in Arctic ice-covered waters (2002), polar waters (2009, A.1024(26))
- Safety part adopted 21 Nov 2014 (MSC.385(94)); SOLAS Ch XIV added by MSC.386(94)
- Environment part adopted 15 May 2015 (MEPC.264(68))
- In force 1 Jan 2017 (new ships) · existing ships by first intermediate or renewal survey after 1 Jan 2018
- STCW amendments: basic and advanced polar training (in force 1 Jul 2018)
- MARPOL Annex I: Arctic heavy fuel oil use/carriage ban from 1 Jul 2024 (waivers for some ships until 1 Jul 2029)
- MSC.538(107): Chapters 9-1 and 11-1 for non-SOLAS ships — in force 1 Jan 2026

**Applies to**
- SOLAS ships (cargo ≥500 GT, passenger ships) operating in polar waters
- From 1 Jan 2026 (Part I-A Ch 9-1 navigation and Ch 11-1 voyage planning): fishing vessels ≥24 m LOA · pleasure yachts ≥300 GT not in trade · cargo ships 300–<500 GT
  - Built ≥1 Jan 2026: comply before operating in polar waters · built before: comply by 1 Jan 2027
- Polar waters: Arctic waters (defined boundary around 60° N, varies) and Antarctic area (south of 60° S)

**Ship categories**
- Category A: medium first-year ice with old ice inclusions (PC1–PC5)
- Category B: thin first-year ice with old ice inclusions (PC6–PC7)
- Category C: open water/ice conditions less severe than A and B (may be not ice-strengthened)

---

**Introduction**
- Definitions (ice-free waters, ice of land origin, Polar Service Temperature (PST) = 10 °C below lowest Mean Daily Low Temperature, Polar Class)
- Hazards: ice, low temperature, extended darkness/daylight, high latitude (navigation and communication), remoteness, lack of crew experience, lack of SAR, ecosystem sensitivity

**Part I-A — Safety measures (mandatory)**
- Ch 1 General: Polar Ship Certificate · operational assessment (POLARIS — Polar Operational Limit Assessment Risk Indexing System)
- Ch 2 Polar Water Operational Manual (PWOM): capabilities and limitations, procedures for normal and emergency operations, ice operations, contacting icebreakers
- Ch 3 Ship structure: Polar Class structure for Cat A/B · materials suitable for PST
- Ch 4 Subdivision and stability: ice accretion allowances (30 kg/m² on exposed decks, 7.5 kg/m² on lateral area) · damage stability for ice damage (Cat A/B)
- Ch 5 Watertight and weathertight integrity: operable in ice and low temperature
- Ch 6 Machinery installations: protection from ice and snow, sea-water intake for ice
- Ch 7 Fire safety/protection: equipment works at PST, freeze protection of fire mains and extinguishers
- Ch 8 Life-saving appliances and arrangements: survival for ≥5 days (maximum expected time of rescue) · personal and group survival equipment · immersion suits/thermal protection for all
- Ch 9 Safety of navigation: ice detection (two independent echo-sounders, radar for ice), visual aids, two non-magnetic heading sources for high latitudes · Ch 9-1 for non-SOLAS ships (2026)
- Ch 10 Communication: voice and data to RCC/SAR in the area · emergency communication in survival craft
- Ch 11 Voyage planning: ice and weather information, routeing, places of refuge, marine mammals, PSSA, SAR capability · Ch 11-1 for non-SOLAS ships (2026)
- Ch 12 Manning and training: masters, chief mates and OOWs with STCW Basic or Advanced Polar training per category and ice conditions

**Part I-B — Additional guidance** (recommendatory)

**Part II-A — Pollution prevention measures (mandatory)**
- Ch 1 Oil (MARPOL I): no discharge of oil or oily mixtures in polar waters · Cat A/B built ≥2017: fuel and cargo tanks separated from shell (≥0.76 m)
- Ch 2 Noxious liquid substances (MARPOL II): no discharge of NLS in polar waters · Cat A/B new ships — tanks away from shell
- Ch 3 (reserved — packaged harmful substances)
- Ch 4 Sewage (MARPOL IV): discharge limits — distance from ice shelf/fast ice; treatment plant standards
- Ch 5 Garbage (MARPOL V): food waste only comminuted (≤25 mm), ≥12 nm from land and ice shelf/fast ice, not onto ice · cargo residues limited (Arctic)

**Part II-B — Additional guidance** (recommendatory)

**Key Point**
- On board: Polar Ship Certificate + PWOM + POLARIS assessment for the route
- Master and officers need polar training (STCW V/4) for the ship category and ice
- Zero oil and NLS discharge; garbage rules stricter than outside polar waters
- No HFO in the Arctic for most ships from 2024 (all by 2029); Antarctic HFO ban since 2011 (MARPOL I/43)
