## Ship categories (Introduction, 2)
![[diagram:polar-categories|Ship categories A, B and C]]

| Category | Design | Structure (3.3.2) |
|---|---|---|
| A | Polar waters in at least medium first-year ice, which may include old ice inclusions | Polar Class 1–5 |
| B | Not category A; polar waters in at least thin first-year ice, which may include old ice inclusions | Polar Class 6–7 |
| C | Open water, or ice conditions less severe than A and B | Approved against the ice types and concentrations in the area. Need not be ice-strengthened if the Administration judges the structure adequate. |

- Polar Class is defined in 1.2.10, based on the IACS Unified Requirements
- Category C cargo ships may receive the Polar Ship Certificate on documented verification, if no additional equipment or structural modification is needed (1.3.3)

## Hazards the Code addresses (Introduction, 3.1)
1. Ice · 2. Topside icing · 3. Low temperature · 4. Extended darkness or daylight · 5. High latitude · 6. Remoteness and incomplete hydrographic data · 7. Crew inexperience in polar operations · 8. Lack of suitable emergency response equipment · 9. Rapidly changing, severe weather · 10. Environmental sensitivity to harmful substances

## Key definitions (Part I-A, 1.2)
- **Polar Service Temperature (PST)** (1.2.11): set at least **10 °C below the lowest Mean Daily Low Temperature (MDLT)** (1.2.9) for the intended area and season
- **Polar waters:** the Arctic waters and Antarctic area defined by SOLAS regulations XIV/1.2 and XIV/1.3

### Chapter 4 — Icing and ice damage
- **Icing allowances (4.3.1.1):** 30 kg/m² on exposed weather decks and gangways · 7.5 kg/m² on the projected lateral area of each side above the waterplane · for discontinuous surfaces such as rails, booms and rigging, increase the continuous projected area by 5% and static moments by 10%
- **Ice damage (4.3.2)**, category A and B ships built on or after 1 Jan 2017: must survive flooding from ice penetration, with the SOLAS survival factor si equal to 1
  - Longitudinal extent: 4.5% of the upper ice waterline length if centred forward of maximum breadth, otherwise 1.5%
  - Transverse extent: 760 mm
  - Vertical extent: the lesser of 20% of the upper ice waterline draught or the longitudinal extent

### Chapter 7 — Fire safety (7.3)
- Isolating and pressure/vacuum valves in exposed locations protected from ice and kept accessible; two-way portable radios work at the PST
- Fire pumps in compartments kept above freezing · exposed fire main sections can be isolated and drained · firefighter outfits stored in warm locations
- Extinguishers in freezing locations work at the PST

### Chapter 8 — Life-saving appliances
- Where immersion suits are required, they must be the **insulated** type (8.2.3.1 / 8.3.3.1)
- Passenger ships: a properly sized immersion suit or thermal protective aid for each person
- Lifeboats partially or totally enclosed. Where abandonment onto ice or land is possible, personal and group survival equipment for **110%** of persons on board (8.3.3.3)
- Adequate emergency rations for the maximum expected time of rescue (8.3.3.4)
- Ships built on or after 1 Jan 2017: exposed escape routes must not hinder persons in polar clothing (8.3.1.2)

### Chapter 9 — Safety of navigation (9.3)
- Ice-strengthened ships built on or after 1 Jan 2017: **two independent echo-sounding devices**, or one with two separate independent transducers (9.3.2.1.1)
- **Two independent non-magnetic heading means**, both connected to main and emergency power (9.3.2.2.1)
- Ships going above **80° latitude**: at least one GNSS compass or equivalent (9.3.2.2.2)
- Two remotely rotatable searchlights, or another means to see ice in darkness, unless operating only in 24-hour daylight (9.3.3.1)
- Icebreaker-escorted operations: a manually initiated flashing red light visible from astern to show when stopped (9.3.3.2)

### Chapter 10 — Communication
- Two-way ship-to-ship and ship-to-shore communication along intended routes, taking high-latitude limits into account (10.2.1.1 / 10.3.1.1)
- Search and rescue communications include voice and data with rescue centres and voice with aircraft on 121.5 and 123.1 MHz (10.3.1.3)
- Two-way voice and data with a Telemedical Assistance Service (10.3.1.4)
- Rescue boats and lifeboats have devices for distress alerting, location and on-scene communication in low air temperatures (10.3.2.1)

### Chapter 11 — Voyage planning
The master considers (11.3): PWOM procedures · current ice and iceberg information · historical ice and temperature statistics · places of refuge · marine mammal areas · protected areas along the route · operation far from search and rescue capabilities (11.3.9)

### Chapter 12 — Manning and training
- Masters, chief mates and watch officers qualified under STCW chapter V. Requirements vary by ice condition and ship type (12.3.1).
- Open-water operations: basic polar training for tankers and passenger ships. Advanced training for masters and chief mates in "other waters".
- The Administration may allow other qualified persons to meet the training requirements, with enough polar-trained personnel to cover all watches (12.3.2)
- Every crew member is familiar with the PWOM procedures and equipment relevant to their duties (12.3.4)
- STCW polar training became mandatory on 1 Jul 2018

## Part II-A — Pollution prevention (mandatory)
![[diagram:polar-discharge|Discharge distances in Arctic waters]]

### Chapter 1 — Oil (MARPOL Annex I)
- **Arctic waters:** all discharge into the sea of oil or oily mixtures from any ship is prohibited (1.1.1). Clean or segregated ballast is exempt (1.1.2).
- Category A and B ships built on or after 1 Jan 2017, total fuel capacity under 600 m³: fuel tanks at least **0.76 m** from the outer hull. Tanks of 30 m³ or less are exempt (1.2.1).
- Category A and B non-tankers built on or after 1 Jan 2017: oil cargo tanks at least 0.76 m from the outer hull (1.2.2)
- Sludge tanks and oily bilge water holding tanks at least 0.76 m from the outer hull, with the same 30 m³ exemption (1.2.4)
- Tankers under 5,000 dwt: cargo tanks protected along the full length by double bottom and wing spaces (1.2.3)

### Chapter 2 — Noxious liquid substances (MARPOL Annex II)
- Discharge of noxious liquid substances (NLS), or mixtures containing them, is prohibited in **Arctic waters** (2.1.1)
- Category A and B ships built on or after 1 Jan 2017 carrying NLS assigned ship type 3 in the IBC Code need Administration approval (2.1.3)

### Chapter 4 — Sewage (MARPOL Annex IV)
| Sewage | Discharge allowed |
|---|---|
| Comminuted and disinfected | More than **3 nm** from any ice shelf or fast ice, and as far as possible from areas with ice concentration above 1/10 (4.2.1.1) |
| Untreated | More than **12 nm** from any ice shelf or fast ice, and as far as possible from areas above 1/10 (4.2.1.2) |
| Approved treatment plant | As far as possible from the nearest land, ice shelf, fast ice, or areas above 1/10 (4.2.1.3) |

- Category A and B ships, and all passenger ships, built on or after 1 Jan 2017: discharge prohibited unless through an approved treatment plant (4.2.2)

### Chapter 5 — Garbage (MARPOL Annex V)
- **Arctic:** food waste only as far as possible from areas above 1/10 ice concentration, and never less than **12 nm** from the nearest land, ice shelf or fast ice (5.2.1.1)
- Food waste comminuted or ground to pass screens with openings of at most **25 mm**, not mixed with other garbage (5.2.1.2)
- Food waste must not be discharged onto ice (5.2.1.3) · animal carcass discharge prohibited (5.2.1.4)
- **Antarctic:** discharges under Annex V regulation 6.1 as far as possible from areas above 1/10 ice concentration and never less than 12 nm from the nearest fast ice (5.2.2.1)

## Amendments and related rules
### MSC.538(107) — Chapters 9-1 and 11-1
- Adopted 8 Jun 2023, in force **1 Jan 2026**. Part I-B amendments take effect the same date.
- Adds chapter **9-1** (safety of navigation) and **11-1** (voyage planning) for fishing vessels of 24 m and above, pleasure yachts of 300 GT and above not engaged in trade, and cargo ships of 300 GT but below 500 GT
- Existing chapters 9 and 11 are limited to ships certified under SOLAS chapter I
- Ships built on or after 1 Jan 2026: ice-strengthened ships need two independent echo sounders (or one with two independent transducers), and category A and B ships need enclosed or protected bridge wings
- Chapter 9-1 includes: ice information display · navigation equipment that works in expected conditions · two means of illuminating ice in darkness · ice protection for sensors and antennas on ice-strengthened ships · two independent non-magnetic heading sources for ships of 500 GT and above · GNSS compass above 80° latitude

### Other
- **STCW polar training:** minimum training adopted November 2016, mandatory from 1 Jul 2018
- **Arctic heavy fuel oil:** MARPOL Annex I regulation 43A (in force 1 Nov 2022) prohibits using or carrying HFO as fuel in Arctic waters from **1 Jul 2024**

## Not included (not verified against the original text)
- Old claim that existing ships comply by the first intermediate or renewal survey after 1 Jan 2018
- POLARIS risk-indexing as part of the Code (not found in the text checked)
- Radar for ice detection as a requirement
- HFO waivers until 1 Jul 2029 and the Antarctic HFO ban since 2011
- The compliance date of 1 Jan 2027 for existing non-SOLAS ships under MSC.538(107) (the resolution text sets no other build-date compliance dates)
- Chapters 5 and 6 content, and the 2002 and 2009 guidelines history
- Exact Arctic and Antarctic boundaries (defined in SOLAS XIV/1.2 and 1.3)
