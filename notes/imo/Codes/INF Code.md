# INF Code — International Code for the Safe Carriage of Packaged Irradiated Nuclear Fuel, Plutonium and High-Level Radioactive Wastes on Board Ships

## At a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 27 May 1999, resolution MSC.88(71), at MSC 71 |
| Mandatory | SOLAS chapter VII **Part D**, added by MSC.87(71) (adopted 27 May 1999); in force **1 Jan 2001** |
| Cargo | **INF cargo**: packaged irradiated nuclear fuel, plutonium and high-level radioactive wastes (IMDG Class 7) |
| Applies to | All ships, regardless of build date or size, including cargo ships under 500 GT, carrying INF cargo (SOLAS VII/15) |
| Not for | Warships, naval auxiliaries and government non-commercial vessels (their administrations act consistently as far as reasonable) |
| Certificate | International Certificate of Fitness for the Carriage of INF Cargo |
| Structure | 11 chapters and an appendix (certificate form) |

## What it is

- Additional **ship** requirements for carrying INF cargo. The package and transport rules of the IMDG Code also apply to INF cargo (1.2.2).
- SOLAS VII Part D has three regulations: 14 (definitions, including the INF Code), 15 (application) and 16 (requirements: comply with the Code, be surveyed and certified).
- INF certificates are subject to control under SOLAS I/19 and XI/4 and are treated as certificates issued under I/12 or I/13.

## History

- Resolution MSC.88(71) recalls Assembly resolutions A.748(18), A.790(19), A.853(20) and A.854(20), and recognizes the need for mandatory application of the standards.
- Amendments to the Code follow SOLAS article VIII.
- Amendments listed by the IMO: **MSC.118(74)** (2001, in force 1 Jan 2003), **MSC.135(76)** (2002, 1 Jul 2004), **MSC.178(79)** (2004, 1 Jul 2006) and **MSC.241(83)** (2007, 1 Jul 2009).
- **MSC.118(74)**, adopted 6 Jun 2001: paragraph 1.1.1.3 now refers to "transport schedule 10, 11, 12, 13 or 14", adding schedule 14 to align with amendment 30 to the IMDG Code.

## Ship classes (1.1.2)

![[diagram:inf-classes|Class is set by total activity carried]]

| Class | Cargo and limit |
|---|---|
| **INF 1** | Any INF cargo, aggregate activity less than **4,000 TBq** |
| **INF 2** | Irradiated nuclear fuel or high-level wastes less than **2 × 10⁶ TBq**; plutonium less than **2 × 10⁵ TBq** |
| **INF 3** | Irradiated nuclear fuel or high-level wastes, and plutonium, with **no restriction** on aggregate activity |

- INF cargo that would need an INF 3 ship may **not** be carried on passenger ships (1.2.3).

## Certificate and surveys (Chapter 1)

- An **initial survey** is required before carriage (1.3.1).
- The Administration, or a recognized organization, issues the certificate (1.3.2). Certified ships get periodic surveys under SOLAS chapter I (1.3.3).
- The certificate lapses if required surveys are missed or show non-compliance, or when the related SOLAS certificate expires (1.3.4).

## Requirements by class

![[diagram:inf-requirements|Key figures in the Code]]

**Chapter 2: damage stability**
- INF 1: to the Administration's satisfaction (2.1).
- INF 2: SOLAS II-1 Part B if built to passenger-ship standard; Part B-1 if built to cargo-ship standard, regardless of length (2.2).
- INF 3: IBC Code chapter 2 survival capability and cargo location for a **type 1 ship**; or SOLAS II-1 Part B-1, regardless of length, with the subdivision index **R(INF) = R + 0.2(1 − R)** (2.3).

**Chapter 3: fire safety**
- INF 1: to the Administration's satisfaction (3.1).
- INF 2 and 3, any size (3.2): a water fire-extinguishing system (SOLAS II-2/4); fixed fire-extinguishing in category A machinery spaces (II-2/7); fixed cargo space cooling (II-2/54.2.1.3); fixed fire detection and alarm for machinery, accommodation and service spaces (II-2/13).
- INF 3: accommodation, service spaces, control stations and category A machinery spaces are forward or aft of the cargo spaces (3.3).

**Chapter 4: temperature (all classes, 4.1)**
- Enclosed cargo spaces have ventilation or refrigeration keeping the average ambient temperature at or below **55 °C** at all times.
- Cargo space ventilation or refrigeration is independent of systems serving other spaces.
- Essential items (fans, compressors, heat exchangers, cooling water supply) are duplicated for each cargo space, with spare parts.

**Chapter 5: structure**
- Deck areas and support arrangements are strong enough for the loads they carry.

**Chapter 6: securing (6.1)**
- Permanent securing devices prevent package movement, for accelerations of **1.5 g** longitudinally, **1.5 g** transversely, **1.0 g** vertically up and **2.0 g** vertically down.
- On open or vehicle decks, packages may instead be secured under Administration-approved principles based on IMO guidelines (6.2).
- Collision chocks must not block the cooling airflow required by 4.1 (6.3).

**Chapter 7: electrical power**
- INF 1: to the Administration's satisfaction.
- INF 2 and 3: an alternative power source, unaffected by damage to the main supply, supplying for **at least 36 hours** the flooding and cooling equipment and all emergency services required by SOLAS (7.2).
- INF 3: the alternative source is outside the damage extent considered under chapter 2 (7.3).

**Chapter 8: radiological protection**
- Additional radiological protection arrangements or equipment where needed, depending on cargo and ship design, to the Administration's satisfaction.

**Chapter 9: management and training**
- To the Administration's satisfaction, taking into account IMO developments.

**Chapter 10: shipboard emergency plan**
- Every ship carrying INF cargo carries a plan, approved by the Administration, based on IMO guidelines (A.854(20)), in a language the master and officers understand (10.1, 10.2).
- It includes the reporting procedure under chapter 11; a list of authorities or persons to contact; detailed immediate actions to prevent, reduce or control a release; and procedures and contact points for coordinating with national and local authorities.
- It may be combined with other required plans into a single **Shipboard Marine Emergency Plan** (10.3).

**Chapter 11: notification**
- SOLAS regulation VII/7-1 reporting applies to actual or likely loss of INF cargo overboard and to any actual or probable release, whatever the cause (11.1).
- Reports are also required for damage or breakdown affecting safety or navigation: collision, grounding, fire, explosion, structural failure, flooding, cargo shifting, or failure of steering, propulsion, electrical generation or essential navigational aids (11.2).
- "Incident" covers loss of container integrity and probable cargo release; "release" covers escape of INF cargo from containment or loss of a package (1.1.1).

> **Key Point:** Know the three classes: INF 1 under 4,000 TBq, INF 2, INF 3 with no limit. Cargo spaces stay at or below 55 °C on average, securing is designed for 1.5 g / 1.5 g / 1.0 g up / 2.0 g down, INF 2 and 3 need 36 hours of alternative power, and any release or probable release is reported at once under SOLAS VII/7-1.

## Not included (not verified against the original text)

- The old claim that the Code was first adopted as recommendations in 1993 (A.748(18) is recalled in the preamble; its year and nature were not confirmed).
- What MSC.135(76), MSC.178(79) and MSC.241(83) changed: only their numbers and dates are confirmed.
- The old description of chapter 8 as "shielding, radiation monitoring, doses as low as reasonably achievable" and chapter 9 as "crew trained on INF risks": the Code text says only that these satisfy the Administration.
