**INF Code — International Code for the Safe Carriage of Packaged Irradiated Nuclear Fuel, Plutonium and High-Level Radioactive Wastes on Board Ships**

**What is the INF Code**
- Extra ship requirements for carrying **INF cargo**: packaged irradiated nuclear fuel, plutonium and high-level radioactive wastes (IMDG Class 7, in Type B or similar packages)
- The packages follow the IMDG Code and IAEA transport regulations; the INF Code covers the **ship**: stability, fire safety, cooling, securing, power, radiological protection, emergency plans
- Mandatory under SOLAS Chapter VII Part D

**History**
- INF Code first adopted as recommendations, A.748(18) (1993)
- Made mandatory: Code adopted 27 May 1999, resolution MSC.88(71); SOLAS VII Part D by MSC.87(71)
- In force 1 Jan 2001
- Amended later (e.g. alignment with IMDG Code and IAEA regulations; ship notification)

**Applies to**
- All ships, regardless of date of build and size (including cargo ships under 500 GT), carrying INF cargo
- Not: warships and naval auxiliaries (flag States should apply it as far as reasonable)

---

**INF ship classes (by total radioactivity carried)**
- **INF 1**: aggregate activity less than **4,000 TBq**
- **INF 2**: irradiated nuclear fuel or high-level wastes less than **2 × 10⁶ TBq**; plutonium less than **2 × 10⁵ TBq**
- **INF 3**: no restriction on aggregate activity
- Passenger ships may not carry INF cargo that needs an INF 3 ship

**Chapters**
- **1 General**: definitions, application, INF classes, survey and certification — **International Certificate of Fitness for the Carriage of INF Cargo** after initial survey (with SOLAS certificates)
- **2 Damage stability**: INF 1 — to the Administration's satisfaction; INF 2 — SOLAS II-1 Part B (passenger standard) or Part B-1 (cargo standard, any length); INF 3 — IBC Code **type 1 ship** survival capability and cargo location, or SOLAS II-1 B-1 with a higher index (R-INF)
- **3 Fire safety measures**: INF 2/3 — water spray or fire-fighting capability for cargo spaces, fixed extinguishing in machinery spaces, detection and alarm
- **4 Temperature control of cargo spaces**: average temperature not above **55 °C**; ventilation or refrigeration, independent and duplicated where essential
- **5 Structural considerations**: strength of deck and supporting structure for the packages
- **6 Cargo securing arrangements**: permanent fittings for accelerations of **1.5 g** longitudinal and transverse, **1.0 g** vertical up, **2.0 g** vertical down
- **7 Electrical power supplies**: INF 2/3 — alternative power source for at least **36 hours** for cooling, flooding and emergency services
- **8 Radiological protection**: shielding, radiation monitoring, keep crew doses as low as reasonably achievable
- **9 Management and training**: crew trained on INF cargo risks and emergency actions
- **10 Shipboard emergency plan**: approved by the Administration (with SMPEP-like structure: reporting, response, co-ordination)
- **11 Notification** of incidents involving INF cargo (SOLAS VII/7-1) to the nearest coastal State and the flag State

**Key Point**
- Know the three classes: INF 1 < 4,000 TBq · INF 2 · INF 3 no limit
- 55 °C cargo space average · 36 h alternative power (INF 2/3) · securing 1.5 g / 1.0 g up / 2.0 g down
- Any release or probable release is reported at once under SOLAS VII/7-1
