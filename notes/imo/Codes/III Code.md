# III Code — IMO Instruments Implementation Code

## At a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 4 Dec 2013, Assembly resolution A.1070(28), at the 28th Assembly |
| Replaced | A.1054(27), Code for the Implementation of Mandatory IMO Instruments, 2011 |
| Mandatory | From **1 Jan 2016**, through amendments to nine instruments |
| Who it is for | **Governments** (Contracting Governments acting as flag, coastal and port States), not ships or companies directly |
| Role in audits | The **audit standard** of the IMO Member State Audit Scheme (IMSAS) |
| Structure | 4 parts, 63 numbered paragraphs, no chapters; no definitions section |

## What it is

- A code for how States implement and enforce IMO instruments.
- **Objective (para 1):** enhance global maritime safety and protection of the marine environment, and help States implement IMO instruments.
- **Scope (para 6):** safety of life at sea, pollution prevention, seafarer training and certification, load lines, tonnage measurement and collision regulations.
- A State is bound only for the instruments to which it is a Contracting Government or Party (para 2).
- Coastal and port State requirements apply to the extent that ships subject to IMO mandatory instruments can reach the State's ports.

## History

![[diagram:iii-dates|From the voluntary scheme to the 2025 framework]]

- **Voluntary scheme (VIMSAS):** A.946(23) (Nov 2003), framework A.974(24) (Nov 2005). Voluntary audits ran from Sep 2006 to Dec 2015, covering 67 Member States, 2 Associate Members and 5 dependent territories.
- **First code:** A.973(24), Nov 2005; then the 2011 Code, A.1054(27); then the III Code, A.1070(28), Nov 2013.
- **A.1018(26) (Nov 2009):** endorsed a phased introduction of the audit scheme as a mandatory process.
- **A.1068(28):** transitional arrangements for voluntary audits from Dec 2013 to Dec 2015, using the III Code as the standard.

**Made mandatory (in force 1 Jan 2016)**

| Instrument | Amending resolution |
|---|---|
| SOLAS 1974 (new chapter XIII, Verification of compliance) | MSC.366(93), adopted 22 May 2014 |
| STCW Convention and STCW Code | MSC.373(93), MSC.374(93) |
| Load Lines Protocol 1988 | MSC.375(93) |
| MARPOL Annexes I–V | MEPC.246(66), adopted 4 Apr 2014 (and MEPC.247(66)) |
| COLREG 1972, Load Lines 1966, Tonnage 1969 | A.1083(28), A.1084(28), A.1085(28) |

- Each instrument says Contracting Governments **shall use the III Code** in meeting their obligations and are subject to periodic IMO audits.
- Every "should" in the III Code is read as "shall", except in paragraphs 29 to 32.
- In SOLAS chapter XIII the Secretary-General administers the Audit Scheme; each Government must facilitate the audit and carry out a programme of actions to address the findings (regulation 3).

## Structure

![[diagram:iii-structure|Parts and paragraph ranges]]

**Part 1: Common areas (paras 1–14)**
- Strategy (3): develop an overall strategy for flag, port and coastal State obligations, monitor and review it.
- Laws (4–5): promulgate laws giving the instruments full effect; pollution control must not shift damage or hazards from one area to another.
- Initial actions (8): when an instrument enters into force, the State must have legal authority, enforcement processes and enough maritime personnel.
- Records (10): legible, retrievable records under a documented control procedure.
- Improvement (11–14): continual improvement, training, drills, incentives, and corrective and preventive action for non-conformities.

**Part 2: Flag States (paras 15–44)**
- Implementation (15–17): national legislation and guidance; a safety and environmental protection programme; ships sufficiently and efficiently manned.
- Delegation (18–21): recognized organizations act under a formal written agreement; the flag State must oversee them and must not require them to apply more than convention requirements to ships of other flags (para 19).
- Enforcement (22–27): periodic inspections, penalties and proceedings; a control and monitoring programme; oversee corrective measures after a port State detention; issue certificates only after requirements are met.
- Surveyors (28–37): marine qualifications and experience, knowledge of ships and instruments, a documented qualification system, and an identification document.
- Investigations (38–41): impartial and objective; casualties investigated under the Casualty Investigation Code.
- Evaluation (42–44): performance indicators such as detention rates, casualty statistics and loss ratios.

**Part 3: Coastal States (paras 45–51)**
- Services that may be covered (48): radiocommunication, search and rescue, hydrography, ship routeing, vessel traffic services and aids to navigation.
- Enforcement (49–50): a control and monitoring programme, including statistical data, timely response to pollution incidents and cooperation in casualty investigations.

**Part 4: Port States (paras 52–63)**
- Obligations may include reception facilities, port State control and a register of fuel oil suppliers (56).
- Conditions apply to both Parties and non-Parties (59).
- Port State control follows the Organization's procedures (60–61; the Code cites resolution A.1052(27), Procedures for Port State Control, 2011).
- Port State control officers must be free from commercial pressures and must not be employed by recognized organizations or classification societies (62).

## The audit scheme

| Point | Rule (A.1211(34), adopted 3 Dec 2025) |
|---|---|
| Replaces | Framework A.1067(28), revoked from **30 Jun 2026** |
| Cycle | Not more than **9 years**; Member States grouped by previous audit start; each group audited within 2 years |
| Notice | Projected audit date notified at least 18 months ahead |
| Follow-up | Generally 3 to 4 years after the audit |
| Type | Average score of 70% or more across three indicators (verification index, gap analysis, State audit questionnaire) qualifies for a limited on-site audit; others have a full on-site audit |
| Standard | The III Code |
| Confidentiality | Interim report and audit records: audited State, audit team and Secretary-General only. Executive summary, corrective action plan, final report and follow-up reports go to all Member States; public release needs the State's authorization. A limited graphical summary of implementation levels is public |

> **Key Point:** The III Code is the standard governments are audited against. For a flag State, delegation to recognized organizations needs a written agreement and oversight, surveyors must be qualified, and casualty investigations follow the Casualty Investigation Code. For a port State, control follows the IMO procedures and applies to ships of Parties and non-Parties alike.

## Not included (not verified against the original text)

- The old note's "annex of mandatory obligations by instrument": the Code has no such annex; its annex is the Code itself.
- Procedures for Port State Control "A.1185(33)": the Code cites A.1052(27), which is what I confirmed.
- Statements about MARINA, Paris and Tokyo MoU lists and PSC targeting.
