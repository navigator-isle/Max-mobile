### The 2026 changes in detail
**MSC.554(108)** — applies to appliances **installed on or after 1 Jan 2026**
- **Lifejackets (2.2.1.6.2):** must turn an unconscious, face-down person to a face-up position, nose and mouth clear of the water, in an average time not exceeding that of the reference test device (RTD) plus 1 s. No allowance for failing to do so in testing.
- **Release hooks (4.4.7.6.8):** a hook cannot support any load until fully reset. Handles, safety pins and indicators must not show a reset position until then. Danger signs at each hook station.
- **Lowering speed (6.1.2.8, 6.1.2.10):** minimum S = 0.4 + 0.02H, or 1.0 m/s, whichever is less (H = height in metres from davit head to waterline in the lightest sea-going condition). **Maximum 1.3 m/s**, although the Administration may accept a different maximum.

**MSC.535(107)** — totally enclosed lifeboats **installed on or after 1 Jan 2029**
- Ventilation of at least **5 m³/h per person** the lifeboat may carry, for at least **24 hours**
- Operable from inside, no stagnant or unventilated pockets
- Powered ventilation must not draw from the radio batteries. Engine-dependent systems need fuel for the engine endurance requirement.

**MSC.559(108)** (not an LSA Code amendment) — amends MSC.402(96), the maintenance and thorough examination requirements for lifeboats, rescue boats, launching appliances and release gear. In force 1 Jan 2026. Item ".10 ventilation system, where fitted" is added to the annual thorough examination and operational test (6.2.3).

**MSC.594(111)** (adopted 21 May 2026) amends the testing recommendation MSC.81(70) for free-fall lifeboat release systems tested under load without launching the boat (linked to LSA Code amendments MSC.589(111)). Test arrangements installed on or after 1 Jan 2031.

## Chapter I — General
| Item | Requirement (1.2) |
|---|---|
| Stowage temperature | No damage from −30 °C to +65 °C |
| Personal appliances | Operational −15 °C to +40 °C |
| Sea water | Operational −1 °C to +30 °C |

## Chapter II — Personal life-saving appliances
### 2.1 Lifebuoys
- Outer diameter at most 800 mm, inner diameter at least 400 mm
- Mass at least 2.5 kg
- Supports at least 14.5 kg of iron in fresh water for 24 h
- Grabline at least 9.5 mm diameter, length at least 4 times the buoy's outside diameter
- Self-igniting light: at least 2 cd, flashing 50 to 70 times a minute, at least 2 h of energy
- Smoke signal: at least 15 min in calm water
- Buoyant lifeline: at least 8 mm diameter, breaking strength at least 5 kN

### 2.2 Lifejackets
| Size | Weight | Height |
|---|---|---|
| Infant | under 15 kg | under 100 cm |
| Child | 15 to under 43 kg | 100 to under 155 cm |
| Adult | 43 kg or more | 155 cm or more |

- At least 75% of persons unfamiliar with the jacket must don it correctly within 1 min unaided
- Jump from at least 4.5 m with the jacket held, and from at least 1 m with arms overhead
- Buoyancy loss no more than 5% after 24 h in fresh water
- Light: at least 0.75 cd for at least 8 h, with a manual switch. Whistle on a lanyard.

### 2.3 Immersion suits
- Don unaided within **2 min**
- Survive a jump from at least 4.5 m · climb a vertical ladder at least 5 m long
- Mouth at least 120 mm clear of the water
- Turn from face-down to face-up in **no more than 5 s**
- Uninsulated: core temperature falls no more than 2 °C after 1 h in calm water at 5 °C
- Insulated: no more than 2 °C after 6 h in calm circulating water at 0 °C to 2 °C

### 2.5 Thermal protective aids
- Thermal conductance no more than 7,800 W/(m²·K)
- Operating air temperature −30 °C to +20 °C

> **Key Point:** The 5 s righting time belongs to immersion suits. Lifejackets are tested against a reference device (RTD + 1 s).

## Chapter III — Visual signals
| Signal | Requirement |
|---|---|
| 3.1 Rocket parachute flare | Reaches at least 300 m · bright red, at least 30,000 cd · burns at least 40 s · descends at no more than 5 m/s |
| 3.2 Hand flare | Bright red, at least 15,000 cd · burns at least 1 min · keeps burning after 10 s under 100 mm of water |
| 3.3 Buoyant smoke signal | Smoke at least 3 min in calm water · keeps smoking at least 10 s under 100 mm of water |

## Chapter IV — Survival craft
![[diagram:lsa-craft|Key survival craft figures]]

### 4.1 Liferafts (general)
- Withstand a drop from **18 m** (4.1.1.2) · survive a jump from 4.5 m onto it (4.1.1.3)
- Capacity at least **6 persons** (4.1.2.1)
- Maximum mass 185 kg, with exceptions (4.1.2.2)
- Painter: at least 10 m plus the stowage-to-waterline distance, or 15 m, whichever is greater (4.1.3.2)
- Float-free: weak link breaks at 2.2 ± 0.4 kN (4.1.6.2) · hydrostatic release unit releases at no more than 4 m depth (4.1.6.3.2)
- Equipment: water 1.5 L per person, food at least 10,000 kJ per person · quoit with at least 30 m buoyant line

### 4.2 Inflatable liferafts
- Main buoyancy chamber has at least **two separate compartments** (4.2.2.1)
- Design load 82.5 kg per person
- Inflation within 1 min at 18–20 °C, within 3 min at −30 °C (4.2.2.3)
- Carrying capacity is the lowest of: main tube volume (m³) ÷ 0.096 · floor area (m²) ÷ 0.372 · persons of 82.5 kg who can sit comfortably (4.2.3)
- Boarding ramp supports a 100 kg person · boarding ladder's lowest step at least 0.4 m below the light waterline

### 4.4 Lifeboats (general)
- Boarding by full complement: **not more than 3 min** for cargo ship lifeboats (4.4.3.2), **not more than 10 min** for passenger ship lifeboats (4.4.3.1)
- Engine starts at **−15 °C within 2 min** of beginning the start procedure (4.4.6.2)
- Speed at least **6 knots** in calm water, fully loaded (4.4.6.8), with fuel for at least **24 h** at that speed
- Painter device holds the lifeboat stable when towed by a ship making headway up to **5 knots** in calm water (4.4.7.7)
- Release gear safety factors: load-bearing parts 6 · hydrostatic interlock 6 · operating cables 2.5
- Equipment: water 3 L per person · food at least 10,000 kJ per person · 4 rocket parachute flares, 6 hand flares, 2 buoyant smoke signals · searchlight 2,500 cd for at least 3 h
- Exterior light at least 4.3 cd for at least 12 h · interior light at least 0.5 cd for at least 12 h

### Lifeboat types
| Type | Key requirement |
|---|---|
| 4.5 Partially enclosed | Rigid covers over at least 20% of the length from the stem and 20% from the aftermost part |
| 4.6 Totally enclosed | Pressure inside no more than 20 hPa above or below outside while the engine runs, enclosure closed · stable when flooded, water at most 500 mm above the seat pan · impact protection against ship's side at not less than 3.5 m/s |
| 4.7 Free-fall | Withstands a free-fall launch from at least 1.3 times the certified height · release system works from no load up to at least 200% of normal load, factor of safety 6 |
| 4.8 Self-contained air support | Engine runs at least 10 min with all openings closed · internal pressure not below outside and no more than 20 hPa above |
| 4.9 Fire-protected | Protects occupants during a continuous oil fire for at least **8 min** while waterborne |

## Chapter V — Rescue boats
- Rescue boats tow the largest liferaft at not less than **2 knots** (5.1.1.7)
- Speed at least 6 knots for at least 4 h, fully loaded (5.1.1.6)
- **Fast rescue boats** (5.1.4): hull length at least 6 m and no more than 8.5 m · at least **20 knots** in calm water with a 3-person crew, at least **8 knots** fully loaded · run at least 4 h
- Inflated boats: buoyancy at least 0.17 m³ per person at 82.5 kg per person

## Chapter VI — Launching and embarkation appliances
- Lifeboat launching appliances safe with 10° trim and 20° list either way (6.1.1.1)
- Lowering speed: see MSC.554(108) above
- Powered winch hoists a rescue boat at not less than 0.3 m/s (6.1.1.9)
- Fast rescue boat: lowering with full complement at no more than 1 m/s, hoisting with 6 persons and full equipment at not less than 0.8 m/s (6.1.7.5)

## Chapter VII — Other life-saving appliances
- **7.1 Line-throwing appliance:** at least **4 projectiles**, each able to carry the line at least **230 m** in calm weather · at least 4 lines, each with breaking strength at least 2 kN
- **7.2 General alarm:** alarm tone **80 dB(A)** and at least **10 dB(A)** above ambient noise
- **Public address:** 75 dB(A) in interior spaces, 80 dB(A) in exterior spaces
