**LSA Code — International Life-Saving Appliance Code**

**What is the LSA Code**
- Technical standards for life-saving appliances required by SOLAS Ch III
- Mandatory under SOLAS III/3.10 (from 1 Jul 1998)
- Says how equipment must be built and perform; SOLAS III says what each ship carries, drills and maintenance
- Testing: Revised Recommendation on Testing of LSA — MSC.81(70) (prototype and production tests)

**History**
- Adopted 4 Jun 1996 (MSC.48(66)) · in force 1 Jul 1998
- MSC.320(89) (2011): new release-and-retrieval system (on-load hook) requirements — existing hooks evaluated/replaced by first dry-dock after 1 Jul 2014
- MSC.535(107): totally enclosed lifeboat ventilation — lifeboats installed on/after 1 Jan 2029
- MSC.554(108): in force 1 Jan 2026 — lifejacket in-water performance (righting), release hooks, launching appliance lowering speeds
- MSC.402(96): maintenance, thorough examination, operational testing, overhaul and repair of survival craft, rescue boats, launching appliances and release gear — in force 1 Jan 2020
- Amendments adopted MSC 111 (May 2026): simulated launching of free-fall lifeboats — in force 1 Jan 2028
- MSC.559(108) (amends MSC.402(96) maintenance): annual thorough examination includes the lifeboat ventilation system — 1 Jan 2026

**Applies to**
- Life-saving appliances on SOLAS ships, installed per SOLAS Ch III
- Each amendment usually applies to appliances installed on/after its date (existing ones may stay)

---

**Chapters**
- Ch I General: definitions · general requirements (resistant to corrosion, highly visible colour, retro-reflective tape, operate −30 °C to +65 °C stowage, −1 to +30 °C sea water)

- Ch II Personal life-saving appliances
  - 2.1 Lifebuoys: outer diameter ≤800 mm, inner ≥400 mm · mass ≥2.5 kg · supports 14.5 kg iron for 24 h · self-igniting lights (≥2 cd, 2 h) · self-activating smoke signals (15 min) · buoyant lifeline (≥30 m)
  - 2.2 Lifejackets:
    - Adult: turn an unconscious person face-up within 5 s (from 2026: MSC.554 removes allowance for failed righting) · mouth ≥120 mm above water
    - Person can jump 4.5 m into water wearing it · don correctly within 1 min without help
    - Light (≥0.75 cd, 8 h) and whistle · sizes: infant (<15 kg), child (15–43 kg), adult (≥43 kg)
  - 2.3 Immersion suits: don unaided within 2 min · body core temperature falls ≤2 °C — uninsulated suit 1 h in 5 °C water, insulated suit 6 h in 0–2 °C water · jump 4.5 m
  - 2.4 Anti-exposure suits (rescue boat crews) · 2.5 Thermal protective aids (thermal conductance ≤7,800 W/(m²·K))

- Ch III Visual signals
  - 3.1 Rocket parachute flares: height ≥300 m · ≥30,000 cd · burn ≥40 s
  - 3.2 Hand flares: ≥15,000 cd · ≥1 min
  - 3.3 Buoyant smoke signals: orange smoke ≥3 min

- Ch IV Survival craft
  - 4.1 General liferaft requirements: withstand 18 m drop (or stowage height) · jump from 4.5 m onto it · capacity ≥6 persons · canopy
  - 4.2 Inflatable liferafts: two separate buoyancy tubes · container · painter · hydrostatic release unit (float-free)
  - 4.3 Rigid liferafts · 4.4 General lifeboat requirements:
    - Launch with full complement in still water at 5 knots ahead and 10° trim / 20° list
    - Boarding: cargo ships all persons ≤3 min from the order · passenger ships ≤10 min
    - Engine: start at −15 °C within 2 min · speed ≥6 knots in calm water (≥2 knots towing a 25-person liferaft) · fuel for 24 h
    - Release mechanism: off-load and on-load release, protected against accidental operation; hydrostatic interlock
  - 4.5 Partially enclosed lifeboats · 4.6 Totally enclosed lifeboats (self-righting; from 2029 ventilation 5 m³/h per person for 24 h) · 4.7 Free-fall lifeboats (certified free-fall height) · 4.8 Lifeboats with self-contained air support · 4.9 Fire-protected lifeboats (8 min in oil fire, water spray)

- Ch V Rescue boats
  - Length 3.8–8.5 m · carry 5 seated + 1 on stretcher · speed ≥6 knots for 4 h · tow 25-person liferaft at 2 knots
  - Fast rescue boats: 6–8.5 m · ≥20 knots (with 3 crew), ≥8 knots (with 6 persons)

- Ch VI Launching and embarkation appliances
  - Davit-launched: lower at speed per MSC.554 (safe max and min) · gravity or stored mechanical power · manual brake · recovery
  - Free-fall launching, marine evacuation systems (MES), embarkation ladders
  - Release hooks: new release-and-retrieval system requirements (MSC.320(89), MSC.554(108))

- Ch VII Other life-saving appliances
  - 7.1 Line-throwing appliances: ≥4 projectiles · each line ≥230 m · range ≥230 m in calm weather
  - 7.2 General alarm and public address systems (sound ≥80 dB(A) in accommodation, 10 dB above ambient)

**Linked: maintenance and servicing**
- SOLAS III/20 · MSC.402(96): lifeboats, rescue boats, davits, release gear — weekly/monthly ship checks; annual thorough examination and 5-yearly overhaul and load test by manufacturer or authorized provider
- Liferafts and HRUs: serviced every 12 months (≤17 months approved) at approved service station

**Key Point**
- Lifejacket, immersion suit and lifeboat standards here; drill frequency and on-board numbers in SOLAS Ch III
- From 1 Jan 2026: new lifejackets must right an unconscious person; new hooks/launching appliances meet MSC.554
- From 1 Jan 2029: new totally enclosed lifeboats need ventilation — annual check includes it (from 2026)
- PSC hot spots: release gear, falls, engine start, liferaft service dates, HRU expiry
