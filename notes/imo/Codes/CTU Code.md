# CTU Code — IMO/ILO/UNECE Code of Practice for Packing of Cargo Transport Units

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Published as | IMO circular **MSC.1/Circ.1497**, dated **16 Dec 2014** |
| Approved by | UNECE Inland Transport Committee (76th session, 25–27 Feb 2014) · IMO MSC 93 (14–23 May 2014) · ILO Governing Body (322nd session, 30 Oct–13 Nov 2014) |
| Developed by | IMO, ILO and UNECE jointly. A Group of Experts revised the earlier guidelines in 2011–2013. |
| Replaces | The 1997 IMO/ILO/UNECE Guidelines for Packing of CTUs. The circular **revokes MSC/Circ.787**. |
| Status | **Non-mandatory** global code of practice for sea and land transport |
| Aim | Advice on safe packing and securing of cargo in CTUs, for packers, trainers and everyone in the supply chain up to the unpacker |
| Structure | 13 chapters, 10 annexes, 5 appendices |

![[diagram:ctu-structure|Layout of the CTU Code]]

> **Key Point:** The CTU Code is guidance, not law (see 1.1.3 below), but flag States, ports and companies use it as the standard of good practice for packing containers and other cargo transport units.

## Purpose and scope (Chapter 1)
- **1.1.1:** advice on safe packing of CTUs for people who pack and secure cargo and for those who train them, with the theory and practical measures
- **1.1.2:** guidance for all parties in the supply chain, up to and including those who unpack the CTU
- **1.1.3:** not intended to conflict with, replace or supersede existing national or international regulations
- Poor packing, blocking, bracing or lashing can injure people and damage cargo and equipment, even though a CTU reduces some physical hazards

## Who does what (Chapter 4)
![[diagram:ctu-chain|Chain of responsibility]]

| Party | Duty (from Ch 4 and the CTU Code quick guide) |
|---|---|
| Consignor | Protective packaging where appropriate |
| **Packer** | Checks the CTU and cargo for damage and pests, places and secures the cargo, determines the gross mass of the packed CTU (11.3.2) |
| **Shipper** | Classifies and declares the cargo, seals the CTU, makes sure documents are accurate and reach the carrier in time (11.1.2, 11.3.1) |
| Carrier (4.2.8) | Secures the CTU on the means of transport, transports it per agreements and regulations, provides trained personnel |
| Consignee (4.2.9) | Checks the atmosphere before entry, ventilates, returns the CTU empty and clean unless agreed otherwise |
| All parties (4.2.12) | Pass on CTU number, seal number, verified gross mass and an accurate cargo description |

## Forces on cargo (Chapter 5)
![[diagram:ctu-forces|Acceleration coefficients c (a = c · g)]]

- Force is **F = m · a**, and acceleration **a = c · g**, with g = 9.81 m/s² (5.2)
- **Mass and friction alone do not secure cargo**, because vibration can make it move. Lashing is needed where blocking is not possible (5.4).
- Cargo is secured for the worst combination of horizontal and vertical acceleration, each horizontal direction separately (5.3)
- **Sea areas by significant wave height (20-year return)** (5.5): **A** Hs ≤ 8 m · **B** 8 m < Hs ≤ 12 m · **C** Hs > 12 m

| Mode | Longitudinal, forward / rearward (cx) | Transverse (cy) | Vertical down, minimum (cz) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Road | 0.8 / 0.5 | 0.5 | 1.0 |
| Rail | 0.5 (1.0 for shocks ≤ 150 ms) | 0.5 | 1.0 (0.7 for shocks ≤ 150 ms) |
| **Sea A** | 0.3 | 0.5 | 0.5 for longitudinal, 1.0 for transverse |
| **Sea B** | 0.3 | 0.7 | 0.3 for longitudinal, 1.0 for transverse |
| **Sea C** | 0.4 | 0.8 | 0.2 for longitudinal, 1.0 for transverse |

## Packing (Chapter 9)
- **9.1.1 Planning:** plan in advance, segregate incompatible cargo, observe special handling, do not exceed the maximum payload, and respect limits on concentrated loads and centre-of-gravity eccentricity
- **9.2.1 Securing materials:** strong enough, in good condition, suitable for CTU and goods, and meeting phytosanitary standards
- **9.3.1 Principles:** distribute the load properly and use stowage suited to the cargo
- **9.4.1 Securing:** tight stowage must not overstress the CTU boundaries. Weak-boundary CTUs need securing forces from the arrangement itself. Large, heavy or shaped packages are secured individually.
- **9.5.1 Bulk:** observe liquid filling ratios, check tank fittings for compatibility, avoid overstressing the CTU with solid bulk

## On completion of packing (Chapter 11)
- **11.1.2 Seal:** for international transport the shipper seals the CTU immediately after packing with a seal bearing a unique number. Some countries may require **ISO 17712** seals.
- **11.1.3 Tracking devices:** securely fitted. Powered devices must be of a certified safe type. SOLAS requires no ignition sources in enclosed cargo spaces holding highly flammable goods at sea.
- **11.3.2 Gross mass:** the packer determines the gross mass of the packed CTU by any prescribed method
- **11.3.3:** the packer tells the shipper the **CTU number, verified gross mass and seal number**, so they appear on all transport documents
- **11.3.4:** if cargo projects beyond the CTU, state the exact maximum overheight, overwidth or overlength
- **11.3.5:** containers with an allowable stacking mass **under 192,000 kg** must be flagged to the carrier if shipped by sea
- **11.3.6:** for sea transport a container/vehicle packing certificate may be required, certifying compliance with dangerous goods rules

## Training (Chapter 13)
- **13.1.1:** effective use of the Code depends on initial and retraining programmes for everyone involved in packing
- **13.1.2:** in-house, external or e-learning, with external providers able to meet the Code's requirements
- **13.1.4:** training is completed before staff take immediate responsibility
- **13.3.1:** training matches each person's responsibilities. Untrained staff work only under direct supervision of a trained person.
- **13.3.2:** covers consequences of poor packing, legal requirements, transport forces and basic packing principles. Topics are in Annex 10.
- **13.4:** training records kept in line with local practice
