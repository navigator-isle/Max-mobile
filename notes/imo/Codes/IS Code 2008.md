# 2008 IS Code — International Code on Intact Stability, 2008

## At a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 4 Dec 2008, resolution MSC.267(85), at MSC 85 |
| Mandatory | Introduction and **Part A**, through SOLAS (MSC.269(85)) and the 1988 Load Lines Protocol (MSC.270(85)) |
| In force | **1 Jul 2010** |
| Part B | Recommendatory; governments are asked to use it as a basis for standards unless national requirements give at least equivalent safety |
| Applies to | Ships and other marine vehicles of **24 m in length and over** (unless stated otherwise) |
| Ship types | Cargo ships, ships with timber deck cargo, passenger ships, fishing vessels, special purpose ships, offshore supply vessels, MODUs, pontoons, containerships and ships carrying containers on deck |

## What it is

- One document consolidating mandatory (Introduction and Part A) and recommended (Part B) intact stability provisions, based mainly on existing IMO instruments.
- The preamble notes that the criteria reflect the best concepts at the time, should be re-evaluated periodically, and that dynamic stability in a seaway is not fully understood.
- Since 2018/2020 amendments it also covers ships **engaged in anchor handling, towing and escort, and lifting operations**.

- **2016/2018 amendments (Introduction paragraph 1.2):** new subparagraphs 1.2.7 to 1.2.9 for ships engaged in anchor handling, towing and escort, and lifting operations. New definitions 2.27 to 2.31: anchor handling; harbour towing; coastal or ocean-going towing; lifting operation (fishing vessels are not included); escort operation.
- **Second-generation intact stability criteria:** SDC 7 (Feb 2020) approved interim guidelines for five dynamic stability failure modes in waves — pure loss of stability, parametric roll, surf-riding/broaching, dead ship condition and excessive accelerations — covering vulnerability criteria, direct stability failure assessment and operational measures. MSC 102 (4–11 Nov 2020) approved them for use in practice.

## General criteria (Part A, chapter 2)

![[diagram:is-gz-criteria|Righting lever curve criteria (2.2), schematic and not to scale]]

**2.1 General**
- Criteria apply to all loading conditions in Part B 3.3 and 3.4 (2.1.1).
- **Free surface effects** are accounted for in all loading conditions (2.1.2).
- A safe stability margin at all stages of the voyage, allowing for water absorption and icing (2.1.5).
- Each ship has an Administration-approved **stability booklet** (2.1.6).

**2.2 Righting lever (GZ) curve**

| Criterion | Value |
|---|---|
| Area under GZ, 0 to 30° (2.2.1) | at least **0.055 m·rad** |
| Area under GZ, 0 to 40° or downflooding angle if less (2.2.1) | at least **0.09 m·rad** |
| Area between 30° and 40° or downflooding angle (2.2.1) | at least **0.03 m·rad** |
| GZ at 30° or more (2.2.2) | at least **0.2 m** |
| Angle of maximum GZ (2.2.3) | **25° or more** (alternative criteria with Administration approval) |
| Initial metacentric height GM₀ (2.2.4) | at least **0.15 m** |

## Severe wind and rolling (weather) criterion (2.3)

![[diagram:is-weather|Steady wind and gust levers against the GZ curve (2.3), schematic]]

- The ship is tested for combined beam wind and rolling. **Area b** (margin from the roll-to-windward angle φ1 to the second intercept) must be at least **area a** (from the steady-wind equilibrium heel φ0 to the roll angle) (2.3.1).
- Steady wind heel angle φ0 must not exceed **16°** or **80% of the deck-edge immersion angle**, whichever is less (2.3.1).
- Wind pressure **P = 504 Pa** (may be reduced for restricted service with Administration approval). Steady wind lever lw1 = P·A·Z / (1000·g·Δ); gust lever lw2 = **1.5 × lw1** (2.3.2). A is the projected lateral area above the waterline, Z the vertical distance from the centre of A to the centre of the underwater lateral area (or about half the mean draught), Δ displacement.
- The roll angle tables (2.3.4) are based on ships with B/d below 3.5, (KG/d − 1) between −0.3 and 0.5, and roll period T below 20 s; other ships may need model tests (2.3.5, MSC.1/Circ.1200).

## Special criteria (Part A, chapter 3)

| Ship | Criterion |
|---|---|
| Passenger ships (3.1) | Meet 2.2 and 2.3, plus: heel from crowding of passengers to one side not more than **10°** (minimum 75 kg per passenger; passenger centre of gravity 1 m above deck standing, 0.3 m above seat when seated); heel from turning not more than **10°** |
| Oil tankers, 5,000 dwt and above (3.2) | Regulation 27 of MARPOL Annex I |
| Timber deck cargo (3.3) | 2.2 and 2.3, or the alternative in 3.3.2: GZ area to 40° (or flooding angle) at least **0.08 m·rad**; maximum GZ at least **0.25 m**; GM at least **0.1 m** at all times, allowing for water absorption and icing; the 16° steady-heel limit applies but the 80% deck-edge immersion limit may be ignored |
| Grain in bulk (3.4) | International Grain Code (MSC.23(59)) |
| High-speed craft (3.5) | 1994 HSC Code (MSC.36(63)) or 2000 HSC Code (MSC.97(73)), depending on construction date |

## Part B: selected guidance

- **Stability instruments (4.1):** an approved instrument supplements, and does not replace, the approved stability booklet (4.1.1.2). Three software types: Type 1 intact stability only; Type 2 adds a damage check against a limit curve or approved loading conditions; Type 3 applies pre-programmed damage cases to each loading condition (4.1.3). At least four loading conditions from the booklet are test conditions (4.1.6.2.2); one approved condition is tested at each annual survey and all at each renewal survey (4.1.9).
- **Operational provisions (chapter 5):** meeting the criteria does not guarantee immunity from capsizing (5.1.1). Watertight and weathertight closures stay closed at sea unless needed for operations (5.2.2). In following, quartering and head seas change speed or course (5.3.3). Excessive GM can cause harmful accelerations (5.1.6).
- **Icing (chapter 6):** allowance **30 kg/m²** on exposed weather decks and gangways and **7.5 kg/m²** on the projected lateral area of each side above the waterplane (6.3.1).
- **Weathertight integrity (chapter 7):** hatch coamings on exposed working decks at least 300 mm high at 12 m length and 600 mm at 24 m (7.1.3.8); non-wood hatch covers designed for 10 kN/m² or the cargo load (7.1.3.2).
- **Lightship (chapter 8):** every passenger ship and every cargo ship of 24 m or more is inclined on completion (8.1.1). Lightweight surveys at intervals not exceeding five years on passenger ships (8.1.5). Inclining test: ship nearly upright (list up to half a degree), test weights giving 1° to 4° of heel to each side (8.2.2.7, 8.2.2.8); standard test uses eight weight movements (8.4.1.2).

> **Key Point:** Every loading condition must pass the 2.2 GZ criteria and the 2.3 weather criterion, with free surface correction applied, and the stability booklet must be approved. Check with the approved loading instrument, and apply the special criteria for passenger ships, timber deck cargo and grain.

## Not included (not verified against the original text)

- The circular number of the second-generation interim guidelines (the sources did not give it); levels 1 and 2 of the criteria.
- Paragraphs 2.2.5 to 2.2.7 and Part B chapter 2 design criteria (fishing vessels, pontoons, containerships over 100 m, offshore supply vessels, MODUs).
- Content of the 2016 operational criteria for anchor handling, towing and lifting ships beyond the definitions.
- The old note's references to the A.749(18) predecessor and older resolutions, and the 2015–2016 "anchor handling" criteria wording.
