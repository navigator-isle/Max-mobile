**MASS Code — International Code of Safety for Maritime Autonomous Surface Ships**

**What is the MASS Code**
- First international framework for ships whose key functions are autonomous or remotely operated from a Remote Operations Centre (ROC)
- Goal-based and technology-neutral; aims at a level of safety and security expected of a conventional ship
- **Supplementary**: covers MASS operations and functions only where SOLAS, COLREG, STCW, MARPOL etc. are not adequate
- Non-mandatory first version, written so it can later become mandatory

**History**
- Regulatory scoping exercise 2017–2021 (MSC 98–103); MSC–LEG–FAL Joint Working Group
- Adopted 21 May 2026 at MSC 111, resolution MSC.595(111)
- Effective (voluntary) 1 Jul 2026, followed by an experience-building phase
- Mandatory MASS Code planned: entry into force 1 Jan 2032 at the earliest (timeline may be revisited)

**Applies to**
- Cargo ships to which SOLAS Chapter I applies, and their ROCs, that have systems enabling autonomous or remote operation — when the Administration finds the other instruments impracticable or insufficient
- Not passenger ships (first version)

---

**Part I — Introduction**
- Ch 1 Purpose, principles (supplementary, holistic, goal-based, non-mandatory, technology-neutral) and objectives (human oversight, safe coexistence with all ships, responsibility)
- Ch 2 Application · Ch 3 Code structure · Ch 4 Definitions (MASS, MASS function, ROC, remote operator, operational context, fallback / minimum risk condition)

**Part II — Principles for MASS and MASS functions**
- Ch 5 Surveys and certificates: **MASS Safety Certificate**, **MASS ROC Certificate**, provisional certificates, validity (forms in the Appendix)
- Ch 6 Approval process (by the Administration, function by function)
- Ch 7 Risk assessment · Ch 8 Operational context (where, when and in what conditions the ship may operate)
- Ch 9 System design · Ch 10 Software principles
- Ch 11 Management of safe operations (link to ISM; overriding authority of the master)
- Ch 12 Special measures to enhance maritime security (incl. cyber)
- Ch 13 Alert management · Ch 14 Manning, training and watchkeeping · Ch 15 Maintenance

**Part III — Goals, functional requirements and expected performances**
- Ch 16 Safety of navigation (lookout, COLREG compliance, voyage planning)
- Ch 17 Connectivity (redundant links; safe behaviour when a link is lost)
- Ch 18 Remote operations (ROC design, staffing, hand-over)
- Ch 19 Structure, subdivision, stability and watertight integrity
- Ch 20 Fire protection, detection and extinction
- Ch 21 Search and rescue (how a MASS assists persons in distress)
- Ch 22 Carriage of cargoes · Ch 23 Anchoring, towing and mooring · Ch 24 Machinery and electrical installations

**The master (Ch 14)**
- Regardless of the mode of operation, **the master is responsible for the safe operation of the MASS**
- The master may be on board or in an ROC; must receive alerts and be able to intervene

**Degrees of autonomy (IMO scoping, for reference)**
- 1 crewed ship with automated processes · 2 remotely controlled with seafarers on board · 3 remotely controlled without seafarers on board · 4 fully autonomous

**Key Point**
- Voluntary from 1 Jul 2026; mandatory version targeted for 2032
- Certificates: MASS Safety Certificate (ship) and MASS ROC Certificate (control centre)
- One master always responsible — on board or ashore; COLREG still applies to every ship
