# MODU Code 2009 — Code for the Construction and Equipment of Mobile Offshore Drilling Units

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | **2 Dec 2009**, IMO Assembly Resolution **A.1023(26)** (26th session, on MSC 86 recommendation) |
| Applies to | MODUs whose keels are laid, or which are at a similar stage of construction, on or after **1 Jan 2012** |
| Replaces | 1989 MODU Code (A.649(16)), which had replaced the 1979 Code (A.414(XI)) |
| Status | A recommendation to governments. Governments are asked to give effect to it and to consider it **equivalent, for MODUs, to the technical requirements of SOLAS and Load Lines**. |
| Certificate | Mobile Offshore Drilling Unit Safety Certificate (2009), valid for up to **5 years** |
| Structure | 14 chapters in the original text, plus the model certificate. The 2024 edition also lists a Chapter 15, "Special measures to enhance safety". |

![[diagram:modu-structure|The 14 chapters of the 2009 MODU Code]]

> **Key Point:** The MODU Code gives a drilling unit the same level of safety that SOLAS and the Load Lines Convention give a conventional ship on an international voyage. It is a Code for governments to apply, not a convention.

## Definition and aim
- **MODU:** a vessel capable of drilling for resources beneath the seabed, such as hydrocarbons, sulphur or salt (1.3.40)
- The Code aims at safety equivalent to SOLAS and the 1988 Load Lines Protocol for conventional ships
- Existing units that comply may be certified, and are not barred only for non-conformity (preamble)

## Surveys and certificate (Chapter 1)
![[diagram:modu-surveys|Survey cycle for a MODU]]

| Survey | Timing (1.6.1) |
|---|---|
| Initial | Before the unit enters service or first certification |
| Renewal | At intervals not exceeding **5 years** |
| Intermediate | Within 3 months of the 2nd or 3rd anniversary date |
| Annual | Within 3 months of each anniversary date |
| Dry-dock | At least **2 in any 5-year period**, with no interval over **36 months** |
| Radio | Under section 11.9 |

- Certificate is issued after an initial or renewal survey (1.6.7). The Administration keeps full responsibility.
- A Contracting Government may issue it at the Administration's request (1.6.9)
- Validity set by the Administration, **not more than 5 years** (1.6.11.1)
- The certificate ceases to be valid if required surveys are not done, it is not endorsed, or the unit changes flag (1.6.11.8)
- Without a valid certificate the Code's privileges cannot be claimed (1.6.12)

### Equivalents and exemptions
- **Equivalents (1.5):** an alternative design, material, appliance or arrangement may be accepted if at least as effective. The Administration reports it to IMO.
- **Exemptions (1.4):** for units with novel features that the Code would impede, if safety is adequate. Listed on the certificate (1.6.8) and reported to IMO with reasons.

### Machinery and electrical (Chapters 4–7)
| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Static inclination, machinery (4.1.4) | Column-stabilized **15°**; self-elevating **10°**; surface **15°** either way plus up to **5°** trim |
| Electrical design inclination (5.4.15) | Column-stabilized **25°**; self-elevating **15°**; surface **22.5°** longitudinal and **10°** transverse |
| Dynamic angles, self-propelled (7.1.3) | Column-stabilized **22.5°**; self-elevating **15°**; surface **22.5°** roll with **7.5°** pitch |
| Main power (5.3.1) | At least **2** generating sets |
| Emergency lighting (5.4.6) | **18 h**; marking lights **4 days** |
| Emergency generator (5.4.8.3) | Starts and loads within **45 s**, or a transitional battery source is needed |
| Transitional battery (5.4.10) | At least **half an hour** |
| Fuel oil service tanks (4.8.6) | **2** per fuel type, each at least **8 h** at rated load |
| Ballast, column-stabilized (4.10.2) | Maximum operating draught to severe storm draught within **3 h** |
| Ballast pumps (4.10.3) | At least **2** independent pumps |
| Steering, main (7.5.3) | Rudder **35°** each side, 35° to 30° opposite side in not more than **28 s** |
| Steering, auxiliary (7.5.7) | **15°** each side in not more than **60 s** |

### Hazardous areas (Chapter 6)
- **Zones 0, 1 and 2** by likelihood of ignitable gas or vapour (6.1)
- Hazardous mud processing spaces ventilated at **12 air changes per hour** (6.4.1)
- Temperature classes from **T1 (450 °C)** to **T6 (85 °C)** (6.6.7 table)
- Hazardous drilling well and mud areas need at least **Group IIA, class T3** equipment (6.6.7)

### Fire safety (Chapter 9)
| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Fire pumps (9.7.1) | At least **2** independently driven power pumps drawing directly from the sea |
| Pump location (9.7.3) | A fire in one space must not disable both pumps |
| Pump output (9.7.5) | **2 jets** while keeping at least **0.35 N/mm²** at any hydrant |
| Machinery of **750 kW** or more (9.8.2) | Fixed extinguishing system and portable foam extinguishers |
| Fire-fighter's outfits (9.13.1) | At least **2**, with oxygen and flammable vapour instruments |
| H₂S alarm (9.12.1) | If unanswered within **2 min**, it triggers the toxic gas alarm and helideck status light |
| Helideck (9.16.4) | At least 2 dry powder extinguishers totalling **45 kg** or more, and foam at **6 l/m²** for at least **5 min** |

### Life-saving appliances (Chapter 10)
| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Lifeboats (10.3.1) | Each side holds the **total persons on board** |
| Liferafts (10.3.2) | Hold the total persons on board |
| Self-elevating and column-stabilized units (10.3.4–10.3.5) | Lifeboats in at least **2 widely separated locations**, plus liferafts for all |
| Survival craft height (10.6.2) | At least **2 m** above the waterline in the limiting damaged condition |
| Launch preparation (10.6.1.3) | **2 crew** in under **5 min** |
| Lifeboat launch (10.7.8) | Full complement within **10 min** of the abandon signal |
| Rescue boat (10.8–10.9) | At least one, ready to launch within **5 min** |
| Lifejackets (10.11.1) | One per person plus extras for duty stations |
| Immersion suits (10.12.1) | One per person plus extras for duty and remote craft positions |
| Lifebuoys (10.13) | From **8** (under 100 m) to **14** (200 m and over). At least half with self-igniting lights, at least 2 with smoke signals. |

### What MSC.435(98) changed (units on or after 1 Jan 2020)
- **Ch 1:** new definition of **"H" class divisions**, tested against a hydrocarbon fire curve
- **Ch 6:** dynamic positioning shutdown logic revised so well integrity and station keeping are kept (6.5.2). Hazardous-area equipment repaired by qualified personnel and kept in a **register** (6.6.3).
- **Ch 9:** A-60 becomes **H-60** (9.2.4). Radiant heat at escape routes from a drill floor fire limited to **2.5 kW/m²** (9.4.5). New section 9.8 on fixed water spray or monitor protection for the drill floor.
- **Ch 10:** lifeboat design assumes **95 kg** average person and **265 mm** seat radius (10.3.7–10.3.8). At least one rescue boat on each unit, and **a lifeboat cannot be accepted as the rescue boat** (10.8).
- **Ch 14:** where a master is assigned, the master is in charge at all times (14.10.3). **Man overboard drills at least quarterly**, with quarterly lowering of a training liferaft for davit-launched liferafts (14.13). Records of the hazardous-area equipment register (14.16). New 14.17 limits portable or spark-producing equipment in zones 0, 1 and 2 unless certified or the area is free of flammable vapours.
