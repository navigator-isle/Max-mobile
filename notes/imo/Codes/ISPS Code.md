# ISPS Code — International Ship and Port Facility Security Code

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Full name | International Code for the Security of Ships and of Port Facilities |
| Adopted | December 2002 (Conference on Maritime Security) |
| In force | 1 Jul 2004 |
| Legal basis | SOLAS chapter XI-2 (regulation XI-2/2) |
| Part A | **Mandatory** |
| Part B | **Recommendatory** guidance |
| Key people | CSO (Company Security Officer) · SSO (Ship Security Officer) · PFSO (Port Facility Security Officer) |
| Ship certificate | International Ship Security Certificate (ISSC), up to 5 years |

![[diagram:isps-flow|From assessment to certificate]]

## What the Code does
- Creates an international framework of co-operation between Contracting Governments, ships and port facilities to detect security threats and take preventive measures
- Only the SOLAS Contracting Governments are bound. IMO does not impose penalties or keep a blacklist; enforcement is by governments.

> **Key Point:** Part A is mandatory. Drills every 3 months and the 25% crew change rule are in **Part B** (guidance), so check what your flag State requires.

## Application (Part A, 3)
- Passenger ships, **cargo ships including high-speed craft of 500 GT and above**, and mobile offshore drilling units, on international voyages
- Port facilities serving those ships
- **Not** covered: warships, naval auxiliaries, government ships on non-commercial service
- Domestic voyages are left to each government

## Security levels
![[diagram:isps-levels|The three security levels]]

| Level | Meaning (Part A, 2.1) |
|---|---|
| 1 | Minimum appropriate protective security measures maintained at all times |
| 2 | Additional protective measures for a period, because of a heightened risk of a security incident |
| 3 | Further specific protective measures for a limited period, when an incident is probable or imminent |

- Governments set the security levels (4.1), considering how credible, corroborated, specific or imminent the threat is and the possible consequences
- Levels can move directly from 1 to 3. Level 3 should last only as long as the threat.
- The ship acts upon the levels set by the Contracting Government (7.1)

### Level 1 activities on all ships (Part A, 7.2)
- Ensuring performance of all ship security duties
- Controlling access to the ship
- Controlling embarkation of persons and their effects
- Monitoring restricted areas so only authorized persons enter
- Monitoring deck areas and areas surrounding the ship
- Supervising handling of cargo and ship's stores
- Ensuring security communication is readily available

At levels 2 and 3, the additional or further measures **specified in the ship security plan** are implemented for each of these activities (7.3, 7.4).

## Part A — Mandatory
| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| 4 Governments | Set security levels · test approved plans to the extent they consider appropriate (4.4) |
| 5 Declaration of Security (DoS) | Agreement between ship and port facility, completed when the government or a ship considers it necessary (5.1, 5.4) · signed and dated, states duration, security level(s) and contacts · in English, French, Spanish or a language common to the parties (5.5) |
| 6 Company | **Master's overriding authority** and responsibility on ship safety and security, and the right to request Company help (6.1) · Company supports CSO, master and SSO (6.2) |
| 7 Ship security | Measures at each level |
| 8 Ship Security Assessment (SSA) | CSO ensures it is done (8.1) · covers physical security, structural integrity, personnel protection systems, procedures, radio and telecom systems, other vulnerable areas (8.3) · report protected from unauthorized access (8.12) |
| 9 Ship Security Plan (SSP) | Approved by the Administration (9.1) · provides for all three levels · protected from unauthorized access or disclosure (9.7) |
| 10 Records | Kept on board at least the minimum period set by the Administration (10.1) · any format, protected from unauthorized access (10.2) |
| 11 CSO | Company designates a CSO (11.1) |
| 12 SSO | Designated on each ship (12.1) · regular security inspections, maintains the SSP, reports security incidents (12.2) |
| 13 Training, drills, exercises | Drills at appropriate intervals (13.4) |
| 14–18 | Port facility security, PFSA, PFSP, PFSO |
| 19 Verification and certification | See below |

### What the SSP contains (Part A, 9.2 and 9.4)
- Ship's security organizational structure and its relationships with the Company, port facilities, other ships and authorities
- Communication systems for effective continuous communication
- Basic measures for level 1, and additional measures for levels 2 and 3
- Regular review or audit of the SSP, and reporting to Contracting Government contact points
- Location of the SSAS activation points (9.4.17)
- Procedures for using the SSAS: testing, activation, deactivation, resetting, limiting false alerts (9.4.18)

### Records kept on board (Part A, 10.1)
- Training, drills and exercises
- Security threats and security incidents
- Breaches of security
- Changes in security level
- Communications directly related to ship security
- Internal audits and reviews of security activities
- Periodic reviews of the SSA and the SSP
- Implementation of plan amendments
- Maintenance, calibration and testing of security equipment, including the SSAS

### Confidentiality of the SSP (9.8)
- Officers authorized by a Contracting Government generally **cannot inspect** the SSP
- Limited access to specific sections only where there are clear grounds for non-compliance, and only with the consent of the government concerned or the master
- Provisions on the SSAS are confidential unless the governments agree otherwise

## Certification (Part A, 19)
![[diagram:isps-cycle|ISSC verification cycle]]

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| ISSC validity | Period set by the Administration, **not more than 5 years** (19.3.1) |
| Renewal verification | At intervals set by the Administration, not exceeding 5 years (19.1.1.2) |
| Intermediate verification | At least one. If only one, between the 2nd and 3rd anniversary (19.1.1.3) |
| Interim ISSC | Valid 6 months or until the full certificate is issued, whichever is first. **Cannot be extended** (19.4.4) |

### Conditions for an Interim ISSC (19.4.2)
- SSA completed
- Copy of the SSP on board, submitted for approval and being implemented
- SSAS fitted, if required
- CSO has arranged drills, exercises and internal audits so the ship passes its verification within 6 months
- Master, SSO and personnel with security duties know their duties and the plan, in a language they understand

## Part B — Guidance (recommendatory)
- Drills at least once every 3 months (B/13.6)
- If more than 25 percent of personnel have changed, with people who have not drilled on that ship in the last 3 months, a drill within 1 week of the change (B/13.6)
- Exercises at least once each calendar year, no more than 18 months apart (B/13.7)

## Linked SOLAS XI-2 rules
- **XI-2/6 Ship Security Alert System (SSAS):** ship-to-shore alert to the authority designated by the flag. It raises **no alarm on board**. Activated from the bridge and at least one other location.
- **XI-2/8 Master's discretion:** if, in the master's professional judgement, safety and security requirements conflict, the master gives effect to those needed to maintain the safety of the ship. The master may take interim security measures and must promptly inform the Administration and, where relevant, the port State.
- **XI-2/9 Control and compliance:** port States may delay, detain, restrict operations, expel or refuse entry to non-compliant ships. Authorities must avoid undue delay.

## Not included (not verified against the original text)
- Rule that the ship's security level must be at least the port's
- Records language and DoS retention periods
- Part B guidance on pre-arrival information and the Continuous Synopsis Record
- Cyber risk guidance links, USCG 33 CFR Part 104
- SOLAS XI-1 (ship number, CSR) and V/19 (AIS) details
- Wording of XI-2/5 and XI-2/10
- The statement "adopted 12 Dec 2002" (source gives December 2002)
