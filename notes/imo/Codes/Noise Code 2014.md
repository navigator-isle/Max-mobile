# Noise Code 2014 — Code on Noise Levels on Board Ships

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | **30 Nov 2012**, MSC resolution **MSC.337(91)** (MSC 91) |
| In force | **1 Jul 2014**, with SOLAS regulation **II-1/3-12** |
| Status | **Mandatory under SOLAS**, but some parts are recommendatory or informative (1.1.3) |
| Applies to | **New ships of 1,600 GT and above** (1.3.1) |
| Existing ships 1,600 GT+ | Hazardous-noise, mitigation and PPE provisions may be applied as far as reasonable and practicable (1.3.2) |
| Ships under 1,600 GT | The Code may be applied to new ships, on the same basis (1.3.3) |
| Structure | 7 chapters and 4 appendices |
| Interpretations | MSC.1/Circ.1509, 5 Jun 2015 |

## Maximum noise levels (4.2)
| Space | 1,600 to < 10,000 GT | 10,000 GT and above |
|---|---|---|
| **Machinery spaces** | 110 | 110 |
| Machinery control rooms | 75 | 75 |
| Workshops (outside machinery spaces) | 85 | 85 |
| Non-specified work spaces | 85 | 85 |
| **Navigating bridge and chartrooms** | 65 | 65 |
| Look-out posts, including bridge wings and windows | 70 | 70 |
| Radio rooms (no audio signals) | 60 | 60 |
| Radar rooms | 65 | 65 |
| **Cabins and hospitals** | 60 | **55** |
| Messrooms | 65 | 60 |
| Recreation rooms | 65 | 60 |
| Open recreation areas | 75 | 75 |
| Offices | 65 | 60 |
| Galleys (food processing equipment off) | 75 | 75 |
| Serveries and pantries | 75 | 75 |
| Normally unoccupied spaces | 90 | 90 |

- Values are dB(A) and are **maximum levels, not targets** (4.1.1)
- Each space is assessed with the equivalent continuous level **LAeq** before the ship enters service (4.1.2)

## Hearing protection and exposure
![[diagram:noise-signs|Entrance warning notices (7.4)]]

- **Above 85 dB(A):** personnel should wear hearing protectors (4.1.3, 5.1.2)
- **Peak limit (5.1.4):** no crew member exposed unprotected to peaks above **135 dB(C)**
- **Protector standard (7.2.2):** should reduce the level to **85 dB(A) or less**, selected by the HML method (ISO 4869-2). Noise-cancelling headsets qualify only if they perform like passive protectors when unpowered.
- **Warning notices (7.4)** at entrances to spaces above 85 dB(A):

| Level | Notice |
|---|---|
| 80 to 85 dB(A) | High noise level, use hearing protectors |
| 85 to 110 dB(A) | Dangerous noise, protectors mandatory |
| 110 to 115 dB(A) | Short stay only, protectors mandatory |
| Above 115 dB(A) | No stay longer than **10 minutes**, protectors mandatory |

### Exposure zones (5.3)
| Zone | Rule |
|---|---|
| **A** | No exposure above **120 dB(A)** or Lex(24) above **105 dB(A)**, even with protectors |
| **B** | Occasional exposure only, with protectors giving **25 to 35 dB(A)** attenuation |
| **C** | Occasional exposure only, protectors giving at least **25 dB(A)** |
| **D** | Daily exposure allowed with protectors giving at least **25 dB(A)** |
| **E** | Unprotected. Exposures under 8 hours should not exceed **85 dB(A)**. Longer stays: Lex(24) at or below **80 dB(A)**, with at least a third of each 24 hours below **75 dB(A)**. |

- **Alternative (5.4):** unprotected 24-hour equivalent at or below 80 dB(A). Protected daily exposure in high-noise spaces not more than **4 hours continuously or 8 hours total**.
- **Hearing conservation programme (5.5):** optional for spaces above 85 dB(A LAeq), with audiometric testing, training and records
- **SMS (5.1.3):** include a hearing protection policy, logged training and exposure limits

## Measurement
- **Timing (3.1.1):** on completion of the ship or as soon as practicable after, in sea-trial and port conditions
- **Sea trial (3.3.2):** normal service speed and not less than **80% of MCR**
- **LCeq and LCpeak** are also measured where LAeq exceeds 85 dB(A) (3.1.3)
- **Equipment (2.2.2):** calibrator and sound level meter **verified at least every two years** by a national standard laboratory or an ISO 17025 accredited laboratory. Meter calibrated with the calibrator **before and after** measurements (3.8).
- **Environment (3.5):** wind force **4** and wave height **1 m** should not be exceeded, or actual conditions are reported. Water depth less than 5 times the draught or nearby reflecting surfaces may affect readings and must be noted. Extraneous noise must not affect readings.
- **Exposure (3.7, 5.1.1):** determined per ISO 9612:2009, or by the simplified method in Appendix 4
- **Survey report (4.3):** one for each ship, with readings at each point marked on plans, **kept on board and accessible to the crew** (4.3.3), in the format of Appendix 1

## Acoustic insulation (6.2.1)
![[diagram:noise-rw|Minimum Rw between spaces]]

| Partition | Minimum Rw |
|---|---|
| Cabin to cabin | **35** |
| Messrooms, recreation rooms, public spaces and entertainment areas to cabins and hospitals | **45** |
| Corridor to cabin | **30** |
| Cabin to cabin with communicating door | **30** |

- The requirements apply to components within bulkheads, such as doors. A composite bulkhead is acceptable if each component meets the Rw, or if the combined Rw calculated from component areas and sound reduction indices is not below the requirement (MSC.1/Circ.1509 on 6.2.1).
- Decks meet the requirements together with their coverings, tested as installed, but ceiling panels are excluded (MSC.1/Circ.1509).

## Exclusions and flexibility
- **Not covered (1.3.4):** dynamically supported craft, high-speed craft, fishing vessels, pipe-laying barges, crane barges, MODUs, pleasure yachts not engaged in trade, warships and troopships, unpowered ships, pile drivers, dredgers
- **Dispensation (1.3.6):** by the Administration where compliance is documented as impossible despite reasonable measures. Cabins are excluded unless exceptional circumstances apply.
- **Short voyages (1.3.7):** 4.2.3 and 4.2.4 may apply only in port condition if rest periods are adequate
- **Passenger spaces (1.3.8):** not intended to apply, except work spaces within them
- **Major conversions (1.3.9):** changed areas meet the Code for new ships as far as the Administration finds reasonable
- **Out of scope (1.3.10):** wind, wave and ice noise, alarms and public address systems

## Interpretations (MSC.1/Circ.1509, 5 Jun 2015)
- Passenger spaces also used by crew, such as recreation rooms, count as "other passenger spaces" and are outside the Code. Adjacent crew cabin and hospital bulkheads and decks must still meet Chapter 6.
- Navigating bridge wings include enclosed navigating bridge spaces
- A bridge with radio equipment is still a navigating bridge (**65 dB(A)**). Radio rooms are separate rooms dedicated to radio messages.
- A day-room and bedroom serving one occupant count as **one cabin**
- Bathrooms and toilets count as sources of airborne sound, not as cabins
- During measurement keep air conditioning vents and cabin door grilles open unless they normally operate closed
- The uncertainty provision (3.9) acknowledges uncertainty, it is **not an allowance**
