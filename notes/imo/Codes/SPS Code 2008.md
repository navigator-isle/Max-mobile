# SPS Code 2008 — Code of Safety for Special Purpose Ships

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | **13 May 2008**, MSC resolution **MSC.266(84)** (MSC 84) |
| Applies to | Special purpose ships of **500 GT or more** certified on or after **13 May 2008** |
| Replaces | The code in Assembly resolution **A.534(13)** for ships certified from that date |
| Status | Contracting Governments to SOLAS are invited to give effect to it. It is a recommendation, not a convention. |
| Certificate | **Special Purpose Ship Safety Certificate**, with Record of Equipment (Form SPS) |
| Idea | Safety equal to SOLAS 1974 for ships carrying **more than 12 special personnel** without treating them as passengers |
| Structure | Preamble, 11 chapters, Annex (certificate and record) |

![[diagram:sps-bands|What applies depends on persons on board]]

> **Key Point:** A special purpose ship carries **more than 12 special personnel** besides its crew. Special personnel are expected to be fit, know the ship's layout and have basic safety training, so the ship need not be treated as a passenger ship. What is required then depends on the **total number of persons on board**: more than 240, 60 to 240, or 60 or fewer.

## Definitions (Chapter 1)
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Special purpose ship** (1.3.12) | A mechanically self-propelled ship that, because of its function, carries **more than 12 special personnel** (500 GT or more per the preamble) |
| **Special personnel** (1.3.11) | People who are not passengers, crew or infants, carried for the ship's special purpose or special work. The count may include **up to 12 passengers**. |
| Examples | Research and survey staff, trainees, fish-processing staff on factory ships, salvage, diving, cable, pipe-laying, seismic and crane personnel |
| Passenger (1.3.8) | Anyone other than the master, crew or others engaged on ship's business, and children under one year |
| Crew (1.3.3) | People carried to navigate and maintain the ship or to serve others on board |

- A ship carrying more than 12 **SOLAS passengers** is a passenger ship, not a special purpose ship (footnote to 1.3.12)
- The Code does not set a cap on special personnel in its definition. **240 is a threshold of total persons**, not a limit on special personnel.

## Application (1.2) and flexibility
- **1.2.1:** ships of 500 GT or more certified on or after 13 May 2008. Administrations may apply it "as far as reasonable and practicable" to smaller and older ships.
- **1.2.2:** does not apply to ships that meet the **MODU Code**
- **1.2.3:** not intended for ships carrying **industrial personnel who do not work on board**
- **1.4 Exemptions:** a ship not normally special purpose may be exempted for one exceptional voyage if the safety measures suit that voyage
- **1.5 Equivalents:** accepted if at least as effective, and reported to IMO
- The preamble also extends voluntary use of the Code to **training ships**, whether or not SOLAS applies

## Chapters
| Ch | Subject | Main rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | General | Purpose, application, definitions, exemptions, equivalents, surveys, certification |
| 2 | Stability and subdivision | Intact: refers to Part B of the Intact Stability Code (2.1). Damage: SOLAS II-1 as a passenger ship with special personnel as passengers, with an R-value by persons certified |
| 3 | Machinery | SOLAS II-1 Part C. Steering gear per II-1/29: ships up to 240 persons may use 29.6.1.2, over 240 use 29.6.1.1 |
| 4 | Electrical | SOLAS II-1 Part D. Emergency power per II-1/43 for **60 or fewer** persons, II-1/42 for **more than 60** |
| 5 | Unattended machinery spaces | SOLAS II-1 Part E. Over **240** persons: Administration decides case by case |
| 6 | Fire protection | Over **240**: SOLAS II-2 for passenger ships carrying more than 36 passengers · **60 to 240**: passenger ships up to 36 passengers · **60 or fewer**: cargo ships |
| 7 | Dangerous goods | Ships' stores are generally outside the IMDG Code but IMDG guidance should be followed. Safety assessment for goods in use. |
| 8 | Life-saving appliances | SOLAS Chapter III. Over 60 persons: passenger ship standard. 60 or fewer: cargo ship standard. "Passenger" reads as "special personnel". |
| 9 | Radiocommunications | SOLAS Chapter IV as for cargo ships |
| 10 | Safety of navigation | SOLAS Chapter V |
| 11 | Security | SOLAS Chapter XI-2 |
| Annex | Certificate | Special Purpose Ship Safety Certificate, endorsements, Record of Equipment (Form SPS) |

## Damage stability by persons on board
![[diagram:sps-rvalue|R-value by certified persons]]

- The ship is treated as a **passenger ship** with special personnel counted as passengers
- **240 persons or more:** required index **R**
- **60 persons or fewer:** **0.8 R**
- **Between 60 and 240:** linear interpolation
- Since MSC.408(96), where full N = N1 + 2N2 is impractical for the service and the Administration finds a suitably reduced hazard, a lower N may be used, but **not below N = N1 + N2**

## Surveys and certificate (1.6–1.7)
- Surveys follow those for **SOLAS cargo ships other than tankers**, and cover the Code's provisions
- Certificate issued by the Administration or an authorized body. The Administration keeps **full responsibility**.
- Validity and duration follow the SOLAS **cargo ship** provisions
- Certificate for ships **under 500 GT** must state the relaxations accepted
- Every special purpose ship holds the Special Purpose Ship Safety Certificate. Ships on international voyages also carry the relevant SOLAS certificates.
