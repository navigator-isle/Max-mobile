**Grain Code — International Code for the Safe Carriage of Grain in Bulk**

**What is the Grain Code**
- IMO code on stability of ships carrying grain in bulk (grain shifts and causes heel)
- Mandatory under SOLAS Ch VI Part C (Reg 8–9)
- Ship needs a Document of Authorization (DoA) and approved grain loading stability information
- Grain: wheat, maize (corn), oats, rye, barley, rice, pulses, seeds and processed forms that behave like grain

**History**
- 1960/1974 SOLAS grain rules → Grain Code adopted 23 May 1991 (MSC.23(59)) · in force 1 Jan 1994
- MSC.552(108): new loading condition (holds partly filled at the hatch with ends untrimmed) and updated heeling moment calculation — in force 1 Jan 2026

**Applies to**
- Ships (regardless of size) carrying grain in bulk under SOLAS Ch VI Part C
- Not for: grain in bags (general cargo rules)

---

**Part A — Specific requirements**
- A 1 Application · A 2 Definitions:
  - Filled compartment, trimmed · filled compartment, untrimmed · partly filled compartment
  - Stowage factor (m³/t) · specially suitable compartment
- A 3 Document of Authorization (DoA): issued by the Administration (or RO) · ship cannot load grain without it (or must prove compliance to the loading port)
- A 4 Equivalents · A 5 Exemptions for certain voyages
- A 6 Grain loading information:
  - Curves/tables of grain heeling moments per compartment · maximum permissible heeling moments · typical loading conditions
- A 7 Stability requirements (intact, with assumed grain shift):
  - Angle of heel due to grain shift ≤12° (or the angle at which the deck edge immerses, if less; ships built ≥1994)
  - Residual area between heeling arm and righting arm curves up to the smaller of 40°, angle of flooding, or max difference angle ≥0.075 m·rad
  - Initial GM (corrected for free surface) ≥0.30 m
  - Before loading, the master must prove these criteria for departure and the worst voyage condition
- A 8 Stability requirements for existing ships
- A 9 Optional stability requirements for ships without documents of authorization (limited cargo)
- A 10 Stowage of bulk grain:
  - Filled compartments trimmed to fill all spaces under decks and hatch covers
  - "Filled, untrimmed" allowed for specially suitable compartments (end of holds)
  - Partly filled: surface levelled
- A 11–14 Securing of grain surface (partly filled compartments):
  - Overstowing (bags or other cargo on top), strapping or lashing, securing with wire mesh, bundles of bulk (saucers)
  - Shifting boards (longitudinal divisions) — design and strength
- A 15 Grain-tight subdivision and feeders

**Part B — Calculation of assumed heeling moments**
- B 1–6: assumed void spaces and shifting of grain surface
  - Filled, trimmed: grain surface shifts 15° from horizontal
  - Partly filled: grain surface shifts 25°
  - Void depth under hatch covers and deck, calculated per Code
- Volumetric heeling moment ÷ stowage factor = heeling moment (t·m)
- 2026: added partly-filled-at-hatch, ends-untrimmed condition and revised calculation (MSC.552(108))

**Grain Form (loading port)**
- Master submits grain stability calculation to the loading port authority (e.g., NCB/USCG in the US, AQIS/AMSA in Australia, Argentine/Brazilian authorities)
- Includes: DoA, displacement, KG, GM, heeling moments, angle of heel, residual area

**Key Point**
- No grain loading without DoA and approved grain stability booklet
- Check three criteria: heel ≤12° · residual area ≥0.075 m·rad · GM ≥0.30 m
- Partly filled holds: secure the surface (bagging, strapping) or accept the bigger 25° shift moment
- Fumigation in transit: follow IMO recommendations; crew away from fumigated spaces
