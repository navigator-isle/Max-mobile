# Grain Code — International Code for the Safe Carriage of Grain in Bulk

## At a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 23 May 1991, resolution MSC.23(59) |
| In force | 1 Jan 1994 |
| Status | Mandatory under SOLAS chapter VI, part C |
| Structure | Part A (specific requirements) · Part B (calculation of assumed heeling moments and general assumptions) |
| Applies to | All ships regardless of size, including under 500 GT, carrying grain in bulk |
| Key documents | Document of Authorization · grain loading manual |
| Stability criteria | Heel ≤ 12° · residual area ≥ 0.075 m·rad · GM ≥ 0.30 m |
| Latest amendment | MSC.552(108), in force 1 Jan 2026 |

## What is the Grain Code

- An international standard for the safe carriage of grain in bulk: stability, stowage, fittings and securing
- **Grain** means wheat, maize, oats, rye, barley, rice, pulses, seeds and processed forms with similar behaviour (A 2.1)
- Each ship loaded under the Code needs a **Document of Authorization** showing it can meet the Code
- The document accompanies or is included in the **grain loading manual**, which gives the master the stability information
- A copy of the document, with the stability data and plans, is kept on board for inspection by the loading port's authorities

**Definitions (A 2)**

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Filled compartment, trimmed (2.2) | Grain at its highest possible level after loading and trimming under A 10.2 |
| Filled compartment, untrimmed (2.3) | Filled to the maximum in way of the hatch but not trimmed outside the hatch periphery |
| Partly filled compartment (2.4) | Grain not loaded as in 2.2 or 2.3 |
| Angle of flooding (2.5) | Heel angle at which openings that cannot be closed weathertight immerse |
| Stowage factor (2.6) | Volume per unit weight, with no allowance for lost space |
| Specially suitable compartment (2.7) | At least two grain-tight longitudinal divisions, coincident with hatch side girders or positioned to limit transverse shift; sloping divisions at least 30° to the horizontal |

## Stability requirements (A 7)

![[diagram:grain-criteria|The three stability criteria, with a schematic righting and heeling arm diagram]]

| Criterion | Requirement |
|---|---|
| 7.1.1 Angle of heel from grain shift | Not more than **12°**, or for ships constructed on or after 1 Jan 1994 the deck-edge immersion angle, whichever is less |
| 7.1.2 Residual area | Between the heeling arm and righting arm curves, up to the smaller of the angle of maximum difference, 40° or the flooding angle, at least **0.075 m·rad** in all loading conditions |
| 7.1.3 Initial GM | Corrected for free surfaces in tanks, at least **0.30 m** |
| 7.2 | The master may be required to demonstrate stability at all stages of the voyage before loading |
| 7.3 | The ship must be upright after loading and before sailing |

**Existing ships (A 8):** ships loaded under previously approved documents (SOLAS 1960 regulation 12, resolutions A.184(VI) or A.264(VIII)) are considered equivalent to A 7. Ships without a document may use A 9 with no deadweight limit.

**Partial cargoes without documents (A 9):**
- Grain not more than **one third of deadweight**
- Centreline divisions in filled, trimmed compartments, extending below deck at least 1/8 of the breadth or 2.4 m, whichever is greater (saucers may replace them in and beneath a hatch, except for linseed and similar seeds)
- Hatches to trimmed compartments closed and secured
- Free grain surfaces in partly filled spaces levelled and secured under A 16, 17 or 18
- GM at least 0.3 m, or the greater value from the formula in the Code
- The master demonstrates compliance to the Administration or port State

## Stowage (A 10)

- Trim free surfaces and minimize shifting.
- **Trimmed compartments (10.2):**
  - filled under decks and hatch covers as far as possible
- **Untrimmed compartments (10.3):**
  - filled in way of the hatch
  - grain may rest at its natural angle outside the hatch periphery
  - only for compartments with feeder ducts or perforated decks, or specially suitable compartments
- **Hatch covers:**
  - non-grain-tight 'tween-deck hatch covers are sealed by taping, tarpaulins or similar
- **Partly filled compartments:**
  - surface levelled
  - secured against overstowing (A 16), or strapped or lashed (A 17 or A 18), unless the heeling effect is accounted for

## Securing the grain surface (A 11 to A 18)

| Section | Key figures |
|---|---|
| 11 Strength of fittings | Working stresses: 19.6 kN/cm² for steel, 1.57 kN/cm² for wood. Upright housing at least 75 mm at each end |
| 12 Shifting boards | At least **50 mm** thick, grain-tight. Maximum unsupported span 2.5 m (50 mm), 3.0 m (60 mm), 3.5 m (70 mm), 4.0 m (80 mm). Ends housed at least 75 mm. Steel wire stays: working load not more than one third of breaking load, designed for 4.9 kN/m². Shores not steeper than 45° |
| 13 One-sided loading | Loads from Tables A 13-1 to A 13-6; stays and shores not over one third of breaking load |
| 14 Saucers | Only in filled, trimmed compartments, not for linseed-type seeds. Depth from saucer bottom to deck line at least 1.2 m (moulded breadth up to 9.1 m) to at least 1.8 m (18.3 m or more) |
| 15 Bundling | Alternative to saucer filling; lining of 2,687 N per 5 cm strip tensile strength, or 1,344 N per 5 cm with athwartship lashings at 2.4 m or less |
| 16 Overstowing | Separation cloth or platform on levelled grain; bearers at most 1.2 m apart; boards 25 mm thick with gaps of at most 100 mm; topped with tightly stowed bagged grain to at least 1/16 of the free surface breadth or 1.2 m, whichever is greater |
| 17 Strapping or lashing | Grain slightly crowned; separation cloths overlapping at least 1.8 m; lashings of 19 mm steel wire, 50 × 1.3 mm steel strapping (breaking load at least 49 kN) or equivalent chain; 32 mm turnbuckles; attached about 450 mm below the final surface, at most 2.4 m apart; inspect and tighten during the voyage |
| 18 Wire mesh | Burlap (at least 1,344 N per 5 cm), then two layers of 3 mm wire mesh with 150 mm squares; hold-down lashings at most 2.4 m apart, first and last within 300 mm of bulkheads |

## Part B — Assumed heeling moments

![[diagram:grain-shift-angles|Assumed grain surface angles after shifting for the three cases]]

| Case | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Trimmed (B 2) | Grain surface after shifting assumed at **15°** |
| Untrimmed (B 3) | **25°** generally, or 15° where the void area in a section is no greater than the trimmed-equivalent void. End-untrimmed: 15° abreast the hatch, 25° in the ends |
| Trunks (B 4 in the 1991 text) | 25° shift where wing spaces cannot be trimmed |
| Partly filled (B 5 in the 1991 text) | **25°** if not secured under A 16, 17 or 18; total heeling moment × **1.12** (B 1.5) |
| Void depth (B 1.1.1) | Vd = Vd1 + 0.75(d − 600) mm, Vd1 from Table B 1-1 (430–570 mm), never less than 100 mm |
| Credited underdeck voids (B 1.3) | Total heeling moment multiplied by 1.06 |
| Divisions in partly filled compartments (B 5.2) | Extend 1/8 of the maximum breadth above and below the grain surface |

## 2026 amendments (MSC.552(108))

- Adopted 23 May 2024 at MSC 108; deemed accepted 1 Jul 2025 unless objections; **in force 1 Jan 2026**.
- **New definition A 2.8:** a "specially suitable compartment, partly filled in way of the hatch opening, with ends untrimmed": filled to at least the bottom edge of the hatch end beams and not trimmed outside the hatch periphery under the new A 10.4.
- **New A 10.4:** grain outside the hatch periphery may rest at its natural angle of repose; end trimming may be waived for specially suitable compartments. Only the surface in way of the hatch must be level (A 10.7).
- **New B 1.1.5:** grain surfaces assumed to slope at **30°** from the lower edge of the hatch end beam.
- **B 1.5 replaced:** total heeling moment = 1.12 × the calculated transverse heeling moment, for partly filled compartments and the new category.
- **New B section 4:** these compartments follow the B 2 trimmed rules, but the surface in way of the hatch and in the ends is assumed at **25°** after shifting. The old section 4 is renumbered section 5 (section numbers above are those of the 1991 text).
- Practical point (Gard): the grain loading manual and loading computer must carry the new heeling moment data and be approved before the new condition is used.

> **Key Point:** No grain loading without a Document of Authorization and the approved grain loading manual. Check the three criteria: heel at most 12°, residual area at least 0.075 m·rad, GM at least 0.30 m. A partly filled hold must have its surface levelled and secured, or the larger 25° shift moment applies.
