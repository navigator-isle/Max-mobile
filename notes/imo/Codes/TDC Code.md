# TDC Code — Code of Safe Practice for Ships Carrying Timber Deck Cargoes, 2011

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Resolution | A.1048(27), adopted 30 Nov 2011 (Assembly, 27th session) |
| Replaces | The 1991 Code, A.715(17) (revoked by the resolution) |
| Status | Voluntary (recommendatory), not mandatory |
| Applies to | All ships of **24 m or more in length** carrying timber deck cargo (1.2.1) |
| Structure | Chapter 1 · Part A Operational requirements (chapters 2–4) · Part B Design of cargo securing arrangements (chapters 5–8) · Annexes A–D |
| Related | Cargo Securing Manual (CSM) · CSS Code Annex 13 · 2008 IS Code (stability) |

![[diagram:tdc-structure|Structure of the 2011 TDC Code]]

> **Key Point:** Timber is secured according to the ship's Cargo Securing Manual, based on chapter 5 or chapter 6 of Part B (1.2.2). National rules may restrict either chapter and require third-party inspection (1.2.3).

## What the Code does
- Gives the IMO standard for safe stowage and securing of timber deck cargo
- Covers round and sawn wood, loose or packaged
- Intended for shipowners, operators and ship's officers
- Includes guidance for drafting cargo securing manual procedures and checklists (Annex A)
- Loop lashing was added as an optional practice alongside top-over lashing

## Definitions (1.3)
- **Timber** (1.3.1.15): a collective term for wooden material covered by the Code, including round and sawn wood. Wood pulp and similar cargo are excluded.
- **Timber deck cargo** (1.3.1.18): timber carried on an uncovered part of a freeboard or superstructure deck

### Stability (2.4)
- The ship complies with its **stability booklet at all times** (2.4.1)
- Damage stability and the **2008 IS Code**, particularly its timber deck cargo requirements, must still be met (2.4.2)
- GM should preferably **not exceed 3% of the ship's breadth**, because excessive GM causes large accelerations (2.4.2)
- Timber buoyancy may be considered at up to **25% permeability** for sawn wood. Round wood is typically 40% to 60%, depending on log diameter (2.4.4).
- **Water absorption** (4.5, Annex C): sea spray can increase cargo weight, and the increase is considered under the 2008 IS Code
- **Icing** (4.6): refers to 6.2 of the 2008 IS Code. Any water-absorption increase is counted before the icing increase.
- The Code does not repeat the stability criteria (GZ area, residual stability). Those come from the 2008 IS Code.

### Stowage (2.9)
- Avoid voids in the stow. Where they occur, fill with loose timber or block with H-frames (2.9.5).
- Timber overhanging hatch coamings by more than **one-third of the package length** is supported at the outer end (2.9.6)

### Access and fall protection (2.8)
- Where there is no convenient passage for crew, guard lines or rails no more than **330 mm apart vertically**, extending to at least **1 m above the cargo** (2.8.6)
- Where cargo is uneven, a walking surface at least **600 mm wide** (2.8.6)
- Alternative: a taut lifeline about **2 m above the cargo**, near the centreline, used with a fall protection system (2.8.9)

### Voyage (2.10, 2.12, Annex A)
- Examine and tighten lashings at the start of the voyage, then at regular intervals and as needed, because vibration and working of the ship make the cargo settle (2.10.7)
- Enter all examinations and adjustments in the ship's logbook (2.10.8)
- Reduce ship motion before checking or re-tightening lashings (2.8.12)
- Severe weather: reduce speed or alter course early. Avoid roll amplitudes above **30°** (2.12.4).

### Jettison (Annex A, A.4.22–A.4.23)
- A **final resort**, after other options are exhausted
- Jettison is unlikely to clear the cargo entirely, risks propeller damage if still turning, and is dangerous to those involved
- Report the position and navigational hazard immediately to coastal authorities
- If timber is lost overboard, the master warns nearby ships and the nearest competent authority, giving the kind of danger, position and time in UTC

### Lashing requirements
![[diagram:tdc-lashing|Lashing spacing]]

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Breaking strength (5.1.2) | At least **133 kN** · elongation not more than 5% at 80% of breaking strength · no permanent deformation after a proof load of at least 40% of breaking strength |
| Tightening device (5.1.3) | At least 27 kN in the horizontal part and 16 kN in the vertical part |
| Spacing, sawn wood (5.3.2) | Maximum **3 m** for heights up to 2.5 m · maximum **1.5 m** above 2.5 m · halved at the foremost and aftmost sections |
| Spacing, round wood (5.4.1) | No more than **1.5 m** apart |
| Alternative design (chapter 6) | Pretension at least 16 kN (vertical) and 27 kN (horizontal) (6.5.20) · Maximum Securing Load derived from the Minimum Breaking Load using CSS Code Annex 13 (6.4) · elongation 2% for chain and wire, 7% for web lashings, unless certified otherwise (6.5.16) · heel from cargo movement due to lashing elongation not more than **5°** |

### Uprights (5.2, chapter 7)
- Used where the nature, height or character of the cargo requires them (5.2.1, 2.10.12)
- Spaced no more than **3 m** apart between centrelines, so most of the stow is supported by at least two uprights (5.2.2.2)
- Fastened to the deck or hatch and restrained from falling inward during loading and discharge (2.10.13)
- Bending stress not more than 50% of the material's ultimate stress (7.6)

## Not included (not verified against the original text)
- Old claim of "GM ≥0.10 m at all times". The Code points to the stability booklet and the 2008 IS Code, and says GM should preferably not exceed 3% of breadth.
- Old claim "water absorption +10% weight of deck timber". Annex C gives the calculation method.
- History "1972 first Code, 1991 Code" details (a secondary page gives 1972, 1978 and 1991) and "timber load lines LT, LS, LW" marks
- Old claim "height on deck within SOLAS V/22 visibility limits" (chapter 3 content not checked)
- Old claim that securing is "calculations like CSS Annex 13" for all cases (Annex 13 applies in chapter 6)
- Old structure "Part A chapters 1–7" and "Part B Ch 3 physical properties"
- The formula for Calculated Strength (garbled in the source) and the Annex B stowage samples
- IMO's summary page describes the Code as five parts; the Code text itself has Part A, Part B and Annexes A–D, which is what this note follows
