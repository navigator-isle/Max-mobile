**CSS Code — Code of Safe Practice for Cargo Stowage and Securing**

**What is the CSS Code**
- IMO guidance for stowing and securing cargo units (not bulk) so cargo does not shift, damage the ship or endanger crew
- Adopted as Assembly resolution A.714(17) — recommendatory, but made binding through SOLAS VI/5 and VII/5:
  - Cargo, cargo units and CTUs loaded, stowed and secured per the **Cargo Securing Manual (CSM)** approved by the Administration
  - CSM drawn up to a standard at least equal to IMO guidelines (MSC.1/Circ.1353/Rev.2)
- Main tool on board: the ship's approved Cargo Securing Manual

**History**
- Adopted 6 Nov 1991 (A.714(17))
- Cargo Securing Manual required on board since 1 Jan 1998 (SOLAS VI/5 and VII/5)
- Amended 1994–95, 2002, 2003, 2010 (MSC/Circ.1352 — Annex 13 & 14), 2014 (MSC.1/Circ.1497 — Annex 14 safe container securing)
- Revised CSM guidelines MSC.1/Circ.1353/Rev.2 (2014)
- Paris and Tokyo MoU CIC 2026: Cargo Securing Arrangements campaign (Sep–Nov 2026)

**Applies to**
- All ships carrying cargo units other than solid and liquid bulk cargo (general cargo, containers, ro-ro units, heavy lifts, steel, timber on deck — see TDC Code)

---

**Chapters**
- Ch 1 General: purpose, definitions (cargo unit, CTU, securing device), application
- Ch 2 Principles of safe stowage and securing of cargoes:
  - Suitability of cargo for transport · cargo information from shipper (mass, dimensions, centre of gravity, special securing needs)
  - Cargo distribution (stability and strength), securing equipment certified (MSL — Maximum Securing Load)
  - Accelerations from ship motions considered · securing to be checked during the voyage
- Ch 3 Standardized stowage and securing systems: containers on purpose-built ships (cellular), ro-ro units, rail cars
- Ch 4 Semi-standardized stowage and securing: road vehicles on ro-ro ships, roll-trailers
- Ch 5 Non-standardized stowage and securing: general cargo, heavy items
- Ch 6 Actions to be taken in heavy weather: alter course/speed to reduce motions, avoid synchronous and parametric rolling
- Ch 7 Actions after cargo has shifted: reduce motions, re-secure only when safe, consider deviating/port of refuge

**Annexes (cargoes difficult to stow and secure)**
- Annex 1 Containers on deck of ships not designed for containers
- Annex 2 Portable tanks · Annex 3 Portable receptacles · Annex 4 Wheel-based (rolling) cargoes
- Annex 5 Heavy cargo items (locomotives, transformers) · Annex 6 Coiled sheet steel · Annex 7 Heavy metal products
- Annex 8 Anchor chains · Annex 9 Metal scrap in bulk · Annex 10 Flexible IBCs
- Annex 11 Logs under deck · Annex 12 Unit loads
- Annex 13 Methods to assess the efficiency of securing arrangements for non-standardized cargo:
  - MSL of devices (e.g., shackles, rings 50% of breaking load; web lashing 50%; wire rope single use 80%, re-used 30%; chains 50%)
  - Rule-of-thumb: sum of MSL each side ≥ cargo weight (×9.81 kN)
  - Advanced calculation: transverse sliding and tipping balance with accelerations (Table 1)
- Annex 14 Safe containership operations: lashing, twistlocks, parametric rolling, container stack weights, cell guides

**Cargo Securing Manual (CSM) — contents**
- Ch 1 General · Ch 2 Securing devices and arrangements (fixed and portable, MSL, maintenance)
- Ch 3 Stowage and securing of non-standardized and semi-standardized cargo (calculation examples, Annex 13 method)
- Ch 4 Stowage and securing of containers and other standardized cargo (lashing plans, stack weight limits)
- Inspection and maintenance records of securing gear (kept on board)

**Key Point**
- Secure per the CSM: right devices, right MSL, inspection records up to date
- Check lashings before departure and after heavy weather; re-tighten turnbuckles and web lashings
- Container losses: avoid parametric rolling (head/stern seas, roll period ~ half the encounter period)
- From 2026 SOLAS V/31 & V/32: loss of containers must be reported (number, position) — part of the container-securing picture
