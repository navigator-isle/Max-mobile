# CSS Code — Code of Safe Practice for Cargo Stowage and Securing

## At a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 6 Nov 1991, Assembly resolution A.714(17) (effective 1 Jul 1996) |
| Replaced | Resolution A.288(VIII) |
| Structure | 7 chapters · 14 annexes (Annexes 13 and 14 added later) |
| Applies to | Cargoes other than solid and liquid bulk cargoes and timber stowed on deck |
| Main tool on board | Cargo Securing Manual (SOLAS VI/5 and VII/5) |
| Responsibility | The master remains responsible for the voyage, the ship, the crew and the cargo |

## What is the CSS Code

- An international standard for safe stowage and securing of cargo
- Covers: ship suitability and equipment, general stowage advice, cargoes known to be hard to stow, actions in heavy weather, and what to do after cargo shifts
- The Assembly urged Governments to implement it at the earliest opportunity and asked the MSC to keep it under review
- SOLAS VI/5 and VII/5: cargo units must be loaded, stowed and secured throughout the voyage under a **Cargo Securing Manual approved by the Administration**, to a standard at least equivalent to the IMO guidelines

## Chapters

| Ch | Title | Key content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | General | Application, definitions (cargo unit, portable tank, ro-ro ship, unit load), forces, cargo behaviour, risk criteria, the Cargo Securing Manual, equipment, special cargo, cargo information |
| 2 | Principles of safe stowage and securing | Cargo suitability, distribution, securing arrangements, residual strength, friction, supervision, entry into enclosed spaces, pre-loading checks, cargo stowage and securing declarations (including dangerous goods) |
| 3 | Standardized systems | Ships for standardized cargo (containers, railway wagons) must be designed and equipped to secure it and give the master adequate information |
| 4 | Semi-standardized systems | Securing points and arrangements for road vehicles and roll-trailers on ro-ro ships; acceptance of road vehicles |
| 5 | Non-standardized systems | General advice and twelve cargo types with annexes; equivalent methods allowed if they give at least the same safety |
| 6 | Heavy weather actions | Course or speed changes, heaving to, avoiding bad weather, timely ballasting or deballasting, voyage planning |
| 7 | Actions after cargo shift | Course or speed changes, monitoring the ship's integrity, restowing or resecuring, route diversion; ballasting only if stability is adequate |

**Forces:** longitudinal and transverse forces are predominant. Transverse forces increase with stow height, distance from the ship's centre of motion, and metacentric height.

## Annex 13 — assessing securing arrangements

![[diagram:css-msl|MSL as a percentage of breaking strength, Annex 13 Table 1]]

- Timber: MSL is 0.3 kN/cm² normal to the grain.
- A device marked with a permissible working load may use that value as its MSL (para 4.3).

![[diagram:css-rule-of-thumb|The rule-of-thumb check for transverse securing]]

- **Rule of thumb (section 5):** the total MSL of the devices on each side, port and starboard, should equal the weight of the item in kN. It assumes 1 g (9.81 m/s²) transverse acceleration and ignores lashing-angle effects, uneven load sharing and friction.
- Transverse lashing angles to the deck should not exceed 60°. Lashings above 60° can help against tipping but are not counted in the rule-of-thumb total.
- The full method uses basic acceleration data (Table 2) with length and speed correction factors (Table 3) and B/GM correction factors (Table 4).

## Annex 14 — safe working conditions for securing containers

Applies to dedicated containerships and parts of other ships designed and fitted to carry containers on deck. It protects the people who lash and unlash.

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| 4 | General: reduce work on container tops and heavy manual equipment; approved **Cargo Safe Access Plan (CSAP)**; training in lashing, defective gear, working at heights, reefer cable checks |
| 5 | Responsibilities of Administrations, owners and operators, designers, shipbuilders and terminals |
| 6 | Design: risk assessment, safe access, fencing (top rail about 1 m, gaps 300 mm or less), lashing system standards, **lighting at least 10 lux in access ways and 50 lux at work spaces**, at least 70 mm between turnbuckles |
| 7 | Operations and maintenance: lashing in the ISM system, keep transit areas clear, work in pairs with heavy gear, PPE, safety cages for container tops, **no lashing where safe access cannot be provided**, record book |
| 8 | Reefers: outlets interlocked, circuit-breaker protected, de-energized when unplugged; defective plugs locked and tagged out |

For existing containerships (keels laid before 1 Jan 2015), sections 4.4, 7.1, 7.3 and 8 apply, and sections 6 and 7.2 as far as practical.

## Cargo Securing Manual (MSC.1/Circ.1353/Rev.1)

| Ch | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 General | Definitions (securing devices, **MSL**, standardized, semi-standardized, non-standardized cargo); written in the ship's working language, with an English, French or Spanish translation if needed; consistency with the trim and stability booklet, Load Line Certificate and IMDG Code; reserve devices; maintenance |
| 2 Securing devices and arrangements | Fixed devices and portable devices with MSL; inspection and maintenance: the master's responsibility, routine and periodic examinations, a record book kept with the manual |
| 3 Non-standardized and semi-standardized cargo | Forces, accelerations, calculation of devices and safety factors (refers to Annex 13); ro-ro ships |
| 4 Containers and other standardized cargo | Stowage and securing plan: stack masses, sequences, heights, sight lines; alternative patterns; forces |
| 5 Cargo Safe Access Plan | Required for containerships; refers to Annex 14 |

- The full guidelines apply to containerships with keels laid on or after 1 Jan 2015; chapters 1 to 4 apply to existing containerships.
- A manual drafted to the older MSC/Circ.385 is acceptable if it meets these guidelines.

> **Key Point:** Secure cargo as the approved Cargo Securing Manual says: right devices, correct MSL, records of inspection and maintenance. Check securing arrangements and act early in heavy weather (Chapter 6). On a containership, the Cargo Safe Access Plan and Annex 14 protect the lashing crew.
