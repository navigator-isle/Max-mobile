**ESP Code — International Code on the Enhanced Programme of Inspections during Surveys of Bulk Carriers and Oil Tankers (2011 ESP Code)**

**What is the ESP Code**
- Enhanced survey programme for hull structure of ageing bulk carriers and oil tankers
- Mandatory under SOLAS XI-1/2 (and MARPOL Annex I for tankers)
- Focus: close-up surveys, thickness measurements, tank and hold coatings, corrosion
- Survey planning, survey report file and planning documents kept on board

**History**
- Born from bulk carrier and tanker losses in the late 1980s–1990s (structural failures)
- 1993 Guidelines A.744(18) · mandatory 1 Jan 1996 (SOLAS XI-1/2)
- 2011 ESP Code adopted 30 Nov 2011 by Assembly resolution A.1049(27) · in force 1 Jan 2014 (replaced A.744(18))
- Amended several times by MSC resolutions (survey planning, thickness measurement)
- MSC.553(108): approval and audit of thickness measurement firms (Administration may audit/certify directly) — 1 Jan 2026
- MSC.584(111) (adopted MSC 111, May 2026): Remote Inspection Techniques (drones, crawlers, ROVs) allowed for close-up surveys — in force 1 Jan 2028

**Applies to**
- Bulk carriers (single and double side skin) ≥500 GT
- Oil tankers (single and double hull) ≥500 GT — incl. chemical tankers carrying oil
- Surveys done by the Administration or RO (class)

---

**Annex A — Bulk carriers** (Part A single side skin · Part B double side skin)
- 1 General: definitions (ballast tank, spaces, close-up survey, transverse section, suspect areas, substantial corrosion >75% of allowable)
- 2 Renewal survey (every 5 years):
  - Dry-dock survey · cargo holds, ballast tanks, hatch covers and coamings
  - Overall survey of all spaces · close-up survey extent grows with age: renewal survey No. 1 (≤5 years), No. 2 (5–10), No. 3 (10–15), No. 4 and later (>15)
  - Thickness measurements: minimum per age table (e.g., suspect areas, transverse sections amidships)
  - Tank testing (ballast tanks, hold boundaries)
- 3 Annual survey: hatch covers, weathertight, cargo holds (more for older ships), ballast tanks where coating poor
- 4 Intermediate survey (between 2nd and 3rd year): ballast tanks overall, holds; >10 years more extensive
- 5 Preparations for survey: Survey Programme agreed with owner · safe access (staging, rafts), gas-free, cleanliness, lighting
- 6 Documentation on board: Survey Report File · Supporting documents (main structural plans, repair history, cargo and ballast history, inspections by crew)
- 7 Procedures for thickness measurements (approved firm, surveyor present)
- 8 Reporting and evaluation of survey: Executive Hull Summary

**Annex B — Oil tankers** (Part A double hull · Part B other than double hull)
- Same structure: renewal, annual, intermediate surveys; cargo tanks and ballast tanks close-ups; transverse sections; longitudinal strength evaluation for tankers >130 m and >10 years
- Condition Assessment Scheme (CAS) for single-hull tankers >15 years (MARPOL I, mostly historical now)

**Key documents on board**
- Survey Report File (reports, thickness measurements, Executive Hull Summary)
- Survey programme · Planning questionnaire · Coating condition records · Owner's inspection records of ballast tanks

**Key Point**
- Owner/crew must keep ballast tank inspection records and coating condition reports
- Safe access and gas-freeing before close-up surveys — enclosed space entry procedures apply
- Substantial corrosion found = more thickness measurements and annual close-up of those areas
- Thickness firms must be approved (rules tightened from 2026)
