# IMSBC Code — International Maritime Solid Bulk Cargoes Code

## At a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 4 Dec 2008, resolution MSC.268(85), at MSC 85 |
| Mandatory | Voluntary from 1 Jan 2009; **mandatory from 1 Jan 2011** under SOLAS chapters VI and VII |
| Replaced | Code of Safe Practice for Solid Bulk Cargoes, 2004 (MSC.193(79)) |
| Applies to | SOLAS ships carrying solid bulk cargoes as defined in SOLAS VI/1-1 (1.4.1) |
| Current mandatory amendment | 07-23 (MSC.539(107)), mandatory since 1 Jan 2025 |
| Next | 08-25 (MSC.575(110)), voluntary 1 Jan 2026, mandatory **1 Jan 2027** |
| Structure | 13 sections and 5 appendices |

## How the Code works

- A cargo **listed in Appendix 1** follows its individual schedule plus sections 1 to 10 and 11.1.1 (1.2.2).
- An **unlisted cargo** (1.3.1): before loading, the shipper gives the loading-port competent authority the cargo's characteristics under section 4, and that authority assesses acceptability. If the cargo may present Group A or B hazards, the port-of-unloading and flag State authorities are consulted and the three set preliminary conditions (1.3.1.1). The loading-port authority must apply to IMO within one year to add the cargo to Appendix 1.
- Each cargo has a **Bulk Cargo Shipping Name (BCSN)**, shown in capitals in the schedules.

## Cargo groups

![[diagram:imsbc-groups|Group A, B and C (section 1.7)]]

- **Group A:** cargoes with a moisture hazard that may cause **liquefaction or dynamic separation** if shipped above their transportable moisture limit (TML). Some Group A cargoes may also heat spontaneously (7.1.3).
- **Group B:** cargoes with chemical hazards that could create a dangerous situation on a ship. Three kinds (section 9): dangerous goods under the IMDG Code (classes 4.1 to 9); materials hazardous only in bulk (MHB); and dangerous goods that also have MHB hazards.
- **Group C:** cargoes that are neither Group A nor Group B.
- A cargo can have more than one group: coal is Group A and B unless classified Group B only by a test determined by the appropriate authority.

| Term | Meaning (1.7) |
|---|---|
| TML | For a Group A cargo, the maximum moisture content considered safe for carriage on ships not meeting 7.3.2 |
| Flow moisture point (FMP) | Moisture content (wet mass basis) at which a flow state develops in the prescribed test (Appendix 2, paragraph 1) |
| Dynamic separation | Forming a liquid slurry of water and fine solids above the solid material, creating a free surface effect that can significantly affect stability |
| Angle of repose | Maximum slope angle of non-cohesive granular material, between a horizontal plane and the cone slope |
| MHB | Materials that may have chemical hazards when carried in bulk, other than dangerous goods under the IMDG Code |

MHB notation codes: CB (combustible), SH (self-heating), WF (flammable gas when wet), WT (toxic gas when wet), TX (toxic), CR (corrosive), OH (other hazards).

**06-21 (MSC.500(105))**
- Group A redefined to cover **dynamic separation** as well as liquefaction; "dynamic separation" newly defined.
- New schedules for ammonium nitrate based fertilizer, ammonium nitrate based fertilizer MHB, clam shell and leach residue containing lead; new triple granular superphosphate schedule. Old schedules for non-hazardous ammonium nitrate based fertilizer and triple granular superphosphate deleted.

**07-23 (MSC.539(107))** — a consolidated version of the whole Code
- Shippers must declare **bulk density** (new 4.2.2.7), in line with SOLAS XII/10 (ships 150 m and above).
- Twelve new schedules, including barytes, brown fused alumina, dunite, potassium and sodium nitrate, and electric arc furnace dust (pelletised). Direct reduced iron (D) by-product fines added. Stabilized fish meal (anti-oxidant treated) no longer under UN 2216 but stays Group B (MHB).
- List of non-cohesive cargoes amended (angle of repose must be determined before loading).

**08-25 (MSC.575(110))**
- **12 new schedules:** Aluminium Sulphate Granular; Apatite Concentrate; Asphalt Granulates; Crushed Granodiorite (Coarse); Ferric Sulphate Granular; Fish Meal (Stabilized); Fish Scrap (Stabilized); Iron Ore Briquettes; Pea Protein Concentrate Pellets; Phosphate Rock Fines (uncalcined); Tuff (Coarse); Zinc Slag (Coarse).
- **11 amended schedules:** Aluminium Ferrosilicon Powder (UN 1395); Aluminium Silicon Powder, Uncoated (UN 1398); Aluminium Smelting or Remelting By-products (UN 3170); Castor Beans, Meal, Pomace or Flake (UN 2969); Direct Reduced Iron (A) and (B); Ferrosilicon (two schedules); Ferrous Metal Borings, Shavings, Turnings or Cuttings (UN 2793); Fish Meal (Fish Scrap), Stabilized, Anti-oxidant treated; Iron Ore Pellets.
- Section 3: updated fumigation guidance (revised pesticide recommendations, MSC.1/Circ.1264/Rev.1).
- Section 9.3.3: segregation table amended (updated terminology, clearer separation for radioactive materials, outdated "MHB" references removed).
- Section 13: references updated (for example MSC.1/Circ.1395/Rev.7).

Appendices: 1 Individual schedules of solid bulk cargoes; 2 Laboratory test procedures, associated apparatus and standards; 3 Properties of solid bulk cargoes; 4 Index; 5 Bulk Cargo Shipping Names in three languages.

**Section 2: loading**
- Cargo is distributed to keep adequate stability and avoid overstressing the structure (2.1.1); special attention to high-density cargo (2.1.2); cargo spaces are inspected and prepared before loading (2.2.1).
- The loading or unloading plan is agreed by the master and terminal representative under SOLAS VI Regulation 7, paragraph 3.
- Trimming (5.1.1): cargo is trimmed reasonably level, as necessary, to reduce shifting and air entry.

**Section 4: cargo information (4.2.2)**
- The shipper gives the master, before loading: BCSN, group, IMO class, UN number, total quantity, stowage factor, bulk density, trimming needs, likelihood of shifting (angle of repose), moisture certificate and TML for Group A cargoes, likelihood of a wet base, toxic or flammable gas, flammability, toxicity, corrosiveness, oxygen depletion, self-heating, flammable gas with water, radioactive properties, MARPOL Annex V classification, and other national requirements.
- Cargo information must say whether the cargo is harmful to the marine environment (4.2.2.2, added by an earlier amendment).

## Group A cargoes: certificates and acceptance

![[diagram:imsbc-timing|TML and moisture test timing before loading]]

- The shipper gives the master a signed **TML certificate** and a signed **moisture content certificate or declaration**, issued by an entity recognized by the port's competent authority (4.3.2).
- The TML test must be done **within six months** of loading and repeated if the cargo's composition or characteristics are reasonably assumed to have changed (4.5.1).
- Moisture sampling and testing must be as close as practicable to the start of loading, and the interval must **never exceed seven days** (4.5.2).
- The shipper sets sampling, testing and moisture-control procedures to keep moisture below the TML while the cargo is on board (4.3.3). The shipper gives the ship's nominated representative access to stockpiles for inspection, sampling and testing (4.4.3).
- If Group A cargo is loaded into several spaces, the certificate states the moisture content of each finely grained material in each space (4.3.5).
- **Acceptance (7.3.1.1):** loaded only if the actual moisture content is **less than the TML**. Cargo above the TML may be carried only in a specially constructed or fitted ship under 7.3.2, or a specially constructed dry powdery cargo ship under 7.3.3.
- At sea, introducing water may bring the cargo to a flow state (7.3.1.5).
- **Can test** (crew check): free moisture on the sample at the end means laboratory testing is needed. It is not quantitative and must not be the only check: a pass does not guarantee moisture is below the TML.

## Trimming and angle of repose (Section 5.4)

| Angle of repose | Rule |
|---|---|
| 30° or less | Cargo flows freely like grain |
| Over 30° to 35° inclusive | Surface unevenness Δh not more than **B/10** (B = beam in metres), maximum **1.5 m**; or approved trimming equipment |
| Over 35° | Δh not more than B/10, maximum **2 m**; or approved trimming equipment |

## Other points

- **Section 9:** segregation of Group B cargoes from packaged dangerous goods (9.3.3.1, 9.3.3.2, IMDG Code consulted) and from each other (9.3.4). Cargo spaces should be cleaned after unloading because residues may be highly corrosive (9.3.2.4.4).
- **Section 10:** transboundary movements of wastes under the Basel Convention may start only after notification and written consent of the destination country (10.4).
- **Section 11:** high-consequence cargoes include ammonium nitrate UN 1942 and ammonium nitrate based fertilizers UN 2067 (11.3.1).
- **Section 12:** 1 m³/t = 35.87 ft³/ton; 1 ft³/ton = 0.02788 m³/t.
- **Examples from schedules:** ammonium nitrate and fertilizer UN 1942/UN 2067 not accepted if cargo temperature is above 40 °C; ammonium nitrate based fertilizer MHB not stowed next to heated fuel oil tanks unless controlled to not exceed 50 °C; aluminium ferrosilicon powder UN 1395 needs at least two extra sets of self-contained breathing apparatus; aluminium silicon powder UN 1398 needs mechanical ventilation of at least six air changes per hour.

> **Key Point:** Never load Group A cargo without a valid TML certificate (not older than six months) and a moisture certificate (sampled within seven days) showing moisture below the TML. A passing can test is not proof. Check the cargo's schedule and group, and have the latest amendment on board: 07-23 now, 08-25 mandatory from 1 Jan 2027.
