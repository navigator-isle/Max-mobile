# IP Code — International Code of Safety for Ships Carrying Industrial Personnel

## At a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 10 Nov 2022, resolution MSC.527(106), at MSC 106 |
| Mandatory | SOLAS **chapter XV** (MSC.521(106)); both take effect **1 Jul 2024** |
| Applies to | Cargo ships and high-speed cargo craft of **500 GT and above** carrying **more than 12 industrial personnel**, on international voyages |
| Voluntary | Governments are invited to apply it, as far as practicable, to ships under 500 GT and to ships not on international voyages |
| Certificate | Industrial Personnel Safety Certificate, with Record of Equipment (Form IP) |
| Structure | Parts I to V and an appendix |
| Pending | MSC.588(111): dimensioning weight of a person raised from 75 kg to 90 kg (from 1 Jan 2028) |

## What it is

- Safety standards for ships carrying **industrial personnel (IP)** and for those people, including risks during **personnel transfer** at sea.
- **Overall goal (Part I, 1):** safe carriage of industrial personnel and their safety during personnel transfer, addressing risks not adequately covered by SOLAS.
- **Industrial personnel (2.4):** all persons transported or accommodated on board for offshore industrial activities on other ships or offshore facilities.
- **Offshore industrial activities (2.6):** construction, maintenance, decommissioning, operation or servicing of offshore facilities, such as renewable or hydrocarbon energy, aquaculture and ocean mining.
- **Personnel transfer (2.7):** the full sequence of transferring personnel and their equipment at sea between the ship and another ship or offshore facility.
- IP are **not passengers** (SOLAS XV).

## SOLAS chapter XV (MSC.521(106))

- Adopted 10 Nov 2022; deemed accepted 1 Jan 2024; in force **1 Jul 2024**. It adds chapter XV, "Safety measures for ships carrying industrial personnel", after chapter XIV.
- When the number of IP is a parameter, it is the combined total of IP, special personnel and passengers, with passengers capped at 12.
- **Regulation 3 (application):** cargo ships and high-speed cargo craft of 500 GT and above, constructed on or after 1 Jul 2024, carrying more than 12 IP.
- **Existing ships** already authorized to carry more than 12 IP must meet the specified IP Code regulations by their first intermediate or renewal survey after 1 Jul 2024; high-speed cargo craft by their third periodical or first renewal survey.
- Ships not authorized before 1 Jul 2024 must comply and be certified before carrying more than 12 IP, regardless of construction date.
- **Regulation 5:** the ship is certified as a cargo ship or high-speed cargo craft, meets the IP Code, and is surveyed and certified under it. The certificate is subject to port State control and treated as issued under SOLAS I/12 or I/13.
- For high-speed craft, certification under the chapter is deemed to satisfy HSC Code chapters 2 to 12 and 18.
- Ships under 500 GT carrying more than 12 persons (combined) may have the goals and functional requirements applied as far as practicable, with relaxations noted on the certificate.

## Certificate and survey (Part I, 3)

- Every covered ship carries a valid Industrial Personnel Safety Certificate, issued after an initial or renewal survey (3.1, 3.2).
- Issued by the Administration or a recognized organization; the Administration takes full responsibility (3.3).
- Follows the model in the appendix; translated into English, French or Spanish if not in one of them (3.4).
- Validity, survey dates and endorsements are harmonized with SOLAS I/14 or X/3.2 (3.5). It is issued in addition to certificates required under SOLAS XV/5.1.1 (3.6).

## Part III: before and during transfer

![[diagram:ip-flow|From joining the ship to a safe transfer]]

**Regulation 1: industrial personnel**
- **Age and fitness (1.1):** at least **16 years**; the master holds proof of physical and medical fitness to a standard the Administration accepts.
- **Language (1.2):** understand the working language well enough to communicate and follow crew instructions.
- **Pre-boarding training (1.3):** personal survival (including entering water from a height, surviving in water, boarding a survival craft in a lifejacket); fire safety; personal safety (master's authority, crew directions, signs, symbols and alarms). Training meeting accepted offshore industry standards (a footnote mentions GWO or OPITO) may be accepted.
- **Documents (1.4):** nobody is carried unless the master holds documentation of the required training.
- **Ship familiarization (1.5):** before departure or right after boarding: layout, life-saving equipment, muster and embarkation stations, escape routes, first aid stations, signs and alarms, and action on an alarm or emergency.
- **Transfer familiarization (1.6):** before transfer, a briefing on the ship's transfer procedures, arrangements and added safety measures or equipment.

**Regulation 2: safe transfer**
- Equipment kept in good condition, maintained and regularly inspected; a responsible officer supervises rigging and use, with a means of communication to the bridge (2.1.1 to 2.1.3).
- Each appliance **permanently marked**, with a record of use and maintenance on board (2.1.4); checked for function before operations (2.1.5).
- A safe, unobstructed route from the transfer arrangements to where personnel are carried or accommodated (2.1.6); lighting from the emergency source over the transfer arrangements, the water below and the route (2.1.7); a designated, clear transfer deck area (2.1.8).
- A **job safety analysis** during planning and before execution, accounting for environmental conditions and equipment limits (2.1.9). The regulation sets no numerical weather limits.
- Transfer arrangements designed, built, tested and installed to standards accepted by the Administration or a recognized classification society (2.2).
- A **failure analysis** (qualitative or FMEA) covers single failures, fire in any space and flooding of any watertight compartment, to keep transfer available and people safe (2.3).
- Position keeping: evaluate manoeuvrability and the likely need to hold position (2.4).
- **Headcount:** procedures keep the number and identity of persons on board accurate at all times (2.5).

## Part IV: number of persons

![[diagram:ip-thresholds|What changes as the number on board grows]]

| Persons certified | Requirement (Part IV) |
|---|---|
| More than 240 | Subdivision as passenger ship (2.1.1); fire safety as SOLAS II-2 for passenger ships carrying more than 36 passengers (6.1) |
| More than 60, up to 240 | Fire safety as passenger ships carrying not more than 36 passengers, except II-2/21 and 22 (6.2); subdivision factor interpolated |
| 60 or fewer | Subdivision reduction: R = 0.8R |
| More than 60 | SOLAS chapter III as for passenger ships on international voyages that are not short; III/2 and III/19.2.3 do not apply; "passengers" read as industrial personnel (7.1 to 7.3). Toxic products, low-flashpoint products and acids are not allowed (8.4.1.1) |

- Subdivision formula (2.1.2): **R = 1 − 5,000 / (Ls + 2.5N + 15,225)**, where N = N1 + 2N2 (N1 persons with lifeboats provided; N2 additional persons).
- Infant and child lifejacket numbers are calculated from passengers only (7.4).
- **Part V (high-speed craft):** at most **60 persons** (Reg 1.1); stability calculations use **90 kg** per person instead of 75 kg (Reg 2.2).

## MSC 111 amendment

- **MSC.588(111)**, adopted at MSC 111 (13–22 May 2026), amends the IP Code. ABS's summary lists it as increasing industrial personnel weight from **75 kg to 90 kg**, with entry into force on **1 Jan 2028**.

> **Key Point:** More than 12 industrial personnel means the ship needs an Industrial Personnel Safety Certificate. Nobody boards without training documents; everyone gets ship familiarization and a transfer briefing. Each transfer needs a job safety analysis, a checked and marked appliance, and an accurate headcount.

## Not included (not verified against the original text)

- Numerical weather limits and specific rescue provisions for transfers: the Code sets none in Regulation 2.
- The detail of MSC.588(111) beyond the weight change (the annex text could not be read).
- Details of Part IV regulations 3 to 5 (machinery, electrical, unattended spaces) beyond the thresholds shown.
