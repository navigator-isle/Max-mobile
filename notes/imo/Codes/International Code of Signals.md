# International Code of Signals — visual, sound and radio communications

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | **27 Sep 1965**, IMCO Assembly Resolution **A.80(IV)** (4th session, on approval by the Maritime Safety Committee) |
| Effective | Resolution: **1 Jan 1968**, provided the Radio Conference had finalized the parts needing ITU agreement. The US edition says the **1969 edition took effect on 1 Apr 1969**. |
| Aim | Safe communication in situations related to the safety of navigation and of persons, **especially when language difficulties arise** |
| Methods | Flag signalling, flashing light, sound, radiotelephony and radiotelegraphy, hand flags or arms, Morse |
| Structure | 4 chapters, an appendix and indexes |
| Edition checked | US edition, Pub. 102, 2005 edition, corrected through Notice to Mariners 24/06 |

![[diagram:ics-structure|The four chapters]]

> **Key Point:** The Code uses **complete-meaning signals**. A single letter or a short group of letters carries a whole message, so ships with no common language can still understand each other.

## Recommendations in A.80(IV)
- Governments should make carriage of the Code **mandatory** on ships required to carry radiotelephone or radiotelegraph installations under SOLAS 1960
- Other ships with an operational need should be required to carry it
- Knowledge of the **spelling tables** should be required of masters and navigating officers
- The Maritime Safety Committee may incorporate amendments from future Administrative Radio Conferences
- Governments are invited to send comments so the Code is kept up to date

## History (preface)
![[diagram:ics-history|A short history]]

- **1855–1857:** a British Board of Trade committee drafted the first International Code, with about **70,000 signals using eighteen flags**
- **1887–1897:** revision, completed 1897. It did not hold up during World War I.
- **1927–1932:** the International Radiotelegraph Conference in Washington decided on a new revision, finished in 1930 and adopted at Madrid in **1932**
- **1959–1965:** IMCO took over the Code's functions. The revision was completed in 1964 and adopted in **1965**.
- The revised Code uses complete-meaning signals and dropped the old vocabulary method and the Geographical Section

## How signals are built
- **Numerals:** numeral pennants in flag signalling, Morse numerals or spelled out in light or sound, and the Figure Spelling Table in voice
- **Decimal point:** shown with the answering pennant, **AAA**, or the word DECIMAL
- **Complements** qualify a basic signal. Example: **DY** reports a sinking, **DY 4** asks for the depth where the vessel sank.
- **Formats:** bearings and courses 3 figures · dates **D** + numerals · latitude **L** · longitude **G** · distance **R** · speed **S** (knots) or **V** (km/h) · time **T** (local) or **Z** (GMT)

## Chapter 4: distress and lifesaving signals
- **Section 1: Distress signals.** **14 distress signals**, including a gun or explosive signal fired about once a minute, orange smoke, flares, slowly raising and lowering outstretched arms, EPIRB signals, and the spoken word **MAYDAY** (signal 5). SOS in Morse is signal 4. The **radiotelegraph alarm is a series of twelve dashes**.
- **Section 2: Lifesaving signals.** Six parts: I landing signals for small boats, II shore lifesaving apparatus signals, III replies from lifesaving stations ("You are seen, assistance will be given as soon as possible"), IV air-to-surface signals from aircraft and ship responses, V surface-to-air signals, VI signals to survivors. Also medical transport emblems, a flashing blue light of **60 to 100 flashes per minute**, and the group **YYY** for radar and underwater identification.
- **Section 3: Radiotelephone procedures.** Prefixes **MAYDAY** (distress), **PAN** (urgency), **SECURITE** (safety). Table 1 phonetic alphabet and figure spelling, Table 2 position coding, Table 3 nature-of-distress and answer codes.

## Medical Signal Code (Chapter 3)
- **Section 1:** explanation and the standard way to describe a case: patient description, previous health, localization of symptoms, general symptoms, particular symptoms. Instructions to doctors for diagnosis and dosing.
- **Section 2:** request for medical assistance (codes start with **MAA to MAP** for general information)
- **Section 3:** medical advice
- **Section 4:** tables M-1 body regions, M-2 common diseases, M-3 medicaments
