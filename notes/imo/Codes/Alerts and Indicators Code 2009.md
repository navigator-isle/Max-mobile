# Alerts and Indicators Code 2009 — Code on Alerts and Indicators

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | **2 Dec 2009**, IMO Assembly Resolution **A.1021(26)** (26th session). Document dated 18 Jan 2010. |
| Replaces | Resolution **A.830(19)**, the 1995 Code on Alarms and Indicators |
| Status | Governments are recommended to implement it and to use it as an **international safety standard for designing alarms and indicators** on ships, equipment and machinery |
| Purpose | Design guidance and uniform **type, location and priority** of the alerts and indicators required by SOLAS, MARPOL and IMO codes |
| Structure | Sections 1–11 plus an appendix with sample indicator columns |
| Related | MSC.302(87), Bridge Alert Management performance standards (17 May 2010) |

![[diagram:alert-priorities|The four alert priorities]]

> **Key Point:** Every alert has one of four priorities: **emergency alarm, alarm, warning, caution**. The priority decides how it sounds, what colour it shows, and where it appears. Emergency alarms mean immediate danger to human life or to the ship.

## Alert priorities (3.1)
| Priority | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Emergency alarm** (3.1.1) | Immediate danger to human life or to the ship and its machinery exists, requiring immediate action |
| **Alarm** (3.1.2) | High-priority alert. Needs immediate attention and action to maintain safe navigation and operation. |
| **Warning** (3.1.3) | No immediate attention or action needed. Precautionary, for changed conditions that are not yet hazardous but may become so. |
| **Caution** (3.1.4) | Lowest priority. A condition that does not warrant an alarm or warning but needs attention beyond normal consideration. |

### Other definitions
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Indicator (3.4) | Visual indication of the condition of a system or equipment |
| Signal (3.5) | Audible indication of the condition |
| Required (3.6) | Alerts and indicators required by the instruments listed in 1.1. All others are non-required. |
| Call (3.7) | A request for contact, assistance or action |
| Silence (3.8) | Manually stopping an audible signal |
| Acknowledge (3.9) | Manual response to receiving an alert or call |
| Aggregation (3.10) | Combining individual alerts into one |
| Grouping (3.11) | Arranging alerts or indicators on panels |
| Prioritization (3.12) | Ordering alerts by severity, function or sequence |

## Which alerts are which
### Emergency alarms (3.2)
- General emergency alarm
- Fire alarm
- Water ingress detection **main** alarm
- Alerts warning of immediate personnel hazard: **fire-extinguishing pre-discharge alarm** and **power-operated sliding watertight door closing alarm**
- Additional emergency alarms may be defined for special ships such as high-speed craft

### Alarms (3.3)
Machinery · steering gear · control system fault · bilge · water ingress **pre**-alarm · engineers' · personnel · BNWAS second and third stage remote audible · fire detection · fixed local application fire-extinguishing activation · faults in alert management or detection systems or loss of their power · cargo · gas detection · power-operated watertight door faults · navigation-related alarms under INS

- No specific warnings or cautions are listed in the annex. Examples in Table 10.1.1: a **BNWAS malfunction or power failure is a warning**, and a condition needing action to prevent degradation to an unsafe condition is a caution.

## General requirements (Section 4)
- **4.1:** clear, distinctive, unambiguous, consistent presentation
- **4.2:** required alerts need **both audible and visual** indication. Emergency alarms are primarily signal-based. In high-noise machinery spaces signals are supplemented by indicators.
- **4.4:** a new alert must be clearly distinguishable from acknowledged ones. Unacknowledged alarms and warnings **flash and sound**, acknowledged ones show a **constant light**. Audible signals stop when silenced or acknowledged.
- **4.5:** alerts stay until acknowledged. Visual indication stays until the fault is corrected. A second fault after acknowledgement repeats the signals.
- **4.6:** reset only after the abnormal condition is rectified
- **4.8:** required alert systems continuously powered with automatic changeover to standby power
- **4.10:** failure of the alert power supply triggers an audible and visual alarm or warning
- **4.11:** fail-to-safety where practicable. A detection circuit fault gives an audible and visual alarm.
- **4.12:** alerts must be testable, and crews trained and drilled on all alerts
- **4.13:** alert systems functionally independent of control systems, or with equivalent redundancy
- **4.15:** cables for fire and emergency alarms fire-resistant through high fire-risk areas and routed to limit total loss from fire, collision or flooding
- **4.16:** general emergency, fire and pre-discharge signals work despite failure of any one circuit or component, where practicable
- **4.17:** time delays or similar means prevent false alerts from normal transients
- **4.18:** non-required alerts on the bridge minimized

## Sound and light
![[diagram:alert-signal|Audible and visual characteristics]]

### Audible (Section 5)
- **5.1:** required alerts clearly audible and distinguishable throughout the spaces where required
- **5.2:** the fire-extinguishing pre-discharge alarm and fire alarms easily distinguishable from all other signals
- **5.6:** adjusting the sound pressure level of required signals is **not permitted**
- **5.10:** general emergency alarm performance follows LSA Code Chapter VII, 7.2. **No signal may exceed 120 dB(A)** in a space.
- **5.11:** except bells, audible signals between **200 Hz and 2,500 Hz**
- **5.13:** bridge navigational alerts **75–85 dB(A) at one metre**, or at least 10 dB(A) above ambient noise with an upper limit of 85 dB(A)

### Visual (Section 6)
- **6.1:** supplemental indicators in high-noise machinery spaces and accommodation clearly visible from all parts of the space, of high luminous intensity, multiple in large spaces
- **6.2:** flashing indicators lit for at least **50% of the cycle**, at **0.5–1.5 Hz**
- **6.3:** bridge indicators must not interfere with night vision
- **6.5:** colours follow ISO 2412, as the Administration deems appropriate
- **6.6:** on MODUs the general emergency alarm indicator may be amber if it flashes at **4 Hz or faster**

## Colours (Tables 7.1.1–7.1.3)
![[diagram:alert-colours|Indicator colours]]

| Alert | Colour |
|---|---|
| General emergency alarm | **Green/white** for summoning passengers and crew. **Red** for summoning crew to fire stations on passenger ships. |
| Fire alarm | **Red** |
| Fire-extinguishing pre-discharge alarm | **Red** for CO₂ |
| Sliding watertight door closing alarm | Red and green indicators. The red bridge indicator flashes while the door closes. |
| Water ingress main alarm | **Red** |
| Machinery, steering gear, control system fault, bilge, engineers', personnel, water ingress pre-alarm, alert system fault, cargo, gas detection (except chlorine), watertight door fault alarms | **Amber** |
| Fire detection, fixed local application fire-extinguishing activation, **chlorine** gas alarms | **Red** |
| Calls (telephone, engine-room telegraph) | **White** |

- Audible waveforms (Table 7.2): code 1.a/1.b general emergency alarm per the muster list, code 2 continuous until silenced or acknowledged, codes 3.a–3.d optional with pulse frequency 0.5–2.0 Hz

## Particular alarms (Section 8)
![[diagram:alert-timers|Alarm time limits]]

| Alarm | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Personnel alarm** (8.1) | Pre-warning **3 minutes** before the alarm. Alarm sounds on the bridge or in officers' quarters if not reset within a period **not exceeding 30 minutes**. Can be automatic or manual. The engineer acknowledges in the machinery space before disconnection. |
| **BNWAS** (8.2) | Conforms to MSC.128(75) |
| **Engineers' alarm** (8.3) | On periodically unattended machinery spaces, activates if the machinery alarm is **not acknowledged within 5 minutes at most** |
| **General emergency alarm** (8.4) | Operable from the bridge and at least one other strategic point. Passenger ships also from the safety centre. Audible throughout accommodation and normal crew workspaces, and on passenger ships recognizable in passenger places and on open decks. |

## Grouping and locations
- **Section 9:** grouping and aggregation must not hide information needed for safe operation. Individual visual alerts remain at the appropriate position when alerts are aggregated (9.4).
- **Section 10, Tables 10.1.1–10.1.9:** locations for alerts: navigation bridge, machinery space or control room, central fire control station, at the equipment, engineers' accommodation, miscellaneous, cargo control station, not specified by IMO instruments, and central ballast control station of column-stabilized MODUs. Priority abbreviations are EM, A, W, C and I.
- **Section 11:** references the IBC, IGC, SFV 1993, IGS, HSC 2000, IMDG, MODU 2009, FSS and LSA Codes, MSC.128(75) and A.481(XII)

## Bridge Alert Management, MSC.302(87)
- Adopted **17 May 2010** at MSC 87. Applies to all alerts presented on or transferred to the bridge, and takes precedence over conflicting alert requirements in existing performance standards.
- Recommended for a central alert management system (CAM) and its interface if installed on or after **1 Jul 2014**
- Four modules: **A** alert priorities and states, **B** CAM interface (including an alert history kept for at least **24 hours**), **C** interfacing and power supply, **D** documentation and familiarization
