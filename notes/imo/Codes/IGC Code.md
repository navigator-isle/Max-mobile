**IGC Code — International Code for the Construction and Equipment of Ships Carrying Liquefied Gases in Bulk**

**What is the IGC Code**
- IMO code for design, construction and equipment of gas carriers (LNG, LPG, ammonia, ethylene, chemical gases)
- Mandatory under SOLAS Ch VII Part C
- Certificate: International Certificate of Fitness for the Carriage of Liquefied Gases in Bulk (CoF) — lists permitted cargoes

**History**
- Gas Carrier Codes: Existing Ships Code (before 1976), GC Code (1976–1986)
- IGC Code adopted 1983 (MSC.5(48)) · ships built on/after 1 Jul 1986
- Revised IGC Code adopted 22 May 2014 (MSC.370(93)) · in force 1 Jan 2016 · applies to ships with keel laid on/after 1 Jul 2016
- Which Code applies (keel-laying date): before 1 Jul 1986 = GC Code · 1 Jul 1986 to 30 Jun 2016 = IGC Code as amended up to MSC.220(82) · from 1 Jul 2016 = revised IGC Code
- MSC.476(102) Ch 6 weld testing · MSC.492(104) watertight openings — in force 1 Jan 2024
- MSC.523(106): high-manganese austenitic steel for cargo tanks (−55 to −165 °C) — 1 Jan 2026
- MSC.566(109): toxic cargo (e.g., ammonia) may be used as fuel — in force 1 Jul 2026 (MSC.1/Circ.1681 allows early use)
- MSC.1/Circ.1702 (Jul 2026): interim guidelines for ammonia cargo as fuel
- Comprehensive IGC amendments approved MSC 111 (May 2026) · adoption expected Dec 2026 · in force 1 Jul 2028 ("one ship, one code" for gas carriers burning cargo)

**Applies to**
- Ships of any size carrying liquefied gases with vapour pressure >0.28 MPa absolute at 37.8 °C, and other products listed in Ch 19
- Regardless of size (incl. <500 GT)

---

**Chapters (revised Code)**
- Ch 1 General: definitions, surveys, CoF (≤5 years)
- Ch 2 Ship survival capability and location of cargo tanks: ship types **1G** (most hazardous, e.g., chlorine), **2G**, **2PG**, **3G** · damage assumptions and tank distances from shell
- Ch 3 Ship arrangements: segregation of cargo area, accommodation, air locks, access
- Ch 4 Cargo containment:
  - Independent tanks type A (full secondary barrier), type B (partial secondary barrier, e.g., Moss/SPB), type C (pressure vessels, no secondary barrier)
  - Membrane tanks (full secondary barrier) · integral, semi-membrane
- Ch 5 Process pressure vessels, liquid, vapour and pressure piping
- Ch 6 Materials of construction and quality control
- Ch 7 Cargo pressure/temperature control: reliquefaction, gas combustion unit, boil-off use as fuel
- Ch 8 Vent systems for cargo containment (PRVs, vent masts)
- Ch 9 Cargo containment system atmosphere control (inerting, nitrogen)
- Ch 10 Electrical installations
- Ch 11 Fire protection and extinction: water spray on tank domes and manifolds, dry chemical powder for deck, CO₂ for compressor rooms
- Ch 12 Artificial ventilation in the cargo area
- Ch 13 Instrumentation and automation: level, pressure, temperature, overflow (high-high alarm, ESD), gas detection
- Ch 14 Personnel protection: protective clothing, SCBA, decontamination showers, eye wash
- Ch 15 Filling limits for cargo tanks (generally ≤98% at reference temperature)
- Ch 16 Use of cargo as fuel (methane boil-off; from 2026 other cargoes like ammonia under conditions)
- Ch 17 Special requirements (toxic, flammable, ammonia, chlorine, ethylene oxide, vinyl chloride…)
- Ch 18 Operating requirements: cargo information, compatibility, training, cargo transfer, ESD/ship-shore link
- Ch 19 Summary of minimum requirements — product list (ship type, tank type, vapour detection, gauging, special requirements)

**Key Point**
- Cargo must be on the CoF list; check Ch 19 row for that product
- ESD tested before transfer · ship-shore safety checklist · filling limit
- Ammonia: toxic — gas detection, PPE and water spray ready; from 2026 can be burned as fuel only with flag approval and equivalent safety
