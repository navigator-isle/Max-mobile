# IGC Code — International Code for the Construction and Equipment of Ships Carrying Liquefied Gases in Bulk

## At a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 17 Jun 1983, resolution MSC.5(48); mandatory under SOLAS chapter VII (MSC.6(48)) |
| Original Code | Ships built on or after **1 Jul 1986** |
| Revised Code | MSC.370(93), adopted 22 May 2014; in force **1 Jan 2016**; applies to keels laid on or after **1 Jul 2016** |
| Applies to | Ships of any size, including under 500 GT, carrying liquefied gases with vapour pressure above **0.28 MPa absolute at 37.8 °C**, and other products listed in chapter 19 |
| Certificate | International Certificate of Fitness for the Carriage of Liquefied Gases in Bulk, up to 5 years |
| Structure | 19 chapters and 5 appendices |

## Which version applies

| Ship constructed | Requirements |
|---|---|
| 1 Jul 1986 to before 1 Jul 2016 | The earlier IGC Code, as amended by the resolutions listed in the revised Code |
| On or after 1 Jul 2016 | The revised IGC Code (MSC.370(93)) |
| Converted to a gas carrier on or after 1 Jul 2016 | Counts as constructed when the conversion begins |

- "Keel laid or similar stage" means identifiable construction begins and assembly reaches at least **50 tonnes or 1%** of the estimated structural mass, whichever is less.

- The revised Code was the first major overhaul since 1983, after a five-year review.
- Corrections to MSC.370(93) were issued in 2015 and 2016 (for example paragraphs 5.9.3.1, 5.11.6.3 and 13.6.4).

**MSC.566(109): paragraph 16.9.2**
- Cargoes that must be carried in **type 1G** ships (column "c" of chapter 19) are not allowed as fuel.
- Toxic products in column "f" that must be carried in **type 2G/2PG** ships may be used as fuel only if the Administration accepts it, the same level of safety as natural gas (methane) is ensured, and the Administration has given special consideration, taking IMO guidelines into account.

**MSC 111 (13–22 May 2026)**
- Approved draft amendments to the IGC Code (annex 20 of the report), for adoption at MSC 112, with entry into force on **1 Jul 2028**. They follow the "one ship, one code" policy for gas carriers.
- Approved MSC.1/Circ.1702, Interim Guidelines for use of ammonia cargo as fuel.
- These amendments are **not yet adopted**; treat them as pending.

## Certificate and surveys (Chapter 1)

- Certificate valid for a period set by the Administration, not more than **5 years**.
- Renewal surveys at intervals not over 5 years; annual surveys within 3 months before or after each anniversary; intermediate survey within 3 months before or after the 2nd or 3rd anniversary.
- If renewal is completed within 3 months before expiry, the new certificate runs up to 5 years from the old expiry date.
- The certificate stops being valid if surveys are not completed on time, endorsements are missing, or the ship changes flag.

| 3G | Moderate preventive measures | |

The required ship type for each product is in column "c" of the chapter 19 table.

| Damage assumption | Extent |
|---|---|
| Side, longitudinal | 1/3 L^(2/3) or 14.5 m, whichever is less |
| Side, transverse | B/5 or 11.5 m, whichever is less |
| Side, vertical | Upwards from the shell, without limit |
| Bottom, transverse | B/6 or 10 m (forward 0.3L); B/6 or 5 m (elsewhere) |
| Bottom, vertical | B/15 or 2 m, whichever is less |

**Tank location (2.4.1)**
- Type 1G: at least the transverse side-damage extent from the shell, and the vertical bottom-damage extent from the bottom; never less than d.
- Types 2G/2PG: at least the vertical bottom-damage extent from the bottom; never less than d.
- Type 3G: at least the vertical bottom-damage extent from the bottom; at least 0.8 m from the shell.
- d depends on tank volume Vc: 0.8 m for up to 1,000 m³; 2 m for 30,000 m³ or more.
- Cargo tanks must not be forward of the collision bulkhead.

**Survival (2.7)**
- Waterline must stay below the lower edge of openings that could cause progressive flooding or downflooding.
- Maximum heel from unsymmetrical flooding: 30°.
- At final equilibrium: righting lever range at least 20° beyond equilibrium, residual righting lever at least 0.1 m, area at least 0.0175 m·rad.

## Ship arrangements (Chapter 3)

- Hold spaces are segregated from machinery, accommodation, service spaces and stores.
- No accommodation, service spaces or control stations in the cargo area.
- Entrances, air inlets and openings must not face the cargo area. Where on the end bulkhead facing it, they must be at least 4% of L and at least 3 m from the superstructure end (need not exceed 5 m).
- Windows facing the cargo area in that zone are fixed and A-60 class.
- Airlock between hazardous weather deck and non-hazardous spaces: two self-closing, substantially gastight steel doors, **1.5 to 2.5 m apart**, held at overpressure.

## Cargo containment (Chapter 4)

![[diagram:igc-tank-types|Containment types and design vapour pressure Po]]

- Secondary barrier: not required if the cargo is at or above **-10 °C** at atmospheric pressure (4.4.5). Hull structure may act as the secondary barrier where cargo temperature is not below -55 °C (4.6.1). A secondary barrier must contain leakage for **15 days**, unless other criteria apply for particular voyages (4.6.2.1).
- Membranes normally do not exceed 10 mm thickness.

> **Key Point:** The cargo must appear on the ship's Certificate of Fitness. Check its row in chapter 19: ship type, tank type and special requirements. Test emergency shutdown before cargo operations, keep the filling limit at 98% unless a higher limit is approved, and remember that type 1G cargoes can never be used as fuel.
