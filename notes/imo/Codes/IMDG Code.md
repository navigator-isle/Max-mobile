**IMDG Code — International Maritime Dangerous Goods Code**

**What is the IMDG Code**
- IMO code for the safe carriage of dangerous goods in packaged form (packages, IBCs, portable tanks, road tank vehicles, containers) by sea
- Mandatory under SOLAS Ch VII Part A and MARPOL Annex III (marine pollutants)
- Based on the UN Recommendations on the Transport of Dangerous Goods ("Orange Book") · updated every 2 years
- 2 volumes + Supplement (EmS Guide, MFAG, IMO packing/securing guidance)

**History**
- 1965 first adopted (recommendatory) · adopted as mandatory Code 24 May 2002 (MSC.122(75); SOLAS VII amended by MSC.123(75)) · voluntary 1 Jan 2003 · mandatory 1 Jan 2004
- 2-year amendment cycle: each amendment voluntary from 1 Jan of the odd year, mandatory from 1 Jan of the next year
- Amdt 41-22 (MSC.501(105)): mandatory 1 Jan 2024
- Amdt 42-24 (MSC.556(108)): voluntary 1 Jan 2025 · mandatory 1 Jan 2026
- Amdt 43-26 (adopted MSC 111, May 2026): voluntary 1 Jan 2027 · mandatory 1 Jan 2028

**Applies to**
- All ships to which SOLAS applies carrying dangerous goods in packaged form, and all ships under MARPOL Annex III
- Also: shippers, packers, freight forwarders, terminals (training in Ch 1.3 is mandatory for shore staff)
- Not covered: bulk cargoes (IMSBC, IBC, IGC), ship's own stores and equipment

**Volume 1 — Parts 1, 2, 4–7**

*Part 1 — General provisions, definitions, training*
- 1.1 Application · 1.2 Definitions, units, abbreviations
- 1.3 Training (mandatory for shore-side personnel; records kept)
- 1.4 Security provisions (high-consequence dangerous goods, security plans)
- 1.5 Radioactive material general provisions

*Part 2 — Classification*
- Class 1 Explosives (Divisions 1.1–1.6, compatibility groups A–S)
- Class 2 Gases: 2.1 flammable · 2.2 non-flammable, non-toxic · 2.3 toxic
- Class 3 Flammable liquids (flashpoint ≤60 °C)
- Class 4: 4.1 flammable solids, self-reactive, desensitized explosives · 4.2 spontaneously combustible · 4.3 dangerous when wet
- Class 5: 5.1 oxidizing substances · 5.2 organic peroxides
- Class 6: 6.1 toxic · 6.2 infectious
- Class 7 Radioactive material
- Class 8 Corrosive substances
- Class 9 Miscellaneous (incl. lithium and sodium-ion batteries, environmentally hazardous substances)
- 2.10 Marine pollutants (MARPOL Annex III) — "Marine Pollutant" mark
- Packing groups: I high danger · II medium · III low

*Part 4 — Packing and tank provisions*
- 4.1 Packagings, IBCs, large packagings (packing instructions P/IBC/LP)
- 4.2 Portable tanks and MEGCs (T codes) · 4.3 Bulk containers (BK)

*Part 5 — Consignment procedures*
- 5.1 General · 5.2 Marking and labelling of packages (UN number, proper shipping name, labels, marine pollutant mark)
- 5.3 Placarding and marking of cargo transport units (placards ≥250 × 250 mm on each side)
- 5.4 Documentation:
  - Dangerous Goods Declaration (UN number, PSN, class, PG, quantity, marine pollutant, flashpoint)
  - Container/Vehicle Packing Certificate (signed by packer)
  - Dangerous goods manifest or stowage plan on board (from 42-24: shows subsidiary hazards too)
  - 5.4.4.2 (42-24): exemption certificates go with the cargo information
- 5.5 Special provisions (fumigated units — FUMIGATED mark; CTUs with coolant/conditioner)

*Part 6 — Construction and testing of packagings, IBCs, tanks*
- UN specification marks · drop, stacking, leakproofness, hydraulic pressure tests

*Part 7 — Operations (shipboard)*
- 7.1 Stowage: categories A–E (passenger and cargo ships); "on deck only" / "under deck" codes
- 7.2 Segregation: terms "away from", "separated from", "separated by a complete compartment or hold from", "separated longitudinally by an intervening complete compartment or hold from" · Segregation Table 7.2.4
- 7.3 Consigning operations for CTUs (packing, securing, CTU Code)
- 7.4–7.7 Container ships, ro-ro ships, general cargo ships, barge-carrying ships
- 7.8 Special requirements in the event of an incident and fire precautions
- 7.9 Exemptions, approvals and certificates

**Volume 2 — Part 3 and Appendices**
- 3.1 General · 3.2 Dangerous Goods List (by UN number: PSN, class, subsidiary risk, PG, special provisions, limited/excepted quantities, packing, tanks, EmS, stowage & handling, segregation, properties)
- 3.3 Special provisions · 3.4 Limited quantities · 3.5 Excepted quantities
- Appendix A Generic and N.O.S. names · Appendix B Glossary · Index

**Supplement**
- EmS Guide: emergency schedules — Fire (F-A to F-J) and Spillage (S-A to S-Z)
- MFAG: Medical First Aid Guide for accidents involving dangerous goods
- Reporting procedures · CTU Code reference · IMO Packing guidance · Safe use of pesticides (fumigation)
- INF Code (irradiated nuclear fuel, plutonium, high-level radioactive waste)

**Amendment 42-24 (mandatory 1 Jan 2026) — what changed**
- Sodium-ion batteries (Class 9, UN 3551/3552) now handled in line with lithium battery entries
- New vehicle entries: UN 3556 (lithium-ion), 3557 (lithium metal), 3558 (sodium-ion) — packing instruction P912
- UN 1361 Carbon: SP925 deleted; new SP978 (weathering, temperature checks, packing, documents) · UN 1362 activated carbon SP979
- Stowage moved to Category D (deck only; not on passenger ships): UN 3129, 3130, 3148, and UN 3536 (lithium batteries installed in CTUs)
- UN 3423 reclassified Class 6.1 (sub 8), PG I
- Stowage plan must show subsidiary hazards

**Key Point**
- Check every DG unit against the Declaration: UN number, PSN, class, labels/placards, marine pollutant mark, segregation
- Stowage plan and DG manifest on the bridge; EmS numbers ready for fire/spill
- Most container fires come from mis-declared cargo (charcoal, lithium batteries, calcium hypochlorite)
- Record the amendment in use: 42-24 from 1 Jan 2026
