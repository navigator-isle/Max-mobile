# IMDG Code — International Maritime Dangerous Goods Code

## At a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Safe carriage of dangerous goods in **packaged form** by sea; harmonize requirements; prevent pollution |
| First adopted | 1965, as a recommendatory instrument |
| Mandatory Code | Adopted 24 May 2002, MSC.122(75); SOLAS VII amended by MSC.123(75); voluntary from 1 Jan 2003; **mandatory from 1 Jan 2004** |
| Legal basis | SOLAS chapter VII (carriage of dangerous goods) and MARPOL Annex III (harmful substances in packaged form) |
| Amendments | Every **two years**, adopted by MSC |
| Current mandatory edition | 2024 edition, Amendment 42-24, mandatory **1 Jan 2026** |
| Next | Amendment 43-26 (adopted MSC 111, May 2026), MSC.587(111), in force **1 Jan 2028** |

## What it covers

- Packing, container traffic and stowage, including keeping incompatible substances apart
- Detailed requirements for each individual substance, material or article
- Mandatory under SOLAS VII and MARPOL Annex III, which the Code extends
- Some parts remain **recommendatory**: training paragraphs 1.3.1.4 to 1.3.1.7, security provisions in chapter 1.4 (except 1.4.1.1), and several sections including 7.8 and appendix B

## History and amendments

![[diagram:imdg-cycle|The two-year amendment cycle]]

- The IMDG Code was recommended by a 1960 SOLAS conference; an IMO working group began drafting in 1961 and the 4th Assembly adopted it in 1965.
- MSC.122(75) adopted the Code in mandatory form on 24 May 2002; it supersedes the earlier version adopted by resolution A.716(17). Future amendments follow SOLAS article VIII.
- Amendments come from Member Government proposals and from updates to the UN Recommendations on the Transport of Dangerous Goods. Amendments that do not change the Code's principles can be adopted by MSC alone.

| Amendment | Resolution | Adopted | Voluntary from | Mandatory from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 41-22 | MSC.501(105) | 28 Apr 2022 | 1 Jan 2023 | 1 Jan 2024 |
| 42-24 | MSC.556(108) | 23 May 2024 | 1 Jan 2025 | 1 Jan 2026 |
| 43-26 | MSC.587(111) | MSC 111, 13–22 May 2026 | Not confirmed | 1 Jan 2028 |

## Segregation (7.2)

- **Segregation** is separating two or more mutually incompatible substances or articles (7.2.2.1). It is done by distance, by one or more steel bulkheads or decks, or both. Compatible cargo may fill the space between.
- Four terms: "Away from", "Separated from", "Separated by a complete compartment or hold from", and "Separated longitudinally by an intervening complete compartment or hold from" (7.2.2.2).
- Goods subject to these terms may not be packed in the same outer packaging or carried in the same cargo transport unit, with exceptions (7.2.3.2).
- A reference to a class in the list (for example "away from class …") covers every substance of that class and every substance needing a subsidiary risk label for that class.
- Chemically similar goods (acids, alkalis, peroxides) form **segregation groups** (7.2.5).
- Dangerous goods of certain hazard classes must be kept separate from foodstuffs (7.3.4, 7.6.3, 7.7.3).

## Documents

| Document | Who | Points |
|---|---|---|
| Dangerous Goods Declaration (5.4.1.6) | Consignor or shipper, signed and dated | Accompanies the consignment or is part of the transport document; confirms contents are fully and accurately described, classified, packaged, marked, labelled and in proper condition |
| Container/Vehicle Packing Certificate (5.4.2) | The person who packs the unit (consignor, or consolidator for groupage) | Required for any container or vehicle, not for tanks. Certifies the unit was clean, dry and fit; segregation met; packages inspected and sound; drums upright; goods secured; unit marked and placarded; a transport document received for each consignment |

- Masters and operators must not accept dangerous goods without the required document and signed certification.
- Personnel involved in sea transport of dangerous goods need training suited to their responsibilities.

## Amendment 42-24 (mandatory 1 Jan 2026)

- **Lithium batteries:** articles that may contain lithium cells or batteries are classified as dangerous goods N.O.S.; damaged or defective batteries in an article must be removed and packed under special packaging instructions; manufacturers must make test summaries available.
- **New Class 9 entries:** UN 3556 (vehicle, lithium ion battery powered), UN 3557 (vehicle, lithium metal battery powered), UN 3558 (vehicles powered by sodium-ion batteries) and UN 3551/3552 (sodium-ion batteries and equipment). Eleven new substances and articles in total. A new packing instruction applies to battery-powered vehicles.
- **UN 3171** no longer covers lithium batteries; it applies only to vehicles and equipment powered by wet batteries, metallic sodium batteries or sodium alloy batteries.
- **Charcoal and carbon (UN 1361, 1362):** SP 925 and SP 223 withdrawn; SP 978 and SP 979 introduced. SP 978 sets conditions on weathering, packaging (including a 40 °C limit on the day of packing) and stowage. SP 979 exempts steam- or chemically-activated charcoal that meets its criteria.
- **Stowage plan** must show primary and subsidiary hazard classes and the location of all dangerous goods and marine pollutants, and can replace a manifest or special list.
- Data loggers, sensors and tracking devices in a cargo transport unit must be securely installed and certified safe for the goods carried.
- A new stowage code for flammable gases or liquids with flashpoint below 23 °C requires separation from potential ignition sources.
- Some existing labelling and warning mark provisions may continue to apply until **31 Dec 2026**.

> **Key Point:** Check every dangerous goods unit against its declaration and packing certificate, keep the stowage plan showing primary and subsidiary hazards, and apply segregation from the Dangerous Goods List (column 16b prevails over the table). Record which amendment is in use: 42-24 from 1 Jan 2026, then 43-26 from 1 Jan 2028.

## Not included (not verified against the original text)

- Packing group definitions, placard minimum size, stowage categories A–E, and EmS schedule codes.
- Class 1 and class 2 divisions, class 3 flashpoint limit.
- Voluntary start date of Amendment 43-26 and its list of changes.
- Old note's items on Amendment 42-24: P912, UN 3423 reclassification, and stowage category D for UN 3129, 3130, 3148 and 3536.
- The Supplement contents (EmS Guide, MFAG) and detailed training content.
