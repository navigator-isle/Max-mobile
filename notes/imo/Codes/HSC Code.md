**HSC Code — International Code of Safety for High-Speed Craft (1994 and 2000 Codes)**

**What is the HSC Code**
- Complete safety standards for high-speed craft (fast ferries, catamarans, hydrofoils, hovercraft, surface-effect ships) — design, equipment, operation and crew
- Based on a **risk-management approach**: lighter construction is accepted in return for operational limits (route, weather, rescue close at hand)
- Mandatory under SOLAS Chapter X

**History**
- Code of Safety for Dynamically Supported Craft A.373(10) (1977) — predecessor
- **1994 HSC Code**: MSC.36(63), adopted 20 May 1994 · mandatory with SOLAS Ch X from 1 Jan 1996 (craft built 1 Jan 1996 – 30 Jun 2002)
- **2000 HSC Code**: MSC.97(73), adopted 5 Dec 2000 · in force 1 Jul 2002 (craft built on or after that date)
- Many amendments (LSA, fire safety, navigation equipment, PFOS ban 2026)
- MSC 111 (May 2026): VDES may be fitted (voluntary) as on SOLAS ships · in force 1 Jan 2028

**Applies to**
- High-speed craft on international voyages: passenger craft not more than 4 hours (Cat A) from a place of refuge at operational speed; cargo craft of 500 GT and above not more than 8 hours from a place of refuge
- **High-speed craft** = maximum speed in m/s ≥ **3.7 × ∇^0.1667** (∇ = displacement at design waterline, m³)
- Not: warships, pleasure craft, fishing vessels, WIG craft

---

**Craft categories**
- **Category A passenger craft**: max 450 passengers; route where rescue of everyone is highly probable, within the least of: time to prevent hypothermia, time suited to the route, or 4 hours
- **Category B passenger craft**: more passengers or longer routes; machinery and safety systems arranged so that with essential machinery and systems in any one compartment disabled the craft can still navigate safely
- **Cargo craft**: ≥ 500 GT, within 8 hours of a place of refuge

**Chapters (2000 Code)**
- 1 General comments and requirements (certificates, surveys, definitions)
- 2 Buoyancy, stability and subdivision (intact and damage, for displacement and non-displacement modes)
- 3 Structures · 4 Accommodation and escape measures (seating; everyone evacuated in **(SFP − 7)/3 minutes**, i.e. about one third of the structural fire protection time)
- 5 Directional control systems · 6 Anchoring, towing and berthing
- 7 Fire safety (fire-restricting materials, structural fire protection) · 8 Life-saving appliances (marine evacuation systems, liferafts)
- 9 Machinery · 10 Auxiliary systems · 11 Remote control, alarm and safety systems · 12 Electrical installations
- 13 Navigational systems and equipment and VDR · 14 Radiocommunications · 15 Operating compartment layout
- 16 Stabilization systems · 17 Handling, controllability and performance · 18 Operational requirements (route, weather limits, training, type rating) · 19 Inspection and maintenance
- Annexes: certificate forms, Permit to Operate, FMEA (failure mode and effects analysis), stability of hydrofoils and multihulls, icing

**Certificates and documents**
- **High-Speed Craft Safety Certificate** (up to 5 years) and **Permit to Operate High-Speed Craft** (route, weather limits, number of passengers)
- Craft operating manual, route operational manual, training manual, maintenance manual

**Crew**
- **Type rating certificate** for the craft and route; training in emergency procedures; regular drills and evacuation demonstrations

**Key Point**
- Never operate outside the limits on the Permit to Operate (wave height, wind, distance from refuge)
- Crew need a type rating for each craft type — not just STCW certificates
- Full evacuation must be possible within (SFP − 7)/3 minutes — demonstrated in an evacuation test
