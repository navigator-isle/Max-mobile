# HSC Code — International Code of Safety for High-Speed Craft

**1994 HSC Code and 2000 HSC Code**

## At a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1994 HSC Code | MSC.36(63), adopted 20 May 1994 · mandatory from 1 Jan 1996 · craft built on or after 1 Jan 1996 and before 1 Jul 2002 |
| 2000 HSC Code | MSC.97(73), adopted 5 Dec 2000 · in force 1 Jul 2002 · craft built on or after 1 Jul 2002 |
| Mandatory by | SOLAS chapter X (MSC.99(73) for the 2000 Code) |
| Structure | Preamble · 19 chapters · 11 annexes |
| Certificate | High-Speed Craft Safety Certificate, up to 5 years |
| Also needed | Permit to Operate High-Speed Craft |
| Voyage limit | Passenger craft up to 4 h, cargo craft up to 8 h at operational speed from a place of refuge |

## What is the HSC Code

- Complete safety standards for **high-speed craft** on international voyages: design, equipment, operation and crew
- Requires the Code to be applied in its entirety, a quality-management system, operation only by persons qualified for the craft type, operating limits, proximity to a place of refuge, and no enclosed sleeping berths for passengers (1.2)
- The 1994 Code introduced "assisted craft" (basis for Category A) and "unassisted craft" (basis for cargo craft and Category B)

## What counts as a high-speed craft (1.4.30)

![[diagram:hsc-speed-line|The speed threshold, from 3.7 × displacement^0.1667]]

- A craft whose maximum speed in m/s is equal to or greater than **3.7 ∇^0.1667**, where ∇ is the displacement volume at the design waterline in m³
- Craft supported entirely clear of the water by ground-effect aerodynamic forces are excluded
- **Maximum speed (1.4.37):** at maximum continuous propulsion power certified, at maximum operational weight, in smooth water
- **Operational speed (1.4.44):** 90% of maximum speed

## Application (1.3)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Construction date (1.3.1) | Keels laid, or similar stage of construction, on or after 1 Jul 2002 (2000 Code) |
| "Similar stage" (1.3.2) | Construction identifiable with a specific craft has begun and assembly has started of at least **50 tonnes or 3%** of the estimated mass of all structural material, whichever is less |
| Conversions (1.3.3) | A cargo craft converted to passenger use is treated as a passenger craft constructed when the conversion begins |
| Passenger craft (1.3.4) | Not more than **4 hours** at operational speed from a place of refuge |
| Cargo craft (1.3.4) | 500 GT and above; not more than **8 hours** at operational speed from a place of refuge, fully laden |
| Excluded (1.3.5–1.3.6) | Craft of war and troopcraft, unpropelled craft, primitive wooden craft, non-trading pleasure craft, fishing craft, and craft operating solely on the Great Lakes and the lower St. Lawrence |

## Craft types

![[diagram:hsc-craft-types|Category A, Category B and cargo craft]]

- **Passenger craft (1.4.47):** carries more than **12 passengers**. A passenger excludes the master, crew, persons employed on the craft's business, and children under one year.
- **Place of refuge (1.4.48):** a naturally or artificially sheltered area a craft can use under conditions likely to endanger it.
- **Worst intended conditions (1.4.61):** the specified environmental limits within which the craft's operation is certified.

## Certificates and permits (Chapter 1)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| High-Speed Craft Safety Certificate (1.8) | Issued after an initial or renewal survey to a craft that complies; the Administration assumes full responsibility. Valid **up to 5 years** (1.8.4); renewal surveys at intervals not over 5 years (1.5.1.2); periodical surveys within 3 months before or after each anniversary (1.5.1.3) |
| Short-term endorsement (1.8.9) | An existing certificate may be endorsed for up to 5 months after expiry |
| Extension (1.8.10) | To reach a survey location, limited to one month |
| Permit to Operate (1.9) | Needed for commercial operation in addition to the Safety Certificate (transit voyages without passengers or cargo exempt). Sets the operating conditions from the route operational manual (Ch 18); the Administration consults each port State before issuing; port States may inspect, and deficiencies make the Permit invalid until corrected |
| Type rating | Type rating training: 18.6 (passenger craft) and 18.8 (cargo craft) |

## Selected requirements

| Chapter | Figures stated in the text |
|---|---|
| 2 Stability | Displacement mode, intact: inclination not over **10°** in still water (2.11). Non-displacement: heel from turning not over 8°, total heel from beam wind and turning not over 12° (2.12.2). Damaged passenger craft: normally not over 10°, up to 15° allowed right after damage if reduced to 10° within 15 min (2.13.1). Category B, raking damage: not over 20°, positive righting lever range at least 15°, positive area at least 0.015 m·rad (2.13.2). Damaged cargo craft: normally not over 15°, up to 20° right after damage reducing to 15° within 15 min (2.15). Passenger craft lightweight survey at intervals not over 5 years; re-incline if lightweight displacement deviates more than 2% or LCG more than 1% of L (2.14.1) |
| 4 Accommodation | A seat for each passenger and crew member (4.5.1). At least two unobstructed evacuation paths per person (4.7.12) and two exits from each enclosed public space (4.7.4). Corridors, doorways and stairs at least **900 mm** (passenger craft) and **700 mm** (cargo craft) (4.7.13). Guard rails at least 1 m high (4.11.1). Noise: public spaces and crew accommodation generally not above 75 dB(A), operating compartment not above 65 dB(A) (4.10) |
| 7 Fire safety | Manual call points: no part of the space more than 20 m from one (7.8.3.2). Ro-ro spaces: no point more than about 15 m walking distance from an extinguisher (7.8.4.3). Hinged fire doors close in no more than 40 s and no less than 10 s (7.9.3.3.1). Fire-fighter's breathing apparatus works at least 30 min (7.10.3.2.2) |
| 8 Life-saving | Survival craft for at least **100%** of persons certified, minimum two craft (8.10.1.1), plus additional craft for at least **10%** (8.10.1.2). Davit-launched craft not fitted: marine evacuation system or equivalent (8.7.4). MES serviced at intervals not over 12 months (8.9.7) and deployed at least once every six years (8.9.8) |

**Evacuation time (4.8.1):** the craft is arranged so that evacuation time = **(SFP − 7) / 3 minutes**, where SFP is the structural fire protection time from 7.4.2 for areas of major fire hazard, and 7 minutes is deducted for initial detection and extinguishing. Worked example: the SFP times are based on protection for 60 minutes, so (60 − 7) / 3 is about 17.7 minutes.

## Operational requirements (Chapter 18)

- **Part A (general):** craft operational control · craft documentation · training and qualifications · manning of survival craft and supervision · emergency instructions and drills
- **Part B (passenger craft):** type rating training (18.6) · emergency instructions and drills (18.7)
- **Part C (cargo craft):** type rating training (18.8)

> **Key Point:** The craft must hold both a valid HSC Safety Certificate and a Permit to Operate, and stay within the Permit's limits. Crew need training for the craft type (type rating), not only general certificates. Evacuation time is set by (SFP − 7) / 3 minutes.
