**NOx Technical Code 2008 — Technical Code on Control of Emission of Nitrogen Oxides from Marine Diesel Engines**

**What is the NOx Technical Code**
- How marine diesel engines are tested, surveyed and certified to show they meet the NOx limits of MARPOL Annex VI Regulation 13
- Mandatory under MARPOL Annex VI
- Gives the **EIAPP certificate**, the engine **Technical File** and on-board verification methods

**History**
- 1997: first NOx Technical Code with MARPOL Annex VI (in force 19 May 2005)
- Revised 2008 Code adopted 10 Oct 2008, resolution MEPC.177(58) · in force 1 Jul 2010 (with revised Annex VI)
- Later amendments: e.g. MEPC.251(66) (dual-fuel and gas-fuelled engines, SCR testing)
- MEPC 84 (Apr–May 2026): amendments aligning Annex VI and the Code for engines with **multiple engine operational profiles** (entry into force after adoption procedure)

**Applies to**
- Marine diesel engines with power output **over 130 kW**, installed on a ship (new engines, and engines with a major conversion)
- Not: engines used only in emergencies or only for lifeboats/emergency equipment

---

**Chapters**
- Introduction · abbreviations, subscripts and symbols
- **Ch 1 General**: purpose, application, definitions (engine family, engine group, parent engine, rated power and speed)
- **Ch 2 Surveys and certification**: pre-certification survey → **EIAPP Certificate**; initial survey on board; periodical and intermediate surveys (with the IAPP certificate); Technical File and **Record Book of Engine Parameters**; on-board NOx verification procedures
- **Ch 3 NOx emission standards**: limits from Reg 13 and the **test cycles** (E2, E3 for propulsion; D2 for constant-speed auxiliaries; C1 for variable-speed auxiliaries)
- **Ch 4 Approval of serially manufactured engines**: engine family and engine group concepts, parent engine selection
- **Ch 5 Test-bed measurements**: test conditions, fuels, analysers, calibration, data evaluation
- **Ch 6 Demonstrating compliance on board**: engine parameter check method · simplified measurement method · direct measurement and monitoring method
- **Ch 7 Certification of an existing engine** (pre-2000 engines over 5,000 kW and 90 L/cyl, Reg 13.7 approved methods)
- Appendices I–VIII: EIAPP certificate form; flow charts; analyser specifications; calibration; parent engine test report; exhaust mass flow (carbon balance); parameter check checklist; direct measurement

**NOx Tiers (MARPOL Annex VI Reg 13) — g/kWh by rated speed n (rpm)**
- **Tier I** (ships built 1 Jan 2000 – 31 Dec 2010): n < 130 → 17.0 · 130 ≤ n < 2,000 → 45 × n^(-0.2) · n ≥ 2,000 → 9.8
- **Tier II** (built from 1 Jan 2011): 14.4 · 44 × n^(-0.23) · 7.7
- **Tier III** (built from 1 Jan 2016, in **NOx ECAs** only): 3.4 · 9 × n^(-0.2) · 2.0
  - NOx ECAs: North American and US Caribbean Sea (ships built from 2016); Baltic and North Sea (built from 2021); Norwegian Sea (built from 1 Mar 2026); later ECAs (Canadian Arctic, NE Atlantic) on their own dates

**Documents on board**
- EIAPP certificate for each engine · Technical File (approved) · Record Book of Engine Parameters · Tier III SCR/EGR documentation; Tier change-over log when entering/leaving NOx ECAs

**Key Point**
- The engine must match its Technical File — any change to components or settings (injectors, turbochargers, timing) must be recorded and allowed by the file
- Tier III only applies inside NOx ECAs; log the time and position of Tier changes
- PSC checks EIAPP, Technical File, record book and NOx-critical component markings
