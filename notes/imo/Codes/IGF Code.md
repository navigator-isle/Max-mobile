**IGF Code — International Code of Safety for Ships Using Gases or Other Low-flashpoint Fuels**

**What is the IGF Code**
- IMO code for ships using gas or low-flashpoint fuels (flashpoint <60 °C) — mainly LNG today
- Mandatory under SOLAS Ch II-1 Part G
- Goal-based: each chapter has a goal and functional requirements; detailed rules exist for natural gas, others need "alternative design" approval (SOLAS II-1/55) using IMO interim guidelines
- Gas carriers burning cargo use the IGC Code instead (not IGF)

**History**
- Interim Guidelines for gas-fuelled engine installations (MSC.285(86), 2009)
- Adopted 11 Jun 2015 at MSC 95 (MSC.391(95)) · in force 1 Jan 2017
- Fuel-specific interim guidelines (non-mandatory, MSC.1/Circ.):
  - Methyl/ethyl alcohol: MSC.1/Circ.1621 (2020)
  - Fuel cells: MSC.1/Circ.1647 (2022)
  - Low-flashpoint diesel: MSC.1/Circ.1666 (2023)
  - Ammonia: MSC.1/Circ.1687 (approved MSC 109, circulated Feb 2025)
  - Hydrogen (liquefied and compressed): MSC.1/Circ.1701 (approved MSC 111, circulated Jul 2026)
- MSC.524(106): high-manganese austenitic steel for fuel tanks — 1 Jan 2026
- MSC.551(108): tank design, pressure relief, gas supply, hazardous areas, bunkering, fire safety, redundancy — 1 Jan 2026
- MSC.567(109): further design, fire safety, explosion prevention, ventilation (e.g., fuel tank suction wells) — 1 Jan 2028
- Draft amendments approved MSC 111 (May 2026): Code covers gaseous fuels whether low-flashpoint or not — adoption expected Dec 2026, in force expected 1 Jul 2028

**Applies to**
- SOLAS ships using gases or other low-flashpoint fuels, built (contract/keel/delivery) on/after 1 Jan 2017
- Existing ships converting to such fuels
- Not: gas carriers under the IGC Code

---

**Part A — General**
- Ch 1 Preamble · Ch 2 General: definitions (bunkering, double barrier, ESD-protected machinery space, gas-safe machinery space, hazardous areas zones 0/1/2) · alternative design
- Ch 3 Goal and functional requirements
- Ch 4 General requirements: risk assessment (HAZID) · limitation of explosion consequences

**Part A-1 — Specific requirements for natural gas (LNG/CNG)**
- Ch 5 Ship design and arrangement: fuel tank location (≥B/5 or 11.5 m from side shell for most cases, or probabilistic method) · machinery space concepts — gas-safe (double-walled piping) or ESD-protected
- Ch 6 Fuel containment system: independent tanks type A/B/C, membrane · pressure control (boil-off handling, holding time ≥15 days) · max filling limit
- Ch 7 Material and general pipe design
- Ch 8 Bunkering: bunkering station, manifold, ESD link ship–shore, dry-disconnect couplings · bunkering procedures and checklist
- Ch 9 Fuel supply to consumers: redundancy (single failure does not cause loss of propulsion) · master gas valve outside the machinery space
- Ch 10 Power generation including propulsion and other gas consumers (dual-fuel engines, boilers, GCU)
- Ch 11 Fire safety: tank room A-60 · water spray on bunkering station and tank · dry powder at bunkering station
- Ch 12 Explosion prevention: hazardous area zones · equipment certified for zone
- Ch 13 Ventilation: tank connection space, double-wall piping (≥30 air changes/hour), fuel preparation room
- Ch 14 Electrical installations
- Ch 15 Control, monitoring and safety systems: gas detection (alarm 20% LEL, shutdown 40% LEL), ESD, bilge level, leak detection
- Ch 16 Manufacture, workmanship and testing

**Part B — Manufacture, workmanship and testing** (part B-1 for natural gas)

**Part C — Drills and emergency exercises**
- Ch 17 Drills and emergency exercises: gas-related drills in the SMS (bunkering leak, fire)

**Part D — Training**
- Ch 18 Operation: bunkering procedures, maintenance, enclosed space entry, hot work, approved Fuel Handling Manual on board
- Ch 19 Training: STCW V/3 — Basic and Advanced training for ships subject to the IGF Code (in force 1 Jan 2017)

**Key Point**
- Crew on IGF ships need STCW V/3 Basic (all with duties on fuel) and Advanced (master, engineers, persons in charge of fuel)
- Bunkering: ESD link and checklist, safety zone agreed, water curtain on hull, no simultaneous operations without assessment
- Ammonia, hydrogen and methanol ships still built under "alternative design" with IMO interim guidelines
- From 2026 (MSC.551) and 2028 (MSC.567): more detailed design rules — check build date
