# IGF Code — International Code of Safety for Ships Using Gases or other Low-flashpoint Fuels

## At a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 11 Jun 2015, resolution MSC.391(95), at MSC 95 |
| Takes effect | **1 Jan 2017**, with SOLAS amendments (chapters II-1 and II-2) adopted by MSC.392(95) |
| Mandatory under | SOLAS chapter II-1, **Part G** (added by MSC.392(95)) |
| Applies to | Ships to which SOLAS II-1 Part G applies, unless expressly provided otherwise |
| Not for | Gas carriers covered by the IGC Code (the Code covers low-flashpoint fuel ships other than those) |
| Approach | Goal-based: goals and functional requirements for each section |
| Detailed rules | Part A-1 covers **natural gas (LNG)**; other fuels need other means (alternative design, interim guidelines) |
| Structure | 19 chapters in Parts A, A-1, B-1, C-1 and D |

## What it covers

- Mandatory provisions for the arrangement, installation, control and monitoring of machinery, equipment and systems that use gas or other low-flashpoint fuels
- Aims to reduce risks to the ship, the crew and the environment
- **Low-flashpoint fuel** (2.2): a gaseous or liquid fuel with a flashpoint lower than otherwise permitted under SOLAS II-2/4, paragraph 2.1.1

## History and amendments

![[diagram:igf-dates|From adoption in 2015 to pending 2028 changes]]

| Resolution | Adopted | In force | Subject |
|---|---|---|---|
| MSC.391(95) | 11 Jun 2015 | 1 Jan 2017 | Adopts the Code |
| MSC.422(98) | 15 Jun 2017 | 1 Jan 2020 | Part A-1, paragraph 11.3 |
| MSC.458(101) | 14 Jun 2019 | 1 Jan 2024 | Paragraphs 2.2, 5.3, 6.8, 9.5, 10.3, 11.3 |
| MSC.475(102) | 11 Nov 2020 | 1 Jan 2024 | Paragraphs 6.7, 11.8 and 16.3 |
| MSC.551(108) | 23 May 2024 | 1 Jan 2026 | See below |
| MSC.567(109) | 6 Dec 2024 | 1 Jan 2028 (deemed accepted 1 Jul 2027) | See below |

- MSC.524(106) (adopted at MSC 106) concerns high-manganese austenitic steel for cryogenic service.

**MSC.551(108): main changes (in force 1 Jan 2026)**
- New definition 2.2.43 of a ship "constructed on or after 1 January 2026" (building contract, keel laying or delivery date)
- Airlock rules (5.12.1): gastight bulkheads, two doors **1.5 to 2.5 m** apart, 300 mm sill (unless the Load Line Convention applies), self-closing doors without holdback
- Residual pressure relief valves (6.7.3.1): capacity to cover one valve failing; pressure rise limited to **20% above MARVS**
- Bunkering (8.4): **dry-disconnect** connections; manual, hydraulic or bolted connections need a risk assessment and special consideration; emergency release coupler or equivalent
- Fuel supply (9.3, 9.4): redundancy and segregation in single-fuel systems; automatic venting of gas piping after shutdown; manual shutdown valve for each gas consumer
- Fire safety (11.3.1, 11.6.2): fuel preparation rooms treated as **category A machinery spaces**; a **5 kg** dry powder extinguisher near the bunkering station and in the fuel preparation room (existing ships by first survey on or after 1 Jan 2026)
- Hazardous zones 0 and 1 expanded (12.5); permitted liquid level gauge types for fuel tanks (15.4.1.3)
- Production weld tests about every 50 m of butt weld (16.3.5.1)
- Bunkering (18.4.1.1): the receiving ship's master and the bunkering source's person in charge **agree in writing** on transfer procedures and limits

**MSC.567(109): main changes (in force 1 Jan 2028)**
- New definition 2.2.44 of a ship "constructed on or after 1 January 2028"
- Fuel tank suction wells may protrude below the inner bottom within limits (5.3.3.5.1)
- Pressure relief valve discharge to fuel tanks, or to the vent mast with liquid detection (7.3.1.4)
- A-60 shielding for boundaries facing open-deck fuel tanks (11.3.2); fuel storage hold space may count as a cofferdam with a minimum **900 mm** distance (11.3.3.1)
- Zone 1 and zone 2 around vent mast outlets (12.5.2.4, 12.5.3.3); ventilation air inlet rules (13.3.5, 13.3.8)

**Pending: MSC 111 (13–22 May 2026)**
- Draft amendments (annex 19 of the report) clarify the Code covers "gases or other low-flashpoint fuels", revise the definitions of "gas" and "low-flashpoint fuel", and update risk assessment (4.2.1) and training (19.2) paragraphs. They follow the "one ship, one code" policy and respond to doubt over whether ammonia fell in scope.
- Expected adoption at MSC 112, with deemed acceptance [1 Jan 2028] and entry into force [1 Jul 2028] — dates were still in brackets.
- MSC 111 also approved hydrogen guidelines MSC.1/Circ.1701.

## Key requirements for natural gas fuel

| Ch | Requirement |
|---|---|
| 5 | Fuel tanks at least **B/5 or 11.5 m** (whichever is less) inboard from the ship's side at the summer load line (5.3.3.1). Fuel pipes at least 800 mm from the ship's side (5.7.1) |
| 5.4 | **Gas-safe machinery space:** arranged so it is gas safe in normal and abnormal conditions; a single failure cannot release fuel gas into it. **ESD-protected machinery space:** non-hazardous in normal conditions, but may become hazardous in abnormal conditions, when non-safe equipment is shut down automatically |
| 6 | Secondary barrier contains leakage for **15 days** unless other criteria apply for particular voyages (6.4.4.1). Filling limit **98%** at reference temperature (6.8.1); a higher limit, never above 95%, may be allowed in special cases (6.8.2). Tank types: independent A, B, C and membrane |
| 8 | Bunkering station on open deck (8.3.1.1). Dry-disconnect connection plus a dry break-away or self-sealing quick release coupling (8.4.1). Manual stop valve and remote shutdown valve in series in each bunkering line (8.5.3). Hose bursting pressure at least 5 times the maximum bunkering pressure (8.3.2.2) |
| 9 | Main gas supply line has a manual stop valve and an automatic **master gas fuel valve**, outside the machinery space and operable from safe places including the bridge (9.4.2, 9.4.3). Ducts or double-wall pipes: underpressure ventilation of 30 air changes per hour (9.5.1), reducible to 10 with automatic nitrogen filling on gas detection (9.6.1.2) |
| 11 | Water spray: **10 l/min/m²** on largest horizontal surfaces and 4 l/min/m² on vertical surfaces (11.5.3); main supply stop valves no more than 40 m apart (11.5.4). Dry powder at the bunkering station at least **3.5 kg/s for 45 s** (11.6.1). Smoke detectors alone are not enough for rapid fire detection (11.7.2) |
| 12 | **Zone 0:** inside fuel tanks, relief and vent pipework, and pipes containing fuel. **Zone 1:** includes tank connection spaces, fuel storage hold spaces, interbarrier spaces; on open deck 3 m from tank outlets and bunker manifold valves, 1.5 m from fuel preparation room entrances. **Zone 2:** 1.5 m around zone 1 open or semi-enclosed spaces (12.5) |
| 13 | **30 air changes per hour** for tank connection space extraction (13.4.1), fuel preparation rooms (13.6.1), ducts and double pipes (13.8.1). ESD-protected machinery spaces: 30, or 15 in normal operation rising automatically to 30 on gas detection (13.5.2) |
| 15 | Redundant gas detection in each ESD-protected machinery space (15.8.2) |
| 18 | A suitably detailed **fuel handling manual** on board (18.2.3); fuel system P&ID mounted at the bunker control station and bunker station (18.4.2.2); pre-bunkering verification recorded in the bunker safety checklist (18.4.3.1) |
| 19 | Seafarers complete training matching their duties, taking into account the STCW Convention and Code (19.2) |

![[diagram:igf-gas-detection|Gas detection alarm and shutdown set points]]

> **Key Point:** Check the ship's construction date against the 2026 and 2028 definitions, because the detailed rules changed. Bunkering needs a dry-disconnect connection, a written agreement on transfer limits and a completed bunker safety checklist. Gas detection alarms at 20% LEL and the safety system acts at 40% LEL on two detectors.

## Not included (not verified against the original text)

- Numeric flashpoint limit in the definition, and the alternative-design route under SOLAS II-1/55.
- Entry-into-force date for MSC.524(106).
- Interim guidelines for methyl/ethyl alcohol, fuel cells, low-flashpoint diesel and ammonia: circular numbers and dates (only hydrogen MSC.1/Circ.1701 is confirmed as approved at MSC 111).
- Details of STCW Basic and Advanced training under chapter 19.
