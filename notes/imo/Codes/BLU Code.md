# BLU Code — Code of Practice for the Safe Loading and Unloading of Bulk Carriers

## At a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 27 Nov 1997, Assembly resolution A.862(20) |
| Effective | 1 Jul 1998 |
| Structure | Introduction · 6 Sections · 5 Appendices |
| Aim | Prevent loss of, or damage to, ships carrying solid bulk cargoes through improper loading and unloading |
| Cargo | Solid bulk cargoes, **excluding grain** |
| Amendments | MSC.238(82) (2006) · MSC.304(87) (2010) |
| Linked to | SOLAS Chapter VI, regulation 7 |
| Used with | IMSBC Code (cargo), BLU Manual (for terminals) |

## What is the BLU Code

- Practical recommendations for **ship and terminal** to load and unload solid bulk cargoes safely
- Main tools: the agreed **loading or unloading plan** and the **ship/shore safety checklist**
- The Code revoked MSC/Circ.690 and DSC/Circ.3
- Its recommendations are subject to terminal, port or national requirements
- SOLAS, MARPOL and Load Line issues are not specifically covered

| Date | Step |
|---|---|

| May 2005 | MSC 80 approves the **BLU Manual** for terminal representatives; amendments approved at MSC 87 (MSC.1/Circ.1356) |

- The Code is linked to SOLAS regulation VI/7 (loading, unloading and stowage of bulk cargoes).
- SOLAS VI/7.3: a copy of the plan is lodged with the appropriate authority of the port State.
- **EU:** Directive 2001/96/EC, Article 8, requires the plan to follow the form in BLU Code Appendix 2 and the checklist to follow the Appendix 4 guidelines. The directive defines a bulk carrier as a ship of 500 GT or more of the bulk-carrier type described in it (UK MCA summary).

## Sections

| Section | Title |
|---|---|
| 1 | Definitions (1.1–1.11) |
| 2 | Suitability of ships and terminals |
| 3 | Procedures between ship and shore before arrival |
| 4 | Procedures between ship and terminal before cargo handling |
| 5 | Cargo loading and handling of ballast |
| 6 | Unloading cargo and handling of ballast |

**Section 1 — Definitions:** air draught, combination carriers (OBO or O/O), conveyor system, hot work, list indication lights, master, pour, terminal representative, and three types of trimming (loading cargo, unloading cargo, ship trimming).

**Section 2 — Suitability**

| Ships (2.1–2.2) | Terminals (2.3) |
|---|---|
| Valid statutory certificates, with validity covering loading, voyage and unloading plus a reserve for delays | Accept only ships that can berth safely, considering water depth, maximum ship size, mooring, fendering, safe access and obstructions |
| Weathertight; approved stability and loading booklet | Equipment maintained; weighing equipment calibrated at regular intervals |
| Adequate hatches, hatch numbers clearly shown | Personnel trained and rested to avoid fatigue |
| Approved loading instrument where stress calculations are needed | |
| Machinery and hatches working; list indication lights tested before operations | |

**Section 3 — Before arrival**
- Information is exchanged early enough for preparations.
- Loading rate and cargo distribution are agreed using one of three options: the restrictions in the ship's manual or booklet, an approved loading instrument, or a conservative procedure.
- **Ship to terminal:** ETA (updated), ship particulars, loading plan, draughts, de-ballasting time, hatch distances, accommodation ladder location, air draught, cargo gear, mooring details. Part-loaded ships report cargo on board and the distribution to be unloaded. Combination carriers report previous cargoes and gas-free status. The ship confirms holds are clean.
- **Terminal to ship:** berth details, loader or unloader characteristics and rates, water depth, water density, air draught, gangway arrangements, approach speed, tug availability, cargo sequence, cargo hazards, mooring requirements. The terminal confirms the ship has received the cargo declaration.

**Section 4 — Before cargo handling**
- **4.1:** the master is responsible at all times for safe loading and unloading.
- **4.2 loading:** the plan is signed by both parties; the master specifies hold order and weights; ballast and loading rates are coordinated.
- **4.2 unloading:** the terminal proposes the rate; the master sets hold order and weights.
- **4.3:** the plan follows Appendix 2. It changes only by a revised plan signed by both parties. Plans are kept for **6 months**, and a copy is lodged with the port State authority.

## Who does what

![[diagram:blu-roles|Duties of the master and of the terminal representative]]

**Section 5 — Loading and ballast**
- Confirm there is no excessive hull stress.
- The terminal alerts the ship to high impact loads from heavy cargo until the tank top is covered.
- Trimming follows the BC Code.
- Both parties keep accurate records of total and per-pour quantities.
- Completion of loading is agreed in writing.

**Section 6 — Unloading and ballast**
- Confirm the method avoids excessive stress and damage; keep communication open.
- On completion both parties agree in writing that the ship was unloaded to plan, holds were cleaned to the master's requirements, and any damage was recorded.
- Flammable vapour risks apply when ballasting holds.
- The ship warns the terminal at once of damage or hazards.
- The terminal matches port and starboard unloading, reports when each hold is complete, avoids and reports damage, and forecasts adverse weather.
- Damage affecting structural or watertight integrity is reported to the Administration and the port State authority.

## Right to stop

| Who | Rule |
|---|---|
| Master | May stop loading or unloading if the agreed plan is not followed or anything else endangers the ship (resolution para 3.4) |
| Port authorities | May stop operations when the ship's safety is endangered (para 3.5) |
| Checklist item 18 | Both parties accept that cargo operations must stop if ballasting falls out of step with cargo operations |

## Appendices

| App | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | Recommended contents of port and terminal information books |
| 2 | Loading or unloading plan: sample form and worked examples; the heavy-lined box holds the minimum information; bending moments and shear forces stay within permitted limits |
| 3 | Ship/shore safety checklist: 21 questions, signed by the master and the terminal representative |
| 4 | Guidelines for completing the checklist |
| 5 | Form for cargo information from the shipper: cargo description, stowage factor, angle of repose, trimming procedures, hazards, signed declaration |

The 21 checklist questions cover water depth, mooring, emergency departure, access, communications, crewing, bunkering, repairs, damage reporting, cargo information, atmosphere safety, loader limits, the plan, trimming, ballast-cargo alignment, residue removal, final trim and time to sail.

> **Key Point:** No loading or unloading starts until the master and the terminal representative have agreed and signed the plan and completed the checklist. Keep ballast in step with cargo, and watch hull stresses at every pour. The master can stop the operation when the ship is endangered.
