**BLU Code — Code of Practice for the Safe Loading and Unloading of Bulk Carriers**

**What is the BLU Code**
- Practical rules for ship and terminal to load and unload solid bulk cargoes safely, without overstressing the hull or damaging the ship
- Main tool: an agreed loading/unloading plan and the ship/shore safety checklist
- Born from bulk carrier losses in the 1990s, partly caused by high loading rates and poor ballast co-ordination

**History**
- Adopted 27 Nov 1997, Assembly resolution A.862(20)
- SOLAS VI/7 amended (in force 1 Jul 2004) to make the core duties mandatory: master and terminal representative must agree the plan before loading/unloading starts, and the unloading method must not damage the ship
- Amended by MSC.238(82) (2006) and MSC.304(87) (2010) — e.g. loading rates and hold cleanliness
- BLU Manual (Manual on loading and unloading of solid bulk cargoes for terminal representatives, MSC/Circ.1160 and later revision) supports terminals
- EU: Directive 2001/96/EC makes the BLU Code mandatory for EU terminals

**Applies to**
- Bulk carriers (SOLAS Ch IX / XII definition) loading or unloading solid bulk cargoes, and the terminals they use
- Read with the IMSBC Code (cargo properties) and the ship's loading manual / loading instrument

---

**Sections**
- **Section 1 Definitions**: terminal representative, air draught, hot work, loading/unloading rate, pour, trimming
- **Section 2 Suitability of ships and terminals**
  - Ship: hatch covers and holds suitable, approved loading instrument, cargo-handling gear and safe access
  - Terminal: maximum draught and air draught at berth, loading/unloading equipment, communication, port information book (Appendix 1)
- **Section 3 Procedures between ship and shore before arrival**
  - Information exchange early: ETA, arrival draught and trim, cargo quantity and stowage, ballast quantities and de-ballasting rate, cargo characteristics (IMSBC), loading rates
  - Ship confirms holds are clean and ready
- **Section 4 Procedures between ship and terminal before cargo handling**
  - Agree the **loading or unloading plan** (Appendix 2): sequence and quantity per pour, ballast operations, draught, trim, stresses at every stage
  - Complete the **ship/shore safety checklist** (Appendices 3 and 4)
  - Agree communication, emergency stop, and who signs
  - Cargo information form from the shipper (Appendix 5)
- **Section 5 Cargo loading and handling of ballast**
  - Keep the hull within permissible shear forces and bending moments at all stages; check with the loading instrument
  - De-ballasting rate must match the loading rate; stop loading if ballast falls behind
  - Draught surveys during loading; final trimming of cargo (IMSBC)
  - Terminal must stop loading when the master asks
- **Section 6 Unloading cargo and handling of ballast**
  - Follow the unloading plan; avoid damage by grabs, bulldozers, hammers; record any damage and notify
  - Ballast in step with discharge; keep stresses and list within limits

**Appendices**
- 1 Port and terminal information book · 2 Loading/unloading plan form · 3 Ship/shore safety checklist · 4 Guidelines for the checklist · 5 Cargo information form

**Key Point**
- No loading or unloading before the plan is agreed and signed by the master and the terminal representative (SOLAS VI/7)
- Master may stop the operation at any time for safety; terminal must comply
- Watch shear force and bending moment at every pour — high loading rates have broken ships
