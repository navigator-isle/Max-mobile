# IBC Code — International Code for the Construction and Equipment of Ships Carrying Dangerous Chemicals in Bulk

## At a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 17 Jun 1983, resolution MSC.4(48) |
| Mandatory under | SOLAS chapter VII (MSC.6(48)) and MARPOL Annex II (MEPC.19(22), Dec 1985) |
| Applies to | Chemical tankers of any size, keels laid on or after **1 Jul 1986** |
| Older ships | BCH Code (MARPOL Annex II regulation 11) |
| Certificate | International Certificate of Fitness for the Carriage of Dangerous Chemicals in Bulk, up to 5 years |
| Structure | 21 chapters and an appendix (certificate model) |
| Current text | 2020 edition, with amendments in force 1 Jan 2021 and 1 Jul 2024 |

## What is the IBC Code

- An international standard for the **safe bulk carriage of dangerous and noxious liquid chemicals** listed in Chapter 17
- Covers ship design, construction, equipment and operation, to minimize risk to the ship, crew and environment
- SOLAS covers products marked S or S/P in column d of Chapter 17; MARPOL covers NLS tankers carrying products marked X, Y or Z in column c

## History

![[diagram:ibc-dates|From adoption in 1983 to the 2024 amendment]]

- **BCH Code:** the predecessor. Chemical tankers built before 1 Jul 1986 must comply with it under MARPOL Annex II regulation 11.
- **2004 amendments (MSC.176(79)):** adopted 10 Dec 2004, deemed accepted 1 Jul 2006, in force **1 Jan 2007**. They go with the revised MARPOL Annex II (in force 1 Jan 2007), which brought a four-category system for noxious liquid substances (X, Y, Z, OS).
- **MEPC.318(74) and MSC.460(101):** in force 1 Jan 2021; amend chapters 1, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19 and 21, with a complete revision of the product information in chapters 17 and 18.
- **MEPC.302(72) and MSC.440(99):** new model form of the Certificate of Fitness, in force 1 Jan 2020.
- **MEPC.345(78) and MSC.526(106):** adopted 10 Jun 2022 and 10 Nov 2022; in force **1 Jul 2024**; replace paragraph 2.9.2.1 on the final waterline in damage survival.

## Application and definitions (Chapter 1)

| Point | Rule |
|---|---|
| Size | All ships regardless of size, including under 500 GT |
| Cargo | Bulk dangerous chemicals and noxious liquid substances, excluding petroleum and similar flammable products, where the cargo has greater fire hazards than petroleum products or other significant hazards |
| Vapour pressure | Covered liquids do not exceed 0.28 MPa absolute at 37.8 °C |
| Construction date | Keels laid, or similar stage, on or after 1 Jul 1986; the stage is when identifiable construction begins and assembly reaches **at least 50 tonnes or 1%** of the estimated structural mass, whichever is less |
| Conversions | A ship of any age converted to a chemical tanker on or after 1 Jul 1986 counts as constructed when the conversion begins |

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Chemical tanker | A cargo ship built or adapted to carry in bulk any liquid product listed in Chapter 17 |
| Cargo area | Cargo tanks, slop tanks, cargo pump-rooms, cofferdams, adjacent ballast or void spaces, and the deck areas above them |
| Cofferdam | An isolating space between two adjacent steel bulkheads or decks |
| Noxious liquid substance (NLS) | A substance listed in the Pollution Category column of Chapter 17 or 18, or in the current MEPC circular, or provisionally assessed as category X, Y or Z |
| Products | The collective term for NLS and dangerous chemicals |
| Length L | 96% of the waterline length at 85% of the least moulded depth |

## Certificate and surveys (Chapter 1)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Certificate | Issued after an initial or renewal survey; valid for a period set by the Administration, **not more than 5 years** |
| Renewal | Completed within 3 months before expiry: valid up to 5 years from the existing expiry date. Completed after expiry: same. More than 3 months before expiry: up to 5 years from the survey completion |
| Extension | An existing certificate may be endorsed for up to 5 further months |
| Invalid if | Surveys or endorsements are missed, or the ship changes flag |
| Initial | Before service, a full examination for compliance |
| Renewal survey | At intervals set by the Administration, not more than 5 years |
| Intermediate | Within 3 months before or after the 2nd or 3rd anniversary; replaces one annual survey |
| Annual | Within 3 months before or after each anniversary |
| Additional | After an investigation or important repairs or renewals |

## Ship types and cargo tank location (Chapter 2)

![[diagram:ibc-ship-types|Where cargo tanks may be placed for each ship type]]

| Type | Hazard | Tank location (2.6.1) |
|---|---|---|
| Type 1 | Most severe hazards; maximum preventive measures; survives the most severe damage | Side and bottom setbacks equal to the damage extents, and at least **760 mm** from the shell |
| Type 2 | Appreciably severe hazards; significant preventive measures | Bottom setback equal to the vertical damage extent, and at least 760 mm from the shell |
| Type 3 | Sufficiently severe hazards; moderate containment | No requirement |

- Diluted slops tanks from tank washing are exempt from the 760 mm rule.

**Damage extents (2.5.1)**

| Damage | Extent |
|---|---|
| Side, transverse | B/5 or 11.5 m, whichever is less |
| Side, longitudinal | 1/3 L^(2/3) or 14.5 m, whichever is less |
| Bottom, vertical | B/15 or 6 m, whichever is less |
| Bottom, forward of 0.3L | Longitudinal 1/3 L^(2/3) or 14.5 m; transverse B/6 or 10 m |
| Bottom, elsewhere | Longitudinal 1/3 L^(2/3) or 5 m; transverse B/6 or 5 m |

**Other Chapter 2 figures**
- Suction wells (non-Type 1 ships) may protrude into bottom damage zones by no more than 25% of the double-bottom depth or 350 mm, whichever is less.
- Permeability: stores 0.60, accommodation 0.95, machinery 0.85, voids 0.95.
- Damage standard: Type 1 ships survive damage anywhere along their length; Type 2 and 3 requirements depend on length (thresholds at 150 m, 125 m and 225 m).
- Final equilibrium (2.9): heel not more than 25° (30° if no deck is immersed); righting lever range at least 20° beyond equilibrium, residual righting lever at least 0.1 m, area at least 0.0175 m·rad.

| Ch | Figures stated in the text |
|---|---|
| 11 | Cargo-area deck foam: solution rate at least **2 l/min per m²** (11.3.5.1); at least **four** foam applicators (11.3.10) |
| 12 | Pump-rooms normally entered: at least **20 air changes per hour** (12.2). Cargo spaces generally at least 30 changes per hour (12.1.3); 45 for certain products (15.17) |
| 13 | Cargo tank gauging is open, restricted or closed (13.1.1); overflow control in 15.19 |
| 14 | Protective equipment includes aprons, long-sleeved gloves, footwear, chemical-resistant coveralls, goggles or face shields (14.1.1); ships carrying certain cargoes need at least **three** complete sets of safety equipment (14.2.1) |
| 15 | Applies where column o of Chapter 17 refers to it; molten sulphur temperature limit 155 °C (15.10.6) |
| 16 | Maximum cargo per tank: **1,250 m³** on Type 1 ships (16.1.1), **3,000 m³** on Type 2 ships (16.1.2); tanks with liquids at ambient temperature must not become liquid-full during the voyage (16.1.3) |
| 17 | The product table: pollution category (column c), safety hazards (column d), ship type, and further requirements by column |

> **Key Point:** Before loading, check the product against Chapter 17 and the ship's Certificate of Fitness: ship type, tank requirements and special requirements. Type 1 and Type 2 tanks must stay at least 760 mm from the shell. Pump-rooms normally entered need at least 20 air changes per hour.
