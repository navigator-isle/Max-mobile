# MLC 2006 — Maritime Labour Convention (ILO)

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| What it is | The ILO's seafarers' rights convention, often called the "fourth pillar" with SOLAS, MARPOL and STCW |
| Adopted | **23 Feb 2006**, 94th (Maritime) Session of the International Labour Conference |
| In force | **20 Aug 2013** |
| Ratifications | **113 ILO Members, about 96.6%** of world gross tonnage (ILO page) |
| Replaces | 36 older maritime Conventions and 1 Protocol (ILO) |
| Amended | Code amendments in **2014, 2016, 2018, 2022 and 2025** |
| Latest in force | 2022 amendments, **23 Dec 2024** |
| Next | 2025 amendments, expected **Dec 2027** |
| Ship paperwork | Maritime Labour Certificate and DMLC Parts I and II (ships 500 GT+ on international voyages) |

![[diagram:mlc-structure|How the Convention is built]]

> **Key Point:** The MLC is the seafarer's contract, pay, rest, leave, food, cabin, medical care and complaints. Port State Control checks it, and the flag State certifies it.

## How it is built
- **Articles and Regulations:** the basic rights and principles
- **Code Part A:** mandatory **Standards**
- **Code Part B:** non-mandatory **Guidelines**
- **Five Titles:** each Title holds Regulations, and each Regulation has a Standard (A) and a Guideline (B)
- **Amendments:** made by the Special Tripartite Committee (governments, shipowners, seafarers). Code amendments are accepted tacitly

| Title | Subject |
|---|---|
| 1 | Minimum requirements to work on a ship |
| 2 | Conditions of employment |
| 3 | Accommodation, recreational facilities, food and catering |
| 4 | Health protection, medical care, welfare and social security |
| 5 | Compliance and enforcement |

## Title 1 — Minimum requirements
| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Minimum age (Reg 1.1) | **16**. Night work is prohibited under 18. "Night" is at least **9 hours**, starting no later than midnight and ending no earlier than 5 am |
| Medical certificate (Std A1.2) | Valid up to **2 years**. Up to **1 year** for seafarers under 18. Colour vision: up to **6 years** |
| Certificate expires at sea | Stays valid until the next port where a medical practitioner is available, for no more than **3 months** |
| Training and qualifications (Reg 1.3) | Trained and certified (see the STCW convention) |
| Recruitment and placement (Reg 1.4) | Licensed or regulated services. Seafarers must be told their rights under the agency financial protection system (2022) |

## Title 2 — Conditions of employment
| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Seafarers' Employment Agreement (Std A2.1) | Each party gets a **signed original**. Notice for early termination is **not shorter than 7 days** |
| Wages (Std A2.2) | Paid at intervals of **no more than one month**, with a **monthly account** |
| Hours of work and rest (Std A2.3) | See the box below |
| Annual leave (Std A2.4) | At least **2.5 calendar days per month** of employment |
| Repatriation (Std A2.5.1) | Maximum time on board before the right to repatriation: **less than 12 months** |
| Compensation for loss of ship (Reg 2.6) | Indemnity for unemployment |
| Manning levels (Reg 2.7) | Enough seafarers for safe, efficient and secure operation |
| Career and skill development (Reg 2.8) | Opportunities for seafarers |

![[diagram:mlc-hours|Hours of work and rest: the two limits]]

**Hours of work and rest (Std A2.3).** The flag State sets one of two limits. They are not mixed.
| Limit | Per 24 hours | Per 7 days |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum hours of **work** | **14 hours** | **72 hours** |
| Minimum hours of **rest** | **10 hours** | **77 hours** |

- Rest can be split into **no more than two periods**, one of them at least **6 hours**
- The interval between two rest periods must not exceed **14 hours**

**Abandonment and financial security (Std A2.5.2)**
- Failure to pay contractual wages for at least **2 months** can count as abandonment
- The financial security must cover outstanding wages and other entitlements for up to **4 months**
- The security cannot end before its validity ends unless the provider gives the flag State at least **30 days'** notice
- The financial security certificates must be carried on board (2014 amendments)

## Title 3 — Accommodation, food and catering
| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Headroom (Std A3.1) | At least **203 cm** where full and free movement is needed |
| Hospital (Std A3.1) | Separate hospital accommodation on ships with **15 or more seafarers** on voyages longer than **3 days** |
| Social connectivity (2022) | Internet access on board where reasonably practicable, at reasonable cost |
| Food and water (2022) | Drinking water free of charge and of good quality. Balanced meals. Food and water supplies inspected |
| Ship's cook (Std A3.2) | **Under 18** may not work as a ship's cook. On ships with a prescribed manning below 10, a fully qualified cook may not be needed |

## Title 4 — Health, medical care, welfare and social security
| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Doctor on board (Std A4.1) | Ships with **100 or more persons** on international voyages longer than **3 days** |
| Prompt disembarkation (2022) | A seafarer needing urgent care is disembarked promptly and gets access to shore medical care for serious conditions |
| Remains (2022) | Member States must facilitate repatriation of a deceased seafarer's body or ashes |
| Shipowner's liability (Std A4.2.1) | National law may limit liability for medical costs and wages to not less than **16 weeks** |
| Safety committee (Std A4.3) | Ships with **5 or more seafarers** |
| Protective equipment (2022) | PPE that fits properly, including for women seafarers |
| Deaths (2022) | Every seafarer death is recorded, reported yearly to the ILO and published |
| Social security (Std A4.5) | Protection in at least **3 of the 9** listed branches |
| Shore welfare (Reg 4.4) | Access to shore-based welfare facilities |

## Title 5 — Compliance and enforcement
**Flag State certificate (Reg 5.1.3)**
- Applies to ships of **500 GT or over** on international voyages
- **Maritime Labour Certificate:** valid up to **5 years**
- **Intermediate inspection:** between the **2nd and 3rd anniversary**
- **Interim certificate:** up to **6 months**
- **DMLC Part I:** the flag State's national requirements. **Part II:** the measures the shipowner uses to stay compliant between inspections

![[diagram:mlc-certificate|Certificate cycle]]

**The 16 areas (listed in the DMLC and for detailed port State inspection)**

![[diagram:mlc-areas|The 16 areas checked]]

**Complaints and inspection**
- **On-board complaint procedure (Reg 5.1.5):** every ship has one
- **Marine casualties (Reg 5.1.6):** official inquiry for serious casualties
- **Port State inspection (Reg 5.2):** checks the MLC certificate and DMLC. Detailed inspection when there are clear grounds or a complaint

## Amendments
![[diagram:mlc-amendments|MLC amendments and when each took effect]]

| Year | What changed | In force |
|---|---|---|
| 2014 | Financial security for abandonment, and for death and long-term disability. Certificates on board | **18 Jan 2017** |
| 2016 | Guidance on eliminating harassment and bullying | **8 Jan 2019** |
| 2018 | Shipowners keep paying seafarers held captive after piracy or armed robbery | **26 Dec 2020** |
| 2022 | Eight amendments: see below | **23 Dec 2024** |
| 2025 | Adopted by the Special Tripartite Committee on 7 to 11 Apr 2025: see below | Expected **Dec 2027** |

**2022 amendments, in force 23 Dec 2024**
- **Recruitment:** seafarers must know their rights under the agency financial protection system
- **Repatriation:** States facilitate prompt repatriation of abandoned seafarers. Replacement seafarers get MLC rights
- **Accommodation and welfare:** social connectivity on board. Port States do the same for ships in port
- **Food:** free good-quality drinking water, balanced meals, inspected supplies
- **Medical care:** prompt disembarkation, shore care, repatriation of remains
- **Health and safety:** fitting PPE, all deaths reported to the ILO
- **Financial security:** the certificate shows the registered owner's name if it differs from the shipowner

**2025 amendments, expected Dec 2027**
| Subject | Change |
|---|---|
| Repatriation (A2.5.1, B2.5.1) | Without discrimination, whatever the ship's flag. Shipowner's costs cover passage to the chosen destination, accommodation on the way, **up to 30 kg of luggage** and medical care until the seafarer can travel |
| Shore leave (A2.4.2, B2.4.5) | Without discrimination whatever the flag. A seafarer cannot be required to hold a visa or special permit for shore leave |
| Key workers (B2.5.2) | Seafarers should be recognized as key workers and given safe movement when travelling for work. This is a Guideline |
| Detentions and casualties (B4.4.6, A5.1.6) | Detained seafarers dealt with promptly, using the ILO/IMO fair treatment guidelines. Casualty inquiries consider the IMO [[Casualty Investigation Code]] |
| Medical (B4.1.1) | Medical training reflects the International Medical Guide for Seafarers and Fishers |
| Harassment and bullying | Prevention covered in recruitment, safety and health programmes, investigations and the complaint procedure (B1.4.1, B3.1.10, A4.3, B4.3.1, B4.3.6, B4.3.11, A5.1.5) |

- Four resolutions were adopted with them. They are guidance, not binding
- **No new DMLC inspection items.** DMLC Part II procedures will need updating
- **Hours of work:** no amendment. The Committee asked for a Joint ILO/IMO Tripartite Working Group meeting on hours of work and rest

## What inspectors look at
- Employment agreement against the crew list
- Wage records and monthly accounts
- Hours of work and rest records
- MLC certificate, DMLC Parts I and II, financial security certificates
- Food and water inspection records, cabin condition
- The complaint procedure, and that seafarers have a copy
