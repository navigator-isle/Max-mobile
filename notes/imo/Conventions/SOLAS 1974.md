# SOLAS 1974 — International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | **1 Nov 1974** |
| In force | **25 May 1980** |
| 1978 Protocol | Tanker safety. In force **1 May 1981**. |
| 1988 Protocol | Harmonized System of Survey and Certification (HSSC). In force **3 Feb 2000**. |
| Amendments | **Tacit acceptance**: an amendment takes effect on a set date unless enough Parties object first |
| Parties | 168 States, 98.91% of world tonnage (IMO treaty table printed **5 Sep 2023**) |
| 1978 Protocol parties | 123 States, 97.59% · **1988 Protocol:** 128 States, 97.91% (same table) |
| Structure | Articles plus an Annex of **17 chapter headings** (I to XV, with II-1, II-2, XI-1 and XI-2) |

![[diagram:solas-chapters|The SOLAS chapters]]

> **Key Point:** The Chapter is the rule. The Code is the detailed standard behind it (LSA, FSS, IBC, IGC and others). PSC and MARINA focus on certificates (I), drills and LSA (III), GMDSS (IV), navigation equipment and voyage planning (V), ISM (IX) and ISPS (XI-2).

## What SOLAS is
- The main IMO convention for the safety of merchant ships: minimum standards for **construction, equipment and operation**
- The **flag State certifies**. **Port State Control** inspects.

## History
- 1912 Titanic led to SOLAS **1914** (never in force, because of WWI)
- Later versions: 1929, 1948, **1960** (the first under IMO, then IMCO)
- **1974:** the current Convention, with tacit acceptance of amendments (protocols: see At a glance)

## Applies to
- International voyages
- **Passenger ships:** more than 12 passengers
- **Cargo ships:** 500 GT and above. Chapter IV and parts of Chapter V apply from 300 GT.
- **Not covered (I/3):** warships and troopships, cargo ships under 500 GT, non-mechanically propelled ships, primitive wooden ships, pleasure yachts not in trade, fishing vessels

## Surveys and certificates (Chapter I and the HSSC)
![[diagram:solas-surveys|Survey windows and certificate validity]]

### Survey windows (HSSC Survey Guidelines 2025)
| Survey | Rule |
|---|---|
| Initial | Before the ship enters service, or when a new instrument first applies |
| **Annual** | Within **3 months before or after** each anniversary date |
| **Intermediate** | Within 3 months before or after the **2nd or 3rd** anniversary. It replaces one annual survey. |
| **Periodical** (equipment) | Within 3 months before or after the 2nd or 3rd anniversary. Radio: each anniversary. |
| **Renewal** | May be completed up to **3 months before expiry** with no loss of validity. The construction renewal may start at the 4th annual survey and finish by the 5th anniversary. |
| **Outside of bottom** | At least **2 inspections in any 5 years**, never more than **36 months** apart. One is with the renewal. Passenger ships: 2 dry-dockings in 5 years, 36 months apart. A non-ro-ro passenger ship may be accepted with one, at 60 months. |

### Certificate validity
- **Cargo ship certificates:** up to **5 years**
- **Passenger Ship Safety Certificate:** up to **12 months**
- **Extension** to reach the survey port: **not more than 3 months**, and **1 month** for ships on short voyages (short voyage = neither the distance to the final destination nor the return exceeds 1,000 miles)
- A certificate ceases to be valid if a required survey is not carried out in time. Validity is restored by carrying out the missed survey.
- Certificates: Passenger Ship Safety · Cargo Ship Safety Construction, Equipment and Radio (or one Cargo Ship Safety Certificate) · Polar Ship Certificate · Industrial Personnel Safety Certificate (with the related convention certificates, such as Load Line, IOPP and IBWM)

### Maintenance (III/20)
- **Weekly:** visual check of survival craft, rescue boats and launching appliances · engines run · general alarm tested
- **Monthly:** checklist inspection, recorded in the log
- Liferafts, inflatable lifejackets and MES serviced every **12 months** (max 17)
- Hydrostatic release units serviced every 12 months
- Falls turned end for end at most every **30 months** and renewed at most every **5 years**
- Lifeboat release gear: annual thorough examination, 5-yearly overhaul (MSC.402(96), 1 Jan 2020)
- **Ahead:** new lifeboat ventilation rules for lifeboats installed from 1 Jan 2029 (MSC.535(107))

## Chapter IV — Radiocommunications (GMDSS)
*Applies to passenger ships and cargo ships of 300 GT and above. **Modernized from 1 Jan 2024** (MSC.496(105)).*

![[diagram:solas-gmdss|GMDSS sea areas after 1 Jan 2024]]

- **Regs 8, 9, 10 and 11 now define sea areas A1, A2, A3 and A4 individually.** Before 2024 each covered a combined set (A1, A1+A2, A1+A2+A3, A1+A2+A3+A4).
- **A3:** an area excluding A1 and A2 within the coverage of a **recognized mobile satellite service** (no longer tied to Inmarsat only). **A4:** the area outside A1, A2 and A3.
- **NAVTEX, EGC and HF NBDP receivers are no longer mandatory.** Under MSC.1/Circ.1645 ships should carry NAVTEX where international NAVTEX is provided, otherwise an EGC receiver (SafetyNET or Iridium SafetyCast) covering the operating area. The mandatory NBDP function was removed. See [[GMDSS]].
- **1 Jan 2028:** under MSC.1/Circ.1676 flag States may allow continued installation of VHF, MF/HF and Inmarsat-C equipment approved under the previous standards
- The two-way VHF and SART provisions moved from III/6 to **IV/7**

### Bridge visibility (Reg 22)
![[diagram:solas-visibility|Bridge visibility limits]]

- The sea surface forward of the bow, out to **10° on each side**, must not be obscured by more than **two ship lengths or 500 m**, whichever is less
- Blind sectors: **10° each**, **20° total**. Within that forward view each blind sector is limited to **5°**.

### Pilot transfer (Reg 23)
- **MSC.572(110)** (adopted 26 Jun 2025) replaces V/23 and amends II-2/11. Performance standards in **MSC.576(110)**.
- **In force 1 Jan 2028.** Arrangements installed from then comply at installation. Existing SOLAS ships comply by the **first survey on or after 1 Jan 2029**. Ships outside Chapter I by **1 Jan 2030**.
- **Mechanical pilot hoists are prohibited.** Lighting over the side and at the boarding position. Pilots may refuse a non-compliant arrangement.
- Height of climb (ABS summary): under 1.5 m no ladder, a risk-based alternative · **1.5 to 9 m** pilot ladder · **over 9 m** combination arrangement
- Side ropes at least **24 kN**, securing points and shackles at least **48 kN**. Inspection every **3 months**. At least one spare ladder and one spare set of manropes. Replace within **36 months** of manufacture or **30 months** in service.

### Polar chapter extension from 1 Jan 2026 (MSC.532(107))
- Chapter XIV now also covers **fishing vessels 24 m LOA and above**, **pleasure yachts 300 GT and above** not in trade, and **cargo ships 300 to under 500 GT**
- These ships must meet chapters **9-1 and 11-1** of Part I-A of the Polar Code (voyage planning and navigation). Those built before 1 Jan 2026 have until **1 Jan 2027**.
- Polar Code amendments: MSC.538(107)

## Special areas and routeing (V/10–V/12)
- **V/10:** Traffic Separation Schemes, Areas To Be Avoided, deep-water routes. Follow those IMO made mandatory.
- **V/11:** mandatory ship reporting systems, for example **STRAITREP** (Malacca and Singapore Straits, MSC.73(69), 1 Dec 1998), REEFREP (Torres Strait), OUESSREP, WETREP and others
- **V/12:** follow VTS where a coastal State sets it up

## Beyond the convention
### United States (USCG)
- The US is a SOLAS Party. USCG does PSC and enforces SOLAS, MARPOL and STCW on foreign ships.
- **33 CFR 164.25:** the listed equipment must be tested **within 12 hours** before entering or getting underway: primary and secondary steering gear, internal communications and alarms, standby or emergency generator, emergency batteries, main propulsion ahead and astern
- **Emergency steering drill:** logged within **48 hours** before entry, or conducted and logged regularly at least every **3 months** (164.25(d))
- Notice of Arrival, equipment (164.37), AIS (164.46) and failure reporting (164.53): see Not included

### Other regimes
- **PSC regimes:** Paris MoU (Europe and North Atlantic) · **Tokyo MoU** (Asia-Pacific, including the Philippines) · USCG · Indian Ocean, Black Sea, Mediterranean, Caribbean, Viña del Mar, Abuja and Riyadh MoUs
- **CIC 2026 (Paris and Tokyo MoU):** cargo securing, **1 Sep to 30 Nov 2026**
