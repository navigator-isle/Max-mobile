# AFS 2001 — International Convention on the Control of Harmful Anti-fouling Systems on Ships

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 5 Oct 2001 |
| In force | 17 Sep 2008 |
| Organotin (TBT) | Not applied after 1 Jan 2003 · not borne on hulls after 1 Jan 2008 (or covered by a barrier coating) |
| Cybutryne | MEPC.331(76), in force 1 Jan 2023 · not applied or re-applied from 1 Jan 2023 |
| Ships ≥ 400 GT, international voyages | International Anti-fouling System Certificate |
| Ships 24 m to < 400 GT, international voyages | Declaration on Anti-fouling System |

## What is AFS
- Parties must prohibit or restrict harmful anti-fouling systems on ships flying their flag, on ships operating under their authority, and on ships entering their ports, shipyards or offshore terminals (Art 3(1), Art 4(1))
- Anti-fouling paints stop marine organisms attaching to hulls, which would slow ships and raise fuel use; the metallic compounds in them leach into seawater and can persist (IMO)
- TBT caused deformities in oysters and sex changes in whelks (IMO)
- The prohibited substances are in Annex 1, updated as needed; a technical group reviews proposals for other substances

## History
- Adopted 5 Oct 2001 · open for signature 1 Feb 2002 – 31 Dec 2002 (Art 17) · in force **17 Sep 2008**
- Art 18: in force 12 months after at least 25 States, whose merchant fleets are at least 25% of world gross tonnage
- **Cybutryne amendment MEPC.331(76)**: adopted 17 Jun 2021 · deemed accepted 1 Jul 2022 · in force **1 Jan 2023** (Art 16(2))

**Key articles**
- **Art 3(1)**: applies to ships flying a Party's flag, ships operating under a Party's authority, and ships entering a Party's port, shipyard or offshore terminal
- **Art 3(2)**: not warships, naval auxiliaries and other government ships on non-commercial service (Parties still ensure they act consistently where reasonable and practicable)
- **Art 3(3)**: Parties prevent non-Party ships from getting more favourable treatment
- **Art 4(2)**: a ship with a system that becomes controlled may keep it until its next scheduled renewal, but **not longer than 60 months after application**, unless the Committee finds exceptional circumstances
- **Art 5**: waste from applying or removing controlled systems is handled safely and in an environmentally sound way
- **Art 6**: a Party sends an initial proposal to IMO; the Committee decides if an in-depth review is needed; the proposing Party submits a comprehensive proposal; a technical group evaluates it; the Committee decides
- **Art 11 inspection**: (1) port inspection is limited to checking the certificate or declaration, and brief sampling that does not affect the system's integrity, structure or operation — sample processing time must not prevent the ship leaving; (2) a thorough inspection if there are clear grounds; (3) a Party may warn, detain, dismiss or exclude a ship in violation, and tells its Administration; (4) co-operation on enforcement
- **Art 12**: sanctions adequate in severity to discourage violations
- **Art 13**: a ship unduly detained or delayed is entitled to compensation
- **Art 16**: Annex amendment deemed accepted after 12 months unless more than one third of Parties object; generally in force six months after acceptance

## Annex 1 — Controls
| Substance | Control | Date |
|---|---|---|
| Organotin compounds acting as biocides | Ships shall not apply or re-apply | 1 Jan 2003 |
| Organotin compounds acting as biocides | Ships shall either not bear such compounds on hulls or external surfaces, **or bear a coating that forms a barrier** to them | 1 Jan 2008 |
| Cybutryne | Ships shall not apply or re-apply anti-fouling systems containing cybutryne | 1 Jan 2023 |
| Cybutryne (existing) | Ships with cybutryne in the external coating layer must remove it or apply a barrier (sealer) coat at the next scheduled renewal after 1 Jan 2023, and not later than **60 months** after the last application | from 1 Jan 2023 |

- Organotin 1 Jan 2008 control: does not apply to fixed and floating platforms, FSUs and FPSOs built before 1 Jan 2003 that have not been in dry dock on or after that date
- Cybutryne exceptions: fixed and floating platforms, FSUs and FPSOs built before 1 Jan 2023 and not dry-docked on or after that date · ships not on international voyages · ships under 400 GT on international voyages, if accepted by the coastal State(s)
- Annexes 2 and 3: information needed in an initial proposal (five elements) and a comprehensive proposal (properties, environmental fate and effects, non-target organisms, human health, methods and peer review)

## Annex 4 — Surveys and certification
**Regulation 1 — Surveys** (ships of 400 GT and above on international voyages, except platforms, FSUs and FPSOs)
- (a) an **initial survey** before the ship is put into service, or before a certificate is first issued
- (b) a survey when the anti-fouling systems are **changed or replaced**

**Regulation 2 — Certificate**
- Issued after a successful survey; the issuing authority has full responsibility
- Cybutryne amendment: for a ship with a system applied before the control took effect, the Administration issues a certificate not later than two years after entry into force of the control (MEPC.331(76) guidance: affected flagged ships obtain a valid certificate within 24 months)

**Regulation 3** — another Party may survey and certify at the Administration's request; no certificate to a ship of a non-Party State

**Regulation 4 — Validity**: the certificate ceases to be valid if the anti-fouling system is changed without endorsement, or on transfer to another flag

**Regulation 5 — Declaration**: ships of 24 m or more but under 400 GT on international voyages (except platforms, FSUs and FPSOs): the owner or authorized agent signs a **Declaration on Anti-fouling System**, with supporting documents

**Certificate:** the International Anti-fouling System Certificate is supplemented by a **Record of Anti-fouling Systems**; Reg 1(4) allows survey and certification to be delegated to Recognized Organizations

## Key Point
> At every dry dock with new hull paint: keep the paint documents, and get the Record of Anti-fouling Systems updated
> Check the record shows no organotin (or a barrier coat) and no cybutryne (or a barrier coat)
> A port State may check the certificate or declaration and take brief samples, without delaying the ship
