**AFS 2001 — International Convention on the Control of Harmful Anti-fouling Systems on Ships**

**What is AFS**
- Bans harmful anti-fouling systems on ship hulls and sets a process to ban more substances in future
- First ban: organotin compounds (TBT) acting as biocides — they harmed shellfish and marine life
- Second ban (2023): cybutryne

**History**
- TBT damage to oysters and dog-whelks (1970s–80s); IMO resolution A.895(21) (1999) called for a ban
- Adopted 5 Oct 2001 · in force 17 Sep 2008
- Amendment MEPC.331(76) (2021): controls on **cybutryne** · in force 1 Jan 2023
- Revised guidelines (2022) for brief sampling, survey and certification, and inspection of anti-fouling systems

**Applies to**
- Ships flying the flag of a Party or operating under its authority (including fixed and floating platforms, FSUs, FPSOs), and ships entering ports, shipyards or offshore terminals of a Party
- Not: warships and State non-commercial ships

---

**Articles (key)**
- Art 4 Controls on anti-fouling systems (Annex 1) · Art 6 Process for proposing new controls (Annexes 2 and 3)
- Art 10 Survey and certification (Annex 4) · Art 11 Inspection of ships (PSC; brief sampling)
- Art 12 Violations · Art 13 Undue delay or detention to be avoided (compensation)

**Annex 1 — Controls**
- **Organotin compounds acting as biocides (TBT)**
  - Not applied or re-applied after 1 Jan 2003
  - Not present on hulls after 1 Jan 2008 — or covered by a sealer coat that stops leaching
- **Cybutryne**
  - Not applied or re-applied from 1 Jan 2023
  - Ships with cybutryne coatings: remove it or apply a sealer coat at the next renewal of the coating, but no later than 60 months after the last application
  - Exceptions: fixed/floating platforms, FSUs and FPSOs built before 1 Jan 2023 and not dry-docked since; ships not on international voyages; ships under 400 GT on international voyages (with Declaration)

**Annex 2–3** — Information for initial and comprehensive proposals to add new substances

**Annex 4 — Survey and certification**
- Ships ≥ 400 GT on international voyages (except platforms/FSUs/FPSOs): **initial survey** before entering service or before first issue of the certificate; survey when the anti-fouling system is changed or replaced
- **International Anti-fouling System Certificate (IAFS)** with the **Record of Anti-fouling Systems** (updated after each change)
- Ships ≥ 24 m but < 400 GT on international voyages: **Declaration on Anti-fouling Systems** signed by the owner, with documentation (paint receipt or contractor invoice)

**Key Point**
- At every dry dock with new hull paint: keep the paint manufacturer's declaration and get the IAFS Record updated
- Check the IAFS certificate/record shows no TBT and no cybutryne (or a sealer coat)
- PSC may take brief samples of hull coating to check
