# Intervention 1969 — International Convention Relating to Intervention on the High Seas in Cases of Oil Pollution Casualties

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 29 Nov 1969, Brussels |
| In force | 6 May 1975 |
| Parties | 90 (as of 10 Jul 2024, NUS CIL) |
| 1973 Protocol | Extends it to substances other than oil · in force 1983 |
| Core rule | Coastal State may act on the high seas against grave and imminent danger from oil pollution after a maritime casualty |

## What is the Intervention Convention
- Lets a coastal State take measures on the high seas after a maritime casualty, to prevent, mitigate or eliminate grave and imminent danger to its coastline or related interests from oil pollution or the threat of it (Art I(1))
- Measures must be limited to what is necessary, after consulting the flag State, owners and, where feasible, independent experts (IMO)
- A State that goes beyond what is permitted pays compensation (Art VI)
- Covers seagoing vessels; warships and State ships on non-commercial government service are excluded (Art I(2))

## Key definitions (Art II)
- **Maritime casualty**: collision, stranding, other incidents of navigation, and other occurrences causing material damage or the imminent threat of it
- **Ship**: any seagoing vessel of any type whatsoever, and any floating craft, except installations for exploring or exploiting sea-bed resources
- **Oil**: crude oil, fuel oil, diesel oil and lubricating oil
- **Related interests** of the coastal State directly affected or threatened:
  - (a) coastal, port or estuarine activities, including fisheries that are an essential means of livelihood
  - (b) tourist attractions of the area
  - (c) the health of the coastal population and the well-being of the area, including conservation of living marine resources and wildlife
- **Organization**: the Inter-Governmental Maritime Consultative Organization (now IMO)

---

## Procedure before acting (Art III)
- (a) Consult the other States affected, in particular the flag State(s)
- (b) Promptly notify known persons whose interests may be affected (owners, others) and consider their views
- (c) May consult independent experts from the list kept by the Organization
- (d) In case of **extreme urgency**, may act at once without prior notice or consultation
- Must use best endeavours to avoid any risk to human life
- Must notify the measures taken to the States and known persons concerned, and to the IMO Secretary-General

## Experts (Art IV)
- IMO keeps the list of experts and sets its rules; Member States and Parties may nominate experts; the States using the experts pay for them

## Proportionality (Art V)
- V(1): measures must be proportionate to the damage, actual or threatened
- V(2): must not go beyond what is reasonably necessary; must stop once the aim is achieved; must avoid unnecessary interference with the rights of others
- V(3): proportionality is judged by (a) the extent and probability of imminent damage if no measures are taken, (b) the likelihood of the measures being effective, (c) the extent of damage the measures may cause

## Compensation and disputes
- **Art VI:** a Party that took measures beyond what is reasonably necessary pays compensation for the damage they caused
- **Art VII:** the Convention does not prejudice other applicable rights, duties, privileges, immunities or remedies
- **Art VIII:** disputes on whether measures broke the Convention, on compensation and its amount go to **conciliation, then arbitration** under the Annex if negotiation fails; the acting Party cannot refuse because its own courts' remedies are not exhausted
- Annex, conciliation: commission of three (one per party + chairman by agreement) · respondent names its conciliator within 60 days, otherwise the IMO Secretary-General · recommendation: parties have up to 90 days to accept or reject
- Annex, arbitration: request within 180 days after conciliation fails · tribunal of three · award is reasoned, final, no appeal, to be complied with at once

## Final clauses
- Art IX: open for signature until 31 Dec 1970, then accession
- Art XI: in force on the 90th day after 15 States signed without reservation or deposited instruments
- Art XII: denunciation takes effect one year after deposit
- Art XIV: revision conference convened by IMO, or if one third of Parties ask
- Art XVII: authentic languages English and French
- IMO Secretary-General is the depositary

## 1973 Protocol — substances other than oil
- Adopted at the 1973 London Conference on Marine Pollution · in force 1983
- Only States that are Parties to the 1969 Convention can join (Art IV); it cannot enter into force before the Convention (Art VI(1)); in force 90th day after 15 States
- Art I(1): same right of intervention on the high seas for grave and imminent danger from pollution by non-oil substances, after a maritime casualty or related acts, reasonably expected to cause major harmful consequences
- Art I(2): applies to (a) substances on the list in the Protocol's Annex, and (b) other substances liable to harm human health, living resources, amenities or other legitimate uses of the sea
- Art I(3): for (b), the intervening Party must show the substance could reasonably pose a danger similar to the listed ones
- Art II: Art I(2) and Arts II–VIII of the 1969 Convention, and its Annex, apply to non-oil substances; the expert list also covers non-oil substances
- Art III — the list: an IMO body keeps it · amendments need a two-thirds majority of Parties present and voting · deemed accepted after six months unless one third of Parties object · in force three months after acceptance

## Key Point
> After a casualty, a coastal State may require action on the high seas — only if the danger is grave and imminent, and the measures are proportionate
> Compensation is due if the State went beyond what was reasonably necessary
> Keep records of orders and of consultations with the coastal State
> Related instruments: Salvage 1989 and Nairobi WRC 2007 (wrecks)
