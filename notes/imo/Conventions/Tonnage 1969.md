**Tonnage 1969 (International Convention on Tonnage Measurement of Ships)**

**Status**
- Entry-into-force date checked against IMO status of treaties
- Other details written from knowledge, still to be verified

**What is Tonnage 1969**
- One international system to measure ship size: gross tonnage (GT) and net tonnage (NT)
- GT and NT are used for crew, safety, equipment and fees

**History**
- Adopted 23 Jun 1969
- In force 18 Jul 1982
- Existing ships kept their old tonnage until 18 Jul 1994 (grandfather rule)

**Applies to**
- Ships of 24 m and above on international voyages
- Not warships, ships under 24 m

**Annex I: Regulations for determining tonnage**
- Reg 3 Gross tonnage: GT = K1 × V
  - V = total volume of all enclosed spaces in m³
  - K1 = 0.2 + 0.02 log10 V
- Reg 4 Net tonnage: NT = K2 × Vc × (4d / 3D)² + K3 × (N1 + N2 / 10)
  - Vc = total volume of cargo spaces
  - NT is not less than 0.30 GT
- Other regs cover enclosed spaces, exempted spaces, definitions

**Annex II**
- International Tonnage Certificate (1969)

**Key Point**
- GT: used for SOLAS and STCW thresholds (500 GT, 3,000 GT and so on)
- NT: used mostly for port dues
- The 1969 Tonnage Certificate must be on board
