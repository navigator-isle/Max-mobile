# Tonnage 1969 — International Convention on Tonnage Measurement of Ships

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 23 Jun 1969 |
| In force | 18 Jul 1982 |
| Existing ships | Could keep old tonnage until 18 Jul 1994 (12 years) |
| Gross tonnage | GT = K1 × V |
| Net tonnage | Never less than 0.30 GT |
| Not applied to | Warships · ships under 24 m in length (Art 4) |
| Certificate | International Tonnage Certificate (1969) |

## What is Tonnage 1969
- IMO calls it "the first successful attempt to introduce a universal tonnage measurement system"
- Replaced varying national methods, all descended from Moorsom's 1854 approach
- Separate **gross tonnage (GT)** and **net tonnage (NT)**, each calculated independently; the terms changed from grt and nrt to GT and NT
- GT depends on the moulded volume of all enclosed spaces; NT comes from a formula based on the moulded volume of cargo spaces
- GT governs manning, safety and registration fees; both GT and NT are used for port dues (IMO)

## History
- Adopted 23 Jun 1969 · open for signature six months from 23 Jun 1969, then accession (Art 16)
- Art 17: in force **24 months after at least 25 Governments, whose merchant fleets are at least 65% of world gross tonnage**, signed without reservation or deposited instruments → in force **18 Jul 1982**
- Ships built earlier could keep their old tonnage for 12 years, until 18 Jul 1994, to protect operators from higher charges tied to tonnage

**Key articles**
- **Art 2**: nine defined terms — Regulations, Administration, international voyage, gross tonnage, net tonnage, new ship (keel laid on or after entry into force), existing ship, length, Organization. **Length** = 96% of the total length on a waterline at 85% of the least moulded depth, or the length from the foreside of the stem to the axis of the rudder stock on that waterline if greater (Art 2(8))
- **Art 3**: applies to ships on international voyages that are registered in Contracting States, or unregistered and flying their flag; covers new ships, existing ships with substantial alterations, existing ships on owner's request, and all existing ships 12 years after entry into force
- **Art 4**: excludes warships and ships under 24 m in length; also ships navigating only the Great Lakes and St Lawrence, the Caspian Sea, or the Plate, Paraná and Uruguay Rivers
- **Art 5**: deviation due to weather or other force majeure does not make a ship subject to the Convention
- **Art 6**: the Administration determines tonnages; may delegate to recognised persons or organizations but keeps full responsibility
- **Art 7**: an International Tonnage Certificate (1969) is issued to every ship whose tonnages were determined under the Convention
- **Art 8**: another Contracting Government may determine tonnages and issue the certificate at the flag State's request; none to ships of non-Contracting States
- **Art 9**: in the issuing country's language, with English or French translation; follows the Annex II model
- **Art 10**: cancelled when alterations increase gross or net tonnage; generally lapses on transfer to another flag (for a transfer between Contracting States, up to three months or until replaced)
- **Art 11**: other Contracting Governments accept the certificate as valid as their own
- **Art 12**: port inspection is limited to checking the certificate's validity and that the main characteristics match; must not delay the ship; flag State informed of any tonnage increase
- **Art 13**: Convention privileges only for ships with a valid certificate
- **Art 18**: amendments in force 12 months after acceptance by two-thirds of Contracting Governments

## Annex I — Regulations
**Reg 2 — Definitions**
- **Upper deck**: the uppermost complete deck exposed to weather and sea, with permanent weathertight closings, and watertight closings for side openings below it
- **Moulded depth**: vertical distance from the top of the keel to the underside of the upper deck at side
- **Breadth**: maximum breadth amidships (moulded frame line for metal shells)
- **Enclosed spaces**: spaces bounded by the hull, fixed or portable partitions, decks or coverings other than permanent or movable awnings; breaks or openings do not by themselves exclude a space
- **Excluded spaces** (a)–(e): for example spaces within an erection opposite an end opening · spaces under an open overhead deck covering · spaces in a side-to-side erection opposite side openings · spaces immediately below an uncovered weather-exposed deck opening, limited to the opening's area · recesses in a bulkhead with weather-exposed deck-to-deck opening
- **Passenger**: any person other than the master, crew or others employed on the ship's business, and other than a child under one year old
- **Cargo spaces** (for NT): enclosed spaces for cargo to be discharged, already counted in GT, marked "CC" at least 100 mm high

**Reg 3 — Gross tonnage**
> GT = K1 × V
> V = total volume of all enclosed spaces, in m³ · K1 = 0.2 + 0.02 log₁₀ V (or the Appendix 2 table, with interpolation)

| V (m³) | K1 |
|---|---|
| 100 | 0.2400 |
| 1,000 | 0.2600 |
| 10,000 | 0.2800 |
| 100,000 | 0.3000 |
| 1,000,000 | 0.3200 |

**Reg 4 — Net tonnage**
> NT = K2 × Vc × (4d/3D)² + K3 × (N1 + N2/10)
- Vc = total volume of cargo spaces (m³) · K2 = 0.2 + 0.02 log₁₀ Vc
- K3 = 1.25 × (GT + 10,000) / 10,000
- D = moulded depth amidships (m) · d = moulded draught amidships (m)
- N1 = passengers in cabins with not more than 8 berths · N2 = other passengers · N1 + N2 = passengers permitted by the passenger certificate; if less than 13, N1 and N2 are zero
- Limits: (4d/3D)² not more than 1 · the first term K2 × Vc × (4d/3D)² not less than 0.25 GT · **NT not less than 0.30 GT**
- **Moulded draught d**: (a) ships under the Load Line Convention — the Summer Load Line draught (excluding timber) · (b) passenger ships — deepest subdivision load line draught · (c) ships with a national load line — summer load line draught · (d) ships with a restricted draught and no load line — maximum permitted draught · (e) other ships — 75% of the moulded depth amidships

**Reg 5 — Change of net tonnage**
- Increase: apply the new net tonnage without delay
- Decrease: a new certificate with lower net tonnage cannot be issued until 12 months after the current certificate was issued, except on transfer to another flag, after major alterations approved by the Administration, or for passenger ships in special trades (such as the pilgrim trade)

**Reg 6 and 7**
- Volumes measured to the inner side of metal shell or structural boundary plating, regardless of insulation; volume of appendages included; volumes of spaces open to the sea may be excluded
- Measurements to the nearest centimetre; volumes by generally accepted methods acceptable to the Administration

## Annex II — International Tonnage Certificate (1969)
- Ship name, distinctive number or letters, port of registry
- Date keel laid (or similar stage), or date of major alteration
- Main dimensions: length, breadth, moulded depth to upper deck
- **Gross tonnage and net tonnage**
- Tables of spaces included in GT and NT; passengers (N1, N2); excluded spaces and moulded draught; dates and places of original and last measurement

## Key Point
> The International Tonnage Certificate (1969) must be on board and match the ship
> GT is a number from volume, not weight; DWT is weight
> Alterations that increase tonnage cancel the certificate (Art 10)
> A lower net tonnage cannot be certified until 12 months after the current certificate (Reg 5)
