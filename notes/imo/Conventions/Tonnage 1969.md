**Tonnage 1969 — International Convention on Tonnage Measurement of Ships**

**What is Tonnage 1969**
- The first universal system to measure a ship's size: **gross tonnage (GT)** (overall size) and **net tonnage (NT)** (useful earning capacity)
- Replaced many national systems (Moorsom, Panama and Suez Canal rules) for international purposes
- GT is a unitless number calculated from volumes — not a weight

**History**
- Adopted 23 Jun 1969 (London) · in force 18 Jul 1982
- Existing ships could keep their old tonnage until 18 Jul 1994 (12-year transition), unless altered
- Interpretations and unified guidance: TM.5/Circ.6 and later circulars (e.g. measuring enclosed spaces, open-top containerships)

**Applies to**
- Ships on international voyages, of Parties
- Not: warships; ships under 24 m in length (they may still be measured)

---

**Articles (key)**
- Art 2 Definitions: "international voyage", "length" (96% of the total length on a waterline at 85% of the least moulded depth, or bow-to-rudder-stock length if larger), "enclosed spaces", "excluded spaces", "passenger", "cargo spaces", "moulded depth/draught"
- Art 3 Application · Art 4 Exceptions · Art 5 Force majeure
- Art 6 Determination of tonnages by the Administration (may delegate to an RO)
- Art 7 Issue of the **International Tonnage Certificate (1969)** · Art 8 by another Government · Art 9 Form (Annex II)
- Art 10 Cancellation: on alteration that increases tonnage, or change of flag
- Art 11 Acceptance of the certificate by other Parties · Art 12 Inspection (verify the certificate and that the main characteristics match)

**Annex I — Regulations for determining gross and net tonnages**
- Reg 1–2 General and definitions (upper deck, moulded depth, breadth, enclosed spaces, excluded spaces such as open spaces in the side, under certain shelters)
- **Reg 3 Gross tonnage**: GT = K1 × V
  - V = total volume of all enclosed spaces in m³
  - K1 = 0.2 + 0.02 log₁₀ V
- **Reg 4 Net tonnage**: NT = K2 × Vc × (4d / 3D)² + K3 × (N1 + N2/10)
  - Vc = total volume of cargo spaces (m³); K2 = 0.2 + 0.02 log₁₀ Vc
  - d = moulded draught amidships; D = moulded depth amidships; (4d/3D)² not more than 1
  - K3 = 1.25 × (GT + 10,000) / 10,000
  - N1 = passengers in cabins with not more than 8 berths; N2 = other passengers (if N1 + N2 < 13, both are zero)
  - The first term K2·Vc·(4d/3D)² not less than 0.25 GT; **NT not less than 0.30 GT**
- Reg 5 Change of net tonnage: only once in 12 months (except on change of passengers or load lines)
- Reg 6 Calculation of volumes · Reg 7 Measurement and calculation (to the inner side of the shell plating)

**Annex II — International Tonnage Certificate (1969)** form: GT, NT, main dimensions, spaces included/excluded

**What GT and NT are used for**
- GT: thresholds in SOLAS, MARPOL, STCW, MLC, ISM, ISPS (e.g. 500 GT, 3,000 GT), manning, registration fees, P&I
- NT: port dues, pilotage, light dues, canal-related charges (Suez and Panama keep their own systems)

**Key Point**
- International Tonnage Certificate (1969) must be on board and match the ship
- GT is a volume-based number, not weight; DWT is weight
- Structural changes that alter enclosed volume → re-measurement and a new certificate
