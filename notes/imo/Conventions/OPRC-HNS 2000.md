# OPRC-HNS 2000 — Protocol on Preparedness, Response and Co-operation to Pollution Incidents by Hazardous and Noxious Substances

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | **15 Mar 2000**, London (Diplomatic Conference at IMO) |
| In force | **14 Jun 2007** |
| Parties | 43 (as of 2 Sep 2024, NUS Centre for International Law database) |
| Parent treaty | [[OPRC 1990]]. Only OPRC Parties can join. |
| Covers | Pollution incidents by hazardous and noxious substances (HNS), meaning substances **other than oil** |
| Entry into force rule | 12 months after at least **15 States** sign without reservation or deposit instruments (Art 15) |
| Denunciation | After 5 years from entry into force for that Party, effective 12 months after notice (Art 16) |
| Depositary | IMO Secretary-General |
| Languages | Arabic, Chinese, English, French, Russian, Spanish, all equally authentic |

![[diagram:oprchns-system|Three layers of preparedness under the Protocol]]

> **Key Point:** The Protocol does for HNS incidents what OPRC 1990 does for oil: ships carry an emergency plan, States have a national response system, and Parties help each other. It deals with **preparedness and response**, not liability. Liability and compensation are in [[HNS 2010]].

## Definitions (Art 2)
- **Pollution incident:** an occurrence or series of occurrences with a common origin, including fire or explosion, that releases or may release HNS, threatens the marine environment or a State's coastline or related interests, and needs emergency or other immediate response
- **HNS:** any substance **other than oil** which, if introduced into the marine environment, is likely to create hazards to human health, harm living resources and marine life, damage amenities or interfere with other legitimate uses of the sea
- **Sea ports and HNS handling facilities:** those where HNS are loaded onto or unloaded from ships
- The HNS definition here differs from the one used in the HNS Convention on liability (IMO)

## Articles
| Article | Content |
|---|---|
| **1 General** | Parties take suitable measures, alone or jointly, to prepare for and respond to HNS incidents. The Annex is part of the Protocol. **Warships and state ships** on non-commercial service are excluded, but Parties must act consistently with the Protocol where reasonable and practicable. |
| 2 Definitions | See above |
| **3 Emergency plans and reporting** | Each Party requires **ships flying its flag** to carry a pollution incident emergency plan and follow reporting procedures. Offshore units (FPSOs, FSUs) are covered by national rules or company systems and are excluded. Port and HNS facility operators have emergency plans coordinated with the national system and approved by the competent authority. When an incident becomes known, States likely to be affected are notified. |
| **4 National and regional systems** | National response system with a competent authority, a national operational contact point, and an authority able to request or decide on assistance · a national contingency plan setting out the roles of public and private bodies · within capabilities: pre-positioned equipment, exercises and training, communications, coordination mechanism · information to IMO on contacts, equipment, expertise and the plan |
| **5 International co-operation** | On request, and where the severity justifies it, Parties provide advice, technical support and equipment, subject to their capabilities. Costs follow the Annex. The requesting Party may ask IMO to help find provisional financing. Parties **facilitate the entry, use and departure** of ships, aircraft, personnel, cargo and equipment involved in the response. |
| 6 Research and development | Cooperation on surveillance, containment, recovery, dispersal, clean-up and restoration. Links between research bodies, international symposia, compatible standards. |
| 7 Technical co-operation | Training of personnel, access to technology and facilities, other preparedness measures, joint research, technology transfer subject to national law |
| 8 Bilateral and multilateral agreements | Parties endeavour to conclude them and send copies to IMO |
| 9 Relation to other agreements | No change to rights and obligations under other conventions or agreements |
| 10 Institutional arrangements | IMO, subject to its consent and resources, provides information services, education and training, technical services and technical assistance, with attention to developing countries |
| 11 Evaluation | Parties evaluate the Protocol's effectiveness within IMO |
| 12 Amendments | Via MEPC (proposals circulated at least 6 months before, two-thirds vote of Parties present and voting; articles and Annex take effect 6 months after two-thirds acceptance; appendices by tacit acceptance after at least 10 months unless one-third object) or via a Conference of Parties (needs request of one Party backed by at least one-third) |
| 13 Signature and accession | Open at IMO from 15 Mar 2000 to 14 Mar 2001, then accession. Only OPRC Parties may join. |
| 14 Multiple legal systems | A State with several territorial units may declare which the Protocol covers |
| 15 Entry into force | 12 months after 15 States · later deposits: later of entry into force or 3 months after deposit · after entry into force: 3 months after deposit |
| 16 Denunciation | After 5 years, 12 months' notice. **Denouncing OPRC 1990 automatically denounces the Protocol.** |
| 17 Depositary | Notifications, registration with the UN under Art 102 |
| 18 Languages | Six authentic languages |
| **Annex** | Reimbursement of costs |

## Annex: who pays for assistance
![[diagram:oprchns-costs|Cost reimbursement under the Annex]]

- Default: each Party bears its own response costs, unless a bilateral or multilateral financial agreement exists
- Assistance given at another Party's **express request**: the requesting Party reimburses the assisting Party. The request may be cancelled, but costs already incurred or committed are still paid.
- Assistance on the assisting Party's **own initiative**: it bears the cost
- Reimbursable costs are calculated fairly under the assisting Party's law and practice, unless agreed otherwise
- The requesting Party may ask for a waiver, reduction or deferral, and developing countries' needs are to be considered
- Nothing stops a Party recovering costs from third parties under other law

## What it means on board
- Under Art 3 your flag State requires a pollution incident **emergency plan** and **reporting procedures** that fit the IMO conventions in force for that State
- The Protocol text above does not itself list the plan's contents. See the Not included section.

## Not included (not verified against the original text)

- IMO guidance documents and manuals for HNS response
