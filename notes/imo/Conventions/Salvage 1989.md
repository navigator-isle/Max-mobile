# Salvage 1989 — International Convention on Salvage

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 28 Apr 1989, London |
| In force | 14 Jul 1996 |
| Replaced | 1910 Brussels salvage convention ("no cure, no pay") |
| New feature | Enhanced award and special compensation for protecting the environment |
| Entry into force rule | 1 year after 15 States (Art 29) |
| Time bar | 2 years from the end of the salvage operation (Art 23) |

## What is Salvage 1989
- Rules for salvage operations: duties and rights of salvors, owners and masters, and how rewards are set
- The 1910 rule ignored pollution: a salvor who prevented a major spill but could not save the ship or cargo got nothing, so there was little reason to try long-odds operations (IMO)
- The 1989 Convention gives an enhanced award that counts the salvor's skill and efforts to prevent or minimize environmental damage
- The Convention applies unless a contract provides otherwise (Art 6(1))

## Applies to (Arts 2–5)
- Art 2: when judicial or arbitral proceedings are brought in a State Party
- Art 3: **not** fixed or floating platforms or mobile offshore drilling units engaged in sea-bed mineral work, when on location
- Art 4: **not** warships and non-commercial State vessels with sovereign immunity, unless the State decides otherwise (and notifies the Secretary-General)
- Art 5: does not affect national law or conventions on salvage controlled by public authorities

## Chapter I — General (Arts 1–7)
**Art 1 definitions**
- (a) Salvage operation: any act or activity undertaken to assist a vessel or any other property in danger
- (b) Vessel · (c) Property · (e) Payment · (f) Organization (IMO) · (g) Secretary-General
- (d) **Damage to the environment**: substantial physical damage to human health or to marine life or resources in coastal or inland waters or areas adjacent to them, caused by pollution, contamination, fire, explosion or similar major incidents

**Contracts**
- Art 6: the master has authority to conclude salvage contracts for the owner of the vessel
- Art 7: a contract can be annulled or modified if made under undue influence or the influence of danger and its terms are inequitable, or if the payment is grossly too large or too small

## Chapter II — Performance of salvage operations (Arts 8–11)
- **Art 8 — Salvor:** act with due care · take due care to prevent or minimize damage to the environment · seek help from other salvors when circumstances require · accept the intervention of other salvors when reasonably asked
- **Art 8 — Owner and master:** co-operate fully with the salvor · take due care to prevent or minimize damage to the environment · accept redelivery of the vessel or property when it is reasonably asked and the salvor has secured a place of safety
- **Art 9:** coastal State keeps its right to take measures to protect its coastline from pollution, including giving directions about salvage operations
- **Art 10 — Duty to render assistance:** every master must, as far as he can do so without serious danger to his vessel and persons on board, render assistance to any person in danger of being lost at sea; States Parties adopt measures to enforce this; the owner is not liable for the master's breach
- **Art 11:** States should consider the need for co-operation with salvors when deciding on admission of vessels in distress to ports

## Chapter III — Rights of salvors (Arts 12–19)
- **Art 12:** a useful result is needed for a reward; no useful result, no payment unless otherwise provided
- **Art 13(1) — Criteria for the reward** (no order of priority):
  - (a) salved value of the vessel and other property
  - (b) skill and efforts of the salvors in preventing or minimizing damage to the environment
  - (c) measure of success obtained
  - (d) nature and degree of the danger
  - (e) skill and efforts of the salvors in salving the vessel, other property and life
  - (f) time used and expenses and losses incurred
  - (g) risk of liability and other risks run by the salvors or their equipment
  - (h) promptness of the services
  - (i) availability and use of vessels or other equipment intended for salvage
  - (j) state of readiness and efficiency of the salvor's equipment, and its value
- Art 13(2): payment is shared by the vessel and other property interests in proportion to their salved values
- Art 13(3): the reward, excluding interest and recoverable legal costs, cannot exceed the salved value of the vessel and other property

**Art 14 — Special compensation**
- 14(1): if the salvor worked on a vessel which, by itself or its cargo, threatened damage to the environment, and did not earn a reward under Art 13 at least equal to the special compensation, the salvor is entitled to special compensation from the owner, equal to the salvor's expenses
- 14(3): **salvor's expenses** = out-of-pocket expenses reasonably incurred, plus a fair rate for equipment and personnel actually and reasonably used
- 14(2): if the salvor prevented or minimized environmental damage, the compensation may be increased up to **30%** of expenses; a tribunal may increase it further if fair and just, but the total increase cannot be more than **100%** of expenses
- 14(4): special compensation is paid only to the extent it exceeds the Art 13 reward
- 14(5): if the salvor was negligent and failed to prevent or minimize environmental damage, special compensation may be denied or reduced
- 14(6): the owner keeps his right of recourse

**Other**
- Art 15: apportionment among salvors by the Art 13 criteria; division between owner, master and crew of the salving vessel by the law of its flag
- Art 16: no payment from persons whose lives are saved (national law may differ); life salvors share fairly in the reward for salving the vessel or property or preventing environmental damage
- Art 17: no payment for services under a contract, unless they go beyond what the contract reasonably requires
- Art 18: payment may be reduced or removed for fault, neglect, fraud or dishonest conduct
- Art 19: no payment for services rendered against the express and reasonable prohibition of the owner or master

## Chapter IV — Claims and actions (Arts 20–27)
- Art 20: the salvor's maritime lien is not affected; it cannot be enforced once adequate security has been provided
- Art 21: on request, the liable party provides security; salved property is not removed from the port or place where it is first brought, without the salvor's consent, until security is provided
- Art 22: interim payment may be ordered by a tribunal
- Art 23: actions must be started within **two years** from the day the salvage operations ended
- Art 24: interest — by the law of the State where the tribunal sits
- Art 25: no seizure of State-owned non-commercial cargo with sovereign immunity without the State's consent
- Art 26: no seizure of donated humanitarian cargo if the donor State agreed to pay for the salvage
- Art 27: States should encourage publication of arbitral awards, with the parties' consent

## Chapter V — Final clauses (Arts 28–34)
- Art 28: open for signature 1 Jul 1989 – 30 Jun 1990, then accession
- Art 29: in force one year after 15 States consented
- Art 30 reservations: a State may exclude (a) inland-waters salvage with only inland navigation vessels, (b) inland-waters salvage with no vessel involved, (c) cases where all interested parties are its nationals, (d) maritime cultural property of historic interest on the sea-bed
- Art 31: denunciation after one year from entry into force for that State; effective one year after receipt
- Art 32: revision conference — by IMO, or by the Secretary-General at the request of eight States or one quarter of the Parties, whichever is higher
- Art 34: six authentic languages

## Key Point
> The master's duty to save life at sea (Art 10) is a separate duty from a property salvage contract
> The master may conclude a salvage contract for the vessel's owner (Art 6) — inform the company and P&I at once
> Environmental protection is paid through special compensation (Art 14) even if the ship is lost
> Services against an express and reasonable prohibition are not paid (Art 19)
