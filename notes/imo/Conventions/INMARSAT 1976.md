# INMARSAT 1976 — Convention on the International Maritime Satellite Organization (and Operating Agreement)

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Convention | Adopted 3 Sep 1976, London · in force **16 Jul 1979** |
| Operating Agreement | Done at London 3 Sep 1976 · in force 16 Jul 1979 |
| Depositary | IMO |
| Parties (IMO table, printed 5 Sep 2023) | 89 States for the Operating Agreement, 94.85% of world tonnage |
| Purpose | Improve maritime communications: distress and safety of life, ship efficiency and management, maritime public correspondence, radiodetermination |
| Today | **IMSO** (International Mobile Satellite Organization, intergovernmental, London) oversees public safety services. **Inmarsat** is a commercial company. |
| Link to SOLAS | 1988 SOLAS amendments introduced the GMDSS |

![[diagram:inmarsat-split|From one organization to IMSO and Inmarsat Ltd]]

> **Key Point:** Since the 1998 amendments, the Convention's safety role sits with IMSO. It oversees the performance and availability of satellite services recognized for GMDSS. It does not run the satellites.

## What is INMARSAT 1976
- Set up an international organization to provide space segment for maritime communications, after IMO decided in 1973 to create a satellite-based maritime communications system
- Operations began in 1982, from London
- The Convention is the intergovernmental treaty (**Parties** are States). The **Operating Agreement** is signed by **Signatories**, which are the Parties or their designated entities. A State becomes a Party only after it, or its designated entity, has signed the Operating Agreement (Art 32(4)).
- The preamble cites UN Resolution 1721 (XVI), under which satellite communication should be available to all nations on a global and non-discriminatory basis, and the 1967 Outer Space Treaty

## Convention (1976 text)
| Article | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 Definitions | Operating Agreement (1(a)) · Party (1(b)) · Signatory (1(c)) · space segment (1(d)) · INMARSAT space segment (1(e)) · Ship (1(f)), broad: includes hydrofoils, submersibles and floating platforms |
| 2 Establishment | Establishes INMARSAT as "the Organization". Each Party signs the Operating Agreement or designates a competent entity to sign (2(3)). |
| 3 Purposes | Improve maritime communications, distress and safety of life, ship and aircraft efficiency (3(1)) · serve all areas needing maritime and aeronautical communications (3(2)) · act **exclusively for peaceful purposes** (3(3)) |
| 4 Designated entities | Relations between a Party and its entity are governed by domestic law. The Party is not liable for Operating Agreement obligations (4(c)). |
| 5 Financing | Signatories fund the Organization in proportion to their investment shares (5(1)) · operate on a sound economic and financial basis (5(3)) |
| 7 Access | Space segment open to ships and aircraft of all nations, with **no discrimination by nationality** (7(1)) |
| 10–12 Assembly | See below |
| 13–15 Council | See below |
| 16 Directorate | See below |
| 25 | Legal personality |
| 29 Withdrawal | Written notice to the Depositary, effective 3 months after receipt. Withdrawing Signatories remain liable for previously authorized commitments. |
| 30 Suspension and termination | The Assembly may terminate a Party's membership after at least one year's notice of non-compliance. The Council may suspend a Signatory's rights for unremedied non-compliance, and the Assembly may then terminate after 3 more months. |
| 31 Disputes | Negotiation first. If unresolved within one year, arbitration by consent. |
| 33 Entry into force | 60 days after States representing **95% of initial investment shares** become Parties. Void if not in force within 36 months of opening for signature. |
| 34 Amendments | A Party proposes, the Council reviews, the Assembly considers no earlier than 6 months later. In force **120 days** after two-thirds of Parties at adoption, representing at least two-thirds of total investment shares, accept. Then binding on all. |

### Organs
![[diagram:inmarsat-organs|The three organs of the 1976 organization]]

| Organ | Composition and rules |
|---|---|
| **Assembly** (Arts 10–12) | All Parties. Regular sessions every **2 years**. One vote per Party. Substantive decisions two-thirds, procedural decisions a simple majority, of Parties present and voting. Quorum a majority of Parties (11(4)). |
| **Council** (Arts 13–15) | **22 representatives**: 18 from the largest investment shares and 4 elected by the Assembly for geographic representation (13(1)). Meets at least **3 times a year** (14(1)). No representative may cast more than **25%** of total voting participation for one Signatory, subject to exceptions (14(3)). |
| **Directorate** (Art 16) | The Council appoints the Director General, subject to Party confirmation. Confirmation stands unless more than one-third of Parties object within 60 days. Term **6 years**. |

## Operating Agreement
| Article | Content |
|---|---|
| II | Signatories have the rights and obligations under both the Convention and the Agreement |
| III Capital contributions | In proportion to investment share · capital requirements cover space segment costs (design, development, acquisition, construction, establishment, and leased contractual rights), operating funds and Art XI payments |
| IV Capital ceiling | Initial ceiling **US$ 200 million**, adjustable by the Council |
| V Investment shares | Based on each Signatory's share of total space segment utilization · utilization split evenly between the ship/aircraft part and the land part · a Signatory with a ship-to-land ratio above 20:1 may apply for utilization equal to twice the land part or a 0.1% share, whichever is higher · first determination 2 to 3 years after operational use begins · redetermined yearly, and when a Signatory joins or leaves · no share below **0.05%** · in one redetermination, a share may not rise more than 50% of its initial share or fall more than 50% of its current share |
| VI Financial adjustments | At each redetermination, through the Organization, using a valuation of assets net of amortization and liabilities |
| VII | Utilization charges · sanctions for payments more than 4 months overdue |
| VIII Return on capital | Revenues cover operating costs first, then operating funds, capital repayment, withdrawal settlements, and finally compensation for use of capital at a rate "as close as possible to the cost of money in the world markets" |
| XI | Liability for claims shared by investment share |
| XIII Withdrawal | Within 3 months of the effective date, the Council notifies the financial settlement: investment share × valuation, less amounts owed |
| XIV–XV | Earth station approval and utilization authorization by Council criteria |
| XVI Disputes | Negotiation, then arbitration if unresolved within one year |
| XVIII Amendments | Council approval, Assembly confirmation, two-thirds of Signatories holding at least two-thirds of shares, effect 120 days later |

## Amendments and history
![[diagram:inmarsat-timeline|Key dates]]

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1973 | IMO decides to create a satellite-based maritime communications system |
| 3 Sep 1976 | Convention and Operating Agreement adopted |
| 16 Jul 1979 | Convention in force |
| 1982 | Operations begin, from London |
| 16 Oct 1985 | Amendment extending services to **aircraft**, in force 13 Oct 1989 |
| 19 Jan 1989 | Amendment extending services to **land vehicles**, in force 26 Jun 1997 |
| 1988 | SOLAS amendments introduce the GMDSS and require ships in specified sea areas to carry Inmarsat equipment |
| 9 Dec 1994 | Amendment renaming the organization **International Mobile Satellite Organization** and changing Council composition (listed as not yet in force in the treaty summary checked) |
| 24 Apr 1998 | Amendments for **privatization from April 1999**: a commercial company and an intergovernmental body (IMSO) with public service obligations, including GMDSS. In force **31 Jul 2001**. |
| 1999 | Organization restructured into IMSO and Inmarsat |
| End 2003 | Inmarsat Ltd fully privatised (IMO page) |

## IMSO today
- Intergovernmental body, headquarters London, separate from the commercial company Inmarsat (IMSO site: 112 Member States)
- Oversees the **performance and availability** of the mobile satellite systems recognized by IMO for GMDSS. IMO has recognized **Inmarsat and Iridium**, with recognition given by the MSC under resolution A.1001(25).
- Manages public services agreements with the providers
- IMO appointed IMSO as **Co-ordinator of the Long-Range Identification and Tracking (LRIT)** system. It audits and reviews the system annually.
- Observer at IMO meetings
- IMO is currently reviewing the GMDSS to modernize it
