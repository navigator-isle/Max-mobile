# STCW-F 1995 — International Convention on Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping for Fishing Vessel Personnel

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 7 Jul 1995, London |
| In force | 29 Sep 2012 |
| Structure | 15 Articles · 1 Annex (Ch I–IV) · STCW-F Code (from 1 Jan 2026) |
| Revision | Annex amended by MSC.561(108) · STCW-F Code MSC.562(108) — both adopted 23 May 2024, in force **1 Jan 2026** |
| Main threshold | Skipper and watch officers: fishing vessels of 24 m and over |

## What is STCW-F
- The international standard for training, certification and watchkeeping of crews of seagoing fishing vessels
- Art 6: fishing vessel personnel shall be certificated in line with the Annex
- Art 3: applies to personnel serving on board seagoing fishing vessels entitled to fly the flag of a Party
- "Seagoing" excludes vessels that navigate only in inland waters (Art 2)
- IMO calls it one of the pillars of fishing vessel safety, with the Cape Town Agreement (vessel safety), the ILO Work in Fishing Convention 2007 and the FAO Port State Measures Agreement 2009
- A Party may exempt certain provisions for vessels under 45 m that operate only from its ports and within its limited waters (IMO)

## History
- Adopted 7 Jul 1995 in London
- Art 12: in force 12 months after not less than 15 States signed without reservation or deposited instruments
- In force **29 Sep 2012** (IMO)
- Annex amendments follow a comprehensive review of the Convention, to meet the needs of the fishing industry (IMO)
- **MSC.561(108)** — revised Annex: adopted 23 May 2024 · deemed accepted 1 Jul 2025 unless more than one third of Parties objected · in force **1 Jan 2026**
- **MSC.562(108)** — new **STCW-F Code**, Part A (mandatory) and Part B (guidance): adopted 23 May 2024 · Part A takes effect on the same date as MSC.561(108)

- Art 8 Control: port State control of certification; the vessel may not sail if a danger is not corrected
- Art 10 Amendments: annex amendments are deemed accepted after two years unless a different period is set
- Art 13 Denunciation: possible after five years from entry into force for that Party; takes effect 12 months after notice
- Art 14: the IMO Secretary-General is the depositary

## Age, sea service and medical (MSC.561(108))
| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Candidates for certification (I/12.4) | At least 16, or 15 if authorized by the competent authority |
| OICNW (II/2, II/4), OICEW (II/5-2), GMDSS radio operator (II/6) | At least 18 |
| Skipper, unlimited waters (II/1) | 12 months as OICNW or skipper on vessels of 12 m or more |
| OICNW, unlimited waters (II/2) | 12 months in an approved training programme, or two years in the deck department |
| Second engineer, 3,000 kW or more (II/5-1-1) | 12 months as qualified engineer officer |
| Chief engineer, 3,000 kW or more (II/5-1-1) | 36 months, reducible to 24 months if at least 12 months were served as second engineer |
| Second engineer, 750–3,000 kW (II/5-1-2) | 12 months as assistant or engineer officer |
| Chief engineer, 750–3,000 kW (II/5-1-2) | 24 months, including at least 12 months qualified as second engineer |
| OICEW (II/5-2) | 12 months of workshop training and sea service (at least six months on fishing or training vessels), or 12 months in the engine room |

- Medical certificate (I/12.3): holders serving at sea must hold a valid medical certificate
- Validity (I/12.5): maximum two years, or one year for crew under 18
- Expiry during a voyage (I/12.6): valid until the next port with a recognized practitioner, for not more than three months
- Urgent cases (I/12.7): work allowed for up to three months with a recently expired certificate
- Revalidation (II/7.1, II/8.1): at intervals not exceeding five years, including medical fitness

## Basic training (III/1)
- Before any shipboard duty: approved basic training and onboard safety familiarization, to the standard of competence (III/1.1)
- A certificate of proficiency is issued where basic training is not part of a certificate's qualification (III/1.2)
- Code Table A-III/1 gives four areas:
  - A-III/1-1 Personal survival techniques — including lifejacket and immersion suit, jumping from height, righting an inverted liferaft, streaming a drogue or sea anchor
  - A-III/1-2 Fire prevention and fire fighting — including fire triangle, detection, breathing apparatus, rescue in smoke-filled spaces
  - A-III/1-3 Elementary first aid — casualty assessment, resuscitation, bleeding, shock, burns, safe transport
  - A-III/1-4 Personal safety and social responsibilities — emergency procedures and muster duties, marine pollution precautions (including fishing gear and plastic waste), safe working practices, communication and working relationships, fatigue

## Watchkeeping (IV/1, IV/2)
- Watch arrangements must prevent fatigue; the first watch and relieving watches must be rested and fit for duty (IV/1)
- OICNW must be on the bridge, or a directly associated place, at all times (IV/2.2.1)
- Radio operators keep a continuous radio watch on duty (IV/2.2.2)
- Engineering watch officers must be immediately available and, when required, present in the machinery space (IV/2.2.3)
- Very small fishing vessels in limited waters may be excluded from fully observing the basic principles (IV/2.3)
- Code section A-IV/2 covers: navigational watch (to and from fishing grounds, pilot on board, while fishing, anchor watch) · engineering watch · radio watchkeeping

## STCW-F Code (MSC.562(108))
- Part A: mandatory standards. Part B: recommended guidance, numbered like the Convention's regulations
- Competence standards grouped in seven functions (suffix -F): navigation · catch handling and stowage · controlling the operation of the vessel and care for persons on board · marine engineering · electrical, electronic and control engineering · maintenance and repair · radiocommunications
- Three levels of responsibility: management, operational, support
- Tables have four columns: competence · knowledge, understanding and proficiency · methods for demonstrating competence · criteria for evaluating competence
- Table A-II/1 skippers (24 m and over, unlimited waters), A-II/2 OICNW (unlimited waters), A-II/3 skippers (limited waters) — competences include voyage planning, position fixing, watchkeeping, meteorology, manoeuvring, ECDIS, catch handling, stability, fire fighting, life-saving, medical care, maritime law
- Table A-I/12: minimum in-service eyesight standards

## Key Point
> Fishing skippers and officers need STCW-F certificates, not the STCW merchant certificates
> From 1 Jan 2026 the STCW-F Code competence tables apply
> Certificates are revalidated at intervals not exceeding five years; the medical certificate lasts at most two years (one year under 18)
> Port States can check the certificates of foreign fishing vessel crews (Art 8)
