**BWM 2004 (Ballast Water Management Convention)**

**What is BWM**
- International Convention for the Control and Management of Ships' Ballast Water and Sediments, 2004
- Stops the spread of harmful aquatic organisms and pathogens in ballast water

**History**
- Adopted 13 Feb 2004
- In force 8 Sep 2017
- MEPC.297(72): D-2 implementation schedule, in force 13 Oct 2019
- MEPC.296(72): BWMS Code made mandatory; code adopted as MEPC.300(72)
- MEPC.299(72): endorsement of additional surveys on the certificate

**Applies to**
- Ships registered under a Party that use ballast water on international voyages
- Ships carry a ship-specific BWM Plan, Ballast Water Record Book and BWM Certificate

**Section D, Standards**
- D-1 Exchange standard: at least 95% volumetric exchange
  - at least 200 nm from land and 200 m deep
  - if impossible: at least 50 nm from land and 200 m deep
- D-2 Performance standard:
  - fewer than 10 viable organisms per m³ of 50 µm or larger
  - fewer than 10 viable organisms per mL of 10 to 50 µm
  - Vibrio cholerae: less than 1 cfu per 100 mL
  - E. coli: less than 250 cfu per 100 mL
  - Intestinal enterococci: less than 100 cfu per 100 mL
- D-3 Approval of ballast water management systems (BWMS Code)
- D-4 Prototype technologies

**D-2 implementation for existing ships**
- Comply at the first IOPP renewal survey on or after 8 Sep 2019, with the earlier-survey rule in B-3
- Ships not subject to IOPP renewal surveys: by 8 Sep 2024
- New ships (from 8 Sep 2017): D-2 on delivery

**Key Point**
- Standard: D-1 exchange then D-2 treatment; today D-2 with an approved BWMS is the rule
- PSC checks: plan, record book, certificate, BWMS operation, sampling
