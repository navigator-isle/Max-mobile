# CSC 1972 — International Convention for Safe Containers

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 2 Dec 1972, Geneva (conference of the UN and IMO) |
| In force | 6 Sep 1977 (IMO treaty table) |
| Parties | 85 States, 67.71% of world tonnage (IMO table, printed 5 Sep 2023) |
| Entry into force rule | 12 months after the 10th instrument is deposited (Art VIII) |
| Purpose | Keep a high level of human safety in container transport and handling, and facilitate international transport through uniform rules |
| Key marking | CSC Safety Approval Plate |
| Latest amendments in force | MSC.355(92), 1 Jul 2014 |
| Authentic languages | Chinese, English, French, Russian, Spanish |

## What is CSC 1972
- A uniform set of strength requirements and test procedures for freight containers in international transport, whatever the mode of surface transport
- Applies to new and existing containers in international transport, except containers designed specifically for air carriage (Art III)
- Limited to containers of a prescribed minimum size with corner fittings

### Definition of a container (Art II)
- Permanent and reusable, designed for transport by one or more modes without intermediate reloading
- Equipped with corner fittings
- Floor area within the four bottom corners at least **14 m²**, or **7 m²** if fitted with top corner fittings
- Vehicles and packaging are excluded. Containers on chassis are included.
- **New container:** construction began on or after entry into force. **Existing container:** any other (Art II(8)–(9)).
- **R** = maximum operating gross mass (weight, originally) · **tare** = empty mass · **P** = R minus tare

## Articles
| Article | Content |
|---|---|
| I | Parties give effect to the Convention and Annexes |
| II | Definitions |
| III | Application: new containers need type or individual approval under Annex I. Existing containers had to be approved within 5 years of entry into force. |
| IV | Each Administration sets up approval procedures and may delegate to authorized bodies, notifying the IMO Secretary-General. Non-compliant approved containers are corrected or approval is withdrawn. |
| V | Parties accept each other's approvals. No extra structural tests, except for dangerous goods, bulk liquids and air containers. |
| VI | **Control:** officers may inspect containers, normally only to check for a valid Safety Approval Plate. Deeper checks only with clear evidence of an obvious safety risk. Defects suspected of existing at approval are reported to the approving Administration. |
| VII | Signature until 31 Dec 1973, then ratification, acceptance, approval or accession |
| VIII | Entry into force 12 months after the 10th instrument |
| IX–X | Amendment: two-thirds of the MSC and Assembly adopt, two-thirds of Parties accept, in force 12 months later · annexes by simplified MSC procedure: in force unless one-fifth of Parties or five Parties, whichever is less, object |
| XI–XII | Denunciation effective after one year · lapses if fewer than 5 Parties remain for 12 consecutive months |
| XIII | Disputes: arbitration, binding award |
| XIV | No reservations to Arts I–VI, XIII, XIV or the Annexes |

## Annex I — Testing, inspection, approval and maintenance
| Regulation | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 Safety Approval Plate | Permanently affixed · minimum size **200 × 100 mm** · "CSC SAFETY APPROVAL" lettering at least 8 mm, other text at least 5 mm · the owner removes it if the container is modified so approval no longer applies, is out of service without maintenance, or approval is withdrawn |
| 2 Maintenance and examination | Owner keeps the container safe (2(1)) · first examination within **5 years** of manufacture (2(2)(d)) · optional continuous examination programme, marked **ACEP**, with examinations at least every **30 months** (2(3)) |
| 3 | New containers meet Annex II |
| 4 | Design-type approval after examination and witnessed prototype testing |
| 5 | Manufacturer's duties for design-type approval, modifications and internal production control |
| 6–7 | Administration may inspect production · manufacturer notifies before each new series |
| 8 | Individual container approval |
| 9–10 | Existing containers, and new ones not approved at manufacture (original transitional dates in 1984–85) |
| 11 | Owners report structural changes, and retesting may be required |

## Annex II — Structural safety requirements and tests
Containers must pass the tests without permanent deformation or defects affecting their use.

| Test | Load |
|---|---|
| 1 Lifting from corner fittings | **2R**. Top fittings: vertical lift if over 3,000 mm, 30° sling if 3,000 mm or less. Bottom fittings: sling angles 30°, 37°, 45° and 60° by container length. Hold 5 minutes. |
| 1 Lifting by fork-lift pockets or grappler arms | **1.25R** |
| 2 Stacking | **1.8R**. Offsets of 25 mm lateral and 38 mm longitudinal on the corner fittings. |
| 3 Concentrated loads | Roof: 300 kg over 600 × 300 mm · floor: two 2,730 kg loads on 142 cm² contact areas, moving test device of 5,460 kg |
| 4 Transverse racking | Design forces applied to the top corner fittings |
| 5 Longitudinal restraint | Force **R** on each side, combined **2R** on the base |
| 6 End-walls | **0.4P**, unless a different strength is marked on the plate |
| 7 Side-walls | **0.6P**, unless a different strength is marked on the plate |
| 8 One door removed (added 2012) | Must be specifically approved; stacking and racking results shown on the plate |

### MSC.355(92) in detail
- Defines standard gravity (9.8 m/s²), load as mass, maximum operating gross mass (R), maximum permissible payload (P) and tare
- **Plate** (Reg 1): must show maximum operating gross mass and allowable stacking load for 1.8 g in kg and lbs, and the transverse racking test force in newtons. Markings on the container match the plate.
- Walls tested to 0.4Pg and 0.6Pg · longitudinal restraint Rg on each side, 2Rg on the base
- **Annex III, section 4:** a table of deficiencies for structurally sensitive components (top and bottom rails, headers, sills, corner posts, corner and intermediate fittings, understructure, locking rods). Each component has a threshold for **serious deficiencies** requiring immediate out-of-service determination, and a lower threshold requiring owner notification and transport restrictions. Restrictions differ for empty and loaded containers and for sea versus other modes.
- **Transitional:** plate changes at or before the next scheduled examination, or another date the Administration approves, but **not later than 1 Jul 2015**. Containers completed before 1 Jul 2014 may keep their existing plate if not structurally modified.
