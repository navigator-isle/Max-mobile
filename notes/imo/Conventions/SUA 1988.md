**SUA 1988 — Convention for the Suppression of Unlawful Acts against the Safety of Maritime Navigation (with SUA PROT 1988 and the 2005 Protocols)**

**What is SUA**
- Makes serious acts against ships (seizure, violence, sabotage) international offences
- Parties must make them crimes, establish jurisdiction, and **either prosecute or extradite** offenders ("aut dedere aut judicare")
- The Protocol does the same for fixed platforms on the continental shelf

**History**
- Achille Lauro hijacking (Oct 1985) showed gaps in the law
- SUA and SUA PROT adopted 10 Mar 1988 (Rome) · in force 1 Mar 1992
- After 9/11: 2005 Protocols (SUA 2005, SUA PROT 2005) adopted 14 Oct 2005 · in force 28 Jul 2010 — new terrorism and WMD offences, boarding procedures
- Practical security measures are in SOLAS XI-2 and the ISPS Code

**Applies to**
- Ships navigating, or scheduled to navigate, into, through or from waters beyond the outer limit of a State's territorial sea (or between States)
- Fixed platforms on the continental shelf (Protocol)
- Not: warships, State ships used as naval auxiliaries or for customs/police, ships withdrawn from navigation

---

**Offences (Art 3, 1988)** — unlawfully and intentionally:
- Seizing or controlling a ship by force or threat
- Violence against a person on board if likely to endanger safe navigation
- Destroying a ship or damaging it or its cargo so as to endanger safe navigation
- Placing a device or substance on a ship likely to destroy or damage it
- Destroying or seriously damaging maritime navigational facilities
- Communicating false information endangering safe navigation
- Injuring or killing any person in connection with these; attempts, abetting and threats

**Added by the 2005 Protocol**
- Art 3bis: using a ship (or discharging from it) explosives, radioactive material, BCN weapons, oil or noxious substances to intimidate a population or compel a government; using a ship in a way that causes death or serious injury; transporting such weapons or materials, or terrorist fugitives
- Art 3ter: transporting a person known to have committed an offence, to help them escape prosecution
- Art 8bis **Boarding procedures**: a Party may ask the flag State for authorization to board a ship suspected of SUA offences on the high seas; safeguards for persons on board, use of force only when necessary, compensation for unfounded boarding

**Jurisdiction and extradition**
- Art 6: flag State, State in whose territory, and State of the offender's nationality must establish jurisdiction; others may (victim nationality, attempt to compel that State)
- Art 7: custody of the alleged offender · Art 10: prosecute without exception or extradite · Art 11: SUA offences are extraditable
- Art 8: **master may deliver** an alleged offender to the authorities of a Party (receiving State), with evidence; the receiving State shall accept unless it has grounds

**Key Point**
- ISPS = prevention; SUA = criminal law after an attack
- Master may hand over suspects to a Party's authorities with evidence; inform the flag State
- Piracy on the high seas is under UNCLOS Arts 100–107; SUA covers acts that may not meet the piracy definition (e.g. one ship, political motive)
