# SUA 1988 — Convention for the Suppression of Unlawful Acts against the Safety of Maritime Navigation (with the 1988 Fixed Platforms Protocol and the 2005 Protocols)

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| SUA 1988 and Fixed Platforms Protocol | Adopted 10 Mar 1988, Rome · in force 1 Mar 1992 |
| 2005 Protocols | Adopted 14 Oct 2005 · in force 28 Jul 2010 |
| Core duty | Make the offences crimes, take jurisdiction, **prosecute or extradite** |
| 2005 additions | Art 3bis terrorism-related offences · Art 8bis boarding procedures |
| In force rule (2005 Protocol, Art 18) | 90 days after 12 States |

## What is SUA
- Makes serious unlawful acts against ships international offences, and requires States to prosecute or extradite offenders (Arts 10–11)
- The Fixed Platforms Protocol applies the same system to fixed platforms on the continental shelf; the 2005 amendments add Art 2bis and Art 2ter to it, for similar intimidation-based offences
- Why: hijackings, kidnappings and ships deliberately grounded or blown up increased in the 1980s (IMO)
- IMO's 14th Assembly (Nov 1985) backed a US proposal for preventive measures (resolution A.584(14); MSC/Circ.443 in 1986); in Nov 1986 Austria, Egypt and Italy proposed a convention, which led to the 1988 treaty

## History
- 1988: SUA Convention and Fixed Platforms Protocol adopted 10 Mar 1988 in Rome · in force 1 Mar 1992
- 2005: diplomatic conference 10–14 Oct 2005 adopted the 2005 Protocols · in force 28 Jul 2010
- 2005 Protocol: open for signature 14 Feb 2006 – 13 Feb 2007, then accession (Art 17)

## Application
**Scope (Art 4)**
- Applies when a ship is navigating, or scheduled to navigate, beyond the outer limit of the territorial sea of a single State (or beyond its lateral limits with adjacent States)
- Also applies when the offender is found in another Party's territory (Art 4(2))

**Not covered (Art 2)**
- Warships · State-owned or State-operated ships used as naval auxiliaries or for customs or police purposes · ships withdrawn from navigation or laid up
- Art 2bis: saves other rights under international law (UN Charter, human rights, refugee and humanitarian law); does not cover armed forces in armed conflict; rights under the NPT, BWC and CWC are unaffected

**Ship (Art 1(1)(a))**: a vessel of any type whatsoever not permanently attached to the sea-bed

## Offences
**Art 3(1) — unlawfully and intentionally:**
- (a) Seizing or exercising control over a ship by force, threat or intimidation
- (b) Violence against a person on board, if likely to endanger the safe navigation of the ship
- (c) Destroying a ship, or damaging it or its cargo, so as to endanger safe navigation
- (d) Placing on a ship a device or substance likely to destroy or damage it
- (e) Destroying or seriously damaging maritime navigational facilities or interfering with their operation, so as to endanger safe navigation
- (f) Communicating information known to be false, endangering safe navigation
- **Art 3(2)**: threatening to commit (b), (c) or (e) to compel a person to do or refrain from an act, if the threat is likely to endanger safe navigation

**Added by the 2005 Protocol**
- **Art 3bis(1)(a)**: to intimidate a population or compel a government or organization — using a ship against or on it, or discharging from it, explosive, radioactive or BCN weapons, oil, LNG or other hazardous or noxious substances (where death, serious injury or damage results or is likely); using a ship in a manner that causes death or serious injury; threats
- **Art 3bis(1)(b)**: transporting on a ship explosives, radioactive material, BCN weapons, nuclear material or related equipment, knowing the intended use
- **Art 3ter**: transporting a person, knowing they committed a listed offence, to help them evade prosecution
- **Art 3quater**: injuring or killing in connection with an offence · attempts · complicity · organizing or directing others · contributing to a group's offences
- **Art 5bis**: legal entities can be held liable (criminal, civil or administrative) when a manager or controller commits an offence; sanctions effective, proportionate and dissuasive

## Jurisdiction, custody and extradition
| Art | Content |
|---|---|
| 5 | Penalties reflecting the grave nature of the offences |
| 6(1) | **Mandatory** jurisdiction: offence against or on a ship flying the State's flag · in its territory (including territorial sea) · by its national |
| 6(2) | **Optional** jurisdiction: stateless person with habitual residence · victim is a national · aimed at compelling the State to act |
| 6(4) | The State where the alleged offender is present, and does not extradite, establishes jurisdiction |
| 7 | **Custody**: the State where the offender is present takes custody or other measures, makes a preliminary inquiry, lets the person contact their State's representative, and informs interested States |
| 8 | **Delivery by the master** (see below) |
| 10 | **Prosecute without exception, or extradite**; fair treatment guaranteed |
| 11 | Offences are deemed extraditable; the Convention can be the legal basis |
| 11bis | Offences are not political offences for extradition or mutual assistance |
| 11ter | No obligation to extradite or assist if the request aims to persecute on grounds such as race, religion or nationality |
| 12 | Greatest measure of mutual assistance |
| 13–14 | Co-operation on prevention and exchange of information; avoid undue delay of ships |
| 15 | Reporting to the IMO Secretary-General |
| 16 | Disputes: negotiation, then arbitration; ICJ if arbitration cannot be organized within six months; States may opt out |

**Delivery by the master (Art 8)**
- The master of a flag State ship may deliver a person to the authorities of another Party if there are reasonable grounds to believe the person committed an offence
- The master should notify the receiving State in advance (8(2)) and provide the evidence in the master's possession (8(4))
- The receiving State generally must accept delivery (8(3))

## Boarding procedures — Art 8bis (2005 Protocol)
- 8bis(5): boarding a ship of another Party seaward of any territorial sea requires (a) confirmation of nationality, (b) a request for authorization, and (c) a flag State response — authorize, board itself, join in boarding, or decline
- Boarding without the flag State's express authorization is prohibited
- States may notify an optional rule: authorization deemed given if the flag State does not respond within **four hours**
- 8bis(9): use of force must be minimal and necessary
- 8bis(10) safeguards: safety of life at sea · human dignity and human rights · environmental soundness · notice to the master · States are liable for unfounded or unlawful measures and must give effective recourse · only identifiable government ships and aircraft may act · officials show government identification
- 8bis(15): Parties designate competent authorities and notify the Secretary-General within one month

## Final clauses (2005 Protocol)
- Art 18: in force 90 days after 12 States signed without reservation or deposited instruments
- Art 19: denunciation takes effect one year after deposit
- Art 20: revision conference by IMO, or at the request of one third of States Parties or ten, whichever is higher
- Art 24: authentic in Arabic, Chinese, English, French, Russian and Spanish
- Annex: lists nine treaties (aviation, hostage-taking, nuclear material, terrorist bombings, terrorism financing and others) used for Art 3ter

## Key Point
> SUA is criminal law after an attack — prevention is in SOLAS XI-2 and the ISPS Code
> The master may deliver a suspect to a Party's authorities, giving evidence and advance notice
> Boarding at sea needs the flag State's authorization (or the optional 4-hour rule)
