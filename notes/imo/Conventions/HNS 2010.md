# HNS 2010 — International Convention on Liability and Compensation for Damage in Connection with the Carriage of Hazardous and Noxious Substances by Sea, 1996, as amended by the 2010 Protocol

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 3 May 1996 · 2010 Protocol 30 Apr 2010 |
| In force | **29 Nov 2027** |
| Tier 1 | Shipowner — bulk max 100 million SDR · packaged max 115 million SDR |
| Tier 2 | HNS Fund — total max 250 million SDR |

## What is HNS 2010
- A two-tier compensation system for damage caused by hazardous and noxious substances carried by sea as cargo
- Tier 1: the shipowner — strict liability, compulsory insurance, limited by ship size
- Tier 2: the HNS Fund — financed by contributions from receivers of HNS cargo
- Total compensation for one incident: up to **250 million units of account (SDR)**, including what the shipowner pays (Art 14(5)(a))
- Once in force, the 1996 Convention as amended by the 2010 Protocol is called the "2010 HNS Convention"

## History
- HNS Convention adopted 3 May 1996 (IMO, London) — never entered into force; practical problems stopped States from ratifying
- 2010 Protocol adopted 30 Apr 2010 — solved those problems
- Entry-into-force conditions met on 29 May 2026: at least 12 States, including four each with at least 2 million GT of ships, and more than 40 million tonnes of contributing cargo
- **Enters into force 29 Nov 2027** (18 months after the conditions were met)
- West P&I (July 2026) lists 13 States that had deposited instruments: Belgium, Canada, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Netherlands, Norway, Slovakia, South Africa, Sweden, Türkiye

## Where it applies (Art 3)
- Damage in the territory or territorial sea of a State Party
- Damage in the exclusive economic zone (or equivalent area) of a State Party — environmental damage
- Damage other than environmental damage, outside any State's territory, caused by HNS on a ship registered in a State Party
- Preventive measures, wherever taken

## Not covered (Art 4, Art 5)
- Pollution damage from oil as defined in CLC 1969 (oil pollution is under CLC/Fund)
- Damage caused by radioactive material (class 7)
- Warships and State ships used only on government non-commercial service (unless a State opts in)
- Damage covered by the Bunkers Convention
- Plastic pellets (nurdles) are excluded (West P&I)
- Option (Art 5(1)(a)): a State may declare the Convention not applicable to ships of **200 GT or less** carrying only packaged HNS on domestic voyages, if other conditions are met. West P&I says only Canada and Sweden have used this.

## What counts as HNS (Art 1(5))
- Defined by reference to lists in other IMO conventions and codes:
  - Oils (MARPOL Annex I)
  - Noxious liquid substances (MARPOL Annex II)
  - Dangerous liquids (IBC Code)
  - Liquefied gases
  - Liquid substances with flashpoint not exceeding 60 °C
  - Dangerous, hazardous and harmful materials in packaged form (IMDG Code)
  - Solid bulk materials with chemical hazards (IMSBC Code)
  - Residues from the previous carriage of these (other than packaged residues)
- The HNS Finder tool on hnsconvention.org helps to check a substance

## Damage covered (Art 1(6))
- Loss of life or personal injury, on board or outside the ship
- Loss of or damage to property outside the ship
- Loss or damage by contamination of the environment (limited to reasonable reinstatement measures; loss of profit from impairment is covered)
- Costs of preventive measures, and further loss or damage caused by them
- The Convention covers fire and explosion risks as well as pollution

## Tier 1 — Shipowner (Art 7, 9, 12)
- Owner at the time of the incident is strictly liable (Art 7(1))
- **Limits (Art 9(1)) — tonnage means gross tonnage:**

| Ship size | Bulk HNS (SDR) | Packaged HNS (SDR) |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 2,000 GT | 10 million | 11.5 million |
| 2,001–50,000 GT | + 1,500 per GT | + 1,725 per GT |
| Above 50,000 GT | + 360 per GT | + 414 per GT |
| **Maximum** | **100 million** | **115 million** |

- The packaged-HNS limits also apply where damage involves both bulk and packaged HNS, or the source cannot be determined
- Owner loses the right to limit only for a personal act or omission done with intent, or recklessly and with knowledge that damage would probably result (Art 9(2))

## Owner is not liable if the damage (Art 7(2))
- Resulted from war, hostilities, civil war, insurrection, or an exceptional, inevitable and irresistible natural phenomenon
- Was wholly caused by an intentional act or omission of a third party
- Was wholly caused by negligence of a government authority responsible for lights or other navigational aids
- Was caused by failure of the shipper or another person to give information on the hazardous nature of the HNS, if the owner neither knew nor ought reasonably to have known
- Liability can be reduced or removed if the claimant's own intent or negligence contributed (Art 7(3))

## Insurance and certificate (Art 12)
- Owner of a ship registered in a State Party and actually carrying HNS must keep insurance or other financial security
- Ship must carry a compulsory insurance **certificate** in the Annex I model
- No general minimum tonnage (apart from the 200 GT option above), so small ships may need a certificate too
- The flag State issues it; a State Party may also certify a ship registered in a non-Party State once insurance is checked
- Claims can be made **directly against the insurer** (Art 12(8)); evidence is usually a P&I "Blue Card"

## Tier 2 — HNS Fund (Art 13–21)
- Established to pay when Tier 1 is inadequate or not available (Art 13(1))
- Total limit with Tier 1: **250 million SDR** (Art 14(5))
- Run by an Assembly of States Parties, a claims Committee and a Secretariat headed by a Director (hnsconvention.org)
- **Accounts:** a general account (with sectors) plus separate accounts for **oil, LNG and LPG** (Art 16) — so one cargo type does not subsidise another
- **Who pays:** receivers of contributing cargo in Party States, by quantity received in the previous calendar year; the owner does not pay
- **Thresholds:**
  - General account: receivers of more than 20,000 tonnes of contributing cargo in a year (Art 18(1))
  - Oil account: more than 150,000 tonnes of contributing oil, or more than 20,000 tonnes of other bulk oils (Art 19(1)(a))
  - LPG account: more than 20,000 tonnes (Art 19(1)(b))
  - LNG account: receivers of any quantity — contribution is by the titleholder rules of Art 19(1bis)
- Separate accounts start only after contributing cargo reaches: oil 350 million tonnes, LNG 20 million tonnes, LPG 15 million tonnes (Art 19(3))
- Each State Party must report to the Director the name and address of each person liable to contribute, and cargo data (Art 21)

## Time limits (Art 37)
- Claim must be brought within **3 years** from when the claimant knew (or should reasonably have known) of the damage and the owner's identity
- And never later than **10 years** after the incident

## Key Point
> From 29 Nov 2027 ships carrying HNS to or from Party States need an HNS certificate, as with CLC and Bunkers
> Shipper must declare HNS properly — non-disclosure can free the owner from liability
> Oil pollution from tankers stays under CLC/Fund; bunker oil under Bunkers 2001
