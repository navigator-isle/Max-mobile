**HNS 2010 — International Convention on Liability and Compensation for Damage in Connection with the Carriage of Hazardous and Noxious Substances by Sea, 2010**

**What is HNS 2010**
- A two-tier compensation system for damage from hazardous and noxious substances (chemicals, LNG, LPG, some oils and solids) carried by sea — like CLC + Fund for oil
- Tier 1: the shipowner, with compulsory insurance · Tier 2: the HNS Fund, paid by receivers of HNS cargo
- Up to **250 million SDR** per incident in total

**History**
- HNS Convention adopted 3 May 1996 — never entered into force (problems with reporting of cargo contributions, packaged goods, LNG)
- 2010 Protocol adopted 30 Apr 2010 — fixed the problems; Convention + Protocol = "2010 HNS Convention"
- Conditions met on 29 May 2026 (after Belgium, Germany, Netherlands and Sweden ratified in April 2026) → **in force 29 Nov 2027** (18 months later)
- IOPC Funds Secretariat prepares the HNS Fund

**Applies to**
- Damage in the territory or territorial sea of a Party; environmental damage in its EEZ; and damage (other than environmental) outside any State caused by HNS on a ship of a Party
- All seagoing ships carrying HNS as cargo, of any size (States may exempt ships ≤200 GT carrying only packaged HNS on domestic voyages)
- Not: warships and State non-commercial ships; pollution damage already covered by CLC 1992; radioactive material (class 7)

---

**What counts as HNS**
- Oils (MARPOL Annex I) · noxious liquids (Annex II, IBC Code) · dangerous liquids with flashpoint ≤60 °C · dangerous, hazardous and harmful packaged goods (IMDG Code) · liquefied gases (IGC Code) · liquids with flashpoint ≤60 °C · solid bulk materials with chemical hazards (IMSBC Group B) · residues of these
- Over 6,000 substances on the HNS Finder list

**Damage covered**
- Loss of life or personal injury, on board or outside
- Loss of or damage to property outside the ship
- Loss from contamination of the environment (reinstatement costs, loss of income in fishing and tourism)
- Costs of preventive measures
- Fire and explosion damage as well as pollution

**Tier 1 — Shipowner (strict liability, compulsory insurance, direct action)**
- **Bulk HNS**: 10 million SDR up to 2,000 GT; + 1,500 SDR per GT from 2,001 to 50,000 GT; + 360 SDR per GT above 50,000 GT; maximum **100 million SDR**
- **Packaged HNS** (or mixed/unknown): 11.5 million SDR up to 2,000 GT; + 1,725 SDR per GT from 2,001 to 50,000; + 414 SDR per GT above; maximum **115 million SDR**
- Defences: war or exceptional natural phenomenon; intentional act of a third party; failure of authorities' navigational aids; shipper's failure to declare the HNS (if the owner did not know)
- **HNS certificate of insurance** from the flag State, carried on board

**Tier 2 — HNS Fund**
- Pays above the owner's limit (or when the owner is exempt or cannot pay), up to 250 million SDR in total including tier 1
- Financed by receivers of HNS cargo in Parties above set thresholds; separate accounts for oil, LNG and LPG, plus a general account

**Time limits**
- Claim within 3 years of when the claimant knew of the damage, and not later than 10 years after the incident

**Key Point**
- From 29 Nov 2027, ships carrying HNS to or from Party States will need an HNS certificate (like the CLC and Bunkers certificates)
- Shipper must declare HNS properly — undeclared cargo can shift liability
- Oil cargo of tankers stays under CLC/Fund; bunkers under Bunkers 2001
