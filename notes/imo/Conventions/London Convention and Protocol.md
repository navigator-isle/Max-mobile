# London Convention 1972 and London Protocol 1996 — Prevention of Marine Pollution by Dumping of Wastes and Other Matter

## At a glance
| Item | London Convention (LC) | London Protocol (LP) |
|---|---|---|
| Adopted | 13 Nov 1972, London | 7 Nov 1996 |
| In force | 30 Aug 1975 | 24 Mar 2006 |
| Approach | Lists: banned (Annex I), special permit (Annex II), general permit (all other) | **All dumping banned except the "reverse list"** (Annex 1) |
| Entry into force rule | 15 ratifications (Art XIX) | 30th day after 26 States, incl. 15 LC Parties (Art 25) |
| Administered by | IMO (since 1977) | IMO |

## What is LC / LP
- Global control of the deliberate disposal of wastes at sea
- LC aim (Art I): prevent pollution of the sea by dumping that could harm human health, living resources, amenities or other legitimate uses
- LP is intended to eventually replace the LC (IMO)
- LP principles: **precautionary approach** (Art 3.1 — action even without conclusive evidence of a causal link) · **polluter pays** (Art 3.2) · no transfer of pollution from one part of the environment to another (IMO)
- LP Art 2: Parties act according to their scientific, technical and economic capabilities to prevent, reduce and where practicable eliminate pollution from dumping or incineration

## History
- LC adopted 13 Nov 1972 · in force 30 Aug 1975 · IMO administers it since 1977
- 1993 LC amendments (in force 1994): ban on dumping low-level radioactive waste · industrial waste dumping phased out by 31 Dec 1995 · ban on incineration at sea of industrial waste
- LP adopted 7 Nov 1996 · in force **24 Mar 2006**
- **2006 amendment** (LP.1(1)): adds CO₂ sequestration in sub-seabed geological formations to Annex 1 · adopted 2 Nov 2006 · in force 10 Feb 2007 (Canada 29 Jan 2007) · excludes use of CO₂ streams for enhanced oil recovery (IMO)
- **2009 amendment** (LP.3(4), adopted 30 Oct 2009): Art 6 — export of CO₂ streams for sub-seabed storage allowed between countries with an agreement · not yet in force: takes effect 60 days after two-thirds of Parties accept (IMO) · **2019 resolution LP.5(14)** allows provisional application, by a declaration to the IMO Secretary-General
- **2013 amendment** (LP.4(8), adopted 18 Oct 2013): marine geoengineering — new Art 6bis, Annex 4 (ocean fertilization), Annex 5 (assessment framework)

## London Convention 1972
**Definition of dumping (Art III)**
- Deliberate disposal at sea of wastes or other matter from vessels, aircraft or platforms, and the disposal of vessels or structures themselves
- Not dumping: waste incidental to or from normal operation of vessels and platforms (unless the vessel carries waste for disposal) · placement of matter for a purpose other than mere disposal · waste from seabed mineral exploration and exploitation

**Permits (Art IV)**
- Annex I matter: **prohibited**
- Annex II matter: **prior special permit**
- All other matter: **prior general permit**
- Permits only after careful consideration of the Annex III factors
- Art V: force majeure — dumping allowed to secure the safety of human life or vessels when it is the only way to avert the threat, and must be reported to IMO
- Art VI: each Party designates an authority to issue permits, keep records of the nature and quantities of matter dumped, and monitor the sea
- Art VII: Parties apply the Convention to their vessels and aircraft, those loading in their territory, and must prevent and punish contraventions

**Annex I — prohibited**
- Organohalogen compounds · mercury and compounds · cadmium and compounds
- Persistent plastics and other persistent synthetic materials (for example netting and ropes) that interfere with fishing or navigation
- Crude oil and its wastes, refined petroleum products and distillate residues taken aboard for dumping
- Radioactive wastes or other radioactive matter · materials produced for biological and chemical warfare
- (Added later) incineration at sea of industrial waste and sewage sludge prohibited; industrial waste covered from 1 Jan 1996

**Annex II — special permit**
- Wastes with significant amounts of arsenic, beryllium, chromium, copper, lead, nickel, vanadium, zinc, organosilicon compounds, cyanides, fluorides, and pesticides not covered by Annex I
- Containers, scrap metal and bulky wastes that may sink and obstruct fishing or navigation
- Materials that are non-toxic but may become harmful in quantity or seriously reduce amenities

**Annex III — permit factors**
- A Characteristics and composition of the matter · B Characteristics of the dumping site and method of deposit · C General considerations and conditions

## London Protocol 1996 — the Articles
| Art | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | Definitions |
| 2–3 | Objectives and general obligations |
| 4 | Dumping of wastes or other matter |
| 5 | Incineration at sea |
| 6 | Export of wastes or other matter |
| 7 | Internal waters |
| 8 | Exceptions |
| 9 | Issuance of permits and reporting |
| 10 | Application and enforcement |
| 11 | Compliance procedures |
| 12 | Regional co-operation |
| 13 | Technical co-operation and assistance |
| 14 | Scientific and technical research |
| 15 | Responsibility and liability |
| 18 | Meetings of Contracting Parties |
| 19 | Duties of the Organization |
| 21–22 | Amendment of the Protocol and of the Annexes |
| 25–26 | Entry into force · transitional period |

**Key articles**
- **Art 1 definitions:** dumping = any deliberate disposal into the sea of wastes or other matter from vessels, aircraft, platforms or other man-made structures; includes storage in the seabed and subsoil, and abandonment or toppling of platforms for the sole purpose of deliberate disposal · incineration at sea = combustion on board a vessel, platform or other man-made structure at sea (waste from normal operations excluded) · "sea" = all marine waters other than internal waters
- **Art 4.1.1:** Parties shall prohibit dumping of any wastes or other matter **except those listed in Annex 1**; Art 4.1.2: dumping of Annex 1 matter needs a permit that meets Annex 2
- **Art 5:** incineration at sea prohibited
- **Art 6:** no export of wastes to other countries for dumping or incineration at sea (2009 amendment adds a CO₂ exception)
- **Art 7:** in internal waters a Party may apply the Protocol or other effective permitting and regulatory measures
- **Art 8:** force majeure ("stress of weather") — Arts 4.1 and 5 do not apply when it is the only way to avert a threat; reported forthwith; emergency permits possible after consultation
- **Art 9:** each Party designates authorities that issue permits, keep records and monitor the sea; annual reports to IMO
- **Art 10:** applies to vessels and aircraft registered or flying the flag of a Party, and to those loading waste in its territory
- **Art 11:** compliance procedures set by the Meeting of Contracting Parties
- **Art 26:** a State may notify a transitional period, not longer than five years

## Annex 1 — the "reverse list"
Only these may be considered for dumping, and only with a permit meeting Annex 2:
- 1.1 Dredged material
- 1.2 Sewage sludge
- 1.3 Fish waste, or material from industrial fish processing
- 1.4 Vessels and platforms or other man-made structures at sea
- 1.5 Inert, inorganic geological material
- 1.6 Organic material of natural origin
- 1.7 Bulky items primarily of iron, steel, concrete and similarly unharmful materials
- 1.8 Carbon dioxide streams from CO₂ capture processes for sequestration

**Conditions in Annex 1**
- Items 1.4 and 1.7: material that could create floating debris must be removed, and the item must not seriously obstruct fishing or navigation
- Materials with radioactivity above de minimis levels are not eligible; a scientific study is due every 25 years from 20 Feb 1994
- CO₂ streams: only into a sub-seabed geological formation, must consist overwhelmingly of CO₂, no waste added for disposal

## Annex 2 — assessment of wastes
- General · waste prevention audit · consideration of waste management options · chemical, physical and biological properties · action list · dump-site selection · assessment of potential effects · monitoring · permit and permit conditions

## Marine geoengineering (2013 amendment LP.4(8))
- Marine geoengineering = deliberate intervention in the marine environment to manipulate natural processes, with potential deleterious effects (Art 1.5bis)
- Annex 4 lists **ocean fertilization** only: activity intended mainly to stimulate ocean primary productivity; aquaculture, mariculture and artificial reefs excluded
- All ocean fertilization is prohibited, **except legitimate scientific research**, which may get a permit after assessment under Annex 5 (the 2010 Assessment Framework)
- Annex 5 requires a full project description, a purpose other than mere disposal, and proof of financial resources; research must be peer reviewed, results published, no direct financial gain
- Entry into force: not stated in the text I checked, so it is not given here

## Key Point
> LP bans all dumping except Annex 1 items, and then only with a permit
> Ship garbage, sewage and oily waste are MARPOL matters, not LC/LP dumping — LC excludes waste incidental to normal vessel operation
> Disposing of a vessel at sea is "dumping" — permitted under the LP only as an Annex 1 item (1.4) with a permit
