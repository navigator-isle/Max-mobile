**London Convention 1972 and London Protocol 1996 — Convention on the Prevention of Marine Pollution by Dumping of Wastes and Other Matter**

**What is LC / LP**
- Global control of the deliberate disposal (dumping) of wastes at sea from ships, aircraft and platforms
- London Convention (LC): "black list" banned, "grey list" needs special permit
- London Protocol (LP): stricter — **all dumping is banned except** the few wastes on a "reverse list"; precautionary and polluter-pays approaches
- IMO is the Secretariat

**History**
- LC adopted 13 Nov 1972 (London) · in force 30 Aug 1975
- 1993 amendments: ban on dumping low-level radioactive waste, industrial waste and incineration at sea (from 1994–1996)
- LP adopted 7 Nov 1996 · in force 24 Mar 2006 — replaces LC for Parties to both
- 2006 LP amendment: CO₂ storage in sub-seabed geological formations allowed · in force 10 Feb 2007
- 2009 LP amendment: export of CO₂ streams for sub-seabed storage (provisional application allowed since 2019)
- 2013 LP amendment: regulation of marine geoengineering (ocean fertilization) — not yet in force

**Applies to**
- Dumping: deliberate disposal at sea of wastes from vessels, aircraft, platforms; deliberate disposal of vessels and platforms; storage in the seabed (LP)
- Not: discharges from the normal operation of ships (MARPOL), or disposal from land-based sources

---

**London Convention 1972 (Annexes)**
- Annex I (prohibited): organohalogens, mercury, cadmium, persistent plastics, crude and fuel oils, high-level radioactive waste, chemical and biological warfare agents; (after 1993) industrial waste and low-level radioactive waste
- Annex II (special permit): arsenic, lead, copper, zinc, cyanides, fluorides, pesticides, containers and scrap metal, etc.
- Annex III: factors for issuing permits (characteristics of the waste, dumping site, method)

**London Protocol 1996 — Annex 1 "reverse list" (only these may be considered for dumping, with a permit)**
1. Dredged material
2. Sewage sludge
3. Fish waste or material from industrial fish processing
4. Vessels and platforms or other man-made structures at sea
5. Inert, inorganic geological material
6. Organic material of natural origin
7. Bulky items (iron, steel, concrete) where no practical alternative exists (small islands, isolated communities)
8. CO₂ streams from capture processes for sequestration in sub-seabed geological formations

**Other LP rules**
- Art 5 Incineration at sea prohibited
- Art 6 Export of wastes to other countries for dumping or incineration prohibited
- Art 9 Permits issued by national authorities; records reported to IMO
- Annex 2: waste assessment (waste prevention audit, alternatives, characterization, action list, site selection, monitoring)

**Key Point**
- Dumping needs a national permit (Philippines: EMB-DENR / PCG under national law)
- Ship garbage, sewage and oily waste are MARPOL, not dumping
- Old ships may be dumped only under permit after cleaning — recycling under the Hong Kong Convention is preferred
