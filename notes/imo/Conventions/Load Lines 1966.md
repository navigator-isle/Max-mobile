**LL 1966 — International Convention on Load Lines (with the 1988 Protocol)**

**What is LL**
- Sets the **minimum freeboard** for each ship so it keeps enough reserve buoyancy and strength in the zone and season it sails in
- Freeboard is shown by the **load line mark** (Plimsoll mark) and deck line on each side amidships
- Also sets **conditions of assignment**: weathertight closing of openings, protection of crew (guard rails, bulwarks, freeing ports)

**History**
- 1930 Load Line Convention replaced
- Adopted 5 Apr 1966 (London) · in force 21 Jul 1968
- **1988 Protocol**: harmonized survey and certification (HSSC) with SOLAS and MARPOL, tacit amendment · in force 3 Feb 2000
- 2003 amendments (MSC.143(77)) to the 1988 Protocol: revised technical regulations (Annex B) · in force 1 Jan 2005
- 2008 IS Code Part A made mandatory through LL Protocol (from 1 Jul 2010)
- MSC 111 (May 2026): amendment to 1988 Protocol Reg 25(3) — guard rails on structures other than superstructures to have at least three courses · in force 1 Jan 2028

**Applies to**
- Ships on international voyages of 24 m in length and over
- Not: warships, new ships under 24 m, existing ships under 150 GT, pleasure yachts not in trade, fishing vessels

---

**Articles (key)**
- Art 5 Exceptions · Art 6 Exemptions (Exemption Certificate) · Art 12 Submersion (ship must not be loaded so the mark is submerged at departure, during the voyage or on arrival; allowances for fresh water and fuel used)
- Art 13–19 Surveys and certificates, validity · Art 21 Control (PSC)

**Annex I — Regulations for determining load lines**
- **Ch I General**: definitions (length, perpendiculars, freeboard deck, superstructure), **deck line**, **load line mark** (ring 300 mm outside diameter with horizontal line 450 mm through its centre), marks of the assigning authority (e.g. "AB", "LR"), **load lines** (S, T, W, WNA, F, TF), timber load lines (prefix L)
- **Ch II Conditions of assignment of freeboard**: structural strength and stability information; superstructure end bulkheads; hatchways and hatch covers; machinery space openings; doors; ventilators; air pipes; cargo ports; scuppers and discharges; side scuttles and windows; freeing ports; protection of crew (bulwarks or guard rails ≥ 1 m high, life lines, gangways)
- **Ch III Freeboards**: Type "A" ships (tankers — liquid cargo, high integrity) and Type "B" ships (others); tabular freeboard by length; corrections for block coefficient, depth, superstructures and sheer, bow height and minimum reserve buoyancy
- **Ch IV Special requirements for ships assigned timber freeboards**

**Annex II — Zones, areas and seasonal periods**
- Summer and Tropical zones; Winter seasonal zones and areas (e.g. North Atlantic winter, Southern Winter zone); special areas with dates

**Annex III — Certificates**
- **International Load Line Certificate** (valid up to 5 years) · International Load Line Exemption Certificate

**Freeboard marks (practical)**
- S Summer: line through the centre of the ring
- T Tropical: S − 1/48 of summer draught · W Winter: S + 1/48 of summer draught
- WNA Winter North Atlantic: W + 50 mm (ships ≤ 100 m only)
- F Fresh water: S − Δ/(40 × TPC) mm (FWA) · TF Tropical fresh: T − FWA
- Dock water allowance: DWA = FWA × (1025 − ρdock)/25

**Surveys (1988 Protocol, HSSC)**
- Initial · renewal (≤ 5 years) · **annual** (within 3 months before or after each anniversary date) — checks that hull and closing appliances, freeing ports, guard rails, marks are maintained

**Key Point**
- Never load past the mark for the zone/season at any time of the voyage (allow for fuel used and water density)
- Annual survey ±3 months; marks permanently cut/welded and painted
- PSC checks marks, certificate, closing appliances, freeing ports and stability information
