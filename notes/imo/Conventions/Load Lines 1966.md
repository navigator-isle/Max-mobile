# LL 1966 — International Convention on Load Lines (with the 1988 Protocol)

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 5 Apr 1966 |
| In force | 21 Jul 1968 |
| Annex I | Regulations for determining freeboard |
| Annex II | Zones, areas and seasonal periods |
| Annex III | Certificates |

## What is LL
- Sets the **minimum freeboard** a ship must keep (Annex I) so that load lines are not submerged · zones and seasonal periods in Annex II · certificates in Annex III
- Freeboard = vertical distance, measured amidships, from the upper edge of the deck line down to the upper edge of the load line (Reg 3)
- Load lines and the deck line are marked amidships on each side of the ship
- Conditions of assignment (Chapter II) keep the hull weathertight and watertight: doors, hatchways, openings, ventilators, air pipes, freeing ports, protection of the crew
- Ships carrying timber deck cargo get a smaller (timber) freeboard (Chapter IV)

## History
- Art 28: in force 12 months after at least 15 Governments, including 7 with at least 1 million GT of shipping, signed without reservation or deposited instruments
- Amendments of 1971, 1975, 1979 and 1983 needed acceptance by two-thirds of Parties and never entered into force
- **1988 Protocol**: adopted 11 Nov 1988 · in force **3 Feb 2000** · harmonised survey and certification with SOLAS and MARPOL, revised certain technical annex regulations, and introduced the **tacit amendment** procedure (Annex B amendments deemed accepted after 2 years from communication, or a shorter period of at least 1 year set at adoption; in force 6 months later; a Party may exempt itself for up to 1 year)
- 2003 amendments: MSC.143(77), adopted 5 Jun 2003 · deemed accepted 1 Jul 2004 · in force 1 Jan 2005
- Ships of 24 m and over built on or after 1 Jul 2010 must meet at least Part A of the 2008 IS Code (MSC.270(85)); ships built 21 Jul 1968 – 30 Jun 2010 must meet the stability standards in force before 1 Jan 2005 (per UK MCA guidance)
- Later amendments include MSC.172(79) to MSC.375(93), A.1082(28), A.1083(28) and MSC.491(104) (in force 1 Jan 2024)
- **MSC.591(111)** (adopted 21 May 2026): amends Annex I Ch II **Reg 25 Protection of the crew** (guard rails: see below) · deemed accepted 1 Jul 2027 · in force **1 Jan 2028** · applies to ships whose keel is laid, or which are at a similar stage of construction, on or after 1 Jan 2028

**Applies to** (Arts 4–5)
- Ships of Contracting Governments (and unregistered ships flying their flag) engaged on international voyages
- Annex I applies to new ships; existing ships need not increase freeboard; Annex II applies to new and existing ships
- Art 5 exceptions: warships · new ships under 24 m · existing ships under 150 GT · pleasure yachts not engaged in trade · fishing vessels · ships solely on the Great Lakes/St Lawrence, Caspian Sea, and the Plate, Paraná and Uruguay Rivers (within stated limits)

## Articles (34)
- Art 1 General obligation · Art 2 Definitions (**Length L = 96% of the total length on a waterline at 85% of the least moulded depth, or the stem-to-rudder-stock length on that waterline if greater**) · Art 3 General provisions (ships on international voyages are surveyed, marked and hold an International Load Line Certificate (1966) or an Exemption Certificate; the Administration may assign more than the minimum freeboard) · Art 4 Application · Art 5 Exceptions
- Art 6 Exemptions: short voyages between neighbouring ports · ships with novel features · a single exceptional international voyage — reported to IMO
- Art 7 Force majeure · Art 8 Equivalents · Art 9 Experimental approvals · Art 10 Repairs, alterations and modifications · Art 11 Zones and areas (Annex II)
- Art 12 **Submersion**: load lines must not be submerged at sea, during the voyage, or on arrival; allowances for fresh water (adjusted relative to a density of 1.025) and for deeper loading when departing from rivers or inland waters
- Art 13 Survey and marking (Administration officers, nominated surveyors or recognised organisations) · Art 14 **Surveys and inspections**: initial before service · periodical not exceeding 5 years · annual inspection within 3 months either side of the anniversary date (checks openings, guard rails, freeing ports and crew access; endorsed on the certificate)
- Art 15 Maintenance of conditions after survey (no change without Administration sanction) · Art 16 Issue of certificates · Art 17 Issue by another Government (never to ships of non-Contracting States) · Art 18 Form of certificates (Annex III; English or French translation) 
- Art 19 **Duration**: up to 5 years; may be extended up to 5 months if no relevant alterations; cancelled for material alterations, fittings not maintained, missing inspection endorsements, or structure weakened so the ship is unsafe; ceases when the ship changes flag; single-voyage exemption certificate valid for that voyage
- Art 20 Acceptance of certificates · Art 21 **Control**: foreign port control limited to verifying a valid certificate, that the ship is not overloaded, that the load line position matches the certificate, and that the ship has not been so materially altered as to be manifestly unfit for sea; intervention reported in writing to the flag State's consul or diplomatic representative
- Art 22 Privileges only with a valid certificate · Art 23 Casualties · Art 24 Prior treaties · Art 25 Special rules by agreement · Art 26 Communication of information · Art 27 Signature, acceptance, accession · Art 28 Entry into force · Art 29 Amendments · Art 30 Denunciation (after 5 years, effective 1 year after notice) · Art 31 Suspension · Art 32 Territories · Art 33 Registration · Art 34 Languages

**Annex I — Regulations for determining load lines** (figures below are from the 1966 text; later amendments and the 1988 Protocol Annex B revised some regulations — check the current text before calculating)
- **Chapter I General**: Reg 1 Strength of hull · Reg 2 Application · Reg 3 Definitions (freeboard deck, superstructure, block coefficient, perpendiculars, amidships, freeboard, length) · Reg 4 Deck line · Reg 5 Load line mark · Reg 6 Lines to be used with the load line mark · Reg 7 Mark of assigning authority · Reg 8 Details of marking · Reg 9 Verification of marks
  - **Deck line** (Reg 4): horizontal line 300 mm long × 25 mm wide, amidships on each side; its upper edge normally passes where the upper surface of the freeboard deck, extended outwards, meets the outer shell
  - **Load line mark** (Reg 5): ring 300 mm outside diameter × 25 mm wide, crossed by a horizontal line 450 mm long × 25 mm wide whose upper edge passes through the ring's centre; the centre is amidships, below the deck line by the assigned summer freeboard
  - **Lines** (Reg 6): horizontal lines 230 mm long × 25 mm wide, extending forward of a vertical line 25 mm wide set 540 mm forward of the ring centre: **S** Summer · **W** Winter · **WNA** Winter North Atlantic · **T** Tropical · **F** Fresh water in summer and **TF** Tropical fresh water (both marked abaft the vertical line) · timber load lines (LS, LW, LWNA, LT, LF, LTF) are 230 × 25 mm and extend abaft a vertical line 540 mm abaft the ring centre (LF and LTF forward) · lines that do not apply may be omitted
  - **Mark of assigning authority** (Reg 7): not more than four initials, each about 115 mm high and 75 mm wide, alongside the ring
  - **Painting** (Reg 8): white or yellow on a dark background, or black on a light one; also permanently marked on the sides · **Verification** (Reg 9): the certificate is not issued until the marks are verified correct and permanent
- **Chapter II Conditions of assignment of freeboard**: Reg 10 Information to be supplied to the master (loading, ballasting, stability) · Reg 11 Superstructure end bulkheads · Reg 12 Doors (steel; weathertight; sills at least 380 mm above deck for superstructure access) · Reg 13 Position of hatchways, doorways and ventilators (Position 1 and Position 2) · Reg 14 Cargo and other hatchways · Reg 15 Hatchways closed by portable covers and tarpaulins · Reg 16 Hatchways closed by weathertight steel covers with gaskets and clamping devices · Reg 17 Machinery space openings · Reg 18 Miscellaneous openings in freeboard and superstructure decks · Reg 19 Ventilators · Reg 20 Air pipes · Reg 21 Cargo ports and similar openings · Reg 22 Scuppers, inlets and discharges · Reg 23 Side scuttles · Reg 24 Freeing ports · Reg 25 Protection of the crew · Reg 26 Special conditions of assignment for Type "A" ships
  - **Reg 25 guard rails** (1966 text): at least 1 m high; lowest opening not more than 230 mm; other courses not more than 380 mm apart. MSC.591(111) (ships with keel laid on/after 1 Jan 2028): guard rails or bulwarks around all exposed decks and sea access holes (e.g. moonpool edges) the crew can reach, minimum 1 m high (Administration may approve lower where 1 m interferes with normal operation); guard rails with **at least three courses**; opening below the lowest course not more than 230 mm; other courses not more than 380 mm apart; rounded-gunwale ships: supports on the flat of the deck; chains between two fixed stanchions/bulwarks may replace guard rails where necessary for normal operation (tightened as far as practicable, detachable)
- **Chapter III Freeboards**: Reg 27 Types of ships (Type "A", Type "B"; in practice also Type B reduced, B increased, B timber) · Reg 28 Tabular freeboard (by length, interpolated) · Reg 29 Correction for Type B ships 24–100 m with enclosed superstructures: increase = 7.5 × (100 − L) × (0.35 − E/L) mm · Reg 30 Block coefficient correction (if Cb > 0.68, multiply by (Cb + 0.68)/1.36) · Reg 31 Depth correction (if D > L/15, add (D − L/15) × R; R = 0.48 L mm below 120 m, 250 mm at 120 m and over) · Reg 32 Deck line correction · Reg 33 Standard height of superstructure (raised quarter deck 0.90 m at ≤30 m, 1.20 m at 75 m, 1.80 m at ≥125 m; other superstructures 1.80 m, 1.80 m, 2.30 m) · Reg 34 Length of superstructure · Reg 35 Effective length · Reg 36 Trunks · Reg 37 Deduction for superstructures and trunks · Reg 38 Sheer · Reg 39 Minimum bow height (below 250 m: 56 L (1 − L/500) × (Cb + 0.68)/1.36 mm; 250 m and over: 7000 × (Cb + 0.68)/1.36 mm; Cb not less than 0.68) · Reg 40 Minimum freeboards
  - **Reg 40**: Summer = tabular freeboard with corrections (salt-water minimum 50 mm; 150 mm if non-compliant hatch covers in Position 1) · **Tropical** = summer − 1/48 of summer draught · **Winter** = summer + 1/48 of summer draught · **WNA** = winter + 50 mm, for ships of 100 m or less entering the North Atlantic winter area · **Fresh water** deduction = Δ/(4T) cm (Δ = salt-water displacement in tonnes, T = tonnes per cm immersion); if displacement cannot be certified, 1/48 of summer draught
- **Chapter IV Timber freeboards (Regs 41–45)**: Reg 41 Application · Reg 42 Definitions (timber deck cargo) · Reg 43 Construction (forecastle at least standard height and 0.07 L; ships under 100 m need a poop or equivalent; bulwarks at least 1 m high) · Reg 44 Stowage (lashings at least 19 mm chain or equivalent; uprights and lashings not more than 3 m apart; in a winter seasonal zone in winter cargo height limited to one-third of extreme breadth; guard rails or life lines not more than 33 cm apart vertically) · Reg 45 Computation (winter timber = summer timber + 1/36 of moulded summer timber draught; tropical timber = summer timber − 1/48)

**Annex II — Zones, areas and seasonal periods** (Regs 46–52, 1966 text)
- Winter periods: North Atlantic Winter Zone I 16 Oct – 15 Apr · Zone II 1 Nov – 31 Mar · North Atlantic Winter Seasonal Area (over 100 m) 16 Dec – 15 Feb (100 m and under 1 Nov – 31 Mar) · North Pacific Winter Zone 16 Oct – 15 Apr · Southern Winter Zone 16 Apr – 15 Oct
- Seasonal tropical areas (examples): North Atlantic 1 Nov – 15 Jul · Arabian Sea 1 Sep – 31 May · Bay of Bengal 1 Dec – 30 Apr
- Enclosed seas, ships 100 m and under: Baltic winter 1 Nov – 31 Mar · Black Sea north of 44°N winter 1 Dec – end Feb · Sea of Japan north of 50°N winter 1 Dec – end Feb · Mediterranean (specified western part) winter 16 Dec – 15 Mar
- Reg 52 Winter North Atlantic load line: part of Winter Zone II between 15°W and 50°W, and all of Winter Zone I
- Art 11: a port on a zone boundary is treated as in the zone the ship arrives from or departs to

## Annex III — Certificates
- **International Load Line Certificate (1966)**: ship name, distinctive number or letters, port of registry, length L · assigned type (A, B, B with reduced freeboard, B with increased freeboard) · freeboards from the deck line for T, S, W, WNA and the timber equivalents · fresh water allowance · survey date · reverse side: endorsements from periodical inspections and extensions · valid up to 5 years (Art 19)
- **International Load Line Exemption Certificate**: exemptions under Art 6(2) and any single-voyage exemption under Art 6(4)
- Fresh water allowance adjusted in proportion to the difference of the water density from 1.025; allowance for river or inland-water departure for fuel and consumables used to reach the sea

## Key Point
> Never submerge the load line (for the zone and season) at departure, during the voyage or on arrival (Art 12)
> Annual inspection within 3 months before or after each anniversary; renewal every ≤5 years
> Port State control (Art 21): valid certificate, not overloaded, load line position matches the certificate, no material alteration making the ship unfit for sea
