**LL 1966 (International Convention on Load Lines)**

**Status**
- Entry-into-force dates checked against IMO status of treaties
- Other details written from knowledge, still to be verified

**What is LL**
- Sets the minimum freeboard of ships so they keep enough buoyancy and stability
- Freeboard is marked on the hull as the Load Line mark

**History**
- Adopted 5 Apr 1966
- In force 21 Jul 1968
- 1988 Protocol: harmonized survey and certification, in force 3 Feb 2000
- Replaced the 1930 convention
- Amendment to 1988 LL Protocol Reg 25(3) (guard rails) adopted MSC 111 (May 2026) — in force 1 Jan 2028

**Applies to**
- Ships of 24 m and above on international voyages
- Not warships, pleasure yachts, fishing vessels

**Annex I: Regulations**
- Chapter I General: application, definitions, surveys, certificates
- Chapter II Conditions of assignment of freeboard
  - structural strength and intact stability information
  - hatchways, doors, ventilators, air pipes, freeing ports
  - side scuttles, scuppers, discharges, guard rails, bulwarks
- Chapter III Freeboards
  - Type A and Type B ships, tables, corrections
  - Summer, Tropical, Winter, Winter North Atlantic, Fresh water allowances
- Chapter IV Special requirements for ships assigned timber freeboards

**Annex II**
- Zones, areas and seasonal periods

**Annex III**
- Certificates: International Load Line Certificate; Exemption Certificate

**Marks**
- Deck line and load line mark
- Marks: TF, F, T, S, W, WNA
- Timber load lines carry the letter L before the marks

**Surveys and certificate**
- Initial, renewal, annual (within 3 months before or after the anniversary) and additional surveys
- Certificate valid up to 5 years

**Key Point**
- Never load past the mark for the zone and season; a ship's draught must not put the mark under water
- PSC checks: marks painted, certificate, closing appliances, freeing ports, stability information
