# UNCLOS 1982 — United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 10 Dec 1982 |
| In force | **16 Nov 1994** (12 months after the 60th ratification, Art 308) |
| Parties | **170** (UN status table, 31 Mar 2025) |
| Implementing agreements | **1994 Part XI Agreement** (153 parties, in force 28 Jul 1996). **1995 Fish Stocks Agreement** (93 parties, in force 11 Dec 2001). **BBNJ Agreement** (adopted 19 Jun 2023, in force **17 Jan 2026**, 83 parties) |
| Reservations | None allowed unless another article expressly permits them (Art 309) |
| Why it matters to ships | It sets where a coastal State's power ends and the flag State's begins. IMO conventions work inside this frame |

## Maritime zones
![[diagram:unclos-zones|Maritime zones measured from the baselines]]

| Zone | Limit from baselines | What the coastal State has | Foreign ships |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Territorial sea** (Arts 2–3) | Up to **12 nm** | Sovereignty | **Innocent passage** |
| **Contiguous zone** (Art 33) | Up to **24 nm** | Control to prevent and punish breaches of its laws | Freedoms of navigation |
| **EEZ** (Arts 55–58) | Up to **200 nm** | Sovereign rights over resources (Art 56) | Freedoms of navigation and overflight (Art 58) |
| **Continental shelf** (Art 76) | **200 nm**, or the edge of the margin. Fixed points not beyond **350 nm**, or **100 nm** from the 2,500 m isobath | Sovereign rights over the seabed's natural resources (Art 77) | Water above is EEZ or high seas |
| **High seas** (Arts 86–87) | All sea outside the above | None | Open to all States, coastal or land-locked |

- **Artificial islands and installations** in the EEZ: the coastal State has the exclusive right to build or authorize them. They do not have the status of islands (Art 60)
- **Archipelagic States** (Arts 46–53): baselines not longer than **100 nm** (with limited exceptions). All ships have the right of **archipelagic sea lanes passage**

## Passage
- **Innocent passage** (Arts 17, 19, 25): all ships have it in the territorial sea, as long as it is "not prejudicial to the peace, good order or security of the coastal State". The coastal State may take necessary steps to prevent passage that is not innocent
- **Transit passage** (Arts 37–39): in straits used for international navigation, all ships and aircraft have it and it "shall not be impeded". Ships must proceed without delay

## Flag State and the high seas
- **Genuine link** between the State and the ship (Art 91). A ship sails under **one flag** and is generally under that State's exclusive jurisdiction on the high seas (Art 92)
- **Flag State duties** (Art 94): effectively exercise jurisdiction and control in administrative, technical and social matters. Keep a ship register. Deal with manning, labour conditions and crew training, signals, communications and collision prevention, and safety at sea
- After certain high seas casualties the flag State holds an inquiry by a suitably qualified person (Art 94(7))
- **Rescue** (Art 98(1)(a)): the master must render assistance to any person found at sea in danger of being lost
- **Piracy** (Art 101): illegal violence, detention or depredation for private ends by the crew or passengers of a private ship or aircraft. Any State may seize a pirate ship on the high seas (Art 105)
- **Drugs** (Art 108): all States cooperate to suppress illicit drug traffic by ships on the high seas
- **Right of visit** (Art 110): a warship may board a foreign ship only with reasonable grounds to suspect piracy, slave trade, unauthorized broadcasting, statelessness, or that the ship has the warship's own nationality
- **Hot pursuit** (Art 111): may start when the coastal State has good reason to believe the ship broke its laws, and extends to the EEZ and continental shelf

## Pollution from ships (Part XII)
![[diagram:unclos-pollution|Who enforces against ship pollution]]

| Level | Role |
|---|---|
| General | States protect and preserve the marine environment (Art 192), with the best practicable means (Art 194) |
| **Flag State** (Arts 211(2), 217) | Make laws for its ships, ensure they comply with applicable international rules and standards, and investigate at once and prosecute where appropriate |
| **Port State** (Arts 218–219) | May investigate when a ship is voluntarily in port. Takes administrative measures to stop a substandard ship sailing, as far as practicable |
| **Coastal State** (Art 211(4), 220) | May make laws for foreign ships in its territorial sea, but they must not hamper innocent passage. May start proceedings for violations in its territorial sea or EEZ |

**Safeguards for the ship**
- Only **monetary penalties** may be imposed on foreign ships for violations beyond the territorial sea (Art 230)
- Proceedings by the coastal State stop if the flag State starts its own penalty proceedings within **6 months**, unless exceptions apply (Art 228)
- States are **liable** for loss caused by unlawful enforcement (Art 232)
- Art 233: none of this changes the regime of straits used for international navigation
- MARPOL and the other IMO conventions supply the "applicable international rules and standards" (see [[MARPOL 73/78]])

## Settling disputes
- States settle disputes by peaceful means (Art 279) and may choose any means (Art 280). Any party may submit a dispute to the competent court or tribunal (Art 286)
- **Art 287 choices:** the International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea (**ITLOS**), the International Court of Justice, an arbitral tribunal (Annex VII), or a special arbitral tribunal (Annex VIII). A State with no declaration is deemed to accept **Annex VII arbitration**
- **Provisional measures** (Art 290) can be ordered
- **Prompt release** (Art 292): applies where a State detains another State's vessel
- **ITLOS** has **21** independent members (Annex VI, Art 2). Its Seabed Disputes Chamber has 11 (Annex VI, Art 35)
- Optional exceptions (Art 298) and limits (Art 297, for example marine scientific research) apply to some disputes
