# Bunkers 2001 — International Convention on Civil Liability for Bunker Oil Pollution Damage

## At a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 23 Mar 2001 |
| Opened for signature | 1 Oct 2001 to 30 Sep 2002 (Art 12), then accession |
| In force | 21 Nov 2008 |
| Entry-into-force rule | 1 year after 18 States, including 5 with ships totalling at least 1 million GT (Art 14) |
| Liability | Strict, joint and several on all "shipowners" (Art 3) |
| Insurance | Ships over 1,000 GT (Art 7) |
| Time limits | 3 years from the damage · 6 years from the incident (Art 8) |
| Structure | 19 Articles and an Annex (certificate model) |

## What is Bunkers 2001

- Compensation for people harmed by spills of **bunker oil**: oil carried as a ship's fuel
- A standalone convention covering pollution damage only
- Modelled on the 1969 Civil Liability Convention
- Three tools: strict liability of the shipowner, compulsory insurance, and direct action against the insurer
- Ships over 1,000 GT carry a certificate on board

## Scope (Art 2)

Applies only to:
- pollution damage in the territory, including the territorial sea, of a State Party, or in its exclusive economic zone (or equivalent zone up to 200 nautical miles from the baselines); and
- preventive measures, **wherever taken**, to prevent or minimise such damage.

## Key definitions (Art 1)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ship | Any seagoing vessel or seaborne craft, of any type |
| Shipowner | The owner (including the registered owner), bareboat charterer, manager and operator |
| Registered owner | The person registered as owner or, if unregistered, the owner. For a State ship run by a company registered as operator, that company |
| Bunker oil | Hydrocarbon mineral oil, including lubricating oil, used or intended for operating or propelling the ship, and its residues |
| Incident | An occurrence, or series with the same origin, that causes pollution damage or a grave and imminent threat of it |
| Preventive measures | Reasonable measures taken after an incident to prevent or minimise pollution damage |
| Pollution damage | (a) Loss or damage outside the ship from contamination by bunker oil escaping or discharged, wherever it occurs; environmental impairment compensation is limited to reasonable reinstatement costs. (b) The costs of preventive measures and further loss or damage they cause |
| Gross tonnage | Calculated under Annex I of the Tonnage Convention 1969 |

## Liability (Art 3)

![[diagram:bunkers-liability|Who is liable, who can be sued and what insurance covers]]

- The shipowner at the time of the incident is liable for pollution damage from bunker oil on board or originating from the ship. For a series of occurrences, liability attaches to the owner at the first occurrence.
- Where several persons are liable, liability is **joint and several**.
- Claims against the shipowner may be made only under this Convention. The shipowner's independent rights of recourse are preserved.

| No liability if the shipowner proves |
|---|
| War, hostilities, civil war, insurrection, or an exceptional, inevitable and irresistible natural phenomenon |
| Damage wholly caused by a third party's intentional act or omission |
| Damage wholly caused by the negligence or wrongful act of a government authority responsible for lights or navigational aids |

- If the claimant's intentional act or negligence caused the damage wholly or partly, the shipowner may be wholly or partly exonerated towards that claimant.
- **Two or more ships (Art 5):** all shipowners concerned (unless exonerated) are jointly and severally liable for damage that is not reasonably separable.

## Exclusions (Art 4)

- Damage that is "pollution damage" under the CLC, whether or not CLC compensation is payable
- Warships, naval auxiliaries and other State-owned or operated ships used only on non-commercial government service. A State may elect to apply the Convention to them.
- For State-owned ships used commercially, the State may be sued in the Art 9 courts and must waive defences based on its sovereign status

## Limitation (Art 6)

- The shipowner, insurer and security provider keep the right to limit liability under any applicable national or international regime, **such as the 1976 LLMC Convention, as amended**.

## Compulsory insurance (Art 7)

| Point | Rule |
|---|---|
| Who | The registered owner of a ship over 1,000 GT registered in a State Party |
| Cover | Insurance or other financial security (e.g. a bank guarantee) for pollution liability up to the applicable limitation amount, never more than the LLMC 1976 amount, as amended |
| Certificate | Issued after the State authority checks compliance. The registry State issues or certifies it; for an unregistered ship, any State Party may |
| Contents | Ship name, distinctive number or letters and port of registry · registered owner's name and principal place of business · **IMO number** · type and duration of security · insurer or guarantor's name and principal place of business · validity period, not longer than the security |
| Language | The issuing State's official language; if that is not English, French or Spanish, a translation into one of them is needed |
| On board | **Carried on board**; a copy is deposited with the registry authorities |
| Early termination | Security does not count if it can end, other than by expiry, within 3 months of notice of termination to the authorities, unless the certificate is surrendered or replaced in that period |
| Recognised bodies | A State may authorise a recognised organisation to issue certificates, but remains fully responsible for them |
| Mutual acceptance | Certificates issued by one Party are accepted by the others with equal force |
| Flag State | Must not allow a ship under its flag to operate without a certificate |
| Port State | Each Party must ensure by national law that the cover is in force for any ship over 1,000 GT, wherever registered, entering or leaving its ports or an offshore facility in its territorial sea |
| Electronic records | A State may notify that ships need not carry or produce the certificate in its ports, if the issuing State keeps electronic records open to all Parties |
| State-owned ships | No insurance needed, but the ship carries a registry-State certificate stating the ship is State-owned and liability is covered within the limit |
| Domestic trade | A State may declare Art 7 not applicable to ships operating only in its own territory and territorial sea |

**Direct action (Art 7(10)):**
- Claimants may sue the insurer or security provider directly.
- The insurer may use the shipowner's defences (except bankruptcy or winding up), including Art 6 limitation, and the shipowner's **wilful misconduct**. No other defences.
- Even if the owner cannot limit, the insurer may limit to the required insurance amount.
- The insurer may require the shipowner to be joined.

## Time limits (Art 8)

![[diagram:bunkers-timebar|Action within 3 years of the damage, and never later than 6 years after the incident]]

## Jurisdiction and judgments (Arts 9–10)

- Actions against the shipowner, insurer or security provider may be brought **only in the courts of States Parties** where the incident caused damage (territory, territorial sea or EEZ area), or where preventive measures were taken.
- Each defendant must get reasonable notice. Each Party must ensure its courts can hear such claims.
- A final, enforceable judgment from such a court is recognised in every Party, unless it was obtained by fraud or the defendant had no reasonable notice and fair chance to present its case. The merits cannot be reopened.

> **Key Point:** Ships over 1,000 GT carry the Bunkers certificate on board, and PSC checks it. The shipowner is strictly liable and the insurer can be sued directly. Claims are barred 3 years after the damage, and never later than 6 years after the incident. Cargo oil from tankers falls under the CLC, not Bunkers 2001.
