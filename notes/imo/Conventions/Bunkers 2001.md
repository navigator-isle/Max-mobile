**Bunkers 2001 — International Convention on Civil Liability for Bunker Oil Pollution Damage**

**What is Bunkers 2001**
- Liability and compensation for pollution damage caused by spills of bunker oil (fuel and lubricating oil) from ships other than those covered by CLC
- Compulsory insurance, a State certificate on board, and direct action against the insurer

**History**
- Adopted 23 Mar 2001 (London)
- In force 21 Nov 2008
- Fills the gap left by CLC 1992 (which covers tankers' cargo and bunkers of laden tankers)

**Applies to**
- Pollution damage in the territory, territorial sea and EEZ (or 200 nm zone) of a Party, and preventive measures wherever taken
- All seagoing ships of any type; compulsory insurance for ships **over 1,000 GT**
- Not: warships and State non-commercial ships (unless the State decides); damage covered by CLC 1992

---

**Key articles**
- Art 1 Definitions: **shipowner** = registered owner, bareboat charterer, manager and operator; bunker oil = hydrocarbon mineral oil used to operate or propel the ship, and its residues
- Art 3 **Liability**: strict and joint-and-several on all "shipowners"
  - Defences: act of war or exceptional natural phenomenon; act done with intent by a third party; negligence of authorities maintaining navigational aids; damage caused by the claimant
- Art 6 **Limitation**: under the applicable national or international regime — usually LLMC 1976 as amended (1996 Protocol, 2012 limits)
- Art 7 **Compulsory insurance**: registered owner of a ship over 1,000 GT registered in a Party must maintain insurance or other financial security up to the LLMC limit
  - **Bunkers certificate** issued by the flag State (or another Party for ships of non-Parties), carried on board, copy with the registry
  - Claimants may sue the insurer directly
- Art 8 Time limits: 3 years from the damage, never more than 6 years from the incident
- Art 9 Jurisdiction: courts of the State where damage occurred

**Damage covered**
- Loss or damage outside the ship by contamination, including environmental impairment limited to reasonable reinstatement costs
- Cost of preventive measures and further loss caused by them

**Key Point**
- Ships over 1,000 GT carry the Bunkers certificate (issued against a P&I "blue card") — PSC checks it
- Report any bunker spill at once (MARPOL Annex I reporting) and keep records for claims
- Tanker cargo oil → CLC/Fund; bunkers of other ships → Bunkers 2001; chemicals and gases → HNS 2010
