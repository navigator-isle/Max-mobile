# Cape Town Agreement 2012 — Agreement on the Implementation of the Provisions of the 1993 Torremolinos Protocol relating to the Torremolinos International Convention for the Safety of Fishing Vessels, 1977

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 11 Oct 2012, Cape Town |
| In force | February 2027 (12 months after the conditions were met) |
| Applies to | Fishing vessels of 24 m in length and over (Protocol Art 3(3)) |
| Certificate | International Fishing Vessel Safety Certificate — up to 5 years (Reg I/13(1)) |
| Parties | 28 States (24 Feb 2026) |

## What is the Cape Town Agreement
- Makes the technical rules of the 1993 Torremolinos Protocol work: the 1977 Convention and its 1993 Protocol never entered into force
- Mandatory safety standards for fishing vessels of 24 m and over (IMO says more than 45,000 vessels, FAO data)
- Covers design, construction, equipment, inspection, stability and seaworthiness, machinery and electrical installations, life-saving appliances, fire protection and communications equipment
- IMO calls it the fourth pillar of fishing vessel safety, with STCW-F (training), the ILO Work in Fishing Convention 2007 and the FAO Port State Measures Agreement 2009

## History
- Adopted 11 Oct 2012 ("Done at Cape Town this eleventh day of October two thousand and twelve")
- Open for signature at IMO HQ 11 Feb 2013 – 10 Feb 2014, then accession (Art 3)
- Art 4(1): in force 12 months after **not less than 22 States**, whose fishing vessels of 24 m and over operating on the high seas total **not less than 3,600**, have consented to be bound
- Conditions met when Argentina deposited its instrument of accession at IMO on 24 Feb 2026: **28 States, 3,754 vessels** (24 m and over)
- **In force February 2027**

## How the Agreement is built
- Art 1(1): Parties give effect to the Agreement and to the 1993 Protocol, except Protocol Art 1(1)(a), 1(2), 1(3), Art 9 and Art 10
- Art 2: Protocol Arts 2–8 and 11–14 apply, read as references to this Agreement
- Annex = integral part of the Agreement (Art 1(3))
- Once in force, States must put it into national law; flag States make sure their vessels comply; port States may inspect foreign vessels in their ports (IMO)

## Where it applies
- Protocol Art 3(3): the Annex applies to fishing vessels of **24 m in length and over**, unless a provision says otherwise
- Art 3(4): where a chapter sets a limit above 24 m, the Administration decides which of its regulations apply to vessels of 24 m and over below that limit
- Art 3(2): not applied to vessels used only for sport or recreation, fish processing, research and training, or as fish carriers
- Reg I/1(1): the Annex applies to **new vessels**, unless it says otherwise
- Reg I/1(2): the Administration may use gross tonnage instead of length:

| Length | Gross tonnage |
|---|---|
| 24 m | 300 |
| 45 m | 950 |
| 60 m | 2,000 |
| 75 m | 3,000 |

## New vessel (Reg I/2)
- A vessel for which, on or after entry into force of the Protocol: (a) the building or major conversion contract is placed; or (b) the contract was placed earlier but delivery is 3 years or more after entry into force; or (c) with no contract, the keel is laid, construction identifiable with the vessel begins, or assembly of at least 50 tonnes or 1% of the estimated structural mass (whichever is less) has started
- Existing vessel = a vessel that is not a new vessel
- Length (L) = 96% of the total waterline length at 85% of the least moulded depth, or the length from the foreside of the stem to the rudder stock axis on that waterline if greater

## Annex — the ten chapters
| Ch | Title | Main application rule |
|---|---|---|
| I | General provisions | New vessels (Reg 1) |
| II | Construction, watertight integrity and equipment | Length limits in individual regulations (45 m, 75 m) |
| III | Stability and associated seaworthiness | No general length limit; vessels designed and built to satisfy it |
| IV | Machinery and electrical installations and periodically unattended machinery spaces | 45 m and over (Reg 1) |
| V | Fire protection, fire detection, fire extinction and fire fighting | New vessels 45 m and over (Reg 1(1)) |
| VI | Protection of the crew | See regulations |
| VII | Life-saving appliances and arrangements | New vessels 45 m and over (Reg 1(1)) |
| VIII | Emergency procedures, musters and drills | New and existing vessels 24 m and over (Reg 1) |
| IX | Radiocommunications | New and existing vessels 45 m and over (Reg 1) |
| X | Shipborne navigational equipment and arrangements | New and existing vessels; each regulation sets its own limit |

## Chapter I — Surveys and certificates
- Reg titles: 1 Application · 2 Definitions · 3 Exemptions · 6 Inspection and survey · 7 Surveys of life-saving appliances and other equipment · 8 Surveys of radio installations · 9 Surveys of structure, machinery and equipment · 10 Maintenance of conditions after survey · 11 Issue or endorsement of certificates · 12 Issue or endorsement by another Party · 13 Duration and validity · 14 Forms of certificates and records of equipment · 15 Availability · 16 Acceptance · 17 Privileges
- Reg 6: inspections and surveys by Administration officers, nominated surveyors or recognized organizations; they must be able to require repairs and to carry out inspections requested by a port State
- Reg 7: life-saving appliances — initial, renewal (not more than 5 years), periodical, annual (within 3 months before or after each anniversary date) and additional surveys
- Reg 9: structure, machinery and equipment — includes **at least two bottom inspections in any five-year period, with not more than 36 months between any two**
- Reg 10(3): after an accident or a defect, the skipper or owner also reports at once to the port State authorities if in another Party's port
- Reg 6(3): where a vessel is unfit, the port State makes sure it does not sail until it can go to sea without danger

**Certificate validity (Reg I/13)**
- International Fishing Vessel Safety Certificate: period set by the Administration, **not more than 5 years**
- If a new certificate cannot be put on board before expiry, the old one may be endorsed for up to **5 months**
- Extension of up to 3 months only to reach the port where the survey is done
- Certificate stops being valid if surveys are not done in time, it is not endorsed, or the vessel changes flag (Reg 13(8))

## Chapter III — Stability (Reg III/2 intact stability)
- GZ curve area: at least 0.055 m-rad up to 30° heel; at least 0.09 m-rad up to 40° (or the flooding angle if lower); at least 0.03 m-rad between 30° and 40°
- GZ at least 200 mm at heel of 30° or more
- Maximum GZ preferably above 30°, and not less than 25°
- Initial GM at least 350 mm for single-deck vessels
- Reg III/7: stability calculated for set operating conditions — departure with full fuel and stores, arrival with 10% stores and a minimum catch
- Reg III/8: icing allowance in ice-prone areas — 30 kg/m² on exposed weather decks
- Reg III/9 and III/10: inclining test on completion and stability information for the skipper
- Reg III/14: subdivision and damage stability for vessels of 100 m and over carrying 100 or more persons

## Chapter V — Fire protection
- Part A Reg 1(1): new vessels of 45 m and over · Part B (Regs 3–27): vessels of 60 m and over · Part C (Reg 28 on): vessels of 45 m to under 60 m
- Reg 1(2): accommodation and service spaces use one of three methods — IF (non-combustible divisions), IIF (automatic sprinkler and fire alarm), IIIF (automatic fire alarm and detection; spaces bounded by A or B class divisions limited to 50 m²)
- Regs 17–19: at least two fire pumps and a fire main with hydrants, hoses and nozzles; vessels of 75 m and over need an independently driven emergency fire pump
- Reg 22: machinery spaces — fixed extinguishing systems; new halogenated hydrocarbon systems prohibited
- Regs 24–25: at least two fireman's outfits in widely separated places; fire control plan displayed permanently

## Chapter VI — Protection of the crew
- Reg 1: deck openings with coamings or sills under 600 mm need guards; skylights with protective bars not more than 350 mm apart; non-skid decks in working areas
- Reg 2: access hatches at least 600 mm × 600 mm (or 600 mm diameter); escape hatch covers open from both sides
- Reg 3: bulwarks or guard rails at least **1 m** high; gap under the lowest course not more than 230 mm; other courses not more than 380 mm apart; stanchions not more than 1.5 m apart
- Reg 4: stairways and ladders — adequate size and strength, handrails, non-slip treads

## Chapter VII — Life-saving appliances (new vessels 45 m and over)
- Reg 5: at least two survival craft; vessels under 75 m carry craft for everyone on board on each side
- Reg 8: one approved lifejacket for each person on board
- Reg 9: immersion suits — one for each person assigned to the rescue boat crew, and at least three per lifeboat
- Reg 10: lifebuoys — 8 on vessels of 75 m and over, 6 on vessels under 75 m; at least half with self-igniting lights, at least two with smoke signals
- Reg 11: one line-throwing appliance · Reg 12: at least 12 rocket parachute flares
- Reg 13: at least three two-way VHF radiotelephone sets · Reg 14: at least one radar transponder on each side
- Reg 16: weekly and monthly inspections; falls turned end for end at intervals of not more than 30 months and renewed at least every 5 years; inflatable liferafts serviced every 12 months (extendable to 17)
- Lifeboats (Reg 17): capacity not more than 150 persons; full boarding in not more than 3 min; fuel flashpoint above 43 °C
- Liferafts (Regs 20–21): must stay afloat 30 days and survive a drop from 18 m; minimum capacity six persons
- Lifejackets (Reg 24): donned within 1 min; light of at least 0.75 cd for 8 h
- Immersion suits (Reg 25): donned within 2 min
- Rocket parachute flares (Reg 29): reach at least 300 m and burn at least 40 s
- Hand flares (Reg 30): at least 15,000 cd for at least 1 min

## Chapter VIII — Emergency procedures, musters and drills (24 m and over)
- Reg 2: muster list; general alarm of **seven or more short blasts followed by one long blast**
- Reg 3(1)(a): abandon ship and fire drills — monthly for each crew member; vessels under 45 m may follow an approved schedule of at least every three months
- Reg 3(1)(f), (g): lifeboat and rescue boat launching — at least once every three months
- Reg 3(3): drills recorded in a log-book

## Chapter IX — Radiocommunications (45 m and over)
- Functional requirements (Reg 4), general radio equipment (Reg 6), sea-area equipment (Regs 7–10), watches (Reg 11), reserve power (Reg 12), radio personnel (Reg 15)
- Certified sea areas are recorded on the certificate (Reg IX/2)

## Chapter X — Navigational equipment
- Standard magnetic compass: vessels of 24 m and over (Reg 3(1)(a))
- Gyro-compass: vessels of 45 m and over built on or after 1 Sep 1984 (Reg 3(3))
- Radar: 45 m and over (Reg 3(6)) · echo-sounder (Reg 3(9)) · speed and distance device (Reg 3(11))
- Bridge visibility (Reg 6): new vessels of 45 m and over — view of the sea surface ahead not blocked for more than the lesser of two vessel lengths or 500 m

## Key Point
> From February 2027, fishing vessels of 24 m and over of Party States need the International Fishing Vessel Safety Certificate (up to 5 years), and Party port States may inspect foreign vessels
> Many requirements start at 45 m — fire protection, life-saving appliances, radiocommunications, radar
> Drills: abandon ship and fire drills monthly; bottom inspections at least twice in five years
