**Cape Town Agreement 2012 — Agreement on the Implementation of the Provisions of the 1993 Torremolinos Protocol relating to the Torremolinos International Convention for the Safety of Fishing Vessels, 1977**

**What is the Cape Town Agreement**
- The first binding global safety standard for fishing vessels — design, construction, equipment, stability, inspection and certification
- Brings into force (amended) the technical rules of the 1977 Torremolinos Convention and its 1993 Protocol, which never entered into force
- Also helps fight illegal, unreported and unregulated (IUU) fishing by making vessels certified and inspectable

**History**
- 1977 Torremolinos Convention and 1993 Torremolinos Protocol: never in force (too few ratifications)
- Cape Town Agreement adopted 11 Oct 2012 (Cape Town)
- Torremolinos Declaration (2019): States pledged to ratify
- Entry-into-force conditions met when Argentina acceded on 24 Feb 2026: 28 States with 3,754 fishing vessels of 24 m and over (needed 22 States and 3,600 vessels)
- **In force February 2027** (12 months after the conditions were met)

**Applies to**
- Seagoing fishing vessels of 24 m in length and over (also fish-processing on board) flying the flag of a Party
- Some chapters apply from 45 m; flag States may exempt vessels on certain limited voyages
- Port States may inspect foreign vessels in their ports

---

**Annex — Regulations (Torremolinos 1993 as amended)**
- **Ch I General provisions**: application, definitions, exemptions, surveys (initial, periodical, intermediate), **International Fishing Vessel Safety Certificate** (up to 4 years), Record of Equipment, port State control
- **Ch II Construction, watertight integrity and equipment**: strength, doors, hatchways, ventilators, air pipes, freeing ports, anchoring and mooring
- **Ch III Stability and associated seaworthiness**: stability criteria (incl. effect of fishing gear, icing, water on deck), stability booklet, inclining test, flooding of fish holds
- **Ch IV Machinery and electrical installations and periodically unattended machinery spaces**
- **Ch V Fire protection, fire detection, fire extinction and fire fighting**
- **Ch VI Protection of the crew**: deck openings, bulwarks, guard rails, stairways and ladders
- **Ch VII Life-saving appliances and arrangements** (survival craft, lifejackets, immersion suits, EPIRB/SART, muster list, drills)
- **Ch VIII Emergency procedures, musters and drills**
- **Ch IX Radiocommunications** (GMDSS)
- **Ch X Shipborne navigational equipment and arrangements**

**Related instruments**
- STCW-F 1995 (crew training, revised 2026) · ILO C188 Work in Fishing (living and working conditions) · FAO Port State Measures Agreement (IUU fishing) · Polar Code (fishing vessels ≥24 m in polar waters from 1 Jan 2026)

**Key Point**
- From February 2027: fishing vessels ≥24 m of Parties need the International Fishing Vessel Safety Certificate and can be inspected in foreign ports
- Philippines (MARINA) was reported in Feb 2026 to be studying accession
- Stability, freeing ports, life-saving and radio rules are the main changes for older fleets
