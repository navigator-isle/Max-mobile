**Nairobi WRC 2007 — Nairobi International Convention on the Removal of Wrecks**

**What is the WRC**
- IMO convention giving States a legal basis to remove, or arrange the removal of, wrecks that threaten safety at sea or the marine environment, with uniform rules beyond the territorial sea
- Registered owner is liable for the costs of locating, marking and removing a hazardous wreck (Art 10)
- Compulsory insurance or other financial security for ships ≥300 GT flying a Party's flag · certificate carried on board (Art 12)
- The affected State may remove the wreck at the owner's expense if the owner does not (Art 9)

**History**
- Adopted 18 May 2007 at an international conference in Nairobi, Kenya
- Open for signature at IMO HQ 19 Nov 2007 – 18 Nov 2008, then accession (Art 17)
- Art 18: in force 12 months after 10 States signed without reservation or deposited instruments · in force **14 Apr 2015**

**Applies to**
- Convention area: a Party's exclusive economic zone (or, if none, an area adjacent to the territorial sea extending no more than 200 nm from the baselines) — Art 1
- Territory and territorial sea: only if the Party opts in by notifying the IMO Secretary-General (Art 3(2)); then only Arts 7–9 (locating, marking, removal) apply there
- Ship = seagoing vessel of any type: hydrofoils, air-cushion vehicles, submersibles, floating craft and platforms — except platforms engaged in seabed mineral exploration, exploitation or production
- Not covered (Art 4): measures under the 1969 Intervention Convention and its 1973 Protocol · warships and government non-commercial ships, unless the State decides otherwise

**Key definitions (Art 1)**
- Wreck: after a maritime casualty — a sunken or stranded ship · any part of a ship · any object lost from a ship that is adrift or stranded · a ship about to sink or strand if effective assistance is not already being given
- Hazard: a condition or threat that impedes navigation, or may reasonably cause major harmful consequences to the marine environment, the coastline or related interests of one or more States
- Related interests: e.g. fisheries, tourism, public health, marine conservation, offshore infrastructure
- Affected State: the State in whose Convention area the wreck lies
- Registered owner: the person registered as owner, or if none the owner at the time of the casualty

**Art 2 — Objectives**
- Measures must be proportionate to the hazard, no more than reasonably necessary, and end once the wreck is removed
- The Convention gives no State sovereignty or sovereign rights over the high seas

**Art 5 — Reporting wrecks**
- The master and the operator of a ship flying the flag of a Party report to the affected State without delay (one report from either satisfies both)
- Report contents:
  - Name and principal place of business of the registered owner
  - Precise location of the wreck
  - Type, size and construction of the wreck
  - Nature of the damage and condition of the wreck
  - Nature and quantity of cargo, especially hazardous and noxious substances
  - Amount and types of oil on board, including bunker oil and lubricating oil

**Art 6 — Determination of hazard** (the affected State considers)
- Type, size and construction of the wreck · water depth · tidal range and currents
- Particularly sensitive sea areas (and areas under UNCLOS Art 211(6) special measures)
- Proximity to shipping routes or traffic lanes · traffic density and frequency · type of traffic
- Type and quantity of cargo and oil, and the damage likely if released
- Vulnerability of port facilities · prevailing weather and hydrographic conditions · submarine topography
- Height of the wreck above or below the lowest astronomical tide
- Acoustic and magnetic profiles of the wreck
- Proximity to offshore installations, pipelines, telecommunications cables and similar structures
- Any other circumstances that might require removal

**Art 7 — Locating wrecks**
- On learning of a wreck, the affected State urgently uses all practicable means to warn mariners and concerned States
- If it has reason to believe the wreck is a hazard, it takes all practicable steps to establish the exact location

**Art 8 — Marking wrecks**
- If the wreck is a hazard, the affected State takes all reasonable steps to mark it, using the internationally accepted buoyage system for the area
- It publicizes the marking details, including through nautical publications

**Art 9 — Measures to facilitate removal**
- The affected State informs the registry State and the registered owner at once, and consults them
- The registered owner must remove a wreck found to be a hazard, and give evidence of insurance or financial security (Art 12)
- The owner may contract any salvor; the State may set conditions only as needed for safety and environmental protection
- The State sets a reasonable deadline, tells the owner in writing, and says it will remove the wreck at the owner's expense if the deadline is missed; it may act immediately if the hazard becomes severe
- If the owner misses the deadline or cannot be contacted, or in an emergency, the State may remove the wreck by the most practical and expeditious means, consistent with safety and the environment

**Art 10 — Liability of the owner (strict)**
- The registered owner pays the costs of locating, marking and removing the wreck, unless the owner proves the casualty:
  - resulted from an act of war, hostilities, civil war, insurrection, or an exceptional, inevitable and irresistible natural phenomenon
  - was wholly caused by an act or omission of a third party done with intent to cause damage
  - was wholly caused by the negligence or wrongful act of a government or other authority responsible for the maintenance of lights or other navigational aids
- The owner keeps the right to limit liability under national or international regimes (e.g. LLMC 1976)
- Costs can be claimed against the owner only under this Convention · rights of recourse against third parties are kept

**Art 11 — Exceptions to liability**
- The owner is not liable under this Convention where that would conflict with the applicable liability conventions: the CLC (oil pollution), the 1996 HNS Convention, the Bunker Oil Convention 2001, and nuclear liability conventions or national law
- If the measures count as salvage under national law or another convention, that law or convention governs the salvor's reward

**Art 12 — Compulsory insurance or other financial security**
- Registered owner of a ship ≥300 GT flying a Party's flag must have insurance or other financial security
- Amount: the limitation amount under LLMC 1976 Art 6(1)(b)
- Certificate: issued by the registry State (for an unregistered ship, any Party may issue it) · carried on board · a copy filed with the authority that keeps the registry
- Certificate shows: ship name and port of registry, gross tonnage, IMO number, owner, type and duration of security, insurer, period of validity
- Cover cannot end before expiry without 3 months' notice to the authorities (unless a replacement certificate is issued or the old one is surrendered)
- Each Party ensures, under its national law, that ships ≥300 GT entering or leaving its ports, or arriving at or leaving its offshore facilities in the territorial sea, have this cover, whatever their flag
- Direct action: claims for costs can be brought directly against the insurer · the insurer may use the owner's defences (including limitation) and wilful misconduct, but not the owner's bankruptcy
- State-owned ships: no insurance needed; they carry a certificate from the registry State

**Art 13 — Time limits**
- Right to recover costs lapses unless action is brought within 3 years of the date the hazard was determined
- Never later than 6 years from the date of the casualty (for a series of occurrences, from the first one)

**Key Point**
- Onboard: after a casualty resulting in a wreck, the master and operator report to the affected State without delay (owner, precise position, damage, cargo, oil on board)
- Keep the wreck removal insurance certificate valid and on board (≥300 GT)
- The owner pays for locating, marking and removal; the State may remove the wreck itself if the deadline is missed or in an emergency, at the owner's expense
- The owner may still limit liability (e.g. LLMC 1976)
