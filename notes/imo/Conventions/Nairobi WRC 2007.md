**Nairobi WRC 2007 — Nairobi International Convention on the Removal of Wrecks**

**What is the WRC**
- IMO convention on the prompt and effective removal of wrecks that are a hazard to navigation or the marine environment
- Makes the registered owner strictly liable for locating, marking and removing a hazardous wreck
- Compulsory insurance (or other financial security) for ships ≥300 GT · certificate carried on board
- Gives the coastal State the right to act if the owner does not

**History**
- Adopted 18 May 2007, diplomatic conference in Nairobi, Kenya
- In force 14 Apr 2015 (12 months after the 10th ratification)
- Before WRC: wreck removal depended on each State's national law; no common insurance rule

**Applies to**
- Convention area: a Party's Exclusive Economic Zone (or up to 200 nm from baselines if no EEZ declared)
- Territorial sea: only if the Party opts in (Art 3(2)) — most Parties have opted in
- Any seagoing vessel or seaborne craft (incl. submersibles, floating craft, floating platforms not on location for exploration)
- Not covered: warships, naval auxiliaries, State ships on non-commercial service (unless the State decides) · measures under the Intervention Convention 1969

**Key definitions (Art 1)**
- Wreck: a sunken or stranded ship, any part of it, anything that is or was on board (incl. lost cargo), or a ship about to sink or strand where help is not being taken
- Hazard: danger or impediment to navigation, or a threat of major harmful consequences to the marine environment or coastline
- Affected State: the State in whose Convention area the wreck lies
- Registered owner: person registered as owner (or, if none, the person owning the ship) at the time of the casualty

---

**Articles**
- Art 2 Objectives and principles: measures proportionate to the hazard · no right to claim the wreck itself
- Art 3 Scope of application (EEZ; territorial sea by opt-in)
- Art 4 Exclusions (State ships; Intervention Convention measures)
- Art 5 Reporting wrecks:
  - Master and operator report to the affected State without delay
  - Report: name and position of the wreck · type, size, construction · damage and condition · cargo (esp. hazardous and noxious) · bunkers and oil on board
- Art 6 Determination of hazard — the affected State considers:
  - Size, type, construction of the wreck · depth of water · tidal range and currents · particularly sensitive sea areas
  - Proximity to shipping routes, traffic density and frequency
  - Cargo and fuel (type, quantity, consequences of release)
  - Vulnerability of port facilities · weather · seabed topography · height of the wreck above or below water at low tide
  - Acoustic and magnetic profiles · offshore installations, pipelines, cables nearby · other relevant factors
- Art 7 Locating wrecks: the affected State warns mariners and nearby States and takes steps to locate the wreck
- Art 8 Marking wrecks: marked per the international buoyage system (IALA) · details promulgated (notices to mariners)
- Art 9 Measures to facilitate removal:
  - The affected State informs the flag State and the registered owner
  - The registered owner must remove a wreck found to be a hazard
  - The owner may contract any salvor; the State may set conditions to protect safety and environment
  - The State sets a reasonable deadline · if the owner fails, or in an emergency, the State removes the wreck at the owner's cost
- Art 10 Liability of the owner (strict):
  - Owner pays the costs of locating, marking and removing
  - Exceptions: act of war, hostilities, civil war, insurrection · exceptional natural phenomenon (inevitable, irresistible) · wholly caused by a third party's intent · wholly caused by negligence of an authority responsible for lights and navigational aids
  - Right to limit liability under national or international rules (e.g., LLMC 1976/1996) is kept
- Art 11 Exceptions to liability: not where the CLC 1992, HNS, Bunkers 2001 or nuclear conventions apply
- Art 12 Compulsory insurance or other financial security:
  - Ships ≥300 GT flying a Party flag, and ships entering or leaving a port or offshore facility in a Party's territorial sea
  - Amount: up to the LLMC 1976 as amended (1996 Protocol) limit
  - Certificate issued by the flag State (or by another Party if the flag is not a Party) · carried on board · copy kept by the registry
  - Direct action: claims may be brought straight against the insurer; insurer may use the owner's defences (not insolvency) and limit liability; full defence only for wilful misconduct of the owner
- Art 13 Time limits: 3 years from the date the hazard was determined · in any case 6 years from the date of the casualty
- Art 14 Amendment provisions · Art 15 Settlement of disputes
- Art 16 Relationship to UNCLOS and other conventions · Art 17–21 Signature, ratification, entry into force, denunciation, depositary

**Certificate on board**
- "Certificate of insurance or other financial security in respect of liability for the removal of wrecks" (Nairobi WRC certificate)
- Based on the P&I club "Blue Card" · issued by the flag State or a Party on request
- Checked by PSC like CLC and Bunker certificates
- War and terrorism risks: Blue Card given only if war risks P&I cover is in place

**Key Point**
- Onboard: after grounding, sinking or lost cargo, the master reports at once to the coastal State (position, condition, cargo, fuel)
- Keep the WRC certificate valid with the CLC/Bunker certificates — PSC checks all three
- Owner pays for locating, marking and removal; a State may remove the wreck itself and bill the owner
- Limits apply under LLMC, but some States (e.g., UK, France, Germany, Netherlands, Singapore) reserve unlimited wreck removal liability
