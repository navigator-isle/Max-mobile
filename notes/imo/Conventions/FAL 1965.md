# FAL 1965 — Convention on Facilitation of International Maritime Traffic

## At a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 9 Apr 1965, London |
| In force | 5 Mar 1967 (60 days after 10 States, Art XI) |
| Structure | 16 Articles · an Annex of Standards and Recommended Practices |
| Annex | 7 sections and 3 appendices, replaced in full by FAL.14(46) |
| Electronic exchange | Mandatory from 9 Apr 2019 (2016 amendments) |
| Single window | Mandatory from 1 Jan 2024 (2022 amendments) |
| Not applied to | Warships and pleasure yachts (Art II(3)) |

## What is FAL

- Aim: to **facilitate and expedite international maritime traffic** and prevent unnecessary delays to ships, persons and cargo (Art I)
- Parties work for the highest practicable uniformity of formalities, documents and procedures (Art III)
- Public authorities may require only the declarations listed in the Annex (Standard 2.1)
- Parties cooperate directly or through the IMO (Art IV)

## History

![[diagram:fal-dates|The Convention and its Annex amendments, with adoption and entry-into-force dates]]

- The 2002 amendments added the stowaway standards and practices.
- **2002:** the Dangerous Goods Manifest (FAL Form 7) became the basic document for dangerous goods information.
- **2005:** electronic transmission was encouraged, and submitting all information at a single point was recommended.
- **2009:** new IMO FAL forms were approved.
- **2016:** all IMO FAL forms were revised except the Ship's Stores Declaration (Form 3). Electronic exchange became mandatory from 9 Apr 2019, with a transition period of at least 12 months, and the "Single Window" wording was introduced.
- **2022 (FAL.14(46), 13 May 2022):** the complete Annex was replaced, and single window systems became mandatory. In force 1 Jan 2024.
- **2023 (17 Mar 2023):** an amendment to Recommended Practice 7.11 on combating illicit activities. The IMO page gives its expected entry into force as 1 Jan 2025.

## The Articles

| Art | Content |
|---|---|
| I | General undertaking: facilitate and expedite international maritime traffic |
| II | Co-operation and scope. Measures should be, as far as practicable, no less favourable than for other international transport; apply equally to ships of coastal and non-coastal Parties; **warships and pleasure yachts excluded** |
| III | Uniformity of formalities, documents and procedures |
| IV | Co-operation directly or through the IMO |
| V | Saving clauses: wider facilities allowed; temporary measures for public morality, order, security or health; matters not covered stay under national law |
| VI | Definitions: **Standards** are measures whose uniform application is necessary and practicable; **Recommended Practices** are measures whose application is desirable |
| VII | Amending the Annex |
| VIII | Differences from Standards (notification) |
| IX | Revision of the Convention |
| X | Signature, acceptance, accession |
| XI | Entry into force |
| XII | Denunciation |
| XIII | Territorial extension |
| XIV | Notifications |
| XV | Deposit and registration |
| XVI | Languages and date |

**Differences (Art VIII and IMO page):**
- A Government that cannot comply with a Standard, or needs different rules, must notify the Secretary-General of the differences.
- For a Recommended Practice, notification is required once the Government fully complies.

**Amending the Annex (Art VII):**
- By proposal to the Secretary-General: in force 6 months after a majority of the Parties accept it.
- By a conference called at the request of one third of the Parties: needs a two-thirds majority of those present and voting.
- Art IX allows the Convention itself to be revised by a conference.

**Entry into force and denunciation (Arts XI–XII):**
- In force 60 days after at least 10 States signed without reservation or deposited instruments.
- Denunciation is possible after 3 years in force for a Party, and takes effect 1 year after notice.

## Electronic exchange and single window (Section 1)

| Standard | Content |
|---|---|
| 1.3bis | Public authorities shall establish, maintain and use systems for the electronic exchange of information |
| 1.3ter | A transition period of no less than 12 months, counted from when all functional and technical specifications are published |
| 1.3quart | Alternative means for clearance information during the transition; existing message formats stay accepted |
| 1.3quin | Introduce arrangements so all the information required under Standard 2.1 can be given to a **single window** by electronic means |
| 1.3sext | Data for arrival, stay and departure is submitted once and reused as far as possible |

- **Single window:** an environment that allows standardized, harmonized information to be submitted to a single entry point, typically electronic.
- The IMO says all Member States must use a Maritime Single Window since 1 Jan 2024. It also reports that authorities still required paper or mixed submissions in 64% of port calls in one survey, so adoption is uneven.

## Declarations authorities may require (Standard 2.1)

Public authorities shall **not require more than** these:

| | Declaration |
|---|---|
| a | General Declaration |
| b | Cargo Declaration |
| c | Ship's Stores Declaration |
| d | Crew's Effects Declaration |
| e | Crew List |
| f | Passenger List |
| g | Dangerous Goods Manifest |
| h | Special declaration for postal items (Universal Postal Union Acts) |
| i | Maritime Declaration of Health (International Health Regulations) |
| j | Ship Sanitation Control Exemption Certificate, Ship Sanitation Control Certificate or extension (IHR) |
| k | Security-related information under SOLAS regulation XI-2/9.2.2 |
| l | Advance electronic cargo information for customs risk assessment (WCO SAFE Framework of Standards) |
| m | Advance Notification Form for Waste Delivery to Port Reception Facilities |

**Standard IMO FAL forms (Appendix 2):**

| Form | Document |
|---|---|
| FAL 1 | General Declaration |
| FAL 2 | Cargo Declaration |
| FAL 3 | Ship's Stores Declaration |
| FAL 4 | Crew's Effects Declaration |
| FAL 5 | Crew List |
| FAL 6 | Passenger List |
| FAL 7 | Dangerous Goods Manifest |

> **Key Point:** Ports may ask only for the declarations in Standard 2.1, and since 1 Jan 2024 must take them electronically through a single window. Keep the crew list, stores and cargo data accurate, because the same data is reused. For stowaways, follow Section 4 and Appendix 3.
