**OPRC 1990 — International Convention on Oil Pollution Preparedness, Response and Co-operation (and OPRC-HNS Protocol 2000)**

**What is OPRC**
- A framework so that States, ports and ships are ready to respond quickly to major oil pollution incidents, and help each other
- Requires emergency plans on ships and offshore units, reporting, national contingency plans and stockpiles of equipment
- OPRC-HNS Protocol 2000 applies the same system to hazardous and noxious substances

**History**
- Exxon Valdez spill (Alaska, Mar 1989) led to the Convention
- OPRC adopted 30 Nov 1990 (London) · in force 13 May 1995
- OPRC-HNS Protocol adopted 15 Mar 2000 · in force 14 Jun 2007

**Applies to**
- Parties; ships flying their flag; offshore units, sea ports and oil handling facilities under their jurisdiction
- Not: warships and State non-commercial ships

---

**Key articles**
- Art 1 General: Parties take all appropriate measures to prepare for and respond to oil pollution incidents
- Art 3 **Oil pollution emergency plans**
  - Ships carry a shipboard oil pollution emergency plan as required by IMO (MARPOL Annex I Reg 37: **SOPEP** — oil tankers of 150 GT and above, other ships of 400 GT and above)
  - Offshore units, sea ports and oil handling facilities have emergency plans co-ordinated with national systems
- Art 4 **Oil pollution reporting procedures**: masters report without delay any event involving a discharge or probable discharge, and any oil observed at sea, to the nearest coastal State
- Art 5 Action on receiving a report: assess, inform other States and IMO
- Art 6 **National and regional systems**: a competent national authority, operational contact points, a national contingency plan, minimum pre-positioned equipment, exercises and training
- Art 7 International co-operation in response · Art 8 Research and development · Art 9 Technical co-operation · Art 10 Bilateral and multilateral co-operation
- Art 12 IMO's role (information services, education and training, technical services)
- Annex: reimbursement of costs of assistance

**OPRC-HNS Protocol 2000**
- Same structure for HNS incidents: emergency plans, reporting, national systems, co-operation
- Ships carry a shipboard emergency plan for marine pollution incidents involving HNS — in practice the **SMPEP** (MARPOL Annex II Reg 17 for noxious liquid substances; combined SOPEP+SMPEP allowed)

**Shipboard plan contents (SOPEP / SMPEP)**
- Reporting procedure and contact lists (coastal State contacts in an appendix, kept up to date)
- Steps to control a discharge: operational spills (bunkering, overflows, pipe leaks) and casualties (grounding, collision, fire, hull failure, excessive list)
- Co-ordination with national and local authorities; drawings, SOPEP locker equipment

**Key Point**
- SOPEP (and SMPEP for chemical tankers) approved and on board; drills practised; contact list updated — PSC checks
- Report any spill or sighting of oil at sea to the nearest coastal State without delay
- Philippines: PCG is the national authority for oil spill response (National Oil Spill Contingency Plan)
