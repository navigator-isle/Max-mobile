# OPRC 1990 — International Convention on Oil Pollution Preparedness, Response and Co-operation (and the OPRC-HNS Protocol 2000)

## At a glance
| Item | OPRC 1990 | OPRC-HNS Protocol 2000 |
|---|---|---|
| Adopted | 30 Nov 1990, London | 15 Mar 2000, London |
| In force | 13 May 1995 | 14 Jun 2007 |
| Covers | Oil pollution incidents | Hazardous and noxious substances (anything other than oil likely to harm health, living resources, amenities or other legitimate uses of the sea) |
| In force rule | 12 months after 15 States (Art 16) | 12 months after 15 States (Art 15) |
| Ship plan | SOPEP (MARPOL Annex I Reg 37) | Plan under MARPOL Annex II Reg 17; combined plan = SMPEP |

## What is OPRC
- A global framework for international co-operation in preparing for and responding to major oil pollution incidents or threats
- Started with a July 1989 conference of leading industrial nations in Paris, which asked IMO to develop further measures; the IMO Assembly endorsed this in Nov 1989 (IMO)
- Parties: take all appropriate measures to prepare for and respond to oil pollution incidents (Art 1(1))
- Annex is an integral part of the Convention (Art 1(2))
- Warships, naval auxiliaries and other State ships on non-commercial service are excluded; Parties still ensure they act consistently where reasonable (Art 1(3))
- IMO has an important coordinating role

## Definitions (Art 2)
- **Oil**: petroleum in any form, including crude oil, fuel oil, sludge, oil refuse and refined products
- **Oil pollution incident**: an occurrence or series of occurrences with the same origin that results or may result in a discharge of oil, threatens the marine environment or the coastline or related interests of one or more States, and needs emergency or other immediate response
- **Ship**: any vessel operating in the marine environment, including hydrofoils, air-cushion vehicles, submersibles and floating craft
- **Offshore unit**: fixed or floating installation engaged in oil or gas exploration, exploitation or production, or loading or unloading of oil
- **Sea ports and oil handling facilities**: those that present a risk of an oil pollution incident, including ports, oil terminals and pipelines

## Articles
**Art 3 — Oil pollution emergency plans**
- 3(1)(a): ships flying a Party's flag carry a shipboard oil pollution emergency plan, as required by IMO
- 3(1)(b): such a ship may be inspected in a port or offshore terminal under a Party's jurisdiction
- 3(2): offshore unit operators under a Party's jurisdiction have plans coordinated with the national system (Art 6) and approved by the national authority
- 3(3): ports and oil handling facilities have plans or similar arrangements coordinated with the national system

**Art 4 — Reporting**
- Masters or persons in charge of ships and offshore units report without delay any event involving a discharge or probable discharge of oil — ships to the nearest coastal State; offshore units to the coastal State they are under
- Same duty for any observed event at sea involving a discharge or the presence of oil
- Persons in charge of ports and oil handling facilities report to the competent national authority
- Maritime inspection vessels and aircraft are instructed to report; civil aircraft pilots are requested to report to the nearest coastal State
- Reports follow IMO guidelines

**Art 5 — Action on receiving a report**
- The Party assesses whether an incident has occurred, and its nature, extent and consequences, then promptly informs affected or likely affected States
- Where severity justifies, informs IMO directly or via regional bodies

**Art 6 — National and regional systems**
- Each Party establishes a national response system, with: designated competent authority · national operational contact point · an authority entitled to request or grant assistance · a **national contingency plan**
- Within its capabilities: pre-positioned oil spill combating equipment · programme of exercises and training · detailed response plans and communications · mechanism to coordinate response
- Keeps IMO informed of contact points, equipment, expertise and the national contingency plan

**Other articles**
- Art 7 International co-operation: advice, technical support and equipment on request (subject to capabilities); costs follow the Annex; Parties facilitate entry, use and departure of response ships, aircraft, personnel and equipment
- Art 8 Research and development · Art 9 Technical co-operation · Art 10 Bilateral and multilateral co-operation · Art 11 Relation to other conventions
- Art 12 Institutional arrangements: IMO provides information services, education and training, technical services and technical assistance
- Art 13 Evaluation · Art 14 Amendments (two-thirds of Parties accept; in force six months later for those that accepted)
- Art 15 Signature: 30 Nov 1990 – 29 Nov 1991, then accession
- Art 16 Entry into force: 12 months after 15 States signed without reservation or deposited instruments
- Art 17 Denunciation after five years; takes effect 12 months after notice · Art 19: done at London 30 Nov 1990

**Annex — reimbursement of costs of assistance**
- Without a prior agreement: if a Party acts at another Party's express request, the requester reimburses the assisting Party (it may cancel but pays costs already incurred); if a Party acts on its own initiative, it bears its own costs
- Reimbursable costs are calculated under the assisting Party's law and practice, unless agreed otherwise
- The requesting Party may ask for waivers, reductions or postponements, with regard to developing countries
- Does not prejudice recovery of costs from third parties under other laws (CLC 1969, FUND 1971)

## Shipboard plans (SOPEP / SMPEP)
| Plan | Rule | Ships |
|---|---|---|
| SOPEP | MARPOL Annex I Reg 37 | Oil tankers of 150 GT and above; all other ships of 400 GT and above |
| Marine pollution plan for noxious liquid substances | MARPOL Annex II Reg 17 | Ships of 150 GT and above certified to carry noxious liquid substances in bulk |
| SMPEP | Combined SOPEP + NLS plan | Practical, because the two largely overlap |

- Plans must be approved
- The plan carries, as an appendix, the **List of National Operational Contact Points** — the agencies or officials that receive and process incident reports
- IMO Guidelines for the Development of Shipboard Marine Pollution Emergency Plans (2010 edition): SOPEP guidelines — MEPC.54(32) as amended by MEPC.86(44) · SMPEP guidelines — MEPC.85(44) as amended by MEPC.137(53)

## OPRC-HNS Protocol 2000
- Open for signature 15 Mar 2000 – 14 Mar 2001, then accession; only OPRC Parties may join (Art 13)
- In force 12 months after 15 States; for later States, three months after deposit (Art 15)
- Art 1: same duties, for HNS pollution incidents; warships and non-commercial State ships excluded
- Art 2: HNS = any substance other than oil which, if introduced into the marine environment, is likely to create hazards to human health, harm living resources, damage amenities or interfere with other legitimate uses of the sea
- Art 3: ships carry an on-board pollution incident emergency plan; masters follow reporting procedures; ports and HNS handling facilities have plans coordinated with the national system; a Party that learns of an incident notifies States likely to be affected. Floating production and storage units (FPSO, FSU) are not covered by this article
- Art 4: national and regional systems (authorities, contact points, national contingency plan, equipment, exercises, training)
- Art 5: international co-operation · Art 6 research · Art 7 technical co-operation · Art 8 bilateral/multilateral agreements
- Denouncing the OPRC Convention also denounces the Protocol (Art 16(4))

## Key Point
> SOPEP and, where certified for noxious liquids, a marine pollution plan (SMPEP) must be approved and on board — check the ship-size thresholds above
> Report any discharge or sighting of oil at sea to the nearest coastal State without delay
> Keep the list of national operational contact points up to date
> A port State may inspect the shipboard plan (Art 3(1)(b))
