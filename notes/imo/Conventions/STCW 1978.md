# STCW 1978 — Training, Certification and Watchkeeping for Seafarers

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| What it is | Minimum international standards for training, certification and watchkeeping of seafarers on seagoing ships. The first convention to set them |
| Adopted | **7 Jul 1978** |
| In force | **28 Apr 1984** |
| 1995 amendments | Split the annex into Regulations and the **STCW Code** (Part A mandatory, Part B guidance). In force **1 Feb 1997** |
| 2010 Manila amendments | Adopted **25 Jun 2010**. In force **1 Jan 2012** |
| Latest in force | **1 Jan 2026**: violence and harassment training, MSC.560(108) |
| Under review | Comprehensive review, revised STCW expected to go to MSC in **2029 or 2030** |
| Fishing vessels | Separate convention: [[STCW-F 1995]] |

![[diagram:stcw-structure|How STCW is built]]

> **Key Point:** STCW says who is qualified to do what on board, and how rested they must be. The numbers for sea service are in the Convention annex. The competence tables, rest hours and alcohol limits are in the Code.

## History
![[diagram:stcw-timeline|STCW milestones]]

- **1978:** the first global standards. Before it, each government set its own
- **1995:** a full revision, to fix vague wording that had led to different readings. Parties report to IMO under Reg I/7, and the Maritime Safety Committee confirms a list of Parties that have given full effect to the Convention
- **2010 Manila:** covered fraud prevention, work and rest hours, drug and alcohol rules, medical fitness, and certificates for able seafarers. It added training for ECDIS, leadership and teamwork, electro-technical officers, tankers, security and piracy, distance learning, polar waters and dynamic positioning
- **Manila transition:** seafarers who began approved service or training before **1 Jul 2013** could be certified under the old rules until **1 Jan 2017**

## Chapter I — General provisions
| Regulation | What it requires |
|---|---|
| I/2 Certificates and endorsements | Certificates of competency issued by the Administration only. Endorsements last **no more than 5 years**. Originals kept on board. An English translation is needed if the certificate is not in English |
| I/7 Communication of information | Parties report to IMO. The MSC confirms the Parties that have given full effect to the Convention. Other Parties may accept their certificates in principle |
| I/8 Quality standards | A quality standards system for training and certification, evaluated independently at intervals of no more than **5 years** |
| I/9 Medical standards | Certificate valid up to **2 years**, up to **1 year** if under 18. Colour vision tested every **6 years**. If it expires at sea, it stays valid to the next port with a doctor, up to **3 months** |
| I/10 Recognition of certificates | Needs an evaluation showing full compliance. A non-Party's certificates are not recognized. A seafarer can serve up to **3 months** while an endorsement application is pending |
| I/11 Revalidation | Every **5 years**. See below |
| I/14 Company responsibilities | Seafarers certified and familiarized with the ship before duties |
| I/15 Transitional provisions | Ran to 1 Jan 2017 |

**Revalidating a certificate of competency (A-I/11)**
- At intervals of no more than **5 years**
- Sea service of **12 months in the preceding 5 years**, or **3 months in the 6 months before revalidating**
- Approved refresher courses and updating are also accepted under the Code

## Chapter II — Deck
| Regulation | Rank | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| II/1 | Officer in charge of a navigational watch (500 GT+) | Age **18**. **12 months** of approved seagoing service in an approved training programme, or **36 months** otherwise. At least **6 months** of bridge watchkeeping under supervision |
| II/2 | Chief mate, 3,000 GT+ | **12 months** as officer in charge of a navigational watch |
| II/2 | Master, 3,000 GT+ | **36 months** as OOW, or **24 months** if at least 12 were as chief mate |
| II/2 | Master, 500 to 3,000 GT | **36 months**, or **24 months** if at least 12 were as chief mate. No separate service period is given for the chief mate |
| II/4 | Rating in a navigational watch | Age **16**. **6 months** of training and experience, or special training plus at least **2 months** of approved seagoing service |
| II/5 | Able seafarer deck | Age **18**. **18 months** of deck department service, or **12 months** plus approved training |

![[diagram:stcw-deck|Deck career path (3,000 GT+)]]

**What the OOW tables cover (Code A-II/1)**
- Passage planning, position fixing, keeping a safe navigational watch
- ECDIS: "operational procedures for using ECDIS are established, applied and monitored"
- Use of leadership and managerial skill
- Emergencies, cargo, ship operations

## Chapter III — Engine
| Regulation | Rank | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| III/1 | Officer in charge of an engineering watch (750 kW+) | Age **18**. **12 months** of approved seagoing service in an approved programme, or **36 months** of which at least **30** in the engine department. At least **6 months** of watchkeeping under supervision |
| III/2 | Second engineer, 3,000 kW+ | **12 months** as a qualified engineer officer |
| III/2 | Chief engineer, 3,000 kW+ | **36 months**, or **24 months** if at least 12 were as second engineer |
| III/3 | Second engineer, 750 to 3,000 kW | **12 months** as assistant or engineer officer |
| III/3 | Chief engineer, 750 to 3,000 kW | **24 months**, of which at least **12** as second engineer |
| III/4 | Rating in an engine watch | Age **16**. **6 months** of training and experience, or special training plus **2 months** of approved seagoing service |
| III/5 | Able seafarer engine | Age **18**. **12 months** of engine department service, or **6 months** plus approved training |
| III/6 | Electro-technical officer | Age **18**. **12 months** of combined workshop training and seagoing service (at least 6 months at sea in an approved programme), or **36 months** of which at least 30 in the engine department |
| III/7 | Electro-technical rating | Age **18**. One of: **12 months** of approved seagoing service with training, **6 months** at sea after approved training, or **3 months** at sea with the qualifying technical competence |

## Chapter V — Special ship types
| Regulation | Training |
|---|---|
| V/1-1 Oil and chemical tankers | **Basic:** at least **3 months** of approved seagoing service on oil or chemical tankers, or approved basic training. **Advanced:** at least **3 months** at sea on that tanker type, or at least **1 month** of supernumerary onboard training including **3 loadings and 3 unloadings** |
| V/1-2 Liquefied gas tankers | Basic and advanced training |
| V/2 Passenger ships | Crowd management, passenger safety, crisis management and human behaviour. Refresher at intervals of no more than **5 years** |
| V/3 and V/4 † | Ships under the IGF Code, and polar waters |

|---|---|---|
| VI/1 Basic training | Personal survival techniques, fire prevention and firefighting, elementary first aid, personal safety and social responsibilities | **Every 5 years** |

### 1 January 2026: violence and harassment
- **Resolution MSC.560(108)**, adopted 23 May 2024, in force **1 Jan 2026**
- It amends STCW Code **Table A-VI/1-4** (personal safety and social responsibilities) with a new mandatory competence: **contribute to the prevention of and response to violence and harassment**
- Covers harassment, bullying and sexual assault: how to recognize, report and respond, the effect on safety, health and wellbeing, and support, including trauma-informed principles
- **Example of national implementation (UK, MGN 718):** all UK-certified officers and ratings, and holders of older PSSR certificates, must complete a short module by **31 Mar 2027**. Other flag States set their own deadlines, so check with the flag

## Chapter VIII — Watchkeeping and fitness for duty
**Rest hours (Code A-VIII/1)**
| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Minimum rest | **10 hours** in any 24 hours, and **77 hours** in any 7 days |
| Division | Normally no more than **2 periods**, one of at least **6 hours**. No more than **14 hours** between rest periods |
| Records | Watch schedules posted. Rest records kept |

![[diagram:stcw-rest|Rest limits]]

**Exceptions (need a collective or workforce agreement and flag State approval)**
- Weekly rest may drop to **70 hours** in any 7 days
- The weekly exception may not run for more than **2 consecutive weeks**
- The gap between two exception periods must be at least **twice** the length of the exception
- Daily rest may be split into up to **3 periods**: one of at least **6 hours**, the other two at least **1 hour** each
- Daily rest exceptions may not extend beyond **two 24-hour periods** in any 7 days
- Rest can be overridden only for emergencies or essential work that cannot be delayed for safety, security or environmental reasons, or that could not reasonably have been foreseen. Planned pilotage or routine cargo work in port is not covered

**Alcohol**
- Limit: **0.05% blood alcohol** or **0.25 mg/l in the breath**
- The Manila amendments recommend that companies ban alcohol for **4 hours** before a watch

## Comprehensive review of STCW
| Date | Step |
|---|---|
| Apr 2022 | MSC 105 starts the review |
| Feb 2024 | Guiding principles agreed at HTW 10 |
| May 2024 | MSC 108 endorses a two-phase approach |
| Feb 2025 | Phase 1 done: more than **400 gaps** found |
| Jun 2025 | MSC 110 endorses roadmaps and moves the target to **2029 or 2030** |
| Feb and Mar 2026 | HTW 12 and a second intersessional group continue work on Chapters II and III |

- Topics include advanced training technology, cybersecurity, ballast water management, violence and harassment, mental health and gender and cultural diversity
- Phase 1 focused on Chapters II and III. Chapters IV, VI and the rest of Chapter I come later
- Adoption date is not yet set

## What PSC inspectors look at
- Valid certificates of competency, endorsements and certificates of proficiency
- Medical certificates
- Rest-hour records against the logbook, and watch schedules
- Familiarization records and the working language on board
- Alcohol limits
