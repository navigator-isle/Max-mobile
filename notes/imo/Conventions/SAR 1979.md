# SAR 1979 — International Convention on Maritime Search and Rescue

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 27 Apr 1979, Hamburg |
| In force | 22 Jun 1985 |
| Revised Annex | Adopted May 1998 · in force Jan 2000 |
| 2004 amendments | MSC.155(78), adopted 20 May 2004 · in force 1 Jul 2006 |
| Global plan | 13 SAR areas |
| Manual | IAMSAR Manual (IMO and ICAO), 3 volumes |

## What is SAR
- An international SAR plan, so that a rescue anywhere at sea is coordinated by a SAR organization, helped by neighbouring organizations when needed (IMO)
- Countries delimit their own SAR regions inside the 13 SAR areas set by IMO's Maritime Safety Committee
- Art I: Parties adopt all measures needed to give full effect to the Convention and its Annex
- The Convention has 8 Articles: I General obligations · II Other treaties and interpretation · III Amendments · IV Signature, ratification, acceptance, approval and accession · V Entry into force · VI Denunciation · VII Deposit and registration · VIII Languages

## History
- **1998 revised Annex** (adopted May 1998, in force Jan 2000): clearer government responsibilities, regional approach, better co-ordination between maritime and aeronautical SAR; RCCs to run 24 hours a day with English-speaking staff; encourages immediate entry of other States' rescue units into territorial waters for SAR; operations continue "until all reasonable hope of rescuing survivors has passed"
- **2004 amendments** (MSC.155(78)): adopted 20 May 2004 · deemed accepted 1 Jan 2006 unless more than one third of Parties objected · in force **1 Jul 2006**
- Global plan: provisional plans for all 13 areas completed when the Indian Ocean plans were finalized at a conference in Fremantle, Western Australia, in Sep 1998

## Annex — Chapter 1: Terms (1.3)
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Search (1.3.1) | An operation, normally co-ordinated by an RCC or sub-centre, to locate persons in distress |
| Rescue (1.3.2) | An operation to retrieve persons in distress and **deliver them to a place of safety** |
| SAR region (1.3.4) | An area of defined dimensions associated with an RCC |
| RCC (1.3.5) | A unit responsible for promoting efficient organization of SAR services |
| RSC (1.3.6) | A unit subordinate to an RCC |
| Uncertainty phase (1.3.11) | Uncertainty exists as to the safety of a person, vessel or other craft |
| Alert phase (1.3.12) | Apprehension exists as to the safety of a person, vessel or other craft |
| Distress phase (1.3.13) | Reasonable certainty that a person, vessel or other craft is threatened by grave and imminent danger |

## Chapter 2 — Organization and co-ordination
- 2.1.1: Parties ensure assistance is rendered to any person in distress at sea
- 2.1.2: Parties establish basic service elements — legal framework, responsible authority, resources, communications, operations, improvement processes
- 2.1.3–2.1.7: SAR regions, preferably contiguous and not overlapping, are set **by agreement among the Parties concerned**; delimitation does not prejudice State boundaries
- 2.1.10: assistance is provided to any person in distress at sea, regardless of nationality, status or the circumstances
- 2.3: RCCs operate on a **24-hour basis** with trained personnel
- 2.4: closest practicable co-ordination with aeronautical services; joint centres where practicable
- 2.5: Parties designate facilities able to take part, as SAR units

## Chapter 3 — Co-operation between States
- 3.1.1: Parties co-ordinate their SAR organizations, and with neighbouring States where necessary
- 3.1.2: unless agreed otherwise, a Party should authorize immediate entry of other Parties' rescue units into its territory or territorial sea
- 3.1.3–3.1.4: the requesting Party sends a request with full details to the other Party's RCC; the receiving authority acknowledges promptly and states conditions
- 3.1.5: agreements with neighbouring States on entry conditions
- 3.1.6: RCCs may request assistance and grant entry permissions

## Chapter 4 — Operating procedures
- 4.1 Preparatory measures: up-to-date facility data, vessel position data, operational plans, readiness
- 4.2 Information concerning emergencies: 24-hour capability to receive distress alerts
- 4.3 Initial action: a unit receiving distress information acts at once if it can help, and notifies the RCC or RSC without delay
- 4.4 Emergency phases: 4.4.1 uncertainty · 4.4.2 alert · 4.4.3 distress
- 4.5 Procedures during the phases (including unknown position)
- 4.6 Co-ordination when two or more Parties are involved
- 4.7 On-scene co-ordination: "the most capable person" is designated the on-scene co-ordinator
- 4.8 Termination and suspension: operations continue, when practicable, until all reasonable hope of rescuing survivors has passed

## Chapter 5 — Ship reporting systems
- 5.1 General · 5.2 Operational requirements · 5.3 Types of reports: **sailing plan, position report, final report** · 5.4 Use of the systems

## 2004 amendments (in force 1 Jul 2006) — place of safety
- **2.1.1**: adds a definition of "person in distress at sea" — includes people in need of assistance who reached a remote coast in an area that rescue facilities can reach only as the Annex provides
- **3.1.6.4** (new): RCCs co-operate to identify the most appropriate place(s) for disembarking people found in distress
- **3.1.9** (new): Parties co-ordinate and co-operate so that masters who provide assistance by embarking persons in distress at sea are released from their obligations with **minimum further deviation** from the voyage, provided this does not further endanger life at sea; the **Party responsible for the SAR region** in which the assistance is rendered has **primary responsibility** for this
- **4.8.5** (new): the RCC or RSC starts the process of identifying the most appropriate place(s) for disembarking persons found in distress at sea, and informs the ship and other parties concerned

## IAMSAR Manual
- Developed jointly by IMO and ICAO; three volumes: **Organization and Management · Mission Co-ordination · Mobile Facilities**

## Key Point
> The State responsible for the SAR region has primary responsibility to arrange the place of safety; the master is released with minimum further deviation
> A rescue means delivery to a place of safety (1.3.2)
> Phases: uncertainty → alert → distress
> Operations continue until all reasonable hope of rescuing survivors has passed
