**COLREG 1972 — Convention on the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea**

**What is COLREG**
- The "rules of the road" at sea: look-out, safe speed, risk of collision, action to avoid collision, steering and sailing rules, lights and shapes, sound and light signals, distress signals
- The Convention (Articles I–IX) obliges Parties to give effect to the International Regulations (Rules 1–41 + Annexes I–IV) (Art I)
- On entry into force the Regulations replaced the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea, 1960 (Art IV(5))

**History**
- Done at London 20 Oct 1972 · in force **15 Jul 1977**
- Entry into force (Art IV): 12 months after at least 15 States whose merchant fleets make up at least 65% of the world fleet (vessels of 100 GT and over) became Parties
- Art II: open for signature until 1 Jun 1973, then accession
- Amendments (Art VI): proposed → two-thirds majority in the Maritime Safety Committee → two-thirds majority in the Assembly → in force on the date set by the Assembly unless more than one-third of Parties object (tacit acceptance)

**Applies to**
- All vessels on the high seas and in all waters connected therewith navigable by seagoing vessels (Rule 1(a))
- Rule 1(b): special rules for roadsteads, harbours, rivers, lakes or inland waterways connected with the high seas must conform as closely as possible to these Rules
- Rule 1(c): a State may make special rules for additional station or signal lights, shapes or whistle signals for warships, vessels under convoy, fishing vessels fishing in a fleet — they must not be mistaken for any signal in the Rules
- Rule 1(d): traffic separation schemes may be adopted by IMO
- Rule 1(e): a vessel of special construction or purpose may comply as closely as practicable with the Rules on lights, shapes and signals, as determined by its Government

**Part A — General (Rules 1–3)**
- Rule 1 Application (above)
- Rule 2 **Responsibility**
  - (a) nothing exonerates a vessel, owner, master or crew from the consequences of neglect to comply with the Rules or neglect of any precaution required by the ordinary practice of seamen or the special circumstances
  - (b) due regard to all dangers of navigation and collision and any special circumstances, including the limitations of the vessels involved, which may make a departure necessary to avoid **immediate danger**
- Rule 3 General definitions:
  - (a) vessel: every description of water craft, including non-displacement craft, WIG craft and seaplanes, used or capable of being used as a means of transportation on water
  - (b) power-driven vessel: propelled by machinery · (c) sailing vessel: under sail provided propelling machinery, if fitted, is not being used
  - (d) vessel engaged in fishing: fishing with nets, lines, trawls or other apparatus which restrict manoeuvrability (not trolling lines or other gear that do not restrict manoeuvrability)
  - (e) seaplane: any aircraft designed to manoeuvre on the water
  - (f) vessel not under command: through some exceptional circumstance unable to manoeuvre as required by the Rules because of some exceptional circumstance
  - (g) vessel restricted in her ability to manoeuvre: restricted by the nature of her work — includes vessels laying, servicing or picking up a navigation mark, submarine cable or pipeline; dredging, surveying or underwater operations; replenishing or transferring persons, provisions or cargo while underway; launching or recovering aircraft; mine clearance; towing operations that severely restrict the towing vessel and tow in their ability to deviate from their course
  - (h) vessel constrained by her draught: a power-driven vessel which, because of her draught in relation to the available depth and width of navigable water, is severely restricted in her ability to deviate from the course she is following
  - (i) underway: not at anchor, or made fast to the shore, or aground
  - (j) length and breadth: length overall and greatest breadth
  - (k) in sight of one another: only when one can be observed visually from the other

**Part B — Steering and sailing rules**
- **Section I — Conduct of vessels in any condition of visibility (Rules 4–10)**
  - Rule 4 Application: Rules in Section I apply in any condition of visibility
  - Rule 5 **Look-out**: at all times by sight and hearing as well as by all available means appropriate in the prevailing circumstances and conditions, so as to make a full appraisal of the situation and of the risk of collision
  - Rule 6 **Safe speed**: at all times, so that proper and effective action can be taken to avoid collision and the vessel can be stopped within a distance appropriate to the circumstances
    - (a) all vessels: visibility · traffic density · manoeuvrability (stopping distance and turning ability) · at night, background light · state of wind, sea and current, and proximity of navigational hazards · draught in relation to available depth of water
    - (b) vessels with operational radar: characteristics, efficiency and limitations of the radar · limitations imposed by the radar range scale in use · effect on radar detection of sea state, weather and other sources of interference · possibility that small vessels, ice and other floating objects may not be detected at adequate range · number, location and movement of vessels detected · more exact assessment of visibility possible when radar is used to determine the range of vessels or other objects in the vicinity
  - Rule 7 **Risk of collision**
    - (a) all available means appropriate to the circumstances; if there is any doubt, such risk shall be deemed to exist
    - (b) proper use of radar equipment if fitted and operational, including long-range scanning to obtain early warning, and radar plotting or equivalent systematic observation of detected objects
    - (c) no assumptions on the basis of scanty information, especially scanty radar information
    - (d) in determining whether risk exists: (i) such risk shall be deemed to exist if the compass bearing of an approaching vessel does not appreciably change; (ii) such risk may sometimes exist even when an appreciable bearing change is evident, particularly when approaching a very large vessel or a tow or at close range
  - Rule 8 **Action to avoid collision**
    - (a) if the circumstances of the case admit, positive, made in ample time and with due regard to the observance of good seamanship (and, since 2001, in compliance with the other Rules of Part B)
    - (b) any alteration of course and/or speed large enough to be readily apparent to another vessel observing visually or by radar; avoid a succession of small alterations
    - (c) if there is sufficient sea room, alteration of course alone may be the most effective action, provided it is made in good time, is substantial and does not result in another close-quarters situation
    - (d) action should result in passing at a safe distance; its effectiveness shall be carefully checked until the other vessel is finally past and clear
    - (e) if necessary to avoid collision or allow more time to assess the situation, slacken speed or take all way off by stopping or reversing the means of propulsion
    - (f) (i) a vessel required by these Rules not to impede the passage or safe passage of another vessel shall, when required by the circumstances of the case, take early action to allow sufficient sea room for the safe passage of the other vessel; (ii) this vessel is not relieved of the obligation if approaching so as to involve risk of collision, and shall have full regard to the actions which may be required by the Rules of Part B; (iii) when her passage is not to be impeded, the vessel remains fully obliged to comply with the Rules of Part B when the two vessels are approaching one another so as to involve risk of collision
  - Rule 9 **Narrow channels**
    - (a) keep as near to the outer limit of the channel or fairway which lies on her starboard side as is safe and practicable
    - (b) a vessel of less than 20 m or a sailing vessel shall not impede the passage of a vessel which can safely navigate only within a narrow channel or fairway
    - (c) a vessel engaged in fishing shall not impede the passage of any other vessel navigating within a narrow channel or fairway
    - (d) shall not cross a narrow channel or fairway if such crossing impedes a vessel which can safely navigate only within it; the latter may use the Rule 34(d) doubt signal if in doubt about the intention of the crossing vessel
    - (e) overtaking: (i) where overtaking can take place only if the vessel to be overtaken has to take action to permit safe passing, the overtaking vessel indicates her intention by the appropriate signal of Rule 34(c)(i); the vessel to be overtaken, if in agreement, sounds the Rule 34(c)(ii) signal and takes steps to permit safe passing; if in doubt, she may sound the Rule 34(d) signals; (ii) this Rule does not relieve the overtaking vessel of her obligation under Rule 13
    - (f) a vessel nearing a bend or an area of a channel or fairway where other vessels may be obscured shall navigate with particular alertness and caution and sound the appropriate Rule 34(e) signal
    - (g) any vessel shall, if the circumstances of the case admit, avoid anchoring in a narrow channel
  - Rule 10 **Traffic separation schemes**
    - (a) applies to schemes adopted by IMO; does not relieve any vessel of her obligation under any other Rule
    - (b) a vessel using a scheme shall: (i) proceed in the appropriate traffic lane in the general direction of traffic flow for that lane; (ii) so far as practicable keep clear of a traffic separation line or separation zone; (iii) normally join or leave a lane at the termination of the lane, but when joining or leaving from either side shall do so at as small an angle to the general direction of traffic flow as practicable
    - (c) so far as practicable avoid crossing traffic lanes, but if obliged to do so shall cross as nearly as practicable at right angles to the general direction of traffic flow
    - (d) (i) a vessel shall not use an inshore traffic zone when she can safely use the appropriate lane within the adjacent scheme; vessels of less than 20 m, sailing vessels and vessels engaged in fishing may use the inshore traffic zone at all times; (ii) notwithstanding (d)(i), a vessel may use an inshore traffic zone when en route to or from a port, offshore installation or structure, pilot station or any other place within the inshore traffic zone, or to avoid immediate danger
    - (e) a vessel other than a crossing vessel or one joining or leaving a lane shall not normally enter a separation zone or cross a separation line except in cases of emergency to avoid immediate danger, or to engage in fishing within a separation zone
    - (f) a vessel navigating in areas near the terminations of schemes shall do so with particular caution
    - (g) a vessel shall so far as practicable avoid anchoring in a traffic separation scheme or in areas near its terminations
    - (h) a vessel not using a scheme shall avoid it by as wide a margin as is practicable
    - (i) a vessel engaged in fishing shall not impede the passage of any vessel following a traffic lane
    - (j) a vessel of less than 20 m or a sailing vessel shall not impede the safe passage of a power-driven vessel following a traffic lane
    - (k) a vessel restricted in her ability to manoeuvre when engaged in an operation for the maintenance of safety of navigation in a traffic separation scheme is exempted from complying with this Rule to the extent necessary to carry out the operation
    - (l) a vessel restricted in her ability to manoeuvre when engaged in an operation for the laying, servicing or picking up of a submarine cable, within a traffic separation scheme, is exempted from complying with this Rule to the extent necessary to carry out the operation
- **Section II — Conduct of vessels in sight of one another (Rules 11–18)**
  - Rule 11 Application: vessels in sight of one another
  - Rule 12 **Sailing vessels** (risk of collision): (i) wind on different sides → the vessel with the wind on her port side keeps out of the way; (ii) wind on the same side → the windward vessel keeps out of the way of the leeward vessel; (iii) a vessel with the wind on the port side that sees a vessel to windward and cannot determine whether the other has the wind on the port or starboard side keeps out of the way; windward side = the side opposite to that on which the mainsail is carried (square-rigged: opposite the largest fore-and-aft sail)
  - Rule 13 **Overtaking**
    - (a) any vessel overtaking any other shall keep out of the way of the vessel being overtaken, notwithstanding anything in Sections I and II
    - (b) a vessel is overtaking when coming up with another from a direction more than **22.5 degrees abaft her beam**, i.e. in such a position that at night she would be able to see only the sternlight of that vessel but neither of her sidelights
    - (c) when in doubt whether she is overtaking, a vessel shall assume that this is the case and act accordingly
    - (d) any subsequent alteration of the bearing does not make the overtaking vessel a crossing vessel or relieve her of the duty to keep clear until finally past and clear
  - Rule 14 **Head-on**: two power-driven vessels meeting on reciprocal or nearly reciprocal courses so as to involve risk of collision each alters course to **starboard** so that each passes on the port side of the other; exists when a vessel sees the other ahead or nearly ahead (at night: both masthead lights in a line or nearly in a line and/or both sidelights; by day: the corresponding aspect); in doubt, assume it exists
  - Rule 15 **Crossing**: two power-driven vessels crossing so as to involve risk of collision — the vessel which has the other on her own starboard side shall keep out of the way and, if the circumstances admit, avoid crossing ahead of the other vessel
  - Rule 16 **Give-way vessel**: take early and substantial action to keep well clear
  - Rule 17 **Stand-on vessel**
    - (a)(i) keep her course and speed · (ii) may take action to avoid collision by her manoeuvre alone, as soon as it becomes apparent that the vessel required to keep out of the way is not taking appropriate action
    - (b) when so close that collision cannot be avoided by the action of the give-way vessel alone, shall take such action as will best aid to avoid collision
    - (c) a power-driven vessel taking action in a crossing situation under (a)(ii) shall, if the circumstances admit, not alter course to port for a vessel on her own port side
    - (d) does not relieve the give-way vessel of her obligation to keep out of the way
  - Rule 18 **Responsibilities between vessels** (except Rules 9, 10 and 13)
    - (a) a power-driven vessel underway keeps out of the way of: not under command · restricted in ability to manoeuvre · engaged in fishing · a sailing vessel
    - (b) a sailing vessel underway keeps out of the way of: not under command · restricted in ability to manoeuvre · engaged in fishing
    - (c) a vessel engaged in fishing, when underway, shall so far as possible keep out of the way of: not under command · restricted in ability to manoeuvre
    - (d) (i) any vessel other than one not under command or restricted in her ability to manoeuvre shall, if the circumstances of the case admit, avoid impeding the safe passage of a vessel constrained by her draught, exhibiting the Rule 28 signals; (ii) a vessel constrained by her draught shall navigate with particular caution having full regard to her special condition
    - (e) a seaplane on the water shall in general keep well clear of all vessels and avoid impeding their navigation; where risk of collision exists she shall comply with the Rules of this Part
    - (f) a WIG craft: (i) when taking off, landing and in flight near the surface shall keep well clear of all other vessels and avoid impeding their navigation; (ii) when operating on the water surface shall comply with the Rules of this Part as a power-driven vessel
- **Section III — Conduct of vessels in restricted visibility (Rule 19)**
  - (a) applies to vessels not in sight of one another when navigating in or near an area of restricted visibility
  - (b) every vessel proceeds at a safe speed adapted to the prevailing circumstances and conditions of restricted visibility; a power-driven vessel shall have her engines ready for immediate manoeuvre
  - (c) due regard to the prevailing circumstances and conditions of restricted visibility when complying with Section I
  - (d) a vessel which detects by radar alone the presence of another vessel shall determine if a close-quarters situation is developing and/or risk of collision exists; if so, she shall take avoiding action in ample time; if the action consists of an alteration of course, so far as possible avoid: (i) an alteration of course to port for a vessel forward of the beam, other than for a vessel being overtaken; (ii) an alteration of course towards a vessel abeam or abaft the beam
  - (e) except where it has been determined that risk of collision does not exist, every vessel which hears apparently forward of her beam the fog signal of another vessel, or which cannot avoid a close-quarters situation with another vessel forward of her beam, shall reduce her speed to the minimum at which she can be kept on her course; she shall if necessary take all her way off and in any event navigate with extreme caution until danger of collision is over
  - There is no stand-on/give-way role between vessels not in sight of one another — each vessel acts under Rule 19

**Part C — Lights and shapes (Rules 20–31)**
- Rule 20 Application: in all weathers; lights from sunset to sunrise (no other lights unless they cannot be mistaken for the prescribed lights and do not impair them); prescribed lights, if carried, also from sunrise to sunset in restricted visibility, and may be shown in all other circumstances when considered necessary; shapes by day; lights and shapes must comply with Annex I
- Rule 21 Definitions:
  - masthead light: white, over the fore-and-aft centreline, **225°** arc, from right ahead to 22.5° abaft the beam on either side
  - sidelights: green on starboard and red on port, each **112.5°** arc, from right ahead to 22.5° abaft the beam on its side; in a vessel of less than 20 m the sidelights may be combined in one lantern on the centreline
  - sternlight: white, as near the stern as practicable, **135°** arc, 67.5° from right aft on each side
  - towing light: yellow, same characteristics as the sternlight · all-round light: 360° · flashing light: flashes at regular intervals at a frequency of **120 flashes or more per minute**
- Rule 22 **Visibility of lights** (minimum ranges)
  - Vessels of **50 m or more**: masthead 6 nm · sidelight 3 nm · sternlight 3 nm · towing light 3 nm · white, red, green or yellow all-round light 3 nm
  - Vessels of **12 m or more but less than 50 m**: masthead 5 nm (3 nm if less than 20 m) · sidelight, sternlight, towing and all-round lights 2 nm
  - Vessels of **less than 12 m**: masthead 2 nm · sidelight 1 nm · sternlight, towing and all-round lights 2 nm
  - Inconspicuous, partly submerged vessels or objects being towed: white all-round light 3 nm
- Rule 23 **Power-driven vessels underway**: masthead light forward · second masthead light abaft and higher than the forward one (vessels of less than 50 m need not, but may) · sidelights · sternlight; air-cushion vessels in non-displacement mode also an all-round flashing yellow light; WIG craft only when taking off, landing and in flight near the surface also a high-intensity all-round flashing red light; vessels of less than 12 m may show an all-round white light and sidelights instead; vessels of less than 7 m whose maximum speed does not exceed 7 knots may show an all-round white light and, if practicable, sidelights
- Rule 24 **Towing and pushing**
  - Towing astern: two masthead lights in a vertical line (**three if the tow exceeds 200 m**, measured from the stern of the towing vessel) · sidelights · sternlight · towing light above the sternlight · a diamond shape where best seen if the tow exceeds 200 m
  - Pushing ahead or towing alongside (not a composite unit): two masthead lights in a vertical line, sidelights, sternlight
  - A pusher and pushed vessel rigidly connected as a composite unit are regarded as a power-driven vessel (Rule 23 lights)
  - Towing vessel also shows the Rule 23(a)(ii) light (second masthead light)
  - Other towed vessel or object: sidelights, sternlight and (if the tow exceeds 200 m) a diamond shape
  - Inconspicuous, partly submerged tow: all-round white lights at or near each end (two more at the extremities if 25 m wide or more; additional white lights not more than 100 m apart if over 100 m long); a diamond at or near the aftermost extremity (and another forward if the tow exceeds 200 m)
  - A vessel not normally engaged in towing, towing a vessel in distress, need not show the towing lights, but must take all possible measures to indicate the relationship (e.g. by illuminating the tow line)
- Rule 25 **Sailing vessels underway and vessels under oars**: sidelights and sternlight (under 20 m may combine in one lantern at or near the masthead) · optional at or near the top of the mast two all-round lights in a vertical line, **red over green** (not with the combined lantern) · under 7 m: show the lights if practicable, otherwise have ready a white light (torch or lantern) · sailing vessel also using machinery: a cone, apex downwards, forward
- Rule 26 **Fishing vessels** (underway or at anchor)
  - Trawling: two all-round lights in a vertical line, **green over white** (or a shape of two cones apex to apex); masthead light abaft and higher than the green light (optional under 50 m); sidelights and sternlight when making way
  - Fishing other than trawling: two all-round lights in a vertical line, **red over white** (or two cones apex to apex); if outlying gear extends more than **150 m** horizontally, an all-round white light or a cone apex upward in the direction of the gear; sidelights and sternlight when making way
  - Annex II signals for fishing vessels fishing in close proximity · a vessel not fishing shows the lights for her length
- Rule 27 **Not under command / restricted in ability to manoeuvre**
  - Not under command: two all-round red lights in a vertical line (two balls); sidelights and sternlight when making way
  - Restricted in ability to manoeuvre (except mine clearance): three all-round lights in a vertical line, **red–white–red** (shapes: **ball–diamond–ball**); masthead lights, sidelights, sternlight when making way; at anchor also the Rule 30 signals
  - A towing vessel severely restricted shows the Rule 24(a) lights and the red–white–red lights / ball–diamond–ball shapes
  - Dredging or underwater operations: red–white–red lights; if an obstruction exists, two all-round red lights (or two balls) on the side of the obstruction and two all-round green lights (or two diamonds) on the side on which another vessel may pass
- Rule 28 **Vessels constrained by their draught**: may show three all-round red lights in a vertical line (or a cylinder) in addition to the Rule 23 lights
- Rule 29 **Pilot vessels** on pilotage duty: two all-round lights in a vertical line, **white over red**; sidelights and sternlight when underway; anchor lights/shape when at anchor
- Rule 30 **Anchored vessels and vessels aground**: all-round white light or one ball in the fore part, and an all-round white light at or near the stern lower than the forward one (a vessel of less than 50 m may show only one all-round white light); working lights may be used; vessels of **100 m or more must** illuminate their decks; aground: anchor lights plus two all-round red lights in a vertical line (shapes: three balls in a vertical line); vessels under 12 m aground need not show the red lights or balls; vessels under 7 m at anchor not in or near a narrow channel, fairway, anchorage or where other vessels normally navigate need not show anchor lights
- Rule 31 **Seaplanes and WIG craft** that cannot show lights and shapes with the characteristics or positions prescribed show lights and shapes as closely similar as possible

**Part D — Sound and light signals (Rules 32–37)**
- Rule 32 Definitions: **short blast** about 1 second · **prolonged blast** 4–6 seconds
- Rule 33 Equipment: vessels of 12 m or more — whistle · 20 m or more — whistle and bell · 100 m or more — whistle, bell and gong (tone and sound not to be confused with the bell) · under 12 m — not obliged, but must have some other means of making an efficient sound signal
- Rule 34 **Manoeuvring and warning signals**
  - (a) in sight, power-driven vessel underway manoeuvring: **1 short blast** = I am altering my course to starboard · **2 short** = to port · **3 short** = my engines are going astern
  - (b) optional light signals: one, two or three flashes with the same meanings; flash about 1 second, interval about 1 second, at least 10 seconds between signals; all-round white light visible at least 5 nm
  - (c) in a narrow channel or fairway: **2 prolonged + 1 short** = I intend to overtake on your starboard side · **2 prolonged + 2 short** = on your port side · agreement: **prolonged–short–prolonged–short**
  - (d) in doubt about the other vessel's intentions or actions: **at least 5 short and rapid blasts** (may be supplemented by at least 5 short and rapid flashes)
  - (e) nearing a bend or area where other vessels may be obscured: **1 prolonged blast**, answered by 1 prolonged blast by any approaching vessel within hearing
  - (f) if whistles are more than 100 m apart, only one is used for manoeuvring and warning signals
- Rule 35 **Sound signals in restricted visibility** 
  - Power-driven vessel making way: **1 prolonged blast** at intervals of not more than 2 minutes
  - Power-driven vessel underway but stopped, making no way: **2 prolonged blasts** (about 2 s between) at intervals of not more than 2 minutes
  - Not under command, restricted in ability to manoeuvre, constrained by draught, sailing, fishing, towing or pushing: **1 prolonged + 2 short** at intervals of not more than 2 minutes
  - A vessel towed (or the last vessel of a tow, if manned): **1 prolonged + 3 short**, if possible immediately after the towing vessel's signal
  - At anchor: rapid ringing of the bell for about **5 seconds** at intervals of not more than **1 minute**; vessel of **100 m or more** also gong sounded rapidly aft for about 5 seconds; may add **1 short + 1 prolonged + 1 short**
  - Aground: bell (and gong) as at anchor plus **3 separate and distinct strokes on the bell** immediately before and after the rapid ringing
  - Vessels of 12 m to under 20 m are not obliged to give the bell signals but must give another efficient sound signal at intervals of not more than 2 minutes · vessels under 12 m likewise
  - Pilot vessel on pilotage duty may also sound an identity signal of **4 short blasts**
- Rule 36 Signals to attract attention: any vessel may make light or sound signals, or direct the beam of a searchlight in the direction of the danger, not mistakable for any authorized signal or navigational aid; avoid high-intensity intermittent or revolving lights (e.g. strobe lights)
- Rule 37 **Distress signals**: a vessel in distress and requiring assistance uses or exhibits the signals in Annex IV

**Part E — Exemptions (Rule 38)**: vessels whose keel was laid before the Regulations entered into force (15 Jul 1977) — exempt for a period for lights ranges (Rule 22) and colours (Annex I Sec 7) (4 years), masthead light repositioning (Annex I Sec 3(a), Sec 2(b)), sidelights (Sec 2(g), 3(b)) and sound appliances (Annex III) (9 years); some metric repositioning of lights is permanently exempt

**Part F — Verification of compliance (Rules 39–41)** (in force 1 Jan 2016, A.1085(28))
- Rule 39 Definitions: Audit · Audit Scheme (IMO Member State Audit Scheme) · Code for Implementation (III Code, A.1070(28)) · Audit Standard
- Rule 40 Application: Parties use the III Code to discharge their obligations under the Convention
- Rule 41 Verification of compliance: every Party is subject to periodic IMO audits against the audit standard; the Secretary-General administers the scheme; Parties facilitate the audit and carry out a programme of actions to address findings; audits follow an overall schedule set by the Secretary-General

**Annexes**
- **Annex I — Positioning and technical details of lights and shapes** (per the US publication of the International Rules)
  - Forward masthead light: at least 6 m above the hull for vessels of 20 m or more (not more than 12 m); the after masthead at least 4.5 m higher than the forward · vessels of 12 m to under 20 m: masthead at least 2.5 m above the gunwale
  - Masthead lights horizontally at least half the vessel's length apart (max 100 m)
  - Sidelights not more than three-quarters of the height of the forward masthead light
  - Forward anchor light (50 m or more): at least 6 m above the hull
  - Minimum luminous intensity for visibility ranges (K = 0.8): 1 nm = 0.9 cd · 2 nm = 4.3 cd · 3 nm = 12 cd · 4 nm = 27 cd · 5 nm = 52 cd · 6 nm = 94 cd
  - Sidelights of vessels of 20 m or more need matt black inboard screens · shapes are black; a ball at least 0.6 m diameter; at least 1.5 m between shapes
- **Annex II — Additional signals for fishing vessels fishing in close proximity**: lights at least 0.9 m apart, lower than the Rule 26 lights, visible all round at least 1 nm · trawlers of 20 m or more: shooting nets = two white lights in a vertical line; hauling = white over red; net fast on an obstruction = two red lights in a vertical line · purse seiners hampered by gear: two yellow lights in a vertical line flashing alternately every second
- **Annex III — Technical details of sound signal appliances**: whistle fundamental frequency 70–700 Hz (200 m or more: 70–200 Hz; 75–200 m: 130–350 Hz; under 75 m: 250–700 Hz) · sound pressure level at 1 m: 143 dB (200 m or more), 138 dB (75–200 m), 130 dB (20–75 m) · audibility range 2 / 1.5 / 1 / 0.5 nm by length band · bell or gong: at least 110 dB at 1 m; bell mouth at least 300 mm for vessels of 20 m or more
- **Annex IV — Distress signals** (as revised by A.1004(25), in force 1 Dec 2009): (a) gun or other explosive signal fired at intervals of about a minute · (b) continuous sounding of any fog-signalling apparatus · (c) rockets or shells throwing red stars fired one at a time at short intervals · (d) Morse code SOS · (e) the spoken word "MAYDAY" by radiotelephone · (f) International Code signal N.C. · (g) a square flag with a ball (or anything resembling a ball) above or below it · (h) flames on the vessel (e.g. burning tar barrel) · (i) a rocket parachute flare or hand flare showing a red light · (j) a smoke signal giving off orange-coloured smoke · (k) slowly and repeatedly raising and lowering arms outstretched to each side · (l) a DSC distress alert on VHF channel 70, or on MF/HF · (m) a ship-to-shore distress alert transmitted by Inmarsat or another mobile satellite service ship earth station · (n) signals transmitted by EPIRBs · (o) approved signals transmitted by radiocommunication systems, including survival craft radar transponders · using these signals for any other purpose, or other signals that may be confused with them, is prohibited · aids: orange canvas with a black square and circle, and a dye marker

**Key Point**
- Rules 5–8 (look-out, safe speed, risk of collision, action to avoid collision) and 13–19 are the core of watchkeeping
- Rule 2: depart from the Rules only to avoid immediate danger; Rule 17(b): the stand-on vessel must act when collision cannot be avoided by the give-way vessel alone
- Rule 19: vessels not in sight of one another — safe speed, engines ready, radar-only detection → avoid turning to port for a vessel forward of the beam (except overtaking) and turning towards a vessel abeam or abaft the beam
