**COLREG 1972 — Convention on the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea**

**What is COLREG**
- The "rules of the road" at sea: how vessels keep a look-out, choose a safe speed, judge risk of collision and take action; plus lights, shapes and sound signals
- Applies on the high seas and all connected navigable waters
- Introduced traffic separation schemes (Rule 10) into a binding rule

**History**
- 1960 Collision Regulations replaced
- Adopted 20 Oct 1972 · in force 15 Jul 1977
- Amendments (tacit): 1981 (in force 1983), 1987 (1989), 1989 (1991), 1993 (1995), 2001 (2003 — Wing-in-Ground craft), 2007 (2009 — Annex IV distress signals revised)
- 2013 (A.1085(28)): new **Part F** — Verification of compliance (III Code audits) · in force 1 Jan 2016

**Applies to**
- All vessels on the high seas and connected waters navigable by seagoing vessels (Rule 1)
- Local special rules (harbours, rivers, lakes, TSS) allowed, but must conform as closely as possible

---

**Part A — General (Rules 1–3)**
- Rule 1 Application · Rule 2 **Responsibility**: nothing exonerates neglect of the rules or ordinary practice of seamen; departure from the rules allowed only to avoid **immediate danger**
- Rule 3 Definitions: vessel, power-driven, sailing, engaged in fishing, seaplane, WIG craft, not under command (NUC), restricted in ability to manoeuvre (RAM), constrained by draught (CBD), underway, length, in sight, restricted visibility

**Part B — Steering and sailing rules**
- **Section I — Any condition of visibility (Rules 4–10)**
  - Rule 5 **Look-out**: by sight and hearing and all available means (radar, AIS)
  - Rule 6 **Safe speed**: visibility, traffic density, manoeuvrability, background lights, wind/sea/current, draught; with radar: range scale, sea clutter, small targets
  - Rule 7 **Risk of collision**: if in doubt, risk exists; compass bearing not appreciably changing; radar plotting; avoid scanty information
  - Rule 8 **Action to avoid collision**: positive, in ample time, large enough to be readily apparent; avoid succession of small alterations; reduce speed or stop if needed; "not to impede" duty
  - Rule 9 **Narrow channels**: keep to starboard side; small, sailing, fishing vessels must not impede; overtaking signals; bends
  - Rule 10 **Traffic separation schemes**: proceed in the lane in the general direction; join/leave at the termination or at a small angle; cross at right angles (heading)
- **Section II — Vessels in sight of one another (Rules 11–18)**
  - Rule 12 Sailing vessels · Rule 13 **Overtaking** (from more than 22.5° abaft the beam; overtaking vessel keeps clear) · Rule 14 **Head-on** (both alter to starboard)
  - Rule 15 **Crossing** (vessel with the other on her starboard side keeps clear; avoid crossing ahead) · Rule 16 Give-way vessel (early, substantial action)
  - Rule 17 **Stand-on vessel**: keep course and speed; may act when it becomes apparent the give-way vessel is not acting; must act when collision cannot be avoided by give-way alone; power-driven stand-on should not alter to port for a vessel on her port side
  - Rule 18 **Responsibilities between vessels** (who keeps out of whose way): power-driven → NUC, RAM, fishing, sailing; sailing → NUC, RAM, fishing; fishing → NUC, RAM; all avoid impeding CBD; seaplanes and WIG keep clear
- **Section III — Restricted visibility (Rule 19)**
  - Safe speed, engines ready; a vessel detecting another by radar alone determines risk and takes action in ample time — avoid altering to port for a vessel forward of the beam (except overtaking) and towards a vessel abeam or abaft the beam
  - On hearing a fog signal forward of the beam: reduce to minimum steerage way, take all way off if needed

**Part C — Lights and shapes (Rules 20–31)**
- Lights from sunset to sunrise and in restricted visibility; shapes by day
- Rule 21 Definitions: masthead 225°, sidelights 112.5°, sternlight 135°, towing light, all-round, flashing (≥120 per min)
- Rule 22 Visibility (vessels ≥ 50 m): masthead **6 nm**, sidelight **3 nm**, sternlight **3 nm**, towing **3 nm**, all-round **3 nm**
- Rule 23 Power-driven underway (second masthead light if ≥ 50 m) · Rule 24 Towing and pushing (tow > 200 m: three masthead lights / diamond shape) · Rule 25 Sailing and rowing · Rule 26 Fishing (trawling green over white; other fishing red over white) · Rule 27 NUC (two all-round red; two balls) and RAM (red-white-red; ball-diamond-ball) · Rule 28 CBD (three red; cylinder) · Rule 29 Pilot (white over red) · Rule 30 Anchored (ball) and aground (two red + anchor lights; three balls) · Rule 31 Seaplanes

**Part D — Sound and light signals (Rules 32–37)**
- Rule 32 Short blast ≈ 1 s; prolonged blast 4–6 s
- Rule 33 Equipment: whistle (≥ 12 m); plus bell (≥ 20 m); plus gong (≥ 100 m); smaller vessels — other means of efficient sound signal
- Rule 34 **Manoeuvring**: 1 short = altering to starboard · 2 short = to port · 3 short = engines astern; overtaking in a narrow channel 2 long 1 short / 2 long 2 short; agreement long-short-long-short; **at least 5 short** = doubt/warning
- Rule 35 **Restricted visibility**: power-driven making way 1 prolonged ≤ 2 min; underway not making way 2 prolonged; NUC/RAM/CBD/sailing/fishing/towing 1 long 2 short; anchored rapid bell 5 s ≤ 1 min (+gong aft if ≥ 100 m); aground 3 strokes + bell + 3 strokes
- Rule 36 Signals to attract attention · Rule 37 **Distress signals** (Annex IV)

**Part E — Exemptions (Rule 38)**: older vessels (keel laid before 15 Jul 1977) — light positions
**Part F — Verification of compliance (Rules 39–41)**: definitions, application, IMO audits under the III Code

**Annexes**
- I Positioning and technical details of lights and shapes · II Additional signals for fishing vessels fishing in close proximity · III Technical details of sound signal appliances · IV Distress signals (gun, continuous fog signal, rockets, SOS, "Mayday", NC flags, square-and-ball, flames, orange smoke, arms raised and lowered, DSC, EPIRB, approved radiocommunication signals)

**Key Point**
- Rules 5–8 and 13–19 are the heart of watchkeeping (and the exam)
- Rule 2: depart from the rules only to avoid immediate danger; Rule 17: stand-on vessel must act when collision cannot be avoided by the give-way vessel alone
- In restricted visibility (Rule 19) there is no stand-on/give-way — every vessel acts
