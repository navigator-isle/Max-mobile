# MARPOL 73/78 — International Convention for the Prevention of Pollution from Ships

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| What it is | The main IMO convention against pollution from ships, both operational and accidental |
| Adopted | **2 Nov 1973**. Absorbed by the **1978 Protocol** after the 1976–77 tanker accidents |
| In force | MARPOL 73/78 on **2 Oct 1983** |
| 1997 Protocol | Added **Annex VI** (air pollution). In force **19 May 2005** |
| Annexes | **6**. Annexes I and II are compulsory. III to VI are optional for each State |
| Parties (IMO Status 2026, dated 30 Sep 2026) | Annexes I/II **162 States, about 98.89%** of world tonnage · III **153** · IV **150** · V **157** · Annex VI (1997 Protocol) **114** |
| Flag State issues | IOPP, NLS, ISPP, IAPP, EIAPP, IEE |
| Port State | Inspects (PSC) and can detain. Ships of non-Party States get no better treatment |

![[diagram:marpol-annexes|The six Annexes and when each entered into force]]

## The six Annexes at a glance
| Annex | Subject | In force | Certificate (5 years) | Size trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | Oil | 2 Oct 1983 | IOPP | Tankers 150 GT+, other ships 400 GT+ |
| II | Noxious liquid substances in bulk | Compulsory with Annex I | NLS (or IBC Code certificate) | Ships certified for NLS in bulk |
| III | Harmful substances in packages | 1 Jul 1992 | None | All ships carrying marine pollutants |
| IV | Sewage | 27 Sep 2003 | ISPP | 400 GT+, or certified for more than 15 persons |
| V | Garbage | 31 Dec 1988 | None | All ships |
| VI | Air pollution and energy efficiency | 19 May 2005 | IAPP, EIAPP, IEE | 400 GT+ (engines above 130 kW) |

> **Key Point:** MARPOL tells you what you may discharge, where, and how it must be recorded. Port State Control checks the records against the real situation: soundings, the oily water separator, the 15 ppm alarm, garbage logs, fuel samples.

## The Convention itself
- **Art 4 Violations:** prohibited anywhere. The flag State must sanction them
- **Art 5 Certificates and inspection:** Port States may inspect. Ships of non-Party States get no more favourable treatment
- **Art 6 Detection of violations**
- **Art 7 Undue delay:** a ship wrongly detained or delayed can claim compensation
- **Art 8 and Protocol I:** reports on incidents involving harmful substances
- **Art 16 Amendments:** tacit acceptance. An amendment is deemed accepted on a set date unless enough Parties object

**Who it applies to**
- Ships flying the flag of a Party, including hydrofoils, air-cushion craft, submersibles, floating craft and fixed or floating platforms
- Not warships, naval auxiliaries or State ships on non-commercial service

## Annex I — Oil
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| IOPP Certificate | Oil tankers 150 GT+, other ships 400 GT+. Valid up to 5 years |
| Sludge tank (Reg 12) | Every ship 400 GT+ must have a sludge tank of adequate capacity |
| Oil filtering equipment | Ships 400 GT+ need 15 ppm equipment. Ships 10,000 GT+ also need an alarm and **automatic stop** above 15 ppm |
| Machinery space discharge | Outside special areas, en route, oil content below **15 ppm** with the filtering equipment running |
| Oil Record Book | Part I (machinery spaces), Part II (tankers, cargo and ballast). Kept **3 years** after the last entry |
| Segregated ballast tanks | Crude tankers 20,000 dwt+ and product carriers 30,000 dwt+ |
| Double hull | Tankers 5,000 dwt+ delivered on or after **6 Jul 1996** |
| Pump-room bottom protection | Tankers 5,000 dwt+ built on or after **1 Jan 2007** |
| SOPEP | Tankers 150 GT+, other ships 400 GT+ |

**Cargo area discharge from tankers (Reg 34)**
All of these must be true together:
- The tanker is **not in a special area**
- More than **50 nm** from the nearest land
- En route
- Instantaneous rate not above **30 litres per nautical mile**
- Total quantity not above **1/30,000** of the cargo the residue came from (tankers delivered after 31 Dec 1979)
- The oil discharge monitoring and control system and the slop tank arrangement are working

**Special areas (Annex I)**
- Mediterranean, Baltic, Black Sea, Red Sea, Gulfs, Gulf of Aden, Antarctic, North West European waters, Oman area of the Arabian Sea, Southern South African waters
- **Antarctic:** all oil discharge prohibited. No heavy fuel oil carried or used

**Fuel bans and transfers †**
- **Arctic heavy fuel oil ban:** from 1 Jul 2024, with waivers possible until 1 Jul 2029 (MEPC.329(76))
- **Ship-to-ship transfer of oil:** tankers 150 GT+ need an STS Operations Plan and must notify the coastal State at least 48 hours before

## Annex II — Noxious liquid substances (NLS) in bulk
| Category | Hazard | Discharge |
|---|---|---|
| **X** | Major hazard to marine resources or human health | Prohibited |
| **Y** | Hazard | Limited in quality and quantity |
| **Z** | Minor hazard | Less strict limits |
| **OS** | Other substances, evaluated as outside X, Y and Z | Annex II does not apply |

**Operational discharge conditions (Reg 13)**
- Ship en route at **7 knots** or more (self-propelled)
- Discharge **below the waterline** through the underwater outlet
- At least **12 nm** from the nearest land
- In water at least **25 m** deep
- **Prewash:** a tank that held a Category X substance must be prewashed before the ship leaves the port of unloading

**Other rules**
- **Residue limit:** ships constructed on or after **1 Jan 2007**: no more than **75 litres** left in each tank and its piping (Categories X, Y, Z)
- **Cargo Record Book:** kept **3 years** after the last entry
- **SMPEP:** every ship 150 GT+ certified to carry NLS in bulk
- **Certificate:** valid up to 5 years. Chemical tankers built to the IBC Code carry the IBC certificate of fitness

## Annex III — Harmful substances in packaged form †
- Applies to marine pollutants identified in the IMDG Code ([[IMDG Code]])
- Packing, marking and labelling (the mark must survive 3 months in seawater), documents, stowage, quantity limits
- Shipping documents must identify the goods as **marine pollutant**
- Jettison is allowed only for the safety of the ship or life

## Annex IV — Sewage
**Applies to** ships 400 GT+ on international voyages, and ships certified for more than 15 persons.

**Sewage systems (Reg 9):** an approved sewage treatment plant, **or** an approved comminuting and disinfecting system, **or** a holding tank.

**Discharge (Reg 11)**
| Sewage | Where and how |
|---|---|
| Comminuted and disinfected | More than **3 nm** from the nearest land |
| Untreated | More than **12 nm**, ship en route at **4 knots** or more, moderate rate |
| Treated in an approved plant | No visible floating solids and no discoloration of the water |

**Baltic Sea special area (passenger ships)**
- It is the only special area under Annex IV
- Discharge is prohibited unless the ship has an approved plant that also meets nitrogen and phosphorus standards
- Dates in force: **new ships 1 Jun 2019 · existing ships 1 Jun 2021 · existing ships on direct routes to or from a port outside the area, or east of 28°10'E, 1 Jun 2023** (MEPC.275(69))
- Port States around the area must provide reception facilities (Reg 12bis)

## Annex V — Garbage
**Applies to** all ships and fixed or floating platforms. The revised Annex V has been in force since **1 Jan 2013**. The rule is a general prohibition, with listed exceptions.

| Garbage | Outside special areas | Inside special areas |
|---|---|---|
| Food waste, comminuted (mesh 25 mm or less) | More than **3 nm** | More than **12 nm** |
| Food waste, not comminuted | More than **12 nm** | **Prohibited** |
| Cargo residues not harmful to the marine environment | More than **12 nm** | **Prohibited** (washwater only, under conditions) |
| Cleaning agents in deck and hull wash water | Allowed if not harmful to the marine environment | Allowed in deck and external wash water |
| Animal carcasses | As far from land as possible, split so they sink | **Prohibited** |
| Everything else (plastics, ropes, fishing gear, bags, incinerator ash, cooking oil, paper, glass, metal) | **Prohibited** | **Prohibited** |

- Plastics are never discharged
- Mixed garbage: the stricter rule applies
- **Fixed and floating platforms (and ships within 500 m):** only comminuted food waste may go, and only more than 12 nm out
- **Antarctic:** poultry products may not be discharged unless sterilized
- **Special areas:** Mediterranean, Baltic, Black Sea, Red Sea, Gulfs, North Sea, Antarctic, Wider Caribbean

**Placards, plan and book †**
- Placards for ships 12 m or more
- Garbage Management Plan for ships 100 GT+ or certified for 15 persons or more
- Garbage Record Book for ships 400 GT+ or 15 persons or more. Ships of 100 to 399 GT also from 1 May 2024 (MEPC.360(79)). Kept **2 years**
- **Part I** (all ships): A plastics, B food waste, C domestic waste, D cooking oil, E incinerator ash, F operational waste, G animal carcasses, H fishing gear, I e-waste
- **Part II** (solid bulk cargo ships): J cargo residues (not harmful), K cargo residues (harmful to the marine environment)

## Annex VI — Air pollution and energy efficiency
| Regulation | Subject |
|---|---|
| 12 | Ozone-depleting substances |
| 13 | Nitrogen oxides (NOx) |
| 14 | Sulphur oxides (SOx) and particulate matter |
| 15 | Volatile organic compounds from tankers |
| 16 | Shipboard incineration |
| 18 | Fuel oil availability and quality |
| 19 to 25 | Energy efficiency, including attained EEDI (Reg 22) and EEXI (Reg 23) |
| 26 | SEEMP |
| 27 | Fuel oil data collection |
| 28 | Operational carbon intensity (CII) |

**Certificates:** IAPP for ships 400 GT+ on voyages to other Parties' ports. Valid up to 5 years. Annual surveys within 3 months of the anniversary date. Engines above 130 kW also need an **EIAPP** certificate.

**Ozone-depleting substances (Reg 12)**
- No new installations containing ODS on ships built on or after 19 May 2005
- No new HCFC installations on ships built on or after **1 Jan 2020**
- MEPC 84 noted work on a new rule to stop ODS being reintroduced on ships. It is not adopted

**Incineration (Reg 16)**
- PVC only in an incinerator with an IMO type approval certificate
- No waste fed in when the combustion chamber outlet temperature is below **850°C**

### Nitrogen oxides (Reg 13)
Applies to marine diesel engines **above 130 kW** (not emergency-only engines).
| Tier | Ships built | Where |
|---|---|---|
| I | On or after 1 Jan 2000, before 1 Jan 2011 | Everywhere |
| II | On or after **1 Jan 2011** | Everywhere |
| III | See the ECA table | Only inside NOx ECAs |

### Sulphur (Reg 14)
- **Global limit 0.50%** since 1 Jan 2020
- **Inside SOx ECAs 0.10%** since 1 Jan 2015 (an exhaust gas cleaning system is allowed as an alternative)
- Carrying non-compliant fuel has been banned since 1 Mar 2020, except on ships with a scrubber

### Emission Control Areas
| ECA | Pollutants | Key dates |
|---|---|---|
| Baltic Sea | SOx, NOx | NOx Tier III from 1 Jan 2021 |
| North Sea | SOx, NOx | NOx Tier III from 1 Jan 2021 |
| North American | SOx, NOx | NOx Tier III from 1 Jan 2016 |
| US Caribbean | SOx, NOx | NOx Tier III from 1 Jan 2016 |
| Mediterranean | SOx, PM only | 0.10% from **1 May 2025** |
| Canadian Arctic | SOx, PM, NOx | In force 1 Mar 2026. 0.10% from **1 Mar 2027**. NOx Tier III for ships with keel laid on or after **1 Jan 2025**, from 1 Mar 2026 |
| Norwegian Sea | SOx, PM, NOx | In force 1 Mar 2026. 0.10% from **1 Mar 2027**. NOx Tier III: contract on or after 1 Mar 2026, or keel laid on or after 1 Sep 2026, or delivery on or after 1 Mar 2030 |
| North East Atlantic | SOx, PM, NOx | Adopted at MEPC 84 as **MEPC.407(84)**. In force **1 Sep 2027**. 0.10% from **1 Sep 2028**. NOx Tier III: contract on or after 1 Jan 2027, or keel laid on or after 1 Jul 2027, or delivery on or after 1 Jan 2031 |

- Canadian Arctic and Norwegian Sea: MEPC.392(82)
- North East Atlantic area: the waters of Portugal, Spain, France, Ireland, Iceland, the Faroe Islands, Greenland and mainland UK, not counting areas already in another ECA. The Madeira, Azores and Canary Islands waters are excluded
- Exemptions in the North East Atlantic: recreational ships under 24 m, ships under 750 kW where Tier III is not feasible, and temporary relief for ships in delivery or repair

### Fuel oil quality (Reg 18)
- **Bunker Delivery Note:** kept on board **3 years** after the fuel is delivered
- **MARPOL sample:** sealed and signed by the supplier's representative and the ship's master or officer. Kept under the ship's control until the fuel is substantially consumed, and at least **12 months** from delivery
- Suppliers keep a copy of the BDN for 3 years

### Energy efficiency and carbon intensity
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| EEDI | New ships. Attained index under Reg 22 |
| EEXI | Existing ships 400 GT+. Verified at the first survey on or after **1 Jan 2023** |
| SEEMP (Reg 26) | Every ship 400 GT+ keeps one on board. Ships 5,000 GT+ must include a data collection method |
| Data collection (Reg 27) | Ships 5,000 GT+ collect fuel data from calendar year 2019. Report to the Administration within 3 months after year end |
| CII (Reg 28) | Rated **A to E**. A ship rated **D three years in a row, or E once,** must make a corrective action plan. A or B ships may get incentives |
| Enhanced DCS | From **1 Jan 2026** for existing ships 5,000 GT+ that did not start early (MEPC.385(81)). Fuel use split by consumer, underway and not underway. 2026 data to be submitted for verification by **31 Mar 2027** |
| SEEMP Part III | Ships 5,000 GT+ had to update it, with a plan for 2026 to 2028, by **31 Dec 2025** |

**CII required reduction against the 2019 reference (MEPC.400(83))**
| 2027 | 2028 | 2029 | 2030 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13.625% | 16.250% | 18.875% | 21.500% |

### What changed in 2026
- **NOx Technical Code 2008:** engine certification after substantial modification or a new tier, from **1 Sep 2026** (MEPC.398(83)). Rules for multiple engine operating profiles, for engine groups certified from **1 Jan 2028**
- **MEPC 84 (27 Apr to 1 May 2026):** adopted Annex VI amendments covering data reporting under Regs 27 and 28, the North East Atlantic ECA, access to the IMO fuel oil consumption database, and a review clause for the short-term GHG measure. Expected in force **1 Sep 2027**
- **Also at MEPC 84:** EEDI guidelines cover methanol and ethanol dual-fuel engines. Voluntary guidelines for measuring methane and nitrous oxide. The 2026 strategy on marine plastic litter keeps the goal of zero plastic discharge from ships by 2030
- **Still pending, MARPOL Annex I:** controlled dewatering of oily bilge water by forced evaporation, for final approval at MEPC 85

### IMO Net-Zero Framework (not in force)
- Approved at MEPC 83 (April 2025) as a new chapter of the draft revised Annex VI. Two measures: a fuel GHG intensity standard and a GHG pricing mechanism
- **Not adopted** at the extraordinary session MEPC/ES.2 (14 to 17 Oct 2025), which was adjourned for 12 months
- MEPC 84 made no design decisions and agreed two more intersessional meetings
- **Dates to watch:** intersessional meeting 23 to 27 Nov 2026 · **MEPC 85: 30 Nov to 3 Dec 2026** · the extraordinary session is due to resume on **4 Dec 2026**, subject to confirmation by MEPC 85

> **Key Point:** Nothing in the Net-Zero Framework applies to ships yet. Check the outcome of MEPC 85 before you tell anyone it does.

## Records and retention
![[diagram:marpol-records|How long each MARPOL record is kept]]

| Record | Annex | Kept |
|---|---|---|
| Oil Record Book Part I and II | I | 3 years |
| Cargo Record Book | II | 3 years |
| Garbage Record Book † | V | 2 years |
| Bunker Delivery Note | VI | 3 years |
| MARPOL fuel sample | VI | At least 12 months |

**What a PSC officer looks at**
- Oil Record Book entries against soundings and the oily water separator
- The 15 ppm alarm and automatic stop
- Garbage logs and the Garbage Management Plan
- Fuel sulphur, BDNs and the changeover log for ECAs
- SEEMP, CII rating and the data collection statement
- A false record or a bypass pipe ("magic pipe") is a criminal case in many ports

## Beyond the convention †
These parts were checked on 7 Nov 2026 and were not re-read today.

**United States**
- Party to Annexes I, II, III, V and VI. **Not Annex IV**
- Act to Prevent Pollution from Ships (APPS, 33 USC 1901)
- Sewage is under the Clean Water Act: an approved marine sanitation device (33 CFR 159) and no-discharge zones
- North American ECA: 200 nm from the coast
- Ballast water: the US is not a Party to BWM 2004. It has its own rules ([[BWM 2004]])
- California: distillate fuel within 24 nm, and shore power at berth

**European Union**
- 0.10% sulphur at berth in EU ports
- EU ETS for ships 5,000 GT+: allowances for 40% of 2024 emissions, 70% for 2025, 100% from 2026
- FuelEU Maritime from 1 Jan 2025: GHG intensity of energy fuel down 2% in 2025, rising to 80% by 2050
- EU MRV: CO2 monitoring, reporting and verification

**China**
- Coastal Domestic Emission Control Area: 0.50% sulphur since 1 Jan 2019
- Inland rivers: 0.10% since 1 Jan 2020. Hainan waters: 0.10% since 1 Jan 2022
- Scrubber washwater discharge banned in the coastal area, inland rivers and Bohai
