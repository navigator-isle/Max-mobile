**CLC and Fund 1992 — Civil Liability Convention and Fund Convention for Oil Pollution Damage (with the 2003 Supplementary Fund)**

**What is CLC / Fund**
- **CLC 1992**: International Convention on Civil Liability for Oil Pollution Damage — the tanker owner is strictly liable, with compulsory insurance (tier 1)
- **Fund 1992**: International Convention on the Establishment of an International Fund for Compensation for Oil Pollution Damage — the IOPC Fund pays above the owner's limit (tier 2), financed by oil receivers
- **Supplementary Fund Protocol 2003**: optional third tier
- Covers **persistent oil** spilled from **tankers** (oil carried in bulk as cargo, and bunkers of such ships)

**History**
- Torrey Canyon (1967) → CLC 1969 (in force 1975) and Fund 1971 (in force 1978)
- 1992 Protocols (higher limits, EEZ, preventive measures) adopted 27 Nov 1992 · in force 30 May 1996
- 1969/1971 regimes replaced; Fund 1971 wound up (2014)
- Limits raised 27% by 2000 amendments · in force 1 Nov 2003 (after Erika, 1999)
- Supplementary Fund Protocol adopted 16 May 2003 · in force 3 Mar 2005 (after Prestige, 2002)
- STOPIA 2006 / TOPIA 2006: voluntary P&I agreements — small tanker owners pay more, tanker owners share Supplementary Fund costs

**Applies to**
- Pollution damage in the territory, territorial sea and EEZ of a Party, and preventive measures wherever taken
- Seagoing ships constructed or adapted to carry oil in bulk as cargo (laden, or on ballast voyages after carrying oil unless free of residues)
- Not: non-persistent oil (gasoline, light diesel), bunker spills from non-tankers (→ Bunkers 2001)

---

**CLC 1992 — Tier 1 (shipowner)**
- Registered owner has **strict liability** (channelled — claims cannot be brought against crew, pilots, charterers, salvors unless they acted recklessly)
- Defences: war or exceptional natural phenomenon; wholly caused by intentional act of a third party; negligence of authorities maintaining navigational aids; claimant's own fault
- **Limits (from 1 Nov 2003)**:
  - Ship up to 5,000 GT: **4.51 million SDR**
  - 5,000–140,000 GT: 4.51 million + **631 SDR per GT** above 5,000
  - 140,000 GT and over: **89.77 million SDR**
- Owner loses the right to limit if the damage resulted from his personal act or omission with intent, or recklessly and with knowledge that damage would probably result
- **Compulsory insurance** for ships carrying **more than 2,000 tons of oil in bulk as cargo**; **CLC certificate** issued by the flag State (or a Party) carried on board; direct action against the insurer
- Claims within 3 years of the damage, not later than 6 years after the incident

**Fund 1992 — Tier 2 (IOPC Fund)**
- Pays when: damage exceeds the owner's limit; owner is exempt (defences); owner/insurer cannot pay
- Maximum per incident (incl. CLC): **203 million SDR** (from 1 Nov 2003)
- Financed by levies on persons receiving more than 150,000 tonnes of contributing oil a year by sea in Fund States
- Claims handled by the IOPC Funds Secretariat (London): clean-up, preventive measures, property damage, economic loss (fishing, tourism), reasonable environmental reinstatement

**Supplementary Fund 2003 — Tier 3**
- For States that join: total available per incident **750 million SDR** (incl. CLC and Fund)

**Key Point**
- Tankers carrying > 2,000 t oil cargo carry the CLC certificate (and Bunkers certificate for their fuel is not needed — CLC covers laden tankers' bunkers)
- Philippines is a Party to CLC 1992 and Fund 1992
- Persistent oil = crude, fuel oil, heavy diesel, lube oil; gasoline/kerosene are non-persistent (not covered)
