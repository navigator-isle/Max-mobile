**CLC and Fund 1992 (Oil Pollution Liability and Compensation)**

**Status**
- Dates and structure match what I know; limit figures from memory, to be verified

**What is CLC and Fund**
- CLC: International Convention on Civil Liability for Oil Pollution Damage, 1992
- Fund: International Convention on the Establishment of an International Fund for Compensation for Oil Pollution Damage, 1992
- Two tiers: shipowner pays first, the Fund pays above the shipowner's limit

**History**
- CLC 1969 and Fund 1971 adopted after the Torrey Canyon (1967)
- 1992 Protocols replaced them; in force 30 May 1996
- Limits raised in 2000 (in force 1 Nov 2003)
- 2003 Supplementary Fund Protocol: a third tier

**Applies to**
- Persistent oil pollution damage from tankers carrying oil in bulk as cargo (and bunkers of laden tankers)
- Damage in the territory, territorial sea and EEZ of a Party

**CLC key points**
- Registered owner has strict liability, with few defences (war, natural disaster, third-party intent, negligence of authorities)
- Compulsory insurance for ships carrying more than 2,000 tons of oil in bulk
- Certificate issued by flag State; direct action against the insurer
- Limit of liability depends on ship's GT (limits from 1 Nov 2003, to verify against the IMO text):
  - 4.51 million SDR for ships up to 5,000 GT
  - plus 631 SDR per GT above 5,000 GT
  - maximum 89.77 million SDR
- Fund total per incident (shipowner + Fund): 203 million SDR; Supplementary Fund (2003): 750 million SDR

**Fund key points**
- Pays when the shipowner is exempt, financially unable, or the damage exceeds the CLC limit
- Funded by levies on oil receivers in member States

**Key Point**
- Certificate of insurance for CLC must be carried; PSC checks it
- Bunker oil from non-tankers is covered by the Bunkers Convention, not CLC
