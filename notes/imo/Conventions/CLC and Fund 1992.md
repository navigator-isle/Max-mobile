# CLC and Fund 1992 — Civil Liability Convention and Fund Convention for Oil Pollution Damage (with the 2003 Supplementary Fund)

## At a glance
| Tier | Pays | Limit per incident (SDR) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — CLC 1992 | Shipowner (insured) | 4.51 million up to 5,000 GT; +631 per GT above; max 89.77 million |
| 2 — Fund 1992 | Oil receivers | 203 million in total (including CLC) |
| 3 — Supplementary Fund 2003 | Oil receivers (optional) | 750 million in total |

## What is CLC / Fund
- **CLC 1992**: International Convention on Civil Liability for Oil Pollution Damage — the shipowner is liable for pollution damage, with compulsory insurance (tier 1)
- **Fund 1992**: International Convention on the Establishment of an International Fund for Compensation for Oil Pollution Damage — pays where full compensation cannot be obtained under the CLC (tier 2); financed by contributions from receivers of oil
- **Supplementary Fund Protocol 2003**: optional third tier
- Covers pollution damage from **persistent oil** carried by ships constructed or adapted to carry oil in bulk — laden or unladen, including the bunker oil of such ships

## History
- CLC 1969: adopted 29 Nov 1969 · in force 19 Jun 1975
- Fund 1971: adopted 18 Dec 1971 · in force 16 Oct 1978
- 1992 Protocols (CLC and Fund): adopted 27 Nov 1992 · in force 30 May 1996
  - Extended coverage to the EEZ · preventive measures covered even without a spill, where there is a grave and imminent threat of pollution damage · environmental damage limited to the reasonable cost of reinstatement
  - States Parties to the 1992 CLC ceased to be Parties to CLC 1969 from 16 May 1998
  - CLC 1992 entry into force: 12 months after 10 States, including four each with at least 1 million units of gross tanker tonnage, deposited instruments (Protocol Art 13)
- Original 1992 limits: CLC 3 million SDR (up to 5,000 GT) + 420 SDR per extra GT, max 59.7 million SDR · Fund max 135 million SDR per incident (including CLC)
- 2000 amendments raised the limits by about 50% (see below) · apply to incidents on or after 1 Nov 2003
- 2000 Protocol (adopted 27 Sep 2000, in force 27 Jun 2001) to end the Fund 1971 · Fund 1971 ceased to be in force 24 May 2002
- Supplementary Fund Protocol: adopted 16 May 2003 · in force 3 Mar 2005

## Applies to
- Pollution damage in the territory, territorial sea and EEZ of a Contracting State, and preventive measures wherever taken (CLC Art II)
- "Oil" = any persistent hydrocarbon mineral oil such as crude oil, fuel oil, heavy diesel oil and lubricating oil, whether carried as cargo or in the bunkers of such a ship (CLC Art I)

## CLC 1992 — Tier 1 (shipowner)
- The owner is liable for pollution damage caused by the ship as a result of the incident (Art III)
- Exceptions: act of war or hostilities (and similar) · wholly caused by a third party's act done with intent to cause damage · wholly caused by the negligence or wrongful act of a government or authority responsible for maintaining lights or navigational aids
- Channelling: no claim against the owner except under the Convention; no claim against listed persons such as crew, charterers and salvors, unless the damage resulted from their personal act or omission with intent, or recklessly and with knowledge that damage would probably result
- **Limits (incidents from 1 Nov 2003)**:
  - Ship up to 5,000 GT: **4.51 million SDR**
  - Above 5,000 GT: 4.51 million + **631 SDR per additional GT**
  - Maximum: **89.77 million SDR**
- The owner loses the right to limit if the damage resulted from his personal act or omission, committed with intent to cause such damage, or recklessly and with knowledge that such damage would probably result (Art V(2))
- **Compulsory insurance** for ships carrying **more than 2,000 tons of oil in bulk as cargo** (Art VII) · claims can be brought **directly against the insurer** or other provider of financial security (Art VII(8))
- Time limits (Art VIII): action within 3 years from the date the damage occurred; never later than 6 years from the incident

## Fund 1992 — Tier 2 (IOPC Fund)
- Aim (Art 2): compensation to the extent that the protection under the CLC is inadequate
- Pays any person suffering pollution damage where full compensation cannot be obtained under the CLC (Art 4)
- Maximum per incident (including CLC): **203 million SDR**; a higher cap of 300.74 million SDR applies in certain conditions set in the Convention
- Contributions (Art 10): paid by persons who received in total more than 150,000 tons of contributing oil (crude oil and fuel oil received after sea transport)

## Supplementary Fund 2003 — Tier 3
- Maximum payable for any one incident: **750 million SDR**, including amounts paid under the 1992 CLC and Fund

## Key Point
> Tankers carrying more than 2,000 tons of oil in bulk as cargo must have CLC insurance; the CLC covers the tanker's bunker oil as well as cargo oil
> Three tiers per incident: CLC owner limit (max 89.77 million SDR) → Fund 1992 (203 million SDR total) → Supplementary Fund (750 million SDR total)
> Persistent oil (crude, fuel oil, heavy diesel, lubricating oil) is covered
