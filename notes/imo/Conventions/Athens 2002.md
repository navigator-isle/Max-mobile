# Athens Convention 2002 (PAL)

**Athens Convention relating to the Carriage of Passengers and their Luggage by Sea, 1974, as amended by the 2002 Protocol**

## At a glance

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Subject | Carrier's liability for passengers' death or injury and for luggage |
| Convention | Adopted 13 Dec 1974 · in force 28 Apr 1987 |
| 2002 Protocol | Adopted 1 Nov 2002 · in force 23 Apr 2014 |
| Main changes in 2002 | Strict liability for shipping incidents, higher limits, compulsory insurance |
| Unit of account | SDR |

## History

- The 1990 Protocol never entered into force.
- A State that ratifies the 2002 Protocol must denounce the 1974 Convention and its 1976 and 1990 Protocols, if bound by them.
- The Protocol lets States keep or set higher limits for carriers under their jurisdiction (Art 7).
- **IMO Reservation and Guidelines (Oct 2006):** adopted by the Legal Committee at its 92nd session to help States ratify. A State may limit war and terrorism liability to 250,000 SDR per passenger per occasion, or 340 million SDR per ship per occasion. The Guidelines say war cover should include war-related acts, capture or seizure, derelict weapons, terrorist or malicious acts, and confiscation or expropriation.

## Application (Art 2)

Applies to international carriage when:
- the ship flies the flag of, or is registered in, a State Party; or
- the contract of carriage was made in a State Party; or
- the place of departure or destination is in a State Party.

It does not apply where another convention's mandatory civil liability regime for a different transport mode governs the carriage.

## Key definitions (Art 1)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Carrier | A person by or on behalf of whom a contract of carriage was concluded |
| Performing carrier | A person other than the carrier (owner, charterer or operator) who actually performs all or part of the carriage |
| Ship | A seagoing vessel only, not an air-cushion vehicle |
| Cabin luggage | Luggage in the passenger's cabin, or otherwise in the passenger's possession, custody or control |
| International carriage | Departure and destination in two different States, or in one State with a scheduled intermediate port of call in another |
| Shipping incident | Shipwreck, capsizing, collision or stranding; explosion or fire in the ship; or a defect in the ship |

## Liability for death and personal injury

![[diagram:athens-liability|Death and injury: liability tiers and compulsory insurance (Arts 3, 4bis, 7)]]

| Case | Rule |
|---|---|
| Shipping incident, up to 250,000 SDR per passenger per occasion | Carrier liable; it avoids this only by proving war, hostilities, civil war, insurrection, an exceptional natural phenomenon, or that a third party wholly caused it by an act or omission done with intent to cause the incident |
| Shipping incident, above 250,000 SDR | Carrier liable unless it proves it was without fault or neglect |
| Not a shipping incident | Carrier liable only for fault or neglect; the claimant must prove it |
| Overall limit (Art 7) | 400,000 SDR per passenger per distinct occasion |

- A State Party may set a higher national limit, not lower than 400,000 SDR, and must inform the Secretary-General.
- Contributory fault (Art 6): if the carrier proves the passenger's fault or neglect caused or contributed to the harm, the court may exonerate the carrier wholly or partly under its own law.

## Luggage and vehicles (Art 8, per carriage)

| Item | Limit (SDR) |
|---|---|
| Cabin luggage, per passenger | 2,250 |
| Vehicle, including luggage in or on it | 12,700 |
| Other luggage, per passenger | 3,375 |

- **Deductible:** the carrier and passenger may agree a deductible of up to 330 SDR for vehicle damage, and up to 149 SDR per passenger for other luggage.
- **Valuables (Art 5):** money, negotiable securities, gold, silverware, jewellery, works of art and other valuables are covered only if deposited with the carrier for safekeeping, and then up to the Art 8(3) limit unless a higher limit is agreed.
- Cabin luggage: liability needs fault, and fault is presumed for shipping incidents. Other luggage: carrier liable unless it proves it was without fault.

## Compulsory insurance (Art 4bis)

| Point | Rule |
|---|---|
| Which ships | Registered in a State Party and licensed to carry more than 12 passengers |
| Cover | Death and personal injury, at least 250,000 SDR per passenger per occasion |
| Certificate | Issued after the authority confirms compliance; shows ship, carrier, IMO number, type and duration of security, insurer and validity; **carried on board** |
| Termination | Security cannot end early until 3 months after notice is given to the authorities |
| Direct action | Claims may be brought directly against the insurer; the 250,000 SDR limit applies even if the carrier cannot limit liability |
| Insurer's defences | May use the carrier's defences (except bankruptcy or winding up) and the assured's wilful misconduct |
| State duties | A flag State may not let a ship operate without a certificate; each Party must ensure insurance for such ships entering or leaving its ports |

## Time limits and jurisdiction

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Time bar (Art 16) | 2 years |
| Starts | Injury: disembarkation. Death during carriage: when the passenger should have disembarked. Luggage: disembarkation, or when it should have occurred, whichever is later |
| Outer limit | 5 years from disembarkation; or 3 years from when the claimant knew, or should have known, of the injury, loss or damage, if earlier |
| Extension | By the carrier's written declaration or a written agreement made after the cause of action arose |
| Jurisdiction (Art 17) | Claimant's choice among: defendant's residence or principal place of business; departure or destination; claimant's residence (if defendant does business there); place of contract (same condition). After the incident the parties may agree another court or arbitration |

> **Key Point:** Strict liability for shipping incidents up to 250,000 SDR per passenger; up to 400,000 SDR unless the carrier proves no fault. Ships with more than 12 passengers must carry the insurance certificate on board. Claims can arrive up to 2 years later (5 at most), so record every passenger injury.
