# Nuclear 1971 — Convention relating to Civil Liability in the Field of Maritime Carriage of Nuclear Material

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted | 17 Dec 1971, Brussels |
| In force | **15 Jul 1975** |
| Parties | 18 States, 20.58% of world tonnage (IMO treaty table, printed 5 Sep 2023) |
| Convened by | IMO, with the IAEA and the OECD's European Nuclear Energy Agency |
| Purpose | Settle overlaps between maritime liability conventions and nuclear liability conventions, so the **nuclear installation operator** alone is liable for damage from a nuclear incident during maritime carriage |
| Entry into force rule | 90th day after 5 States sign without reservation or deposit instruments (Art 6) |
| Depositary | IMO Secretary-General |
| Languages | English and French equally authentic; Russian and Spanish official translations |

![[diagram:nuclear-flow|Who is liable for nuclear damage at sea]]

> **Key Point:** If the nuclear installation operator is liable for the damage under the Paris Convention, the Vienna Convention or equivalent national law, no one else, such as the shipowner or carrier, is liable under maritime or other law.

## What is Nuclear 1971
- Nuclear damage during a sea voyage could fall under maritime conventions covering shipowners and also under conventions making the operators of the nuclear installations involved liable. The two systems could conflict (IMO).
- The preamble recalls the **Paris Convention** (29 Jul 1960, with its 1964 Additional Protocol) and the **Vienna Convention** (21 May 1963)
- The Convention contains no definitions of "nuclear incident" or "nuclear material" of its own in the operative articles checked. Those concepts come from the nuclear liability conventions it refers to.

## Articles
| Article | Content |
|---|---|
| Preamble | Recalls Paris 1960 and Vienna 1963. Aims to ensure that the operator "will be exclusively liable" for damage from a nuclear incident in maritime carriage. |
| **1 Exoneration** | A person who might otherwise be liable under maritime or national law is **exonerated** if: (a) the operator of the nuclear installation is liable under the Paris or Vienna Convention, **or** (b) the operator is liable under national law that is "in all respects as favourable" to injured persons as either convention |
| **2 Scope of exoneration** | (1) Extends to damage to the nuclear installation, to property on its site, and to the means of transport carrying the material, where the operator's liability for them is excluded under those conventions · (2) liability of an individual who **caused damage intentionally** is preserved |
| **3 Nuclear ships** | Does not affect the liability of the operator of a **nuclear ship** for incidents involving nuclear fuel, products or waste on that ship |
| **4 Conflicts** | Supersedes earlier maritime conventions only to the extent of conflict. Obligations to non-Contracting States under those conventions are unaffected. |
| 5 Signature and accession | Open for signature in Brussels, then in London at the IMCO headquarters until 31 Dec 1972, then accession. Open to UN members, members of specialized agencies or the IAEA, and parties to the ICJ Statute. |
| 6 Entry into force | 90th day after 5 States have signed without reservation or deposited instruments · for later States, 90 days after signature or deposit |
| 7 Denunciation | At any time after entry into force for that State, effective one year after receipt (or longer if stated) · the Convention still applies to incidents before denunciation takes effect |
| 8 Territories | Extension to territories by notice, withdrawable with one year's notice |
| 9 Revision | IMO may convene a revision conference, and must on request of at least one-third of Contracting Parties |
| 10 Reservations | Allowed if they correspond to those validly made under the Paris or Vienna Convention |
| 11 Depositary | Notifications, certified copies, registration with the UN under Art 102 of the UN Charter |
| 12 Languages | English and French; Russian and Spanish translations prepared by the Secretariat |

## How to read it in practice
1. Identify the nuclear installation operator liable under Paris, Vienna or equivalent national law for the carriage.
2. If that operator is liable for the nuclear damage, the shipowner or carrier cannot be held liable for it (Art 1).
3. The exoneration also covers damage to the nuclear installation and its site property, and to the means of transport (Art 2(1)).
4. It does **not** protect a person who caused the damage **intentionally** (Art 2(2)), and it does **not** change the position of a **nuclear ship** operator for its own fuel, products or waste (Art 3).
