# Torremolinos 1977 — International Convention for the Safety of Fishing Vessels (and the 1993 Protocol)

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Convention | Adopted 2 Apr 1977, Torremolinos, Spain · **never entered into force** |
| 1993 Protocol | Adopted Apr 1993 (done at Torremolinos, 2 Apr 1993) · **not in force** |
| Replaced by | Cape Town Agreement 2012 (see [[Cape Town Agreement 2012]]) |
| Applies to | New, decked, seagoing fishing vessels of **24 m in length and over**, including those that process their catch |
| Why it matters | The first international convention on the safety of fishing vessels. Its Annex is the technical basis of today's rules. |
| Depositary | IMO Secretary-General |

![[diagram:tor-path|From 1977 to the Cape Town Agreement]]

> **Key Point:** Neither the 1977 Convention nor the 1993 Protocol is in force. The rules live on through the Cape Town Agreement 2012, which brings the Protocol's technical rules into force. Conference Resolution 2 says the Agreement "replaces and supersedes" the 1993 Protocol, and States should become Parties to the Agreement instead.

## What is Torremolinos 1977
- Set safety requirements for the construction and equipment of those vessels
- Recognised that fishing vessels differ from other ships: they sail empty and load their catch at sea
- Existing vessels were covered only for radio requirements (IAPH summary of the 1977 text)
- Subject areas: stability · construction, watertight integrity and equipment · machinery, electrical installations and unattended machinery spaces · fire protection · protection of the crew · life-saving appliances · emergency procedures, musters and drills · radiocommunications · shipborne navigational equipment
- Port State officers verify that a valid certificate is on board (Art 4)

## Why it never entered into force
- Entry into force required at least 15 States with fleets of at least 50% of the world's 24 m and over fishing fleet by number, plus 12 months (IAPH summary of Art 10)
- In the 1980s it became clear that it was unlikely to enter into force, largely for technical reasons (IMO)
- IMO therefore prepared the **1993 Protocol** to update, amend and absorb the 1977 Convention

## 1993 Protocol — Articles
| Article | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 General obligations | Parties give effect to the Protocol and to the regulations in the Annex of the 1977 Convention, as modified. The two are read as **one instrument**. |
| 2 Definitions | "Fishing vessel": any vessel used commercially for catching fish, whales, seals, walrus or other living resources of the sea |
| 3 Application | Seagoing vessels flying a Party's flag (3(1)) · **excluded**: vessels used only for sport or recreation, fish processing, research and training, or as fish carriers (3(2)) · the Annex applies to vessels of **24 m and over** unless stated otherwise (3(3)) · where a chapter sets a longer limit, the Administration decides which regulations apply to vessels from 24 m up to that limit (3(4)) · Parties seek uniform regional standards (3(5)) |
| 4 Certification and port State control | Port officers may verify certificate validity. A valid certificate is accepted unless there are clear grounds to doubt it. A vessel with an invalid certificate may be held in port. A vessel unduly detained is entitled to compensation (4(6)). |
| 5 Force majeure | Deviation due to weather or force majeure does not bring a vessel under the Protocol. Persons aboard for force majeure or rescue are not counted. |
| 6 Information | Parties send laws, lists of authorized bodies and certificate specimens to IMO |
| 7 Casualties | Parties may investigate casualties and share findings with IMO, without identifying vessels or assigning fault |
| 8 Other treaties | No prejudice to legal views on the law of the sea or on coastal and flag State jurisdiction |
| 9 Signature and accession | Open 1 Jul 1993 to 30 Jun 1994, then accession. Parties report their fleet counts of 24 m and over vessels. |
| 10 Entry into force | 12 months after **not less than 15 States** whose 24 m and over fishing vessels total **not less than 14,000** are Parties. Later ratifications take effect 3 months after deposit. |
| 11 Amendments | IMO route: articles need two-thirds of Parties. Annex amendments are deemed accepted after 2 years from adoption, or another period of at least 1 year, unless more than one-third of Parties, or Parties with 65% of the fleet, object. Conference route also possible. Structural amendments apply only to vessels whose keel is laid or whose construction begins on or after entry into force (11(5)). |
| 12 Denunciation | After 5 years from a Party's entry into force, effective 12 months after notice |
| 13–14 | Depositary: the IMO Secretary-General · languages: Arabic, Chinese, English, French, Russian and Spanish, all equally authentic |

## The Annex — ten chapters
![[diagram:tor-chapters|Annex chapters and their length thresholds]]

The Annex of the 1977 Convention is the technical core. As carried into the Cape Town Agreement, it has ten chapters:

| Ch | Title | Main application rule |
|---|---|---|
| I | General provisions | New vessels (Reg 1) |
| II | Construction, watertight integrity and equipment | Length limits in individual regulations (45 m, 75 m) |
| III | Stability and associated seaworthiness | No general length limit |
| IV | Machinery and electrical installations and periodically unattended machinery spaces | 45 m and over |
| V | Fire protection, fire detection, fire extinction and fire fighting | New vessels 45 m and over |
| VI | Protection of the crew | See individual regulations |
| VII | Life-saving appliances and arrangements | New vessels 45 m and over |
| VIII | Emergency procedures, musters and drills | New and existing vessels 24 m and over |
| IX | Radiocommunications | New and existing vessels 45 m and over |
| X | Shipborne navigational equipment and arrangements | New and existing vessels; each regulation sets its own limit |

### Chapter I — General provisions (Regulations 1–5)
- **Reg 1 Application:** the Annex applies to new vessels unless stated otherwise. The Administration may use gross tonnage instead of length (L) for all chapters, with **300 GT equal to 24 m**. Existing vessels may be brought into compliance progressively under a plan: up to **10 years** for chapter IX and up to **5 years** for chapters VII, VIII and X. The Administration may also exempt a vessel from annual surveys.
- **Reg 2 Definitions:** new vessel, existing vessel, length (L), breadth, moulded depth, superstructure, weathertight, watertight, collision bulkhead, gross tonnage, anniversary date. Collision bulkhead position: for vessels of 45 m and over, not less than 0.05L and not more than 0.08L from the forward perpendicular. For vessels under 45 m, within 0.05L plus 1.35 m, except as the Administration allows.
- **Reg 3 Exemptions:** for vessels with novel features, and for certain vessels operating in specified zones, subject to safety requirements
- **Reg 4 Equivalents:** alternatives at least as effective as those required
- **Reg 5 Repairs, alterations and modifications:** the vessel must keep meeting at least its previously applicable requirements. Major changes meet new-vessel requirements as far as the Administration finds reasonable and practicable.

## Where the rules are enforced today
- See [[Cape Town Agreement 2012]]: adopted 11 Oct 2012, enters into force in February 2027, the International Fishing Vessel Safety Certificate (up to 5 years), surveys and bottom inspections
- See [[STCW-F 1995]]: training and certification of fishing vessel personnel
