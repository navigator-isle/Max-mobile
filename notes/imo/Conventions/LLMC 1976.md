# LLMC 1976 — Convention on Limitation of Liability for Maritime Claims (with the 1996 Protocol and the 2012 limits)

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1976 Convention | Adopted 19 Nov 1976 · in force 1 Dec 1986 |
| 1996 Protocol | Adopted 2 May 1996 · in force 13 May 2004 |
| 2012 limits | Adopted 19 Apr 2012 · in force 8 Jun 2015 |
| Who can limit | Shipowners, salvors and their insurers |
| Right to limit lost only if | Intent, or reckless and knowing that loss would probably result (Art 4) |

## What is LLMC
- Lets shipowners and salvors limit their total liability for most maritime claims from one occasion to an amount based on the ship's gross tonnage
- IMO describes the limitation as "virtually unbreakable"
- Replaced the 1957 Brussels Convention (in force 1968)
- The 1976 Convention raised limits by up to 250–300% (IMO)
- The 1996 Protocol raised the amounts again and added a tacit-acceptance procedure to update them
- The 2012 amendments raised the 1996 limits further

## Who may limit (Art 1)
- Shipowners and salvors (Art 1(1))
- **Shipowner** = owner, charterer, manager and operator of a seagoing ship (Art 1(2))
- **Salvor** = any person rendering services in direct connection with salvage operations (Art 1(3))
- People for whose acts the shipowner or salvor is responsible may also limit (Art 1(4))
- Liability includes an action brought against the vessel herself (Art 1(5))
- Insurers get the same benefit as the insured (Art 1(6))
- Invoking limitation is not an admission of liability (Art 1(7))

## Claims subject to limitation (Art 2)
- (a) Loss of life or personal injury, or loss of or damage to property (including harbour works, basins, waterways and aids to navigation)
- (b) Loss from delay in carriage by sea of cargo, passengers or their luggage
- (c) Other loss from infringement of rights other than contractual rights
- (d) Raising, removal, destruction or rendering harmless of a ship (wreck)
- (e) Removal, destruction or rendering harmless of the ship's cargo
- (f) Claims of a person other than the person liable, for measures taken to avert or minimize loss
- Art 2(2): these claims are limited even if brought by way of recourse; but (d), (e) and (f) do not apply to the extent they relate to remuneration under a contract with the person liable
- Applies whatever the basis of liability

## Claims not limited (Art 3, as amended by the 1996 Protocol)
- (a) Salvage claims (including special compensation under the 1989 Salvage Convention) and general average contributions
- (b) Oil pollution damage under the 1969 CLC
- (c) Claims subject to any international convention or national law governing or prohibiting limitation for nuclear damage
- (d) Claims against the shipowner of a nuclear ship for nuclear damage
- (e) Claims by servants of the shipowner or salvor, whose duties are connected with the ship or salvage, where the law governing the contract of service does not allow limitation or sets a higher limit

## Conduct barring limitation (Art 4)
> A person is not entitled to limit if it is proved that the loss resulted from his personal act or omission, committed with the intent to cause such loss, or recklessly and with knowledge that such loss would probably result

## Limits of liability (SDR)
**Loss of life and personal injury**

|---|---|---|---|
| Up to 2,000 GT | 333,000 (ships up to 500 tons) + tonnage amounts | 2 million | **3.02 million** |
| 2,001–30,000 GT | | + 800 per ton | **+ 1,208 per ton** |
| 30,001–70,000 GT | | + 600 per ton | **+ 906 per ton** |
| Over 70,000 GT | | + 400 per ton | **+ 604 per ton** |

**Other claims (property)**
| Ship size | 1976 | 1996 Protocol | 2012 (in force 8 Jun 2015) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Up to 2,000 GT | 167,000 (ships up to 500 tons) + tonnage amounts | 1 million | **1.51 million** |
| 2,001–30,000 GT | | + 400 per ton | **+ 604 per ton** |
| 30,001–70,000 GT | | + 300 per ton | **+ 453 per ton** |
| Over 70,000 GT | | + 200 per ton | **+ 302 per ton** |

- **Passengers (Art 7(1), 1996 Protocol)**: 175,000 units of account × the number of passengers the ship is certified to carry (1976 text: 46,666 per passenger, maximum 25 million)
- Example, 30,000 GT ship under the 2012 limits: personal claims = 3,020,000 + 28,000 × 1,208 = 36,844,000 SDR · property claims = 1,510,000 + 28,000 × 604 = 18,422,000 SDR
- Unit of account (Art 8): the Special Drawing Right of the IMF, converted to national currency on the date the fund is constituted, paid or secured
- Art 9: limits apply to the total of all claims from one distinct occasion

## Updating the limits (1996 Protocol Art 8, tacit acceptance)
- A proposed amendment of the limits is deemed accepted 18 months after notification, unless at least one-fourth of the Contracting States object
- Limits may not be raised by more than 6% a year (compound), or above three times the original amount
- No amendment may be considered within five years of the relevant date set in the Protocol

## Fund, procedure and scope
- **Limitation without a fund (Art 10)**: limitation can be invoked without constituting a fund; a State may require one
- **Constituting the fund (Art 11)**: any person alleged liable may constitute a fund with the court, as a deposit or acceptable guarantee, including interest from the date of the occurrence; a fund constituted by one liable person or their insurer is deemed constituted by all
- **Distribution (Art 12)**: in proportion to established claims
- **Bar to other actions (Art 13)**: a claimant against the fund cannot act against other assets of the fund holder; an arrested ship or security is released once the fund exists in the listed places
- **Governing law (Art 14)**: the law of the State where the fund is constituted
- **Counterclaims (Art 5)**: claims from the same occurrence are set off; the Convention applies only to the balance
- **Scope (Art 15)**: applies when limitation is sought before a court of a State Party. States may regulate inland waterway vessels and ships of less than 300 tons by national law. It does not apply to air-cushion vehicles or to floating platforms built to explore or exploit sea-bed resources
- **Reservations (Art 18, as amended 1996)**: a State may exclude Art 2(1)(d) and (e) (wreck and cargo removal) and claims under the 1996 HNS Convention; no other reservation to the substantive provisions

## Entry into force
- 1976 Convention (Art 17): first day of the month after one year, after 12 States signed without reservation or deposited instruments → 1 Dec 1986
- 1996 Protocol: open for signature 1 Oct 1996 – 30 Sep 1997; in force 90 days after 10 States consented → 13 May 2004
- Protocol Art 12: denunciation takes effect 12 months after the instrument is deposited

## Key Point
> The 2012 limits (in force 8 Jun 2015) are the current LLMC 1996 limits
> Wreck removal claims are limited unless the State used its Art 18 reservation — see Nairobi WRC 2007
> Oil pollution under CLC is outside LLMC (Art 3(b))
> Limitation depends on the facts of conduct — report incidents fully to the P&I Club
