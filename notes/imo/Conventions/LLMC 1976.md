**LLMC 1976 — Convention on Limitation of Liability for Maritime Claims (with the 1996 Protocol and 2012 limits)**

**What is LLMC**
- Lets shipowners and salvors limit their total liability for most maritime claims from one incident to an amount based on the ship's gross tonnage
- Keeps shipping insurable: P&I cover is built around these limits
- The right to limit is lost only by the liable person's own intentional or reckless conduct

**History**
- 1924 and 1957 conventions before it
- Adopted 19 Nov 1976 (London) · in force 1 Dec 1986
- **1996 Protocol**: about 2.5× higher limits, passenger limit 175,000 SDR × certified passengers, tacit amendment of limits · in force 13 May 2004
- **2012 amendments** (LEG.5(99)): limits raised about 51% · in force 8 Jun 2015

**Applies to**
- Shipowners (owner, charterer, manager, operator), salvors, and their insurers, when limitation is sought in a court of a Party

---

**Claims subject to limitation (Art 2)**
- Loss of life, personal injury, loss of or damage to property (including harbour works, basins, waterways) in connection with the operation of the ship or salvage
- Delay in carriage of cargo, passengers or luggage
- Infringement of rights other than contractual
- Raising, removal or destruction of a wreck or cargo (States may reserve this)
- Measures to avert or minimize loss

**Claims excepted (Art 3)**
- Salvage and general average contribution claims · oil pollution under CLC · nuclear damage · claims by servants of the owner/salvor where their contract law excludes limits

**Conduct barring limitation (Art 4)**
- Loss resulting from a **personal act or omission, committed with intent to cause such loss, or recklessly and with knowledge that such loss would probably result** — very hard to break

**Limits (1996 Protocol as amended 2012, in force 8 Jun 2015) — in SDR**
- **Loss of life / personal injury**
  - Up to 2,000 GT: 3.02 million
  - 2,001–30,000 GT: + 1,208 per GT
  - 30,001–70,000 GT: + 906 per GT
  - Over 70,000 GT: + 604 per GT
- **Other (property) claims**
  - Up to 2,000 GT: 1.51 million
  - 2,001–30,000 GT: + 604 per GT
  - 30,001–70,000 GT: + 453 per GT
  - Over 70,000 GT: + 302 per GT
- If the personal fund is not enough, the unpaid balance shares the property fund
- **Passenger claims (Art 7)**: 175,000 SDR × number of passengers the ship's certificate allows
- Example: a 30,000 GT ship → personal claims ≈ 3.02M + 28,000 × 1,208 ≈ 36.8 million SDR; property ≈ 1.51M + 28,000 × 604 ≈ 18.4 million SDR

**Limitation fund (Arts 11–13)**
- Owner may constitute a fund in a court of a Party (sum + interest); then other assets are protected from arrest for those claims

**Key Point**
- LLMC limits underpin Bunkers 2001 and Nairobi WRC 2007 insurance amounts
- SDR (Special Drawing Right of the IMF) changes daily against the peso and dollar
- Report incidents fully to P&I — limitation depends on the facts of conduct
