# York-Antwerp, Arrest and Collision Rules — Claims between ship interests

## At a glance
| | **York-Antwerp Rules 2016** | **Arrest of Ships 1999** | **Collision Convention 1910** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kind | Private rules, apply only if the contract says so | Treaty | Treaty (Brussels) |
| Question answered | Who shares the cost of a sacrifice made to save ship and cargo | When a court may arrest a ship for a debt | Who pays when ships collide |
| Status | Used through bills of lading and charterparties | In force **14 Sep 2011** | Not verified |

> **Key Point:** The York-Antwerp Rules are not a treaty. They bind only when written into the contract, usually by a bill of lading clause.

## York-Antwerp Rules 2016 (general average)
**Basic rules**
- **Rule Paramount:** nothing is allowed unless the sacrifice or expense was **reasonably** made or incurred
- **Rule A:** a general average act is an **extraordinary** sacrifice or expense made **intentionally and reasonably** for the common safety, to save the property in a common maritime adventure from peril
- **Rule B:** a common maritime adventure also exists when one vessel tows or pushes another, if all are in commercial activity and none is in a salvage operation
- **Rule C:** only the **direct consequence** of the act is allowed. Environmental damage, demurrage, loss of market and delay are not
- **Rule D:** fault of one party does not stop the claim for contribution. Remedies against the party at fault remain
- **Rule E:** the party claiming bears the **burden of proof** and must notify the average adjuster. The adjuster may estimate the figures if particulars are not supplied within **12 months**
- **Rule F:** an extra expense incurred instead of an allowable one is allowed, up to the amount saved
- **Rule G:** loss and contribution are worked out on values at the **time and place the adventure ends**

**Numbered rules**
| Rule | Subject | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| I | Jettison of cargo | Allowed only if carried in line with the custom of the trade |
| II | Sacrifices for the common safety | Allowed, including water entering through hatches opened to jettison |
| III | Extinguishing fire on board | Allowed (water damage, beaching, scuttling). **Smoke and heat damage not** |
| IV | Cutting away wreck | **Not allowed** |
| V | Voluntary stranding | Allowed if run ashore for the common safety |
| VI | Salvage | Allowed if it saved the property. **SCOPIC** and Salvage Convention Art 14 special compensation excluded |
| VII | Machinery and boilers | Allowed when trying to refloat a stranded ship. **Never** when working afloat |
| IX | Cargo or ship's materials as fuel | Allowed in time of peril. Credit given for fuel otherwise used |
| X | Port of refuge | Entry and exit expenses allowed. Discharge, handling and storage allowed when necessary |
| XI | Crew wages, fuel, stores, port charges | Allowed during the extra detention or longer voyage. **Not** for repairs to damage found with no accident on the voyage |
| XIV | Temporary repairs | Common safety or sacrifice damage: in full. Accidental damage: up to the saving in general average expense. **No** new-for-old deduction |
| XVII | Contributory values | Cargo at discharge (invoice value, with insurance and freight). Ship without regard to charters |
| XVIII | Damage to the ship | Repaired: reasonable actual cost. Not repaired: reasonable depreciation, not above the repair cost |
| XX | Provision of funds | Capital loss on goods sold to raise funds, and insurance of disbursements. **No commission figure** in the 2016 text |
| XXI | Interest | Until **3 months** after the adjustment. Rate: 12-month ICE LIBOR for the currency, set on the first banking day of the year, **plus 4 points** |
| XXII | Cash deposits | Held by the adjuster in a separate account. If the depositor does not answer within **90 days**, the adjuster may deduct the amount |

## Arrest of Ships Convention 1999
- **Arrest** means a court detaining or restricting a ship to **secure a maritime claim**. Seizure to carry out a judgment is not arrest (Art 1)
- **Maritime claims** are a **closed** list, items (a) to (v). Examples: damage from the ship's operation, salvage, wreck removal, supplies and services, port dues, **crew wages**, insurance premiums, mortgages, disputes over sale of the ship
- **Art 2:** only a **court** of the State where the ship is can arrest or release it, and only for a maritime claim. The procedure follows the law of that State
- **Art 3, whose ship:** the ship may be arrested if the liable person owns it (at the time of the claim and of the arrest) or is its **demise charterer**. Mortgage claims, ownership claims and maritime liens are other grounds
- **Art 3, sister ships:** another ship owned by the liable person may be arrested, if that person owned, demise chartered, time chartered or voyage chartered the first ship when the claim arose. Not for ownership or possession claims
- **Art 4, release:** the ship **must** be released once sufficient security is given. If the parties disagree, the court sets it, **never more than the ship's value**. Asking for release is not an admission of liability
- **Art 5, re-arrest:** not allowed for the same claim, unless the security is inadequate (total capped at the ship's value), the security provider is or is likely to be unable to pay, or the release was at the claimant's request or beyond the claimant's control
- **Art 6, wrongful arrest:** the court may require the claimant to give security against loss from a wrongful or unjustified arrest, or from excessive security demanded
- **Art 7, merits:** the courts of the arresting State may hear the case, unless the parties agreed on another court or arbitration. A court may set a deadline for proceedings elsewhere, and if none are brought, release the ship
- **Art 8, scope:** any ship in a State Party's jurisdiction, whatever its flag. **Warships, naval auxiliaries and non-commercial government ships are excluded**

## Collision Convention 1910 (Brussels)
- **Art 1:** applies to collisions between sea-going vessels, or between sea-going and inland vessels, in any waters
- **Art 2:** accidental collision, force majeure, or unexplained cause: each side **bears its own loss**
- **Art 3:** if one vessel alone is at fault, that vessel pays
- **Art 4:** if more than one is at fault, liability is **in proportion to the fault**. If the degrees cannot be shown, or are equal, liability is **shared equally**. For death or injury, the vessels at fault are **jointly and severally** liable to third parties
- **Art 5:** liability remains when the fault is a compulsory pilot's
- **Art 7:** claims are barred after **2 years** from the casualty. A claim for contribution between the vessels must be made within **1 year** of payment
- **Art 8:** after a collision each master must help the other ship, crew and passengers if this does not seriously endanger his own ship. He must also give his ship's name, port of registry, last and next ports. Breach does not by itself make the owner liable
- **Art 11:** warships and government ships used only for public service are excluded
- **Art 13:** also covers damage by manoeuvre or breach of regulations with **no actual contact**
