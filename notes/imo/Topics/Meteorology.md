# Meteorology at Sea — Weather Services, Warnings and Danger Messages

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Legal basis | **SOLAS Chapter V**: Reg 5 (weather services), Reg 6 (ice patrol), Regs 31–32 (danger messages), Reg 34 (voyage planning) |
| Who provides the forecasts | National meteorological services, in **21 METAREAs**, under the **WWMIWS**, run by **WMO and IMO** |
| How you receive them | **NAVTEX** and **SafetyNET (EGC)**, part of the GMDSS ([[GMDSS]]) |
| Main reference | WMO Publication No. 9, **Volume D – Information for Shipping** |
| Wind warning levels | **Gale** = Beaufort 8 to 9. **Storm** = Beaufort 10 or over |
| Master's duty | Send a **danger message** for dangerous ice, a tropical storm, or force 10 or more with no storm warning received |

![[diagram:meteo-solas|Who does what under SOLAS V]]

> **Key Point:** Governments give ships weather warnings and forecasts. Masters must report dangers they meet to nearby ships and authorities, free of charge, and must plan the voyage with weather in mind.

## SOLAS Chapter V: weather duties
### Regulation 5: Meteorological services and warnings
**Governments**
- Encourage ships to collect weather data, and encourage use of accurate instruments. Instrument checks are available on request, **free of charge** to the ship
- **Warn ships** of gales, storms and tropical cyclones, in text and, where practicable, in graphic form
- Issue weather information (data, analyses, warnings and forecasts for weather, waves and ice) **at least twice daily**
- Equip selected ships with tested instruments and have them observe **at least four times daily**, where circumstances permit
- Transmit weather observations free of charge to ships, and receive and relay weather messages

**Masters**
- Are encouraged to report winds of **50 knots or more (Beaufort force 10)** to nearby ships and shore stations

### Regulation 34: Safe navigation and avoidance of dangerous situations
- Before sailing, the master must **plan the voyage** with appropriate charts and publications, following IMO voyage planning guidelines
- The route must allow for relevant ships' routeing systems, **sufficient sea room**, known hazards and **adverse weather**, and marine environmental protection
- The **owner, charterer or company must not stop or restrict** the master from any decision he thinks necessary for safe navigation or environmental protection

## Beaufort scale
![[diagram:meteo-beaufort|Wind force, speed and typical sea state]]

| Force | Name | Wind (knots) | Typical wave height |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Calm | under 1 | – |
| 1 | Light air | 1–3 | 0.1 m |
| 2 | Light breeze | 4–6 | 0.2 m |
| 3 | Gentle breeze | 7–10 | 0.6 m |
| 4 | Moderate breeze | 11–16 | 1.0 m |
| 5 | Fresh breeze | 17–21 | 2.0 m |
| 6 | Strong breeze | 22–27 | 3.0 m |
| 7 | Near gale | 28–33 | 4.0 m |
| 8 | Gale | 34–40 | 5.5 m |
| 9 | Strong gale | 41–47 | 7.0 m |
| 10 | Storm | 48–55 | 9.0 m |
| 11 | Violent storm | 56–63 | 11.5 m |
| 12 | Hurricane | 64 and over | 14 m and over |

- Wave heights are for well-developed wind waves in the open sea
- Waves lag behind the wind, so the sea keeps building after the wind rises

## Warnings and bulletins (IMO/IHO/WMO MSI Manual)
![[diagram:meteo-bulletin|Structure of a weather and sea bulletin]]

- **Warnings are given for** gales (force 8 or 9) and storms (force 10 or over). Near gale (force 7) warnings are optional
- **Tropical cyclone warnings** are required: hurricanes (North Atlantic, eastern North Pacific), typhoons (Western Pacific), cyclones (Indian Ocean), and similar systems elsewhere
- **Bulletins** are issued at least **twice daily**, at a set time, in this order: **Part I** warnings, **Part II** synopsis, **Part III** forecasts
- If no gale, storm or cyclone warning is in force, Part I says so
- A warning is issued **immediately** when the need is clear, and broadcast at once. An unscheduled warning is **repeated 6 minutes later**
- A warning stays in force until amended or cancelled
- Every warning gives: type of warning, UTC date and time, location, extent of the area, wind speed or force and direction. Cyclones and extra-tropical storms also give the time last located
- Visibility is reported when it is **less than 5 nm**:

| Term | Range |
|---|---|
| Very poor | under 0.5 nm |
| Poor | 0.5 to 2 nm |
| Moderate | 2 to 5 nm |
| Good | over 5 nm |

## METAREAs and the WWMIWS
- The oceans are divided into **21 METAREAs**. A **METAREA Coordinator** coordinates the forecasts for each area
- A **Preparation Service** writes the forecasts and warnings. An **Issuing Service** broadcasts them for its METAREA
- The **WWMIWS** (Worldwide Met-Ocean Information and Warning Service) is coordinated by **WMO and IMO**. Its warnings go out by **EGC** and **NAVTEX**
- METAREA limits do **not** affect national boundaries
- Navigational warnings are a different service (NAVAREAs, from the IHO). See [[GMDSS]]
- **WMO Volume D** (Information for Shipping) lists the stations, broadcast schedules, satellite and NAVTEX details, visual storm warning signals, and contacts for METAREA coordinators, Port Meteorological Offices and ship weather routeing services. It is a reference to keep on the bridge

## Danger messages
![[diagram:meteo-danger|When a master must send a danger message]]

### Regulation 31: when you must send one
A master who meets any of these must broadcast it **by all means** to nearby ships and to the competent authorities:
- Dangerous **ice**
- A dangerous **derelict**, or any other direct danger to navigation
- A **tropical storm**
- **Sub-freezing air with gale-force winds**, causing severe ice accretion
- **Force 10 or over with no storm warning received**

Other rules
- No fixed form. Plain language (preferably English) or the [[International Code of Signals]] can be used
- **Free of charge** to the ship. Radio messages are preceded by the **safety signal**
- Governments pass such information on promptly to those concerned and to other governments

### Regulation 32: what the message must contain
| Danger | Information |
|---|---|
| Ice, derelicts, other dangers | Kind of danger, **position** when last seen, **time and date (UTC)** last seen |
| Tropical cyclone | That one has been met, time, date (UTC) and ship position. As far as practicable: corrected barometric pressure, **3-hour pressure tendency**, true wind direction, Beaufort force, sea state, swell (height, true direction, period), and ship's true course and speed |
| Force 10 or over, no storm warning | As for a cyclone, without sea and swell details |
| Sub-freezing air with gale and ice accretion | Time and date (UTC), air temperature, sea temperature if practicable, wind force and direction |

- **Follow-up reports** are desirable, not mandatory: hourly where practicable, and at least every **3 hours** while under the storm's influence

## Ice patrol (Regulation 6)
![[diagram:meteo-ice|North Atlantic Ice Patrol under SOLAS V/6]]

- **Ships** crossing the iceberg region guarded by the Ice Patrol in the ice season must **use its services**
- **Ice season in the SOLAS text: 15 February to 1 July**
- **Governments** keep up the ice patrol and ice study, and guard the south-eastern, southern and south-western limits of the **Grand Banks** iceberg region
- The **United States** manages the service. Governments whose ships pass the region pay a share, based on their average gross tonnage over the previous three seasons
- The US and/or Canada may stop the service with **18 months' written notice**
- **International Ice Patrol (IIP)** is run by the **US Coast Guard**. It was formed after the **Titanic** sank on 15 April 1912 and has worked since 1913, apart from wartime
- IIP bulletins go out by SafetyNET, NAVTEX and radiofax. Ships can send iceberg reports to IIP
- IIP's own working season is longer than the SOLAS dates: in 2022 it ran from 11 January to 7 September

## What inspectors look at
- Weather information and forecast receivers (NAVTEX, EGC, radiofax) working, and used
- Voyage plan shows weather and sea room considered (Reg 34)
- Danger messages sent when required, with the right content
- Publications such as WMO Volume D up to date
