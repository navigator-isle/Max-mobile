# CFR Guide — How US ship rules are numbered, and where to find them

## At a glance
| | |
|---|---|
| **What it is** | The **Code of Federal Regulations**. The general and permanent rules of US federal agencies, in **50 titles** |
| **Ship titles** | **Title 33** (navigation, Coast Guard), **Title 46** (shipping), **Title 40** (environment, EPA) |
| **How to cite** | **Title, CFR, section.** Example: **33 CFR 160.215** is title 33, part 160, section 215 |
| **Law above it** | Statutes in the **U.S. Code** (for example 33 U.S.C. 2701 ff. for [[OPA 90]]). Agencies write the CFR under those statutes |

> **Key Point:** The CFR is the detailed rulebook. Each US ship topic in these notes names its CFR parts. Read the part, not only the summary, before a decision.

## How the CFR is built
| Level | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Title** | 46 | A subject area, 1 to 50 |
| **Chapter** | Chapter I | An agency or part of one. 33 CFR and 46 CFR chapter I are the **Coast Guard** |
| **Subchapter** | Subchapter D | A group of parts, shown by letter |
| **Part** | Part 162 | One regulation topic |
| **Subpart** | Subpart C | A group of sections inside a part |
| **Section** | 160.215 | The rule itself. The number before the dot is the part |

## Marine casualty reporting (46 CFR 4.05)
Subpart 4.05 sections: **4.05-1** notice of marine casualty, **4.05-2** incidents involving foreign tank vessels, **4.05-5** substance of the notice, **4.05-10** written report, **4.05-12** alcohol or drug use by those involved, **4.05-15** voyage records and their retention, **4.05-20** report of an accident to an aid to navigation.

### Immediate notice (4.05-1)
- **Who:** the owner, agent, master, operator, or person in charge
- **When and to whom:** **immediately**, after addressing safety concerns, to the **nearest Marine Safety Office, Marine Inspection Office or Coast Guard Group Office**
- **What must be reported:**

| Casualty | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Grounding or bridge strike** | Any **unintended** grounding or bridge strike. An **intended** one if it creates a hazard to navigation, the environment or vessel safety |
| **Loss of propulsion or steering** | Main propulsion, primary steering, or an associated component or control system, if it reduces maneuverability |
| **Seaworthiness** | An occurrence that materially and adversely affects seaworthiness or fitness for service or route, for example fire, flooding, or failure of fire-extinguishing, lifesaving, auxiliary power or bilge-pumping systems |
| **Loss of life** | Any |
| **Injury** | Needs professional medical treatment beyond first aid. For crew in commercial service, also if it makes the person unfit for routine duties |
| **Property damage** | More than **$25,000**. Cost of labour and materials to restore the property. Salvage, cleaning, gas-freeing, drydocking and demurrage are not counted |

- **Hazardous condition:** notice given under 33 CFR 160.215 meets this rule, if the casualty involves a hazardous condition as defined in 33 CFR 160.203

### Written report (4.05-10)
- **Who:** same persons, for any casualty reportable under 4.05-1
- **Deadline:** within **5 days**
- **Where and form:** a Coast Guard Sector Office or Marine Inspection Office, on **Form CG-2692** (Report of Marine Casualty, Commercial Diving Casualty, or OCS-Related Casualty)
- **Addenda:** **CG-2692A** barge, **CG-2692B** chemical testing after a serious marine incident (commercial vessels), **CG-2692C** personnel casualty, **CG-2692D** involved persons and witnesses
- **Not a replacement** for the immediate notice. The one exception: a written report filed **without delay** after the casualty counts as the notice

## How to look a rule up
| Source | Use |
|---|---|
| **eCFR** | The **current, continuously updated** text on the web. It is an editorial compilation, not the legal edition |
| **Official annual edition** | The **printed or GovInfo edition**, revised once a year. The legal text as of its date |
| **Federal Register** | Daily notices of new, proposed and final rules. A change appears here **before** it is in the CFR. Citations look like "74 FR 31288" (volume and page) |
| **Cornell LII** | Free searchable copy of the CFR and the U.S. Code, used for the checks in these notes |

**Tip:** check the **date of the edition** you read. A rule can change after that date.
