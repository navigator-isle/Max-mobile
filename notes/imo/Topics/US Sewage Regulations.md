# US Sewage Regulations — Marine sanitation devices, no-discharge zones, Alaska cruise ships

## At a glance
| | |
|---|---|
| **Law** | **Clean Water Act section 312** (33 U.S.C. 1322) |
| **Standards** | **EPA**, 40 CFR 140 (what comes out). **Coast Guard**, 33 CFR 159 (design, certification, installation, operation) |
| **Who** | Any vessel with an **installed toilet** on US navigable waters, which includes the **3 nm** territorial sea |
| **Not covered** | Vessels with no installed toilet. Portable toilets |
| **Foreign ships** | The US is **not a party to MARPOL Annex IV**. A valid **ISPP certificate** is accepted as meeting 33 CFR 159 |
| **Special rules** | **No-discharge zones** (NDZs) in many States. Extra rules for **cruise ships in Alaska** (33 CFR 159 subpart E) |

> **Key Point:** Within 3 nm, sewage is either **treated by a certified MSD to the EPA standard**, or **held in a tank**. In a no-discharge zone, even treated sewage stays on board.

## The marine sanitation device (MSD)
An MSD is "equipment installed on a vessel to receive, retain, treat, or discharge sewage," and any process to treat it (33 U.S.C. 1322(a)). **Sewage** is human body wastes and wastes from toilets and similar receptacles. On the **Great Lakes** it also includes graywater from commercial vessels.

| Type | What it is | Standard (33 CFR 159.53, 40 CFR 140.3) |
|---|---|---|
| **Type I** | Flow-through, usually macerate and disinfect. **Vessels 65 ft (about 20 m) or less only** | Fecal coliform **not above 1,000 per 100 mL** and **no visible floating solids** |
| **Type II** | Flow-through, often with biological treatment. **Any length** | Fecal coliform **not above 200 per 100 mL** and suspended solids **not above 150 mg/L** |
| **Type III** | Holding tank, or a device that prevents overboard discharge. Composting toilets may qualify | No numeric standard. Must **prevent overboard discharge** of treated or untreated sewage and any waste from sewage. Pumped out ashore, or discharged beyond 3 nm where allowed |

- **EPA's waters rule (140.3):** in **freshwater lakes, reservoirs and non-navigable rivers** the device must prevent overboard discharge, treated or not (a flow-through device may be carried if secured so it cannot discharge). In **other waters** a discharge meets 1,000 per 100 mL and no visible floating solids
- **Effective dates (140.3(b)):** **30 Jan 1977** for new vessels and **30 Jan 1980** for existing vessels
- **Law:** a covered vessel may not operate on navigable waters without an **operable, certified MSD**. The seller may not sell a covered vessel without one, and nobody may remove or disable a certified MSD before delivery to the buyer (1322(h))
- **Great Lakes:** commercial vessels need at least the equivalent of **secondary treatment** (1322(c)(1)(B))
- **Certification:** the Coast Guard certifies MSDs. Part 159 subpart C sets the tests (vibration, shock, rolling, pressure, temperature, chemical resistance, sewage processing, coliform, visible floating solids, suspended solids) and rules for vents, level indicators, sewage removal, removal fittings, placards and identification

## Where discharge is banned
| Place | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Within 3 nm**, untreated or inadequately treated | Not allowed. Treat in a certified Type I or II MSD, or hold in a Type III |
| **No-discharge zone** | Even treated sewage is banned. Flow-through devices are **secured** |
| **Freshwater lakes or impoundments** with a too-shallow entrance and exit for vessels with installed toilets | Banned, even treated |
| **Rivers that do not carry interstate traffic** of such vessels | Banned, even treated |

**How to secure the device (33 CFR 159.7)**
- **Type I or II** in waters where treated or untreated discharge is banned: **close the seacock and remove its handle**, or **padlock** it closed, or hold it closed with a **non-releasable wire tie**, or **lock the toilet-space door** with a padlock or key lock
- **Type III** where untreated discharge is banned: **close each overboard valve and remove its handle**, or padlock it, or use a non-releasable wire tie

## No-discharge zones (NDZs)
A State cannot set its own MSD design or use rules (houseboats excepted). It can ask EPA for an NDZ (1322(f), 40 CFR 140.4).

| Type | Basis | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **State-wide or broad** | 312(f)(3) | The State applies in writing and certifies that the waters need more protection. The EPA Administrator decides within **90 days** whether **adequate pump-out facilities** are reasonably available to all vessels |
| **Specific waters** | 312(f)(4)(A) | Special waters: recreational areas, drinking water intakes, aquatic sanctuaries, spawning and nursery areas, intensive boating areas. EPA proposes a rule if a ban is warranted |
| **Drinking water intake** | 312(f)(4)(B) | The State describes the intake and community and marks the zone on a USGS or NOAA chart |

- **Examples in 140.4:** the Boundary Waters Canoe Area (Minnesota), State waters in the **Florida Keys National Marine Sanctuary**, **California marine waters**, and two parts of the **Hudson River**
- **California (140.4(b)(2)):** large passenger vessels are fully banned. Large oceangoing vessels (**300 GT or more**) are banned if they do not fully use their holding tank, or hold more than a de minimis amount of sewage generated outside California waters. A designated ballast tank does not count as a holding tank
- **EPA's list:** about **27 States** have NDZs. Examples: Puget Sound and Lake Washington (Washington), Long Island Sound and parts of the Hudson River (New York), 11 areas in California, and State-wide zones in Michigan, Vermont, New Mexico, Missouri and Massachusetts. Check EPA's current list before a voyage

## Foreign ships and MARPOL Annex IV
- The US is **not party to Annex IV** (NVIC 1-09, 74 FR 31288, 30 Jun 2009)
- A foreign-flag ship with a **valid ISPP certificate** showing that its sewage system complies with Annex IV is **accepted as meeting 33 CFR 159** in US waters. This depends on the system being **substantially operable**, and may be verified in a port state control exam
- Annex IV covers ships of **400 GT or more** on international voyages, and ships under 400 GT certified for **more than 15 persons**
- **US-registered ships** cannot get an ISPP certificate. They may get a **Statement of Voluntary Compliance (SOVC)**, issued by the Coast Guard or an authorized class society, valid up to **5 years**. The Coast Guard does not issue SOVCs to foreign ships
- Revised IMO effluent standards (**MEPC.159(55)**) apply from **1 Jan 2010** to ships with keels laid on or after that date, or with a sewage plant installed or delivered after it. Older ships may use plants certified to the 1976 standard or to 33 CFR 159

## Cruise ships in Alaska (33 CFR 159 subpart E)
Applies to **cruise vessels authorized to carry 500 or more passengers** in the **Alexander Archipelago**, other US navigable waters in Alaska, and the **Kachemak Bay** reserve.

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Untreated sewage** | **Prohibited** (159.307) |
| **Treated sewage or graywater** | Discharge only when **underway at 6 knots or more**, **at least 1 nm** from the nearest shore, meeting the effluent standard, and not in a prohibited area (159.309). A safety exception covers saving the vessel or life at sea (159.311) |
| **Effluent standard** | Fecal coliform **200 per 100 mL** and suspended solids **150 mg/L** (159.319) |
| **Interim route to discharge closer or slower** | Notify the Captain of the Port **30 days** ahead with test results. Meet 40 CFR 133.102. Geometric mean fecal coliform **20 per 100 mL** over any 30 days, no more than **10%** of samples over **40**, total residual chlorine **10.0 µg/L**, and results of **at least five samples** on different days over 30 days |
| **Record book** | A **Sewage and Graywater Discharge Record Book** in English, kept **3 years**. Each entry: port, start date, effluent type and treatment, open and close times, position, volume (m³), flow rate (L/min), minimum speed. Signed, with pages signed by the master |
| **Sampling and reporting** | Certify a QA/QC plan **90 days** before the first entry each year. Certify effluent standards **30 to 120 days** before. A vessel sampling plan within **30 days** of first entry. Unannounced sampling. Conventional pollutant results within **15 days**, priority pollutants within **30 days**. Samples kept at least **6 months** |
| **Penalties** | Class I administrative up to **$10,000** per violation (cap **$25,000**). Class II up to **$10,000 per day** (cap **$125,000**). Civil court up to **$25,000 per day**. Negligence is a Class A misdemeanor, knowing violations and false records are Class D felonies. The **vessel is liable in rem** |

## Penalties under the Clean Water Act (1322(j))
- Up to **$5,000** per violation for violations of 1322(g)(1) or of clauses (1) and (2) of (h) (sale and operation without a certified MSD)
- Up to **$2,000** per violation for violations of clause (4) of (h) or of any regulation under the section, such as effluent that fails the standard
- Each violation is a separate offence. The Coast Guard assesses and compromises penalties after notice and a hearing
