# Lifting Appliances — Design features, derricks, blocks, wire rope and safe use

## At a glance
| | |
|---|---|
| **Main source** | **ILO Code of practice, Safety and health in ports** (chapters 1, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9) |
| **Covers** | Ship's and shore **cranes**, **derricks**, **winches**, **blocks** and **wire rope** |
| **Who may drive** | Only **competent, reliable** persons, tested for ability to operate the appliance safely. A **signaller** gives the signals |
| **Records** | **Cargo gear register** and certificates, kept **at least four years** after the last entry |
| **Tests and certificates** | See [[Cargo Gear]] for the test loads, the 12-month and 5-year rules and the register forms |

> **Key Point:** Never let an overload cut-out or a limit switch be your normal way of working. They are the last protection, not the measure of the load.

## Before use and who operates
- Equipment is of **good design and construction**, **adequate strength**, and **kept in good repair and working order**. It is tested, examined and certified by a competent person before first use and after substantial alteration or repair (4.2.1)
- Only drivers who have passed tests for ability to operate the appliance safely drive it (4.2.1)
- A **cargo gear register** and related certificates are kept and available (4.2.2). They are preserved **at least four years** after the last entry (4.2.3)
- Only **competent and reliable** persons operate lifting or transporting machinery (1.6.1)

**Signaller (1.7)**
- Protects the gang working at the hatch
- Stands where he can **best follow the work**
- Gives signals to the winch or crane driver that are **distinct**
- Sees that **no person is carried by a lifting appliance** except by special authority

## Design and safety features (chapter 4)
| Feature | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Brakes (4.4.3)** | Able to exert a braking torque **25% above** the torque needed at the maximum SWL under the most adverse conditions |
| **Rope lead (4.6.1)** | Rope leads onto the drum properly. The lead angle does not exceed **1 in 16** for hoisting ropes and **1 in 12** for derricking ropes |
| **Limit switches (4.11.1)** | Every shore crane, and where practicable every shipboard crane or derrick crane |
| **Overload cut-out (4.12.1)** | Where practicable, cuts power when the load exceeds the SWL by **3% to 10%**. It does **not** stop motion in the reverse direction. The operator must **never** use it as the normal way to find the liftable load |
| **SWL indicator (4.13.4)** | Where SWL is above 1 tonne it is shown in **tonnes** |

## Pulley blocks (chapter 6)
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Single-sheave block, load on the head fitting (6.1.2)** | May lift up to **twice** the SWL marked on the block |
| **Single-sheave block in a derrick rig (6.1.3)** | SWL is **half the resultant load** on the head fitting, allowing for friction and rope stiffness |
| **Sheave diameter (6.4.3)** | At the groove bottom at least **14 times** the rope diameter |
| **Inspection (6.5)** | Check for cracks, groove wear, free rotation and legible markings |
| **Identification plate (6.4.13)** | Carries the safe working load |

## Ship's derricks (chapter 7)
- **Rigging plan (7.1):** shows guy positions, resultant forces, block positions and union purchase arrangements
- **Slewing guys (7.2.1):** designed to work with the ship at a list of **not more than 5°** and a trim of **not more than 2°**, with the derrick at full outreach
- **Rigging check (7.6.1):** condition of wires, shackle pins, sheave rotation and guy attachment. Only rigging personnel stay near the derrick
- **Minimum angle (7.6.3):** a derrick is **never rigged below** the minimum angle stamped on it

**Union purchase (7.7)**
- A **preventer guy** is fitted in addition to the main guy. The **main working guy** is under **slightly more tension** than the preventer
- The angle between the two cargo falls **must not exceed 120°** at any time
- The load is **raised only enough to clear the coaming**, bulwark or railings

**Marking (7.8)**
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Single purchase** | Marked **SWL x t** |
| **Union purchase** | Marked **SWL (U) x t** |
| **Letters** | At least **77 mm** high, in contrasting colours |
| **Lowest angle** | The lowest working angle is also marked (7.8.3) |

## Derrick winches (chapter 8)
- **Whip-driven span gear winches** are **not used** on a derrick with a single-fall SWL **over 3 tonnes** (8.3.1)
- Pawls and sprockets resist at least **1.5 times** the maximum derrick torque (8.1.1). The figure is read from a garbled line in the text
- Someone **stands by the pawl gear**. Pawls are **never engaged** while the drum lowers the derrick (8.2)
- Whip ropes: **no splices**, no more turns than needed, no surging of man-made rope, which can overheat (8.4.1)
- Operators wear gloves and keep the rope coiled on deck, **not standing in a bight** (8.5.1)

## Wire rope (chapter 9)
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Rope** | Meets a recognised standard, has a maker-certified **minimum breaking load**, and suits the use (9.1.1) |
| **Breaking load** | Minimum breaking load = **SWL × coefficient of utilisation** (9.2.1) |
| **Ship's derrick rope** | **Galvanised** (9.3.1) |
| **Hoisting rope** | **No knot or splice** (9.4.2) |
| **Terminal fittings** | Hold at least **95%** of the breaking load for rope up to **50 mm**, and **90%** above 50 mm (9.6.1) |
| **Clamps** | Bolted clamps are **not used** to form the end joins of hoisting or derrick rope (9.6.3) |
| **Inspection** | Whole length, including the turns on the drum (9.8) |

**Replace the rope for (9.9.1)**
- **Corrosion**, especially internal
- **Birdcaging**
- **Flats** on individual wires
- **Broken wires or needles above 5%** of the total number of wires in a length of **ten rope diameters**
- Broken wires in **one strand only**, or concentrated in the splice tucks
- More than one broken wire next to a ferrule or end fitting

The cause of any defect is investigated and remedied (9.9.2).
