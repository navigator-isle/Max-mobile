# Bills of Lading — The cargo document

## At a glance
| | |
|---|---|
| What it is | The transport document the carrier issues for goods carried by sea |
| Three functions | **Receipt**, **evidence of the contract**, **document of title** |
| Not | The contract of carriage itself. The bill is only evidence of it |
| Main rules | Hague and Hague-Visby Rules, Art III. UK Carriage of Goods by Sea Act 1992. UK Electronic Trade Documents Act 2023 |

![[diagram:bl-flow|From issue to delivery]]

> **Key Point:** Whoever holds an original bill can demand the cargo. A carrier that delivers to anyone else carries the risk of the full cargo value.

## The three functions
| Function | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Receipt** | Records that the carrier received the goods: the date the full quantity was received, their apparent condition, and the figures (marks, number, quantity or weight) |
| **Evidence of the contract** | Shows the terms of carriage. The contract exists from loading, before the bill is issued |
| **Document of title** | The rightful holder can take delivery on surrender of an original. Right to delivery is **not** the same as ownership |

## Types
| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Straight** | Names the consignee and **cannot be transferred** |
| **Order** | Delivery "to order". Passes by endorsement |
| **Bearer** | Delivery to whoever holds it. Passes by delivery alone |
| **Shipped (on board)** | States the goods are loaded |
| **Received for shipment** | States the goods are received, not that they are loaded |
| **Clean** | No remark on the goods' apparent order and condition |
| **Claused** | Carries a remark on a defect |
| **Charterparty bill** | Issued under a charter. The Hague Rules apply to it from the point it regulates the carrier and the holder (Art I(b)) |
| **Sea waybill** (not a bill of lading) | Names who gets delivery and **only identification is needed**. Not a document of title. Hague-Visby does not apply to it |

## What the bill must show (Hague Rules Art III)
- **Rule 3:** on the shipper's demand, after receiving the goods, the carrier, master or agent issues a bill showing:
  - the **leading marks**, as the shipper gave them in writing before loading
  - the **number of packages or pieces, or the quantity or weight**, as the shipper gave them in writing
  - the **apparent order and condition** of the goods
- The carrier need **not** state marks, number, quantity or weight it has reasonable grounds to suspect are inaccurate, or had no reasonable means of checking
- **Rule 7:** after loading, if the shipper demands it, the bill must be a **shipped** bill
- The P&I guide adds: place of issue, and the load and discharge ports

## Evidence and the shipper's guarantee
- **Rule 4:** the bill is **prima facie evidence** of receipt of the goods as described. Against a third party acting in good faith, proof to the contrary is **not admissible**
- **Rule 5:** the shipper is deemed to guarantee the accuracy of the marks, number, quantity and weight it gave, and must indemnify the carrier for loss from errors
- **UK COGSA 1992 s.4:** a bill signed by the master or an authorised person, stating goods were shipped or received, is **conclusive evidence against the carrier** in favour of a lawful holder

## Clausing
![[diagram:bl-clause|The master's test]]

- "Apparent" means **observable to the master on a reasonable examination**. It can include smell
- **Pre-shipment damage seen at loading:** clause the bill. Example in the Swedish Club guide: *250 steel coils, wet and rust stained on shipment*
- **Damage after the carrier took the goods:** no clause needed. A plain factual remark can be used, such as *received clean on board but approximately 30 MT subsequently damaged in a fire*
- A hidden fault that no reasonable look would show does not have to be investigated
- If the defects are too many for the bill, refer to a survey report. The report only counts if it is **attached to all bills, pages numbered, and the bill number stamped on it**
- **Refuse to sign** a bill that wrongly states the loading completion date, the port of loading, or clearly misdescribes the cargo's colour, form, quantity or condition
- A bill not properly claused can leave the carrier liable for damage it did not cause, and can put P&I cover at risk

## Holders: rights and liabilities (UK COGSA 1992)
- **s.1:** applies to bills of lading, sea waybills and ship's delivery orders. A bill of lading here excludes any document that cannot be transferred by endorsement, or by delivery if it is a bearer bill
- **s.2:** the **lawful holder** gets the rights of suit under the contract of carriage as if an original party
- **s.3:** that person takes on the liabilities of the contract if he takes or demands delivery, or makes a claim on the carrier for the goods
- **s.5:** a holder is a lawful holder if he became holder in good faith

## Originals and delivery
- Bills are traditionally issued in **sets of three originals**
- Delivery against an original that stayed on board is a practice with risk: it has not passed through the bank chain and gives little proof of title. It raises the risk of misdelivery, which would be for the full value of the cargo
- If agreed, the clubs recommend marking every original that **one original is kept on board and delivery may be made against it on the shipper's or charterer's instruction**
- Check the identity of the person who receives the cargo, and keep proof of delivery

## Letters of indemnity (LOI)
Used when the carrier is asked to deliver without the original bill, or at a port other than the one named.

| International Group form | Cargo delivered |
|---|---|
| **A** | Without the original bill |
| **B** | At a different port, against at least one original |
| **C** | At a different port **and** without the original |
| **AA, BB, CC** | Same, with a bank also signing |

- The requestor indemnifies the carrier and must put up security to prevent or lift an arrest of the ship, or any ship under the same ownership, management or control
- **P&I cover:** unless the Club's committee decides otherwise, there is **no cover** for delivery without an original bill or at another port. Check the signer's standing and authority
- **2023 review** (Gard circular 8/2023, 13 Sep 2023): updated wordings, an introductory warning on cover, and a representation on the status of the receiver. The security duty now also reaches vessels or property owned or controlled by the LOI recipient. The LOI ends only when the bills have reached the party that took delivery. Disputes go to the English courts

## Electronic bills
- The UK **Electronic Trade Documents Act 2023** came into force on **21 September 2023**. It gives certain electronic trade documents, including electronic bills of lading, the same legal recognition as paper in English law
- It meets the requirements of the UNCITRAL Model Law on Electronic Transferable Records (MLETR)
