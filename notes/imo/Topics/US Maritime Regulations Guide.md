# US Maritime Regulations Guide — What a ship must know before it calls at a US port

## At a glance
| Topic | Law and regulation | Enforced by |
|---|---|---|
| Oil spill liability | [[OPA 90]], 33 U.S.C. 2701 ff., with **33 CFR 138, 153 and 155** | USCG |
| Other discharges (graywater, bilge, scrubbers and more) | [[VGP and VIDA]] | EPA, USCG |
| Air emissions | [[US Emission Control Areas]] | EPA, USCG |
| Sewage | [[US Sewage Regulations]] | USCG |
| Ballast water | **33 CFR 151 subpart D** and **46 CFR 162** (type approval) | USCG |
| Arrival and port state control | **33 CFR 160 subpart C** (Notice of Arrival). USCG port state control exams | USCG |

> **Key Point:** Many US rules go beyond the IMO conventions. They apply to foreign ships in US waters. A ship that follows MARPOL and BWM only can still break a US rule.

## Where the rules are written
| Title of the CFR | Subject | Used here |
|---|---|---|
| **33 CFR** | Navigation and navigable waters | Parts 138 (financial responsibility), 151 (ballast and pollution), 155 (oil pollution prevention), 159 (sanitation devices), 160 (notice of arrival) |
| **40 CFR** | Protection of environment (EPA) | Part 140 (sanitation standard), parts 1042 and 1043 (marine engines and fuel) |
| **46 CFR** | Shipping | Part 162 (ballast water system approval) |
| **U.S.C.** | The statutes the CFR rests on | 33 U.S.C. 2701 ff. (OPA 90) |

## Oil Pollution Act 1990 (OPA 90)
Liability, defences, limits, COFR, claims, double hull and response plans are in [[OPA 90]].

## Vessel discharges: VGP and VIDA
The 2013 permit, the replacement law and the 20 discharge standards are in [[VGP and VIDA]].

## Emission Control Areas (ECA)
Sulphur, NOx, fuel changeover, FONAR and enforcement are in [[US Emission Control Areas]].

## Sewage
MSD types and standards, no-discharge zones, foreign ships and Alaska cruise ships are in [[US Sewage Regulations]].

## Ballast water (33 CFR 151 subpart D)
One of **five** methods must be used by a vessel with ballast tanks operating in US waters (151.2025):
| Method | Rule |
|---|---|
| **1. Approved BWMS** | A system approved by the Coast Guard under **46 CFR 162**. It meets the discharge standard at every discharge |
| **2. US public water** | Only water from a US public system that meets 40 CFR 141 and 143. Tanks cleaned, or never holding ambient water. Source records and an invoice kept |
| **3. Exchange** | A **complete exchange at least 200 nm from any shore**. Only if the vessel is not yet required to have an approved BWMS |
| **4. No discharge** | No ballast discharged into US waters |
| **5. Transfer** | To an onshore facility or another vessel for treatment |

- An **alternate management system (AMS)**, fitted before the vessel's compliance date, may be used for **up to 5 years** after that date
- A ship with a BWMS in independent laboratory testing is deemed compliant with method 1

**Discharge standard (151.2030)**
| Measure | Limit |
|---|---|
| Organisms **50 µm or larger** | Fewer than **10 per m³** |
| Organisms **10 to under 50 µm** | Fewer than **10 per mL** |
| *Vibrio cholerae* (O1 and O139) | Less than **1 cfu per 100 mL** |
| *Escherichia coli* | Fewer than **250 cfu per 100 mL** |
| Intestinal enterococci | Fewer than **100 cfu per 100 mL** |

**When an approved system is required (151.2035)**
| Ballast capacity | Built | Compliance |
|---|---|---|
| All | On or after **1 Dec 2013** | On delivery |
| Under 1,500 m³ | Before 1 Dec 2013 | First scheduled drydocking after **1 Jan 2016** |
| 1,500 to 5,000 m³ | Before 1 Dec 2013 | First scheduled drydocking after **1 Jan 2014** |
| Over 5,000 m³ | Before 1 Dec 2013 | First scheduled drydocking after **1 Jan 2016** |

- Alaskan North Slope crude exporters also do the mandatory deep-water exchange under 15 CFR 754.2(j) unless it would endanger the vessel or crew
- Rule 151.2036 lets the Coast Guard **extend a compliance date**. Rule 151.2060 sets the **reporting** and 151.2070 the **recordkeeping**

## Coming into a US port

### Notice of Arrival, NOA (33 CFR 160 subpart C)
| Voyage time | Submit |
|---|---|
| **96 hours or more** | At least **96 hours** before arrival |
| **Less than 96 hours** | Before departure, and at least **24 hours** before arrival |

**Updates** are due as soon as practicable and at least **24 hours** before arrival (voyage of 24 h or more remaining), or **12 hours** before arrival (under 24 h remaining).

- Applies to **US and foreign vessels** bound for or leaving a US port or place. It is **not** required of US recreational vessels
- Exempt unless carrying certain dangerous cargo: vessels of **300 GT or less** (except foreign vessels entering the 7th Coast Guard District), vessels in one Captain of the Port zone, force majeure arrivals, public vessels, US Great Lakes vessels other than tankers
- Two or more consecutive US calls on one voyage can use **one NOA**, at least 96 hours before the first port
- Submit through the **National Vessel Movement Center**, by **eNOAD** (website, XML or InfoPath), email, fax or telephone

| Information | Includes |
|---|---|
| **Vessel** | Name, owner, flag, call sign, IMO number, operator, charterer, class society |
| **Voyage** | Last **five** ports and dates, US destinations, ETA, ETD, current position, 24-hour contact |
| **Cargo** | General description, dangerous cargo names, UN numbers, amounts |
| **Crew and others** | Full name, date of birth, nationality, passport or mariner document number, position, embarkation point |
| **Equipment** | Operational condition under 33 CFR 164.35 |
| **ISM and ISPS** | Issue dates of the DOC and SMC, and of the ISSC with the plan and company officer details. Under 500 GT need not give the ISM notice |

**Hazardous conditions (160.215):** any condition on board, or caused by the vessel, that may hurt the vessel, a bridge, a structure, a shore area, or the environment of the port is reported **immediately** to the nearest Coast Guard Marine Safety Office or Group Office. A written report under 46 CFR 4.05-10 follows.

### Port state control by the Coast Guard
- The USCG examines foreign ships under SOLAS and MARPOL. **2025 figures:** **8,999 SOLAS safety exams**, **77 detentions**, an annual detention rate of **0.85%** (0.94% in the year before)
- Fire safety and life-saving appliance detentions fell again in 2025. Recognised organizations linked to detentions fell from **eight to seven**
