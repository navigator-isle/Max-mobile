# US Maritime Regulations Guide — What a ship must know before it calls at a US port

## At a glance
| Topic | Law and regulation | Enforced by |
|---|---|---|
| Oil spill liability | **OPA 90**, 33 U.S.C. 2701 ff. Limits and financial responsibility in **33 CFR 138**. Response plans in **33 CFR 155** subpart D | USCG |
| Other discharges (ballast, graywater, bilge and more) | **Vessel General Permit 2013**, now being replaced by **VIDA** (EPA standards, USCG rules) | EPA, USCG |
| Air emissions | **MARPOL Annex VI** and the North American and US Caribbean Sea **ECAs**. Engines under **40 CFR 1042 and 1043** | EPA, USCG |
| Sewage | Clean Water Act **section 312**. EPA standards in **40 CFR 140**. USCG design rules in **33 CFR 159** | USCG |
| Ballast water | **33 CFR 151 subpart D** and **46 CFR 162** (type approval) | USCG |
| Arrival and port state control | **33 CFR 160 subpart C** (Notice of Arrival). USCG port state control exams | USCG |

> **Key Point:** Many US rules go beyond the IMO conventions. They apply to foreign ships in US waters. A ship that follows MARPOL and BWM only can still break a US rule.

## Where the rules are written
| Title of the CFR | Subject | Used here |
|---|---|---|
| **33 CFR** | Navigation and navigable waters | Parts 138 (financial responsibility), 151 (ballast and pollution), 155 (oil pollution prevention), 159 (sanitation devices), 160 (notice of arrival) |
| **40 CFR** | Protection of environment (EPA) | Part 140 (sanitation standard), parts 1042 and 1043 (marine engines and fuel) |
| **46 CFR** | Shipping | Part 162 (ballast water system approval) |
| **U.S.C.** | The statutes the CFR rests on | 33 U.S.C. 2701 ff. (OPA 90) |

## Oil Pollution Act 1990 (OPA 90)

### Who pays
- The **responsible party** for a vessel is "any person owning, operating, or demise chartering the vessel" (33 U.S.C. 2701(32))
- It is liable for **removal costs** and **damages** when oil is discharged, or there is a substantial threat of discharge, into navigable waters, the shoreline or the EEZ (2702)
- A **tank vessel** is one built or adapted to carry, or that carries, oil or hazardous material in bulk as cargo or cargo residue

| Damage that can be claimed | Who may claim |
|---|---|
| **Natural resources**, including assessment costs | A US, State, tribal or foreign trustee |
| **Real or personal property** | The owner or lessee |
| **Subsistence use** | Anyone who uses the resource |
| **Lost taxes, royalties, rents, fees** | Government |
| **Lost profits or earning capacity** | Any claimant |
| **Cost of public services** | State or local government |

### Defences (2703)
The responsible party is **not liable** if it proves that the discharge was caused **solely** by:
- an **act of God**
- an **act of war**
- an **act or omission of a third party**, but **not** its own employee or agent, and not a third party in a contractual relationship with it
- or a combination of these

For the third-party defence it must also show **due care** and **precautions against foreseeable acts** of that third party.

The defence is **lost** if the party fails to report the incident, fails to cooperate with the response, or ignores a government order.

### Limits of liability (33 CFR 138.230, as printed in the 2025 CFR)
| Vessel | Limit, whichever is greater |
|---|---|
| Tank vessel, **single hull**, over 3,000 GT | **$4,000 per GT** or **$29,591,300** |
| Tank vessel, **not single hull**, over 3,000 GT | **$2,500 per GT** or **$21,521,000** |
| Tank vessel, single hull, 3,000 GT or less | **$4,000 per GT** or **$8,070,400** |
| Tank vessel, not single hull, 3,000 GT or less | **$2,500 per GT** or **$5,380,300** |
| **Any other vessel** (also edible-oil tankers and oil spill response vessels) | **$1,300 per GT** or **$1,076,000** |

- The limit covers total liability **plus removal costs** per incident
- The figures are raised for inflation. The statute's own base figures were lower: $3,000 and $1,900 per GT for tankers, $950 per GT for other vessels
- **The limit does not apply** if the incident was proximately caused by:
  - **gross negligence or wilful misconduct**
  - **violation of an applicable federal safety, construction or operating regulation**
  - by the responsible party, its agent, employee or a contractor
- **It also does not apply** if the party fails to report, fails to cooperate, or ignores an order without good cause (2704(c))

### Certificate of Financial Responsibility, COFR (33 CFR 138)
| Who needs one | Rule |
|---|---|
| Vessels over **300 GT** using US navigable waters, ports or places under US jurisdiction | Yes |
| **Tank vessels over 100 GT** doing the same | Yes |
| Vessels of **any size** transshipping or lightering oil in the EEZ for a US place | Yes |
| Public vessels | Not covered |
| A barge over 300 GT that carries no oil or hazardous substance as cargo or fuel | Not covered |

- The COFR operator applies at least **21 days** before the certificate is needed
- Changes are reported in writing within **5 business days**
- A **Fleet** certificate covers the largest unmanned non-tank barge. A **Master** certificate covers the largest tank vessel and the largest non-tank vessel
- A US **agent for service of process** is named (138.130)

### Vessel response plans (33 CFR 155 subpart D)
- The plan covers **all US areas** where the vessel handles, stores or carries oil, including offshore transit areas
- Written in **English**, and in a language the crew with plan duties understands
- **11 sections:** general information, notification, shipboard mitigation, shore-based response, contacts, training, exercises, plan review and update, on-board notification checklist (unmanned barges only), a geographic appendix for each Captain of the Port zone, and a vessel-specific appendix
- A **qualified individual** and an alternate are named. Each holds a **full current copy** of the plan
- The vessel carries an English copy of the specified sections and the **USCG approval letter**. An electronic copy is allowed
- The plan agrees with the National Contingency Plan (40 CFR 300) and the Area Contingency Plans
- **Annual review** within **1 month** of the anniversary of approval, recorded on the changes page
- **Resubmit** the whole plan **6 months** before the approval period ends
- **Revisions** for significant changes go in at least **30 days** before operation. Phone list changes need no prior approval
- A deficiency notice can be answered by a petition within the compliance period or **7 days**, whichever is less. An appeal of non-approval is due within **21 days**

## Vessel discharges: VGP and VIDA

| | 2013 Vessel General Permit (VGP) | VIDA national standards |
|---|---|---|
| Status | **In force now** for large commercial vessels, with the USCG ballast rules | EPA rule published **9 Oct 2024**. **Not yet in force** |
| Applies to | Commercial vessels | Non-military, non-recreational vessels **79 ft (24 m) and longer** in US waters or the contiguous zone |
| Excluded | | Armed Forces and recreational vessels, permanently moored craft. Smaller vessels and fishing vessels are excluded **except for ballast water** |
| Law | Clean Water Act permit | **Vessel Incidental Discharge Act**, signed 4 Dec 2018 |

**When VIDA starts:** the EPA standards take effect only when the **Coast Guard's implementing regulations** are final and enforceable. Until then the VGP 2013 and the existing USCG ballast rules, with any state and local rules, stay in force. When the USCG rules take effect, the VGP and the USCG's older NANPCA ballast rules are repealed. The 2014 small vessel permit was repealed when VIDA became law. VIDA gives the Coast Guard **two years** from the EPA publication to develop its rules. No Coast Guard rule had been confirmed published when this note was checked.

**What the EPA standards cover**
- **3 general standards:** general operation and maintenance, biofouling management, oil management
- **20 specific discharges:** ballast tanks, bilges, boilers, cathodic protection, chain lockers, decks, desalination and purification systems, elevator pits, exhaust gas emission control systems, fire protection equipment, gas turbines, graywater systems, hulls and niche areas, inert gas systems, motor gasoline and compensating systems, non-oily machinery, pools and spas, refrigeration and air conditioning, seawater piping, sonar domes
- Extra rules for **federally protected waters**, and a way for **states** to ask for emergency orders, no-discharge zones or reviews of the standards

## Emission Control Areas (ECA)
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| **ECAs near the US** | **North American** area (coasts of the US and Canada, and Saint-Pierre-et-Miquelon) and **US Caribbean Sea** area (Puerto Rico and the US Virgin Islands) |
| **Sulphur in an ECA** | **1.00% m/m** from 1 Jul 2010. **0.10% m/m** from **1 Jan 2015** (MARPOL Annex VI reg 14.4) |
| **Sulphur elsewhere** | **3.50%** from 1 Jan 2012. **0.50%** from **1 Jan 2020** (reg 14.1) |
| **NOx Tier III** | Ships constructed (keel laid) on or after **1 Jan 2016**. Emergency-only engines such as emergency generators, emergency fire pumps and lifeboat engines are excepted |
| **Changeover** | A **written changeover procedure** on board. Enough time to flush before entering. Log the **volume of low-sulphur fuel in each tank**, and the **date, time and position** of the changeover, in the logbook the flag State prescribes (reg 14.6) |
| **US engine rules** | US-flag engines meet **40 CFR 1042 and 1043**. Foreign ships must show that engines that are not Tier III were not and will not be used in the ECA |
| **Pilot fuel** | Within the US ECAs it meets the **0.10%** limit |
| **Scrubber waste** | Under the 2013 VGP, sludge and residue from washwater treatment is **not discharged** within 3 nm. It goes ashore. Monitoring equipment is calibrated **at least yearly** or as the maker says |
| **Dual-fuel engines** | Switching fuel mode happens **outside** the ECA. Averaging of NOx is not allowed |

## Sewage
Under Clean Water Act section 312, a vessel with an **installed toilet** on US navigable waters, including the 3 nm territorial sea, uses a **USCG-certified marine sanitation device (MSD)**. A portable toilet is not covered. EPA sets the performance standard. The USCG sets design, certification and installation.

| Type | What it is | Standard |
|---|---|---|
| **Type I** | Flow-through, usually macerate and disinfect. **Vessels 65 ft (about 20 m) or less only** | Fecal coliform **not above 1,000 per 100 mL** and **no visible floating solids** |
| **Type II** | Flow-through, often biological treatment. Any length | Fecal coliform **not above 200 per 100 mL** and suspended solids **not above 150 mg/L** |
| **Type III** | Holding tank. Disposal ashore, or beyond 3 nm | No numeric standard. Must **prevent any overboard discharge** of sewage |

- **Discharge is banned even when treated** in a designated **no-discharge zone**, in freshwater lakes or impoundments with an entrance too shallow for vessels with installed toilets, and in rivers that do not carry interstate traffic of such vessels
- A State cannot set its own MSD design or use rules (houseboats excepted), but can ask EPA for a **no-discharge zone**

## Ballast water (33 CFR 151 subpart D)
One of **five** methods must be used by a vessel with ballast tanks operating in US waters (151.2025):
| Method | Rule |
|---|---|
| **1. Approved BWMS** | A system approved by the Coast Guard under **46 CFR 162**. It meets the discharge standard at every discharge |
| **2. US public water** | Only water from a US public system that meets 40 CFR 141 and 143. Tanks cleaned, or never holding ambient water. Source records and an invoice kept |
| **3. Exchange** | A **complete exchange at least 200 nm from any shore**. Only if the vessel is not yet required to have an approved BWMS |
| **4. No discharge** | No ballast discharged into US waters |
| **5. Transfer** | To an onshore facility or another vessel for treatment |

- An **alternate management system (AMS)**, fitted before the vessel's compliance date, may be used for **up to 5 years** after that date
- A ship with a BWMS in independent laboratory testing is deemed compliant with method 1

**Discharge standard (151.2030)**
| Measure | Limit |
|---|---|
| Organisms **50 µm or larger** | Fewer than **10 per m³** |
| Organisms **10 to under 50 µm** | Fewer than **10 per mL** |
| *Vibrio cholerae* (O1 and O139) | Less than **1 cfu per 100 mL** |
| *Escherichia coli* | Fewer than **250 cfu per 100 mL** |
| Intestinal enterococci | Fewer than **100 cfu per 100 mL** |

**When an approved system is required (151.2035)**
| Ballast capacity | Built | Compliance |
|---|---|---|
| All | On or after **1 Dec 2013** | On delivery |
| Under 1,500 m³ | Before 1 Dec 2013 | First scheduled drydocking after **1 Jan 2016** |
| 1,500 to 5,000 m³ | Before 1 Dec 2013 | First scheduled drydocking after **1 Jan 2014** |
| Over 5,000 m³ | Before 1 Dec 2013 | First scheduled drydocking after **1 Jan 2016** |

- Alaskan North Slope crude exporters also do the mandatory deep-water exchange under 15 CFR 754.2(j) unless it would endanger the vessel or crew
- Rule 151.2036 lets the Coast Guard **extend a compliance date**. Rule 151.2060 sets the **reporting** and 151.2070 the **recordkeeping**

## Coming into a US port

### Notice of Arrival, NOA (33 CFR 160 subpart C)
| Voyage time | Submit |
|---|---|
| **96 hours or more** | At least **96 hours** before arrival |
| **Less than 96 hours** | Before departure, and at least **24 hours** before arrival |

**Updates** are due as soon as practicable and at least **24 hours** before arrival (voyage of 24 h or more remaining), or **12 hours** before arrival (under 24 h remaining).

- Applies to **US and foreign vessels** bound for or leaving a US port or place. It is **not** required of US recreational vessels
- Exempt unless carrying certain dangerous cargo: vessels of **300 GT or less** (except foreign vessels entering the 7th Coast Guard District), vessels in one Captain of the Port zone, force majeure arrivals, public vessels, US Great Lakes vessels other than tankers
- Two or more consecutive US calls on one voyage can use **one NOA**, at least 96 hours before the first port
- Submit through the **National Vessel Movement Center**, by **eNOAD** (website, XML or InfoPath), email, fax or telephone

| Information | Includes |
|---|---|
| **Vessel** | Name, owner, flag, call sign, IMO number, operator, charterer, class society |
| **Voyage** | Last **five** ports and dates, US destinations, ETA, ETD, current position, 24-hour contact |
| **Cargo** | General description, dangerous cargo names, UN numbers, amounts |
| **Crew and others** | Full name, date of birth, nationality, passport or mariner document number, position, embarkation point |
| **Equipment** | Operational condition under 33 CFR 164.35 |
| **ISM and ISPS** | Issue dates of the DOC and SMC, and of the ISSC with the plan and company officer details. Under 500 GT need not give the ISM notice |

**Hazardous conditions (160.215):** any condition on board, or caused by the vessel, that may hurt the vessel, a bridge, a structure, a shore area, or the environment of the port is reported **immediately** to the nearest Coast Guard Marine Safety Office or Group Office. A written report under 46 CFR 4.05-10 follows.

### Port state control by the Coast Guard
- The USCG examines foreign ships under SOLAS and MARPOL. **2025 figures:** **8,999 SOLAS safety exams**, **77 detentions**, an annual detention rate of **0.85%** (0.94% in the year before)
- Fire safety and life-saving appliance detentions fell again in 2025. Recognised organizations linked to detentions fell from **eight to seven**
