# IAMSAR Manual — International Aeronautical and Maritime Search and Rescue Manual

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| What it is | One shared guide for aviation and maritime search and rescue, published jointly by **IMO and ICAO** |
| Volumes | **3**: I Organization and Management, II Mission Co-ordination, III Mobile Facilities |
| Adopted | **November 1999**. It replaced the **MERSAR** (first published 1971) and **IMOSAR** (first published 1978) manuals |
| Linked to | The 1979 SAR Convention ([[SAR 1979]]) and SOLAS Chapter V ([[SOLAS 1974]]) |
| Updated | Amendments are prepared by the ICAO/IMO Joint Working Group, endorsed by the NCSR Sub-Committee, and adopted in principle by the Maritime Safety Committee, normally every three years |
| Latest amendments | **MSC.1/Circ.1686**, applicable **1 Jan 2026** |
| On board | Ships must carry an up-to-date **Volume III**, in **hard copy**, under SOLAS V/21 |

![[diagram:iamsar-volumes|The three volumes and who they are for]]

> **Key Point:** For a ship, **Volume III** is the one that matters. It tells you what to do on receiving a distress alert, how to coordinate on scene, and how to search and rescue. You must have the current edition in hard copy on board.

## The three volumes
| Volume | Title | Main readers | What it covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Organization and Management | Governments and SAR agencies | International, regional and national SAR systems, and cooperation between States |
| II | Mission Co-ordination | Rescue coordination centres (RCCs, MRCCs, JRCCs) | Planning and carrying out rescue operations and exercises. Also carried by rescue units, and by aircraft and ships that can or must take part in rescue |
| III | Mobile Facilities | Ships, aircraft and rescue units | On-scene communication, organization and search methods, and guidance on evacuating a craft in an emergency |

- Each volume can stand alone or be used with the others
- Volume III must be carried by every craft that may be asked to help, including all merchant ships, because any of them may have to search or act as on-scene commander

## Carrying it on board
- **SOLAS V/21:** all ships carry an up-to-date copy of Volume III
- **MSC-MEPC.2/Circ.2:** Volume III must be available in hard copy, because it is used in emergencies
- **Hard copy on board by 1 Jan 2026**, to match the changes below
- For flag State practice, such as the Isle of Man, the rule applies to ships that must carry life-saving appliances

## What changed on 1 Jan 2026
![[diagram:iamsar-2026|MSC.1/Circ.1686, dated 27 Jan 2025]]

- **Approval:** MSC 109 (2 to 6 Dec 2024), after ICAO approval and agreement at NCSR 11 (4 to 13 June 2024)
- **Volume III:** updated to match the **2024 GMDSS changes** to SOLAS Chapter IV (see [[GMDSS]]). Volume II also revises carriage text for radar SARTs or AIS-SARTs on cargo ships of 300 to 500 GT, and for survival craft VHF
- **Volume I:** new **Appendix P** (sample agreement between a SAR service and an accident investigation authority) and **Appendix Q** (sample process for quick approval of assisting SAR units entering another State's territory). Updated exercise template (Appendix O), and text on vessel and flight tracking
- **Volume II:** new **Appendix W** on night searches by aircraft using EO/IR equipment and night vision devices. New **section 6.15** on SAR near offshore wind and renewable energy installations. Updated flight tracking, GADSS and autonomous distress tracking, Cospas-Sarsat and MEOSAR content, Inmarsat unbarring procedures, search techniques and sweep width
- **What to do on board:** update muster lists, emergency checklists and procedures for the revised distress communications

## Emergency phases
![[diagram:iamsar-phases|The three emergency phases]]

| Phase | Meaning | ICAO term |
|---|---|---|
| **Uncertainty** | Doubt exists about the safety of a vessel or aircraft and the people on board | INCERFA |
| **Alert** | Apprehension about their safety | ALERFA |
| **Distress** | Reasonable certainty of grave and imminent danger, and immediate assistance is needed | DETRESFA |

## The five stages of a SAR response
![[diagram:iamsar-stages|From first report to debrief]]

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Awareness** | Someone or some agency learns an emergency may exist |
| **Initial action** | Preliminary action to alert SAR facilities and get more information |
| **Planning** | Operational plans for search, rescue and final delivery |
| **Operations** | Sending rescue units to the scene, searching, rescuing survivors |
| **Mission conclusion** | Units return and are debriefed. Records are completed |

Stages can overlap, and a mission may not have every stage.

## Who does what
![[diagram:iamsar-roles|Chain of command in a SAR case]]

- **Alerting posts:** relay a distress alert to the responsible rescue centre. Examples: coast radio stations, LUTs, land earth stations, air traffic units
- **SAR coordinator:** appoints a **SAR mission coordinator (SMC)** immediately when an alert is received
- **SMC:** prepares the search plan and sends units for the first search
- **On-scene coordinator (OSC):** appointed by the SMC when **two or more** SAR units work together. **Until then, the first facility to arrive takes the OSC's duties**
- **OSC duties:** coordinate all units on scene, carry out and adjust the plan while keeping the SMC informed, keep units **safely separated**, send regular **SITREPs** (weather, results, plan changes, survivors found and who holds them), keep a detailed record, and tell the SMC when units can be released
- **Communication:** if links to the rescue centre are poor, the OSC has more authority to start actions on scene
- **Avoid changing the OSC often**

## If your ship receives a distress alert
- **Acknowledge** receipt
- Get details of the craft in distress if possible
- **Proceed to help**
- **Relay** the distress and the information to the rescue coordination centre
- Keep watch on the distress frequencies and stay in contact with the craft while keeping the SAR system informed
- Ships should monitor **VHF channel 16** (156.8 MHz). Merchant ships can also be informed on 2182 kHz. For ditched aircraft, contact is often on 121.5 or 243.0 MHz, or 4125 and 5680 kHz
- A merchant ship near the position of distress is a resource the SMC can call on, called a vessel of opportunity

**When survivors are found**
- Rescue them **without delay** if able. After rescue, do not leave survivors alone, especially if injured or showing signs of hypothermia
- Hoisting a hypothermic person **vertically** can cause severe shock or even cardiac arrest
- Carry hot liquids and insulating blankets for hypothermic survivors
- In an aircraft ditching, survivors in the water or on the aircraft are usually taken first and those safe in rafts last

## Searching
### Planning a search
1. Evaluate the situation, including earlier searching
2. Estimate where the incident happened, and the error
3. Estimate how survivors have moved since
4. Fix the **datum** and its uncertainty
5. Allocate search effort to give the best chance of finding survivors
6. Define sub-areas and patterns, then issue a search action plan

The steps repeat until survivors are found or searching is no longer reasonable.

- **Datum:** the most probable location of the search object, corrected for movement over time
- **Track spacing (S):** the distance between two adjacent parallel search legs
- **Sweep width (W):** how well a sensor detects an object in the given conditions
- **Coverage factor:** C = W / S (corrected sweep width over track spacing). Pushing C beyond about 1.25 gives only a small gain in the chance of detection

### Search patterns
![[diagram:iamsar-patterns|The four common patterns]]

| Pattern | Use it when | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Expanding square** | The datum is known within close limits | Starts at the datum, grows outward in squares. A second search shifts the pattern **45°** |
| **Sector** | The datum is known well and the object is hard to see | Searches a circle around the datum. Ships usually use a radius of **2 to 5 nm**, with each turn **120°**. Aircraft 5 to 20 nm |
| **Creeping line** | A large area, and the object is more likely at one end | Legs run parallel to the **short** sides |
| **Parallel track** | A large area where the position is very uncertain | Legs run parallel to the **long** sides. The commence search point is half a track spacing inside the corner |

### Ending a search
- A search ends when the craft is no longer in distress, when survivors are rescued, or when the authority decides further searching has no significant chance of success
- It is not ended while there is any reasonable hope of rescuing survivors
