> **Key Point:** This note lists what each item is, how many, and what is inside the craft. Test figures are in [[LSA Code]]. Craft quantities, drills and maintenance intervals are in [[SOLAS 1974]].

## Personal appliances
![[diagram:lsa-personal|The four personal appliances]]

### Lifebuoys (III/7, III/32)
![[diagram:lsa-lifebuoy|A lifebuoy and its fittings]]

- Spread so they are **readily available on both sides** of the ship, with at least one **near the stern**
- At least **one on each side** has a buoyant lifeline, at least **twice its stowage height above the lightest waterline, or 30 m**, whichever is greater
- At least **half** have a self-igniting light. At least **two** of those also have a smoke signal that can be **released from the bridge**, split equally between the sides
- Each is marked in **block capitals** with the ship's name and port of registry
- On tankers the self-igniting lights are **electric battery type**

**Minimum number on a cargo ship (III/32.1.1)**
| Ship length | Lifebuoys |
|---|---|
| Under 100 m | **8** |
| 100 m to under 150 m | **10** |
| 150 m to under 200 m | **12** |
| 200 m and over | **14** |

### Lifejackets (III/7, III/32)
- One for **every person on board**, plus **children's lifejackets** for at least **10% of passengers**
- **Extra** lifejackets for watchkeepers and for remote survival craft stations
- Stowed where **readily accessible** and clearly marked. Watchkeepers' lifejackets go on the **bridge, in the engine control room** and at other manned stations
- Every lifejacket carries a **light** (LSA Code 2.2.3)

### Immersion suits and thermal protective aids
- Each **rescue boat crew member** and each **marine evacuation system party member** has an immersion suit or anti-exposure suit of the right size
- **Cargo ship:** **three immersion suits per lifeboat**, and thermal protective aids for anyone without a suit
- The three-per-lifeboat rule is dropped for ships with **enclosed lifeboats on both sides** with enough capacity, or **free-fall lifeboats with liferafts on each side**, or ships working only in **warm climates**
- **Cargo ships using the alternative arrangement** (III/31.1.3, ships under 85 m other than tankers, chemical tankers and gas carriers): immersion suits **for everyone**, unless the ship has davit-launched liferafts, an equivalent approved appliance usable on both sides, or works only in warm climates

## Survival craft
![[diagram:lsa-lifeboats|The lifeboat types]]

### Lifeboat design (LSA Code 4.4)
| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Capacity | **Not more than 150 persons**. The lesser of the number at **75 kg** each (passenger ship) or **82.5 kg** each (cargo ship) in lifejackets, and the seats that fit the seating diagram |
| Seats | Each supports **100 kg**. At least **350 mm** between upper and lower seats |
| Extra buoyancy | Inherently buoyant material of **280 N per person** |
| Hull | Rigid. Positive stability upright and when holed below the waterline. Fire-retardant or non-combustible |
| Engine | **Compression-ignition**. Fuel flashpoint **above 43 °C** (closed cup) |
| Drain | Valve that opens out of the water and closes afloat. Not required on free-fall boats |
| Plate | Maker, model and serial number, month and year, persons approved |

### Liferaft markings
- **Inflatable:** maker or trademark, serial number, month and year, approving authority, servicing station, and **persons permitted at each entrance** in characters at least **100 mm** high
- **Ship's name and port of registry** can be changed without opening the container
- **Container:** also shows SOLAS, emergency pack type, service date, painter length, **mass if over 185 kg**, and maximum stowage height

- The Administration may dispense with **food** and **fishing tackle** in lifeboats on certain voyages
- Water: up to 1 L (lifeboat) can be replaced by a **desalting apparatus**, or up to 2 L by a **manual reverse osmosis desalinator**

### Rescue boat equipment (LSA 5.1.2)
- **All boats:** oars or paddles with thole pins, 1 buoyant bailer, binnacle with compass, sea-anchor with hawser of at least **10 m**, 1 painter, **1 buoyant line of at least 50 m**, torch with spares, whistle, first-aid outfit, **2 rescue quoits** with 30 m line, searchlight, radar reflector, thermal protective aids for **10% of capacity or 2**, portable fire extinguishing equipment for oil fires
- **Rigid boat, in addition:** boat-hook, bucket, knife or hatchet
- **Inflated boat, in addition:** buoyant safety knife, 2 sponges, manual bellows or pump, repair kit, safety boat-hook

## Launching and embarkation
![[diagram:lsa-launch|The four ways craft leave the ship]]

- **Launch time:** all survival craft launchable within **10 minutes** on a cargo ship, **30 minutes** on a passenger ship
- **Embarkation ladder** at each embarkation station, or at every two adjacent stations for craft launched down the ship's side. It runs **in a single length from deck to waterline** in the lightest seagoing condition
- **Rescue boat:** launched with the ship making up to **5 knots** in calm water, recovered in **not more than 5 minutes** in moderate seas with the full complement
- **Line-throwing appliance:** one, to LSA Code 7.1
- **Cargo ships of 20,000 GT and over:** lifeboats launched with the ship making up to **5 knots** in calm water

## Approval, marking and certificates
![[diagram:lsa-approval|From design to ship]]

**Approval (SOLAS III/4 and III/5)**
- Every required LSA is **approved by the Administration**, after tests to the [[LSA Code]] following IMO's testing recommendation **MSC.81(70)**, or tests the Administration accepts as substantially equivalent
- **Novel** appliances must give safety at least equal to the chapter and the Code
- The approval states when it **continues or is withdrawn**
- **Production tests** make sure each item is made to the **same standard as the approved prototype**

**Marking and expiry**
- Each item is marked with the **approving Administration** and any **operating limits** (LSA Code 1.2.2.9)
- Age-limited items show their **age or replacement date**, with a **permanent expiry date** preferred. A battery with no expiry date is **replaced yearly**. Pyrotechnics carry the maker's **permanent expiry date**

**Equipment certificate: Record of Equipment (Form E)** lists persons provided for, davit-launched and free-fall lifeboats (by type), motor lifeboats and searchlights, rescue boats, liferafts (with or without launching appliances), lifebuoys, lifejackets, immersion suits, anti-exposure suits, radar SARTs, AIS-SARTs and two-way VHF. It is certified by an authorised official with place, date, signature and seal

**Personal certificate: STCW A-VI/2-1**, proficiency in survival craft and rescue boats (other than fast rescue boats). Five competences: take charge of the craft during and after launch, operate the engine, manage survivors and the craft, use locating devices and pyrotechnics, apply first aid. The renewal interval is in [[STCW 1978]]

**Servicing:** inflatable liferafts, inflatable lifejackets and marine evacuation systems go to an **approved servicing station** with proper facilities and trained personnel (III/20.8.1). **Posters** on or near craft and launching controls show the controls and procedure, readable under **emergency lighting**, with IMO symbols (III/9)

## General requirements for every appliance
| Requirement (LSA Code 1.2.2) | Detail |
|---|---|
| Stowage | No damage from **−30 °C to +65 °C** |
| Personal LSA | Operational **−15 °C to +40 °C** |
| In seawater | Works from **−1 °C to +30 °C** |
| Durability | Resists rot, corrosion, seawater, oil, fungus and sunlight |
| Colour | **Vivid reddish-orange** or equally visible, where it helps detection |

![[diagram:lsa-retro|Retro-reflective material, IMO A.658(16)]]

| Retro-reflective material | Amount |
|---|---|
| **Lifebuoy** | Strips about **5 cm** wide at **four** evenly spaced points |
| **Lifejacket** | Patches of at least **400 cm²** in total, high up, visible from all directions. A reversible jacket must comply both ways |
| **Immersion suit** | At least **400 cm²**, and **100 cm²** on the back if it does not turn the wearer face up |
| **Liferaft** | Pieces of at least **150 cm²**, about **80 cm** apart, round the canopy. Inflatable rafts also have a **cross** under the floor and on top of the canopy, each **half the raft's diameter** |
| **Lifeboat, rescue boat** | Pieces of at least **150 cm²**, about **80 cm** apart, on the gunwale and the outside near it, and on top of the cover. Boats that do not self-right also have it on the bottom |

## Documents
| Document | Where and what |
|---|---|
| **Training manual** (III/35) | One in each crew mess and recreation room, or in each crew cabin. Easy wording, illustrated. Covers lifejackets and suits, mustering, boarding and launching, release gear, survival equipment, radio, engines, recovery, cold exposure and retrieval |
| **Maintenance instructions** (III/36) | Illustrated. Inspection checklist, maintenance and repair instructions, a periodic maintenance schedule, a lubrication diagram, replaceable parts and spares sources, and an inspection and maintenance **log** |
| **Muster list** (III/37) | Alarm and public address details, how the abandon-ship order is given, crew duties, officers responsible for keeping the appliances ready, and substitutes. Made **before the ship sails** and revised if the crew changes |
