# OPA 90 — The US Oil Pollution Act of 1990

## At a glance
| | |
|---|---|
| What it is | US federal law (33 U.S.C. 2701 and following) that makes the **responsible party** pay for oil spill removal and damages |
| Covers | Oil discharged, or a substantial threat of discharge, into **navigable waters**, the shoreline or the **EEZ** |
| In force for | Incidents on or after **18 Aug 1990** |
| Key rules | Liability (2702), defences (2703), limits (2704), claims (2713), financial responsibility (2716), double hull (46 U.S.C. 3703a) |
| Regulations | **33 CFR 138** (limits and COFR), **33 CFR 153** (spill reporting), **33 CFR 155** subpart D (response plans) |
| Enforced by | US Coast Guard, with EPA |

> **Key Point:** OPA 90 is **strict liability**. The ship owner, operator or demise charterer pays first, and the limits of liability break if a safety regulation was violated or the spill was not reported.

## Words that matter (33 U.S.C. 2701)
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Oil** | Oil of any kind or form, including petroleum, fuel oil, sludge and oil refuse. Substances listed as hazardous under CERCLA are not "oil" |
| **Discharge** | Any emission, intentional or not, including spilling, leaking, pumping or dumping. Natural seepage is excluded |
| **Incident** | An occurrence, or series with the same origin, involving vessels or facilities, that causes a discharge or a substantial threat of one |
| **Navigable waters** | US waters including the **territorial sea, 3 miles** seaward from ordinary low water |
| **Barrel** | 42 US gallons at 60°F |
| **Tank vessel** | A vessel built or adapted to carry, or that carries, oil or hazardous material in bulk as cargo or cargo residue, that is US, in navigable waters, or transferring in a place under US jurisdiction |
| **Responsible party, vessel** | "Any person owning, operating, or demise chartering the vessel". For a single-hull tank vessel, also the owner of the oil carried, after 31 Dec 2010 |
| **Removal costs** | Costs after a discharge, or to prevent or reduce a substantial threat |
| **Fund** | The **Oil Spill Liability Trust Fund** (26 U.S.C. 9509) |

## Liability (2702)
The responsible party pays **removal costs** and **damages**.

| Removal costs | By |
|---|---|
| Costs of the United States, a State or an Indian tribe | Under the Clean Water Act, the Intervention on the High Seas Act or State law |
| Costs of **any person** | For acts consistent with the **National Contingency Plan** |

| Damage | Claimant |
|---|---|
| **Natural resources**, including assessment costs | US, State, tribal or foreign trustee |
| **Real or personal property** | Owner or lessee |
| **Subsistence use** | Any person who uses the resource, whoever owns it |
| **Lost taxes, royalties, rents, fees, profit shares** | US, State or local government |
| **Lost profits or earning capacity** | Any claimant |
| **Public services** (fire, safety, health) | State or local government |

- **Natural resource damages (2706)** are measured by the cost of restoration, rehabilitation, replacement or acquisition, plus the loss in value until restored, plus reasonable assessment costs. No double recovery. Trustees' assessments made under the regulations carry a **rebuttable presumption** of validity
- **Interest (2705)** runs from the **30th day** after the claim is presented. It is paid **outside** the liability limits
- **Foreign claimants (2707)** must show they were not otherwise compensated, and a treaty or State Department certification must allow it

## Defences (2703)
No liability if the responsible party proves, by a preponderance of the evidence, that the discharge was caused **solely** by:
- an **act of God**
- an **act of war**
- an **act or omission of a third party**, excluding its employees, agents and parties in a contractual relationship with it
- or a combination of these

For the third-party defence the party must also show **due care** and **precautions against foreseeable acts** of that third party.

**All defences are lost** if the party fails or refuses to:
- report the incident as required by law, when it knows or has reason to know of it
- give all reasonable cooperation and assistance to the responsible official
- comply, without sufficient cause, with an order under 33 U.S.C. 1321(c) or (e) or the Intervention on the High Seas Act

## Limits of liability (2704, 33 CFR 138.230)
The limit covers liability **and removal costs** for each incident. It is raised for inflation by rule. Current vessel figures, as printed in the 2025 CFR:

| Vessel | Limit, whichever is greater |
|---|---|
| Tank vessel, **single hull**, over 3,000 GT | **$4,000 per GT** or **$29,591,300** |
| Tank vessel, **not single hull**, over 3,000 GT | **$2,500 per GT** or **$21,521,000** |
| Tank vessel, single hull, 3,000 GT or less | **$4,000 per GT** or **$8,070,400** |
| Tank vessel, not single hull, 3,000 GT or less | **$2,500 per GT** or **$5,380,300** |
| **Any other vessel** (including edible-oil tankers and oil spill response vessels) | **$1,300 per GT** or **$1,076,000** |

- **Statute's base figures** (before inflation): tankers $3,000 per GT (single hull) and $1,900 (others); other vessels $950 per GT or $800,000
- Adjustment: the President adjusts the limits for the Consumer Price Index at least **every 3 years**. The 138.230 amendment history lists 2015, 2019 and 2022
- **Offshore facility:** all removal costs plus **$75,000,000**. **Onshore facility and deepwater port:** **$350,000,000**

**The limit does not apply (2704(c))** if the incident was proximately caused by:
- **gross negligence or wilful misconduct**, or
- **violation of an applicable federal safety, construction or operating regulation**

by the responsible party, its agent or employee, or a person under contract with it. It also does not apply if the party fails to report, fails to cooperate, or ignores an order without sufficient cause.

## Reporting a spill (33 CFR 153.203)
- The **person in charge** of the vessel or facility **immediately** notifies the **National Response Center (NRC)** as soon as there is knowledge of a discharge in violation of the Clean Water Act
- **NRC:** toll free **800-424-8802**, direct 202-267-2675
- If the NRC cannot be reached, report to the Coast Guard or EPA **On-Scene Coordinator**, who relays it. If neither can be reached, report to the **nearest Coast Guard unit** and notify the NRC as soon as possible
- A report under this section also satisfies **33 CFR 151.15** and **46 CFR 4.05-1**

## Claims (2713, 2714)
1. The claimant first **presents the claim to the responsible party** (or its guarantor)
2. If every party denies liability, or nobody settles within **90 days**, the claimant may **sue** or present the claim to the **Fund**
3. Claims may go **straight to the Fund** where the President has advertised, where a Governor claims State removal costs, where a responsible party claims under 2708, or where a US claimant is hurt by a foreign offshore unit
4. The Fund cannot approve a claim while the claimant is suing for the same costs

**Designation of the source (2714):** the President designates the source of the spill and notifies the responsible party and guarantor. If the party does not deny it within **5 days**, it must advertise the designation and the claim procedure, **starting within 15 days** and running at least **30 days**. The advertisement states that interim short-term claims are allowed and that payment does not bar later recovery.

**Time limits to sue (2717)**
| Claim | Within |
|---|---|
| **Damages** | **3 years** from when the loss and its link to the discharge were reasonably discoverable. For natural resource damages, 3 years from completion of the damage assessment |
| **Removal costs** | **3 years** from completion of the removal action |
| **Contribution** | **3 years** from the judgment or approved settlement |
| **Subrogation** | **3 years** from the date of payment |

- **Court:** US district courts have exclusive original jurisdiction, wherever the discharge or damage happened, or where the defendant lives, has its principal office or an agent. State courts can also hear claims
- **Other parties (2708 to 2710):** a responsible party can claim its own costs only if it has a defence or a limit, and then only the amount above its limit. **Contribution** is allowed against others. **Indemnity** and insurance contracts are allowed, but cannot move the liability off the responsible party

## The Oil Spill Liability Trust Fund (2712)
The Fund pays for:
1. removal costs of the federal government, and of States, local governments or tribes under agreement
2. trustee costs for damage assessment and restoration plans
3. removal costs and damages from a foreign offshore unit
4. claims **not compensated** by the responsible party, including Coast Guard costs of processing them
5. federal administration, capped each fiscal year at **$25,000,000** (Coast Guard operations), **$15,000,000** (NOAA response and damage assessment) and **$27,250,000** (research and development under subchapter IV)

Anyone who pays compensation, including the Fund, takes over the claimant's rights (**subrogation**, 2715). The Fund recovers from the responsible party, with interest and costs.

## Financial responsibility (2716, 33 CFR 138)
| Who must have it | Rule |
|---|---|
| Vessel **over 300 GT** using US waters, ports or places, or deepwater ports | Yes. A non-self-propelled vessel that carries no oil as cargo or fuel is excluded |
| **Tank vessel over 100 GT** | Yes |
| Vessel of **any size** transshipping or lightering oil in the EEZ for a US place | Yes |
| Public vessels | Not covered |

- **Amount:** at least the maximum liability under 2704(a). For several vessels, cover only the one with the highest limit
- **Methods:** insurance, surety bond, guarantee, letter of credit, self-insurance, or other evidence the Secretary accepts
- **CERCLA amount added (138.100):** the greater of **$5,000,000 or $300 per GT** for vessels over 300 GT carrying a hazardous substance as cargo. For any other vessel over 300 GT the greater of **$500,000 or $300 per GT**. The total is the OPA 90 amount plus the CERCLA amount
- **Direct action** lies against the guarantor. The guarantor can use the responsible party's defences and wilful misconduct, and is liable only up to the amount it provided
- **Sanctions:** Treasury withholds or revokes **clearance**. The ship may be refused entry or **detained**, and one found without evidence in navigable waters is liable to **seizure and forfeiture**
- **Civil penalty (2716a):** up to **$25,000 per day** of violation

**The certificate (COFR, 33 CFR 138.80)**
- The **COFR operator** applies to the Director **at least 21 days** before the certificate is needed, with the fee, and names a US **agent for service of process**
- Changes are reported in writing within **5 business days** of discovery
- **Fleet certificate:** evidence covers the largest unmanned non-tank barge. **Master certificate:** the largest tank vessel and the largest non-tank vessel. A master certificate holder reports at application and every **6 months**
- Before operating a vessel above the gross tonnage on the certificate, the operator files a **new or amended application** with new evidence

## Prevention rules
**Double hull (46 U.S.C. 3703a)**
- A tank vessel built or adapted to carry oil in bulk must have a **double hull** to operate in US waters, including the EEZ
- **Not covered:** spill response vessels, vessels **under 5,000 GT** with double containment judged as effective, certain Alaska barges under **1,500 GT**, and vessels in the National Defense Reserve Fleet
- **Phase-out of older ships** (contracted before 30 Jun 1990 and delivered before 1 Jan 1994): a **single-hull vessel may not operate after 1 Jan 2010**, and one with a **double bottom or double sides after 1 Jan 2015**
- Earlier age limits for a single hull: **25 years** (5,000 to under 30,000 GT) and **23 years** (30,000 GT or more)

**Vessel response plans (33 CFR 155 subpart D)**
- The plan covers **all US areas** where the vessel handles, stores or carries oil, including offshore transit areas. It is in **English**, and in a language the crew with plan duties understands
- **11 sections:** general information, notification, shipboard mitigation, shore-based response, contacts, training, exercises, plan review and update, on-board notification checklist (unmanned tank barges only), a geographic appendix for each Captain of the Port zone, and a vessel-specific appendix
- A **qualified individual** and an alternate are named. Each holds a **full current copy**. The vessel carries an English copy of the specified sections and the **USCG approval letter** (an electronic copy is allowed)
- The plan agrees with the National Contingency Plan (40 CFR 300) and the Area Contingency Plans
- **Annual review** within **1 month** of the approval anniversary, recorded on the changes page. **Resubmit** the whole plan **6 months** before the approval period ends
- **Revisions** for significant change go in at least **30 days** before operation. Phone list changes need no prior approval
- A petition on a deficiency notice is due within the compliance period or **7 days**, whichever is less. An appeal of non-approval is due within **21 days**

**Prince William Sound (2737):** a tank vessel that has spilled **over 1,000,000 gallons** of oil into the sea after 22 Mar 1989 may not operate in Prince William Sound, Alaska.

## State law
OPA 90 does **not preempt** State law (2718). A State may impose **additional liability, requirements or penalties** for discharges within its waters, and keep its own oil pollution funds. A State may also enforce the financial responsibility rule in its own waters (2719).
