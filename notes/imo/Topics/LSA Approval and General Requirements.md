# LSA Approval and General Requirements — Rules that apply to every appliance

## At a glance
| | |
|---|---|
| Approval | Every LSA required by SOLAS III must be **approved by the Administration** (III/4) |
| Basis | Tested to the [[LSA Code]] following IMO's testing recommendation, **MSC.81(70)**, or to tests the Administration accepts as substantially equivalent |
| Production | **Production tests** keep each item to the **same standard as the approved prototype** (III/5) |
| On the item | **Approval information** is marked on it: the approving Administration and any operating limits (LSA Code 1.2.2.9) |
| On the ship | Listed in the **Record of Equipment** with the Cargo Ship Safety Equipment Certificate |
| For people | **STCW A-VI/2** proficiency in survival craft and rescue boats |

![[diagram:lsa-approval|From design to ship]]

> **Key Point:** An item with no readable approval marking, or past its expiry date, cannot be shown to be approved and in date. Replace it.

## Approval (SOLAS III/4 and III/5)
- **III/4.1:** approval by the Administration, except for items under paragraphs 5 and 6
- **III/4.2:** before approving, the Administration makes sure the appliance was **tested** to the chapter and the Code, following IMO's recommendations, or passed tests **substantially equivalent** to the Administration's satisfaction
- **III/4.3, novel appliances:** they must give safety at least **equivalent** to the chapter and the Code, and be evaluated and tested to IMO's recommendations or equivalent tests
- **III/4.4:** the approval procedure must state the conditions under which approval **continues or is withdrawn**
- **III/4.5:** an appliance not yet approved by the Administration is accepted only if it satisfies the Administration that it complies with the chapter and the Code
- **III/4.6:** an appliance with no detailed specification in the Code must satisfy the Administration
- **III/5, production tests:** the Administration requires them as necessary to make sure appliances are made to the same standard as the approved prototype
- **MSC.81(70):** the tests are based on the LSA Code. Equipment installed on or after **1 July 1999** should meet it, or substantially equivalent tests specified by the Administration

## Marking and age-limited items
- **Approval marking (1.2.2.9):** clearly marked with the approving Administration and any operational restrictions
- **Age-limited LSA (1.2.3):** the Administration sets the acceptable service period. The item must show its **age or the date it must be replaced**, and a **permanent expiry date** is preferred
- **Batteries** with no expiry date may be used if replaced **every year**. A secondary battery is allowed if its electrolyte condition can be easily checked
- **Pyrotechnics:** the maker must **permanently mark the expiry date**

## General requirements (LSA Code 1.2.2)
Every prescribed LSA must, unless the Code says otherwise:

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Build | Proper materials and workmanship |
| Stowage temperature | No damage at air temperatures from **−30 °C to +65 °C** |
| Personal LSA | Stay operational from **−15 °C to +40 °C** |
| In seawater | Work across seawater from **−1 °C to +30 °C** if likely to be immersed |
| Durability | Resist rot and corrosion, and not be unduly affected by seawater, oil or fungus. Resist deterioration in sunlight |
| Colour | **Vivid reddish-orange** or an equally visible colour on all parts where it helps detection at sea |
| Seaway | Work satisfactorily in a seaway, if used there |
| Electrical | Short-circuit protection where applicable |

### Retro-reflective material (IMO A.658(16))
![[diagram:lsa-retro|Placement on each appliance]]

| Appliance | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Lifebuoy** | Strips about **5 cm** wide | At **four** evenly spaced points, round the body or on both sides |
| **Lifejacket** | Patches totalling at least **400 cm²** | As high as possible, visible from all directions. A reversible jacket must comply both ways |
| **Immersion suit** | Patches totalling at least **400 cm²** | If the suit does not turn the wearer face up, the **back** carries at least **100 cm²** |
| **Canopied liferaft** | Pieces of at least **150 cm²**, about **80 cm** apart centre to centre | Round the canopy. Inflatable rafts also have a **cross** on the underside of the floor and one on top of the canopy, each **half the raft's diameter** |
| **Open (uncanopied) raft** | Pieces of at least **150 cm²**, about **80 cm** apart | On the buoyancy chamber |
| **Lifeboat, rescue boat** | Pieces of at least **150 cm²**, about **80 cm** apart | On top of the gunwale and the outside near it. Canopy must not hide them, and its top carries pieces too. Enclosed and partially enclosed boats also have pieces **half-way up** and **round the top edge** of the fixed cover. Non-self-righting boats have material on the **bottom** |

- Material is **Type I** (flexible, not for continuous outdoor exposure) or **Type II** (highly weather-resistant)

## Servicing station (III/20.8.1)
- Inflatable liferafts, inflatable lifejackets and marine evacuation systems are serviced at an **approved servicing station** that is competent, has proper facilities and uses **trained personnel**
- The interval is in [[SOLAS 1974]]

## Operating instructions (III/9)
- **Posters or signs** on or near survival craft and their launching controls
- They show the **purpose of the controls** and the operating procedure, with instructions or warnings
- They must be **easily seen under emergency lighting**, and use **symbols recommended by IMO**

## Certificates for the ship's equipment
The **Record of Equipment for Cargo Ship Safety (Form E)** lists the life-saving appliances:

| Item | What is recorded |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Persons provided for** |
| 2 | **Davit-launched lifeboats**: number, persons, and how many are self-righting partially enclosed, totally enclosed, self-contained air support, fire-protected or other |
| 3 | **Free-fall lifeboats**: number, persons, and totally enclosed, self-contained air support or fire-protected |
| 4 | **Motor lifeboats**, and how many have **searchlights** |
| 5 | **Rescue boats**, and how many are counted as lifeboats |
| 6 | **Liferafts**: those needing approved launching appliances and those that do not (number and persons), and the number required by III/31.1.4 |
| 7, 8 | **Lifebuoys** and **lifejackets**: numbers |
| 9, 10 | **Immersion suits** (total, and how many also meet the lifejacket requirements) and **anti-exposure suits** |
| 11 | **Radar SARTs**, **AIS-SARTs** and **two-way VHF** radiotelephones |

- The form is certified by a duly authorised official, with place, date, signature and seal or stamp
- It is part of the SOLAS certificates appendix

## Certificates for people (STCW A-VI/2-1)
**Proficiency in survival craft and rescue boats (other than fast rescue boats)** covers five competences:
1. **Take charge** of a survival craft or rescue boat during and after launch
2. **Operate the survival craft engine**
3. **Manage survivors and the craft** after abandoning ship
4. **Use locating devices**, including communication, signalling and pyrotechnics
5. **Apply first aid** to survivors

- Knowledge includes: construction and outfit, launching devices, launching in rough seas, recovery, on-load release hazards, maintenance, painter and sea-anchor, food and water, detectability, helicopter rescue, hypothermia, marshalling liferafts, beaching, EPIRBs and SARTs
- The renewal interval is in [[STCW 1978]]
