# Cargo Handling and Stowage — Which rule applies, what to hold before loading, how to stow safely

## At a glance
| | |
|---|---|
| **Main rule** | **SOLAS chapter VI**, carriage of cargoes. Dangerous goods are in **chapter VII** |
| **Before loading** | The **shipper** gives the master **cargo information** in writing, in advance. Containers also need a **verified gross mass (VGM)** |
| **On board** | An approved **Cargo Securing Manual (CSM)**, and for some bulk cargoes **gas detection and oxygen analysis instruments** |
| **Stowage** | Stow so the cargo cannot slide, tip, rack or collapse, and so stability and ship strength stay within limits for the whole voyage ([[CSS Code]]) |
| **Master's right** | The master may refuse a packed container even with a VGM, if satisfied it cannot be carried safely |

> **Key Point:** The master decides on the information received, not on what the cargo is called. No information, no VGM, no loading.

## Cargo information (SOLAS VI regulation 2)
- **Who:** the **shipper** gives the master or the master's representative the **appropriate cargo information**
- **When:** **sufficiently in advance of loading**, so the stowage and carriage precautions can be applied. It is **confirmed in writing and by shipping documents before loading**
- **General cargo and cargo units:** a **general description**, the **gross mass** and any **relevant special properties**. The content follows subchapter 1.9 of the [[CSS Code]]
- **Solid bulk cargo:** the information follows section 4 of the [[IMSBC Code]]
- **Mass check:** before loading cargo units, the shipper makes sure the gross mass matches the gross mass declared on the shipping documents
- **Electronic data:** EDP and EDI may be used with paper documents. The shipping document may be electronic

## Verified gross mass of containers (SOLAS VI regulation 2, paragraphs 4 to 6)
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Who** | The **shipper**. If several parties pack the container, the shipper still stays responsible |
| **Method 1** | **Weigh the packed and sealed container**, or have a third party weigh it, on calibrated and certified equipment |
| **Method 2** | **Add the weight of all packages, cargo items, pallets, dunnage and securing material to the container tare**, by a certified method approved by the competent authority of the State where packing finished. Sealed packages marked with an accurate mass need not be reweighed. **Bulk cargo such as scrap metal or unbagged grain needs Method 1** |
| **Document** | The VGM is stated in a **shipping document**, clearly marked as verified gross mass, and **signed** by a duly authorized person. A signature can be electronic, or the name in capitals. It goes to the **master or representative and the terminal representative**, in time for the **stowage plan** |
| **Deadline** | SOLAS sets none. The shipping company sets it after talking to the terminal |
| **No VGM** | The container **must not be loaded** unless the master or representative and the terminal representative obtain the VGM by other means, for example weighing in the terminal. Costs follow the commercial contract |
| **Differences** | A discrepancy is settled by using the **verified** mass. If the terminal's own weighing differs from an earlier VGM, the terminal's figure prevails. The circular sets **no tolerance** |
| **Maximum** | A container may not be packed beyond the maximum gross mass on its **CSC Safety Approval Plate**. An overweight container is not loaded |
| **Road vehicles** | Subtract the chassis or trailer tare, and the tractor tare and fuel if applicable. Two containers on one vehicle are weighed separately |
| **Exemption** | Containers on a chassis or trailer driven on or off a ro-ro ship on **short international voyages** (regulation III/3) |

## On board: equipment and documents
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Oxygen analysis and gas detection** (reg 3) | Ships carrying bulk cargoes that may **emit toxic or flammable gas, or deplete oxygen** carry instruments. Crews are trained, use them while monitoring the cargo, and have them serviced and calibrated to the maker's instructions |
| **Pesticides** (reg 4) | Precautions follow IMO circulars **MSC.1/Circ.1358**, **MSC.1/Circ.1264** (as amended by 1396) and **MSC.1/Circ.1361/Rev.1** |
| **Cargo Securing Manual** (reg 5) | Cargo is loaded, stowed and secured under the ship's CSM, **approved by the Administration**, so that it causes no damage or hazard to the ship or people on board throughout the voyage |
| **Safety data sheet** (reg 5-1) | Required **before loading oil that is a MARPOL Annex I product as bulk cargo, or bunkering it**. Format follows **MSC.286(86)** |
| **Cargo loading manual** (reg 7) | For solid bulk carriers. Held in a language the cargo officers understand, with an English version if that is not English. Includes stability data, ballast rates and loading limits |
| **Loading and unloading** (reg 7) | Operational guidance and the duties of the master and terminal are in the [[BLU Code]] |

## Stowage: the principles (CSS Code)
![[diagram:cargo-forces|Where stowage forces are largest]]

**Forces on the stow (1.3)**
- Forces act along the **longitudinal, transverse and vertical** axes. For stowage, **longitudinal and transverse forces are predominant**
- Transverse forces **increase with stow height** and with the **distance from the ship's centre of motion**. The worst places are **furthest forward, furthest aft and highest** on each side
- Transverse forces also **increase with GM**. Too large a GM comes from poor design, unsuitable cargo distribution, or unsuitable bunker and ballast distribution. Distribute cargo so GM is **above the required minimum** and, where practicable, **within an acceptable upper limit**
- **Deck cargo** may also meet wind and green-sea forces
- Wrong **course or speed** can add to the forces
- Anti-roll devices may help, but **do not count their effect** when planning stowage and securing

**Cargo information and acceptance (1.9, 2.9)**
- Before accepting cargo, the shipowner or operator obtains the cargo information and confirms: commodities are **compatible or suitably separated**, the **cargo suits the ship and the ship suits the cargo**, and it can be **stowed, secured and carried safely** under all expected conditions of the voyage. The master gets enough information to plan the stowage
- A unit with dangerous goods that does not meet sections 12 or 17 of the IMDG Code General Introduction, or has **no container packing certificate or vehicle packing declaration**, is **not accepted**
- Where practicable, road vehicles carry a declaration that the cargo is properly stowed and secured for sea transport. The IMDG vehicle packing declaration may serve

**Principles of safe stowage and securing (chapter 2)**
| Principle | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Suitability (2.1)** | Cargo in containers, road vehicles, barges, railway wagons and other units is packed and secured in the unit so it causes no damage or hazard to the ship, people or the marine environment throughout the voyage |
| **Distribution (2.2)** | The master plans and supervises stowage to prevent **sliding, tipping, racking and collapsing**. Stability stays within acceptable limits for the **whole voyage**, with excessive accelerations reduced. Distribution must not harm the **ship's structural strength** |
| **Securing (2.3)** | Forces are shared **as evenly as practicable** among the devices. If not, upgrade the arrangement. A complex arrangement that experience cannot judge is verified by an **acceptable calculation method** |
| **Residual strength (2.4)** | Devices keep enough strength to tolerate normal wear and tear over their life |
| **Friction (2.5)** | Where friction is too low to stop sliding, use **soft boards or dunnage** to increase it |
| **Supervision (2.6)** | Supervising loading and inspecting the stow is **the principal means of preventing improper stowage and securing**. Cargo spaces are inspected **regularly during the voyage** as far as practicable |
| **Enclosed spaces (2.7)** | May lack oxygen or hold flammable or toxic gas. The master makes sure entry is safe |

**Pre-loading checks (2.8).** Before loading the master makes sure that:
- The **deck area is clean, dry and free of oil and grease**, as far as practicable
- The cargo, unit or vehicle is in **suitable condition** and can be effectively secured
- **All securing equipment** is on board and in good working order
- Cargo in or on units and vehicles is **properly stowed and secured**, as far as practicable

## Heavy weather and cargo shift (CSS Code chapters 6 and 7)
| Situation | Actions that may be considered |
|---|---|
| **Heavy weather, excessive accelerations (6.2)** | Change **course or speed or both**. **Heave to**. **Avoid** adverse weather and sea areas early. **Ballast or deballast** in good time, considering actual stability |
| **Voyage planning (6.3)** | Where practicable, plan the route to avoid severe weather and sea, and consult the latest weather information |
| **After cargo has shifted (7.1)** | Change course to reduce accelerations. Reduce speed to reduce accelerations and vibration. **Monitor the ship's integrity**. **Restow or resecure** the cargo and, where possible, increase friction. **Divert** to shelter or better weather |
| **Ballasting after a shift (7.2)** | Consider tank ballasting or deballasting **only if the ship has adequate stability** |

These chapters do not override the master's responsibilities.

## Before, during and after the cargo operation
| When | Check |
|---|---|
| **Before** | Cargo information received in writing. Cargo is a listed one for its code. VGM for each container. Dangerous goods documents. Stowage and loading plan agreed. Stability checked against the [[Ship Stability]] booklet. Gas instruments calibrated where the cargo needs them |
| **During** | Cargo is loaded as the plan and the CSM require. Gas and oxygen are monitored where regulation 3 applies |
| **After** | Cargo stowed and secured under the CSM, and under the CSS Code for hard-to-stow cargoes. Quantity checked ([[Draft Survey]]). Cargo documents ([[Bills of Lading]]) |
