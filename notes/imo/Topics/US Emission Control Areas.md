# US Emission Control Areas — Sulphur, NOx and fuel rules off the US coast

## At a glance
| | |
|---|---|
| **Two areas** | **North American ECA** (US and Canadian coasts) and **US Caribbean Sea ECA** (Puerto Rico and the US Virgin Islands) |
| **Sulphur** | **0.10% m/m** fuel in an ECA since **1 Jan 2015** |
| **NOx** | **Tier III** engines for ships with keels laid on or after **1 Jan 2016**, operating in the ECA |
| **Basis** | **MARPOL Annex VI** regulations 13, 14, 18. US law: the **Act to Prevent Pollution from Ships (APPS)**, 33 U.S.C. 1901 to 1905, and **40 CFR 1043** |
| **Enforced by** | **USCG and EPA**, together under a Memorandum of Understanding of **27 Jun 2011** |

> **Key Point:** The ECA limit applies to **all ships**, of any age. Cost of compliant fuel is not an accepted reason for using non-compliant fuel.

## The areas
| Area | Coverage | In effect |
|---|---|---|
| **North American** | The Pacific, Atlantic and Gulf of Mexico coasts of the US and Canada, out to **200 nm**. Also the eight main Hawaiian Islands and the French territory of Saint-Pierre-et-Miquelon. Exact limits are in MARPOL Annex VI Appendix VII and MEPC.1/Circ.723 | **1 Aug 2012** for the US. Designated by IMO on **26 Mar 2010** (MEPC.190(60)) |
| **US Caribbean Sea** | Waters around Puerto Rico and the US Virgin Islands | **1 Jan 2014** |

The same MARPOL Annex VI also has **Baltic Sea** and **North Sea** ECAs.

## Sulphur (regulation 14)
| Fuel sulphur limit | Where and when |
|---|---|
| **1.00% m/m** | In an ECA from **1 Jul 2010** (the North American ECA used it from 1 Aug 2012 until 31 Dec 2014) |
| **0.10% m/m** | In an ECA from **1 Jan 2015** |
| **3.50% m/m** | Outside ECAs from 1 Jan 2012 |
| **0.50% m/m** | Outside ECAs from **1 Jan 2020** |
| Pilot fuel | Meets the **0.10%** limit inside the US ECAs |

**Ways to comply**
- **Compliant fuel** (0.10% or less) inside the ECA
- An **exhaust gas cleaning system (scrubber)**, as an approved equivalent under the Annex VI equivalence rules. US scrubber washwater limits are in [[VGP and VIDA]]
- **Gas fuel** such as LNG, or other approved equivalents. EPA's 40 CFR 1043.55 covers "applying equivalent controls instead of complying with fuel requirements"

### Fuel changeover (regulation 14.6)
- A ship that uses separate fuels carries a **written changeover procedure**
- Enough time is allowed to **fully flush** the fuel system of fuel above the ECA limit **before entering**
- The ship logs the **volume of low-sulphur fuel in each tank**, and the **date, time and position** when the changeover is **completed before entry** or **started after leaving** the ECA
- The log is kept in the book the flag State prescribes
- Port state control in North America checks the engine room logbook, Oil Record Book, bunker delivery notes and bunker MSDS

## NOx (regulation 13)
Covers installed marine diesel engines **over 130 kW**, except engines used solely for emergencies. Ship tonnage does not matter. The tier depends on the **ship's construction date**.

| Tier | Ship constructed | n below 130 rpm | 130 to under 2,000 rpm | 2,000 rpm and over |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | On or after 1 Jan 2000 | 17.0 g/kWh | 45 × n^−0.2 | 9.8 |
| **II** | On or after 1 Jan 2011 | 14.4 | 44 × n^−0.23 | 7.7 |
| **III** | On or after **1 Jan 2016** | **3.4** | **9 × n^−0.2** | **2.0** |

- **Tier III applies only inside an ECA**: North American and US Caribbean Sea for ships constructed on or after **1 Jan 2016**. The Baltic and North Sea ECAs use **1 Jan 2021**. Outside these, Tier II applies. Reg 13.5.2 lets certain small ships avoid Tier III engines
- **US view of results:** EPA reports that most Tier III engines use **selective catalytic reduction (SCR)**. Auxiliary control devices let engines switch SCR off below **25% load** and exhaust gas recirculation off below **12% load**. EPA estimated that about **38%** of Tier III ECA operation happens below 25% load, so Tier III benefits are not fully achieved
- **For foreign ships**, owners must be able to show that a non-Tier III engine was not and will not be used in the ECA
- **Dual-fuel engines** switch fuel mode outside the ECA. NOx **averaging** is not allowed

## Fuel documents (regulation 18)
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Bunker delivery note (BDN)** | Kept on board **at least 3 years** after delivery. Contents listed in Annex VI Appendix V. Applies to ships that hold an IAPP certificate |
| **Representative fuel sample** | Kept under the ship's control until the fuel is substantially consumed, and **at least 12 months** from delivery |
| **Missing or faulty note or sample** | Record it, and report it to the flag State, the bunkering port authority and the supplier. Keep a copy on board |
| **Supplier duty** | Regulation 18 mainly binds fuel suppliers and the authorities that control them. Owners add contract clauses requiring supply that meets Annex VI |
| **Fuel not available (18.2)** | The owner must have made **best efforts** to obtain compliant fuel. This is weighed when deciding whether to act against a ship using non-compliant fuel |
| **Flag notice (18.2.4)** | If the ship cannot buy compliant fuel, it also tells its flag State |

### Fuel Oil Non-Availability Report (FONAR)
- **Where:** since **30 Jun 2019** EPA no longer takes FONARs through its portal. The **US Coast Guard** is the authority. Under **MSIB 005-19**, the ship notifies the USCG **before arriving** in the North American or US Caribbean Sea ECA if it cannot get enough compliant fuel
- **Format:** no set format. A suggested template is in the annex to PPR 6/8/2. Keep records of every step to comply, including proof of attempts to buy compliant fuel according to the voyage plan
- **Contents under EPA's guidance:** vessel details, voyage plan and when the ship learned it would enter the ECA, expected entry and exit dates, the sulphur content of the fuel to be used, suppliers contacted with dates, the reason compliant fuel was unavailable (**cost is not accepted**), operational constraints, availability at the first US port, 12 months of ECA history, the number of FONARs in the last 12 months, and a **signed certification under penalty of law** (18 U.S.C. 1001)
- **A FONAR does not make the ship compliant.** It is still a violation, and the government decides on action. After entry, the ship buys compliant fuel at the **first US port of call**, if available, in the quantity needed for its ECA voyage
- **Repeat filers** can be asked for more documents and get more extensive port inspections
- **Best efforts** means: plan for compliant fuel, try each port on the voyage, adjust purchases, change berth or anchorage in the port if needed. The ship need not deviate from its intended voyage

## Certificates and records
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| **EIAPP certificate** | Each regulated engine on a US-flag vessel has one, issued by EPA. Reflagging to the US needs an EIAPP for engines over **130 kW** |
| **IAPP certificate** | Issued by the Coast Guard to certain US vessels |
| **US-flag engines** | Meet **40 CFR 1042 and 1043** |
| **On-board records** | Operators keep compliance records. General recordkeeping is in 40 CFR 1043.70 |

## How the US enforces it
- **Law:** Annex VI is implemented through **APPS**. It applies to US-flag ships wherever they are, and to foreign ships in US waters
- **How:** the Coast Guard and EPA do inspections, investigations, enforcement actions, oversight of marine fueling facilities, on-board inspections and record reviews. **Foreign ships** are examined under port state control. Either agency may bring an action
- **Penalty policy:** EPA issued an ECA penalty policy on **16 Jan 2015**, covering violations of the sulphur standard in the North American and US Caribbean ECAs
- **Liability:** violations may bring civil and criminal liability
