# Hague-Visby, Hamburg and Rotterdam Rules — Carrier liability for cargo

## At a glance
| | **Hague-Visby Rules** | **Hamburg Rules (1978)** | **Rotterdam Rules (2008)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full name | Hague Rules as amended by the Brussels Protocol 1968 | UN Convention on the Carriage of Goods by Sea | UN Convention on Contracts for the International Carriage of Goods Wholly or Partly by Sea |
| Status | In force, widely applied | In force (earliest national date shown: 1 Nov 1992). **36 parties** (UNCITRAL table) | **Not in force**. Needs 20 ratifications, then one year. Five ratifications as of Dec 2019 (secondary source) |
| Carrier responsible | **Tackle to tackle** (loading to discharge) | **Port to port**: from taking charge at the port of loading until discharge | Receipt to **delivery**, door to door if there is a sea leg |
| Seaworthiness | Due diligence **before and at the start** of the voyage | Not verified | Due diligence (not strict liability) |
| Nautical fault defence | **Yes** | Not verified | **Removed** |
| Fire | Carrier not liable unless its own actual fault or privity | Carrier liable if the claimant proves fault or neglect of the carrier | Not verified |
| Limit per package | **666.67 SDR** | **835 SDR** | **875 units of account** |
| Limit per kilogram | **2 SDR** | **2.5 SDR** | **3 units of account** |
| Time bar | **1 year** | **2 years** (Art 20) | **2 years** |

> **Key Point:** Which regime applies depends on the bill of lading and the countries involved, not on the ship's flag alone. Check the clause paramount on the bill.

## Hague-Visby Rules
**Application (Art X)**
- Apply to a **bill of lading** (or similar document of title) when the Art X conditions are met. In practice the contract says so, usually by a **clause paramount**. The exact Art X test is not verified here
- Waybills and other non-negotiable documents are outside the Rules
- A bill issued under a charterparty is generally governed by the charter, unless a clause paramount brings the Rules in

**Carrier duties (Art III)**
- **Rule 1, seaworthiness:** exercise due diligence, **before and at the start** of the voyage, to make the ship seaworthy, properly man and supply her, and make the holds fit for the cargo. It is not a continuing duty
- **Rule 2, cargo care:** properly and carefully load, handle, stow, carry, keep, care for and discharge the goods
- **Rule 6, time bar:** the claim is lost unless proceedings start within **one year** of delivery, or of the date delivery should have occurred

**Carrier defences (Art IV rule 2)**
- 17 listed exceptions. The two that matter most:
  - **Nautical fault:** act, neglect or default of the master, mariner, pilot or crew in the navigation or management of the ship
  - **Fire**, unless caused by the carrier's actual fault or privity
- A listed exception cannot be used if the peril could have been avoided with reasonable care, or if unseaworthiness was the operative cause
- Once damage during the voyage is shown, the carrier must bring the cause within an exception or show it was not in breach of its care duty

**Limitation (Art IV rule 5)**
- Not liable beyond **666.67 SDR per package or unit**, or **2 SDR per kilogram** of gross weight lost or damaged, whichever is **higher**. The unit is the IMF special drawing right
- Higher limits apply if the **nature and value** were declared before shipment and put in the bill of lading
- **Containers and pallets:** the packages listed in the bill as packed in the container count. Otherwise the container is the package
- Value is taken at the place and time of discharge (commodity exchange price, then market price, then normal value)
- Parties may agree a **higher** maximum, never a lower one
- **Right to limit lost** if the damage came from the carrier's act done with intent to cause damage, or recklessly with knowledge that damage would probably result
- The carrier is not responsible if the shipper **knowingly misstated** the goods' nature or value

## Hamburg Rules (1978)
- **Scope (Art 2):** contracts of carriage by sea between two different States, when the loading or discharge port, the bill of lading or an agreement links the carriage to a contracting State
- **Period of responsibility (Art 4):** from when the carrier is in charge of the goods at the port of loading until discharge
- **Basis (Art 5):** liability is **presumed**. The carrier escapes only by proving that it, its servants or agents took all measures that could reasonably be required
- **Fire (Art 5(4)):** the claimant must prove the fire came from the carrier's fault or neglect. After a fire, a survey into its cause **must** be held if either side wants one
- **Limits (Art 6):** **835 SDR per package** or **2.5 SDR per kilogram**, whichever is higher. Right to limit lost for intentional or reckless conduct (Art 8)
- **Deck cargo (Art 9):** the carrier may carry on deck only if allowed by agreement (or by trade usage or law)
- **Bill of lading (Art 15):** must include the listed particulars
- **Time bar (Art 20):** **2 years**
- **Unit (Art 26):** the IMF special drawing right
- **Entry into force (Art 30):** first day of the month, one year after the 20th instrument

## Rotterdam Rules (2008)
- Adopted by the UN on **11 Dec 2008**, signing ceremony in Rotterdam on **23 Sep 2009**
- **Door to door:** covers the whole contract if it includes an international sea leg. Contracts with no sea leg are outside
- Removes the **nautical fault** defence. Seaworthiness is a due diligence duty
- Limits **875** units of account per shipping unit or **3** per kilogram. **Time bar** 2 years from delivery
- Allows more electronic transport records, and **volume contracts** that can depart from some liability rules
- A State that ratifies must denounce the Hague-Visby and Hamburg Rules
- Not in force. The shipping industry and ABA supported US ratification, but this is not binding on anyone
