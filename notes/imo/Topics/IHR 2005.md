# IHR 2005 — International Health Regulations, as they apply to ships

## At a glance
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Issued by | **WHO** (not IMO). Legally binding on 196 States Parties, including all 194 WHO Member States |
| Amended | **2024** amendments adopted at the 77th World Health Assembly. In force **19 Sep 2025** (11 States rejected them, so the earlier text still applies to them) |
| Ship documents | **Maritime Declaration of Health** (Art 37, Annex 8). **Ship Sanitation Control Exemption Certificate** or **Control Certificate** (Art 39, Annex 3) |
| Certificate validity | **6 months** maximum. One extra month if the inspection or control cannot be done at the port (Art 39) |
| Applies to | Any ship on an **international voyage** (a "conveyance", Art 1) |

## Main rules for ships
| Article | Rule |
|---|---|
| 20 | States designate ports that develop the core health capacities, and send WHO a list of ports **authorized to issue** the certificates |
| 24 | Operators comply with WHO health measures adopted by the State, and keep the ship **free of sources of infection or contamination** |
| 25 | A ship only passing through a State's waters, without calling at a port, gets no health measure. It may take on fuel, water, food and supplies under supervision |
| 27 | If clinical signs and evidence of a public health risk are found, the ship is treated as **affected**. It may be disinfected, decontaminated, disinsected or deratted, and isolated. Findings and measures go on the Ship Sanitation Control Certificate |
| 28 | A ship **cannot be refused free pratique** for public health reasons, but may be inspected. It can be redirected if the port lacks capacity. The master reports ill persons or health risks on board **before arrival** |
| 37 | The master completes the **Maritime Declaration of Health** and gives it to the port's competent authority. The ship's surgeon countersigns it, if one is carried |
| 39 | Certificates are issued as in Annex 3, valid up to 6 months |
| 41 | One published tariff per State for charges. Not more than actual cost, and the same for all flags |
| 43 | A State may add stricter measures for a specific risk, but not more restrictive than needed, and based on science or WHO guidance. Delays of more than 24 hours must be explained to WHO within 48 hours. Review within 3 months |

## Maritime Declaration of Health (Annex 8)
![[diagram:ihr-mdh|What the declaration asks]]

**Ship details:** port and date, ship name and IMO number, flag, ports of arrival and departure, master, gross tonnage, number of crew and passengers.

**Voyage:** whether a valid sanitation certificate is carried (place and date), whether re-inspection is needed, whether the ship visited an area WHO has identified as affected, and **ports of call with departure dates** for the voyage or the last 30 days, whichever is shorter.

**Nine health questions (yes or no, details in an attached schedule):**
- Any death on board, other than by accident
- Any suspected infectious disease
- More ill passengers than normal or expected
- Anyone ill now
- A doctor consulted, and what advice was given
- Any condition on board that could spread infection
- Any sanitary measure applied (quarantine, isolation, disinfection), with type, place and date
- Any stowaways
- Any sick animal or pet

The attached schedule records for each case: name, rating, age, sex, nationality, joining port and date, illness, onset, whether the port medical officer was told, outcome and treatment. The master signs that it is correct.

## Ship Sanitation Certificates (Art 39, Annex 3)
![[diagram:ihr-certs|Exemption certificate or control certificate]]

- **Exemption Certificate:** issued at an authorized port if the inspection finds no infection or contamination
- **Control Certificate:** issued after control measures are completed, when the inspection finds a risk
- **They replaced** the older Deratting Certificate and Deratting Exemption Certificate (IHR 1969)
- **Inspection covers:** galley, pantry, stores, holds and cargo, crew, officer, passenger and deck quarters, potable water, sewage, ballast tanks, solid and medical waste, standing water, engine room and medical facilities
- **Evidence looked for:** disease vectors at any life stage, animal reservoirs, rodents, microbiological or chemical hazards, and signs of poor sanitation. Sampling and documents (medical log, ship's log) are reviewed
- **Attachment checks:** food (source, storage, preparation), water (source, storage, distribution), waste, pools and spas, medical equipment and medicines
- **Re-inspection** date is written on the certificate
- Only the competent authority of an **authorized port** may issue them. Private certificates are not valid. In the **United States** a cargo ship cannot get one at all and must obtain it at a port in another country on the WHO list
- **Pandemic-era extension (2020):** a WHO joint statement asked States to grant repeated one-month extensions under Art 39(1) when no authorized port on the route could inspect. It could not run past the end of the declared emergency

## What the port must provide (Annex 1)
- Access to an appropriate medical service and diagnostic facilities
- Equipment and personnel to transport ill travellers to a medical facility
- Trained staff to inspect ships
- A safe environment: safe water, food establishments, washrooms and waste disposal
- A vector control programme
- For emergencies: isolation and treatment of affected travellers, separate interview space, quarantine, treatment of baggage and cargo, entry and exit controls

## 2024 amendments in brief
- New alert level, a **pandemic emergency**, above a public health emergency of international concern
- States set up a **National IHR Authority** to coordinate implementation
- Provisions on fair access to medical products and on financing

- Exact Annex 8 wording of the symptom list used when no surgeon is on board
- Which authorities and ports issue certificates in the Philippines or each State
- The WHO Handbook for Inspection of Ships and Guide to Ship Sanitation (3rd edition) contents
- Whether the IMO FAL Convention forms include the Maritime Declaration of Health
- National quarantine flag signals and free pratique procedures
- Specific disease reporting lists, such as the Irish list of cholera, plague, yellow fever and others
- Medical guidance for the ship's medical chest (see the planned Medical note)
