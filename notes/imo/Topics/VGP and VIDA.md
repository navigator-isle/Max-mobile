# VGP and VIDA — US rules for normal ship discharges

## At a glance
| | **2013 Vessel General Permit (VGP)** | **VIDA national standards** |
|---|---|---|
| What | An EPA permit under the Clean Water Act for discharges **incidental to normal operation** | A 2018 law (Vessel Incidental Discharge Act). EPA sets the standards, the **Coast Guard** enforces them |
| Status | **In force now** | EPA rule published **9 Oct 2024**, effective **8 Nov 2024**. The standards **start only when the Coast Guard's rules are final** |
| Waters | US waters out to **3 nm**, including the Great Lakes | US waters and the contiguous zone |
| Vessels | Non-recreational, non-military commercial vessels. Most are over 79 ft | Non-military, non-recreational vessels **79 ft and longer**. About **85,000** vessels |
| Not covered | Recreational and Armed Forces vessels | Armed Forces, recreational, permanently moored craft. **Vessels under 79 ft and fishing vessels are excluded except for ballast water** |

> **Key Point:** Until the Coast Guard publishes its rule, the **2013 VGP** and the **Coast Guard ballast rules (33 CFR 151)** still apply. The VGP expired on 19 Dec 2018 on paper, but it stays in force until VIDA replaces it.

## How VIDA replaces the VGP
- **Congress** passed VIDA in **2018**, adding **section 312(p)** to the Clean Water Act
- **EPA** published *Vessel Incidental Discharge National Standards of Performance* on **9 Oct 2024**, effective **8 Nov 2024**
- The **Coast Guard** has **two years** from EPA's publication to make its implementing, compliance and enforcement rules
- On the date those rules are **final, effective and enforceable**, the VGP and the Coast Guard's older NANPCA ballast rules (as of 3 Dec 2018) are repealed. The small vessel permit was repealed when VIDA became law
- **States lose their own ballast rules.** Seven states had extra ballast rules (California, Michigan, Minnesota, Ohio, Oregon, Washington, Wisconsin). They stop applying when the national standards are in place. A State can still petition EPA for an **emergency order**, a discharge ban or a **no-discharge zone**
- **Ballast water** becomes an incidental discharge under section 312(p), with the VGP as the minimum. The Coast Guard sets the compliance dates. Before a vessel's date it must still exchange ballast water under 40 CFR 139.10(e) or the regional rules in 139.10(f) and (g)
- EPA reviews the standards **every 5 years**

## The 2013 VGP in detail

### Who files what
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Effective** | 19 Dec 2013 (expiry 19 Dec 2018, kept in force) |
| **Notice of Intent (NOI)** | Vessels of **300 GT or more**, or able to hold or discharge **over 8 m³** of ballast water. Filed through EPA's **eNOI** |
| **When** | New or newly delivered vessels file at least **7 days** before discharge (paper: **30 days**) |
| **No NOI** | Vessels under 300 GT with ballast of 8 m³ or less complete a **PARI form** (Appendix K) and keep it on board |
| **Lifeboats, rescue boats and barges** | Covered by the larger vessel's NOI |
| **Annual report** | Due by **28 Feb** of the next year (paper postmarked by **21 Feb**). Appendix H form, all analytical results, and all noncompliance. Filed electronically. Barges and vessels under 300 GT may file a combined report if conditions are met |

### General limits for every vessel (Part 2.1)
- **Material storage:** minimize exposure to rain and spray. Handle oily drainage by dry cleanup, onshore storage or an oily water separator
- **Toxic and hazardous materials:** protected, sealed, labelled, secured, not overfilled. **Jettisoning containers is not allowed**
- **Fuel spills:** no overfilling. Spill containment on vent lines. Clean sheens at once
- **Oil:** MARPOL vessels discharge below **15 ppm** and hold a current **IOPP certificate**. Others discharge no harmful quantities
- **Other laws** still apply: CWA 311, the Act to Prevent Pollution from Ships, the Marine Sanctuaries Act, FIFRA, OPA
- **Training:** crew who manage or affect discharges are trained, including fuel spill response

### Specific discharges (Part 2.2)
| Discharge | Key rule |
|---|---|
| **Deck washdown (2.2.1)** | Broom-clean first. Drip pans under leaking machinery. Phosphate-free, minimally toxic cleaners. Unused paint is not discharged |
| **Bilgewater (2.2.2)** | Over **400 GT**: no untreated bilgewater over **15 ppm**. If sailing beyond the territorial sea, avoid discharge within **1 nm**, and discharge underway (over **6 knots**) when possible. No dispersants to hide a sheen. New builds over 400 GT after 19 Dec 2013 sample oil content yearly, and may stop after two years below **5 ppm**. Data by 28 Feb |
| **Ballast water (2.2.3)** | See the next table |
| **Anti-fouling coatings (2.2.4)** | **No TBT or other organotins**. Follow the FIFRA label |
| **AFFF (2.2.5)** | Emergencies. Maintenance and training discharges restricted |
| **Boiler blowdown (2.2.6)** | Minimize in port |
| **Chain locker (2.2.8)** | Wash the chain as it comes up. Clean the locker in drydock |
| **Distillation brine (2.2.10)** | Must not contact machinery or toxic materials |
| **Elevator pit (2.2.11)** | Untreated discharge only in emergencies. Otherwise treat as bilgewater |
| **Fire main (2.2.12)** | Emergencies and limited testing |
| **Gas turbine washwater (2.2.14)** | No direct discharge in permit waters |
| **Motor gasoline (2.2.16)** | No harmful oil, no sheen, **15 ppm** limit |
| **Non-oily machinery (2.2.17)** | No harmful oils or toxic additives. Packing gland effluent must not make a sheen |
| **Refrigeration condensate (2.2.18)** | Must not contact oily or toxic materials |
| **Seawater cooling and piping (2.2.19, 2.2.20)** | Discharge underway where possible. FIFRA-registered chemicals only, minimum amounts |
| **Sonar dome (2.2.22)** | No sonar dome water discharge for maintenance |
| **Underwater ship husbandry (2.2.23)** | Clean in drydock where possible. Control paint and debris |
| **Graywater (2.2.15)** | See below |
| **Scrubber washwater (2.2.26)** | See below |
| **Fish hold (2.2.27)** | Screen large solids. Use shore facilities where available |

**Ballast water (2.2.3)**
| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Training and plan** | Crew trained. A written training plan and records. A vessel-specific **ballast water management plan** available to EPA |
| **Practices** | Avoid uptake or discharge in sanctuaries and reefs. Minimize uptake in high-risk areas. Clean tanks only mid-ocean or under control. **No sediment discharge** in permit waters. High sea suction when feasible |
| **Numeric limit** | Organisms **50 µm or larger**: fewer than **10 per m³**. **10 to 50 µm**: fewer than **10 per mL**. *V. cholerae*: **under 1 cfu per 100 mL**. *E. coli*: **under 250**. Enterococci: **under 100** |
| **Schedule** | Built after **1 Dec 2013**: on delivery. Existing under 1,500 m³ and over 5,000 m³: first drydocking after **1 Jan 2016**. Existing 1,500 to 5,000 m³: after **1 Jan 2014** |
| **Treatment limits** | pH **6.5 to 9**. Residual biocides: chlorine dioxide **200 µg/L**, chlorine or ozone **100 µg/L**, peracetic acid **500 µg/L** |
| **Exemptions** | Short voyages (within one Captain of the Port zone, or **10 nm** or less), unpowered barges, pre-2009 Great Lakes ships, vessels under **1,600 GRT** |
| **Before treatment is required** | Exchange beyond the EEZ and more than **200 nm** from shore. Pacific nearshore voyages: **50 nm** and **200 m** depth. Empty tanks flushed or sealed |
| **Records** | Kept on board **3 years**. Great Lakes ships also follow 33 CFR 151 subpart C |

**Graywater (2.2.15)**
- Minimize discharge in port
- Vessels over **400 GT** more than **1 nm** from shore discharge beyond 1 nm while underway, unless they treat it
- Phosphate-free, minimally toxic soaps. pH not below **6.0** or above **9.0**
- **Great Lakes:** hold onshore, or meet fecal coliform **200 per 100 mL** and suspended solids **150 mg/L**
- **Monitoring:** two samples a year at least **14 days** apart (BOD, fecal coliform or *E. coli*, suspended solids, pH, residual chlorine). Records **3 years**

**Scrubber washwater (2.2.26)**
| Parameter | Limit |
|---|---|
| **pH** | At least **6.0** at overboard discharge. Up to **2.0** units below the inlet allowed during maneuvering and transit |
| **PAHs** | Up to **50 µg/L** above inlet at 45 t/MWh. The table runs from **2,250 µg/L** at 0 to 1 t/MWh down to **25 µg/L** at 90 t/MWh. A 100% excursion for **15 min per 12 h** at startup |
| **Turbidity** | Up to **25 FNU/NTU** above inlet, 15-minute rolling average. One 15-minute excursion of up to 20% per 12 h |
| **Nitrates and nitrites** | The greater of **12% NOx removal** or **60 mg/L** at 45 t/MWh. The table runs from **2,700 mg/L** to **30 mg/L** |
| **Monitoring** | Continuous pH, PAH (where available), turbidity, temperature. Calibrate **yearly**. Two samples in year one, at least 14 days apart, then yearly. Data by **28 Feb** |
| **Sludge** | Not discharged within 3 nm. Delivered ashore |

### Inspections and records (Parts 3 and 4)
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Routine visual inspection** | At least **weekly**, or each voyage if more often. Ships making several voyages a day: at least **daily** |
| **Record** | Date, time, locations, inspector, findings, signature |
| **Long layup (13 days or more unmanned)** | Pre-layup inspection, every **2 weeks**, and a post-layup inspection before re-entering service |
| **Annual comprehensive inspection** | At least every **12 months**, by qualified personnel. Hull, ballast tanks, bilges, oil systems, pollution controls |
| **Drydock report** | Confirms chain locker cleaning, hull inspection, coating compliance, anode maintenance |
| **Corrective action** | Triggered by a limit violation or an ineffective control. Simple fixes within **2 weeks**. Parts or equipment within **3 months**. Drydock-only work before the ship relaunches. The fix does **not** excuse the original violation |
| **Records kept** | **3 years**: voyage log, violations, inspection logs, analytical results, drydock findings, operational logs, training |
| **Ballast records (4.3)** | Capacity and tank status, management, origin and exchange of discharged ballast. The ballast report goes to the National Ballast Information Clearinghouse before arrival. Not required for a vessel operating in one Captain of the Port zone |
| **Noncompliance** | Oral notice to the EPA Regional Office within **24 hours**, written follow-up within **5 days**. A report to the National Response Center (800-424-8802) satisfies it |

### Special vessel classes (Part 5)
| Class | Main rule |
|---|---|
| **Large cruise ships** (500 or more people) | Treated graywater: fecal coliform **20 per 100 mL** (geometric mean), **40** for no more than 10% of samples, residual chlorine **10 µg/L**. No untreated graywater. Pools and spas dechlorinated. Initial monitoring: **5 samples over 30 days**, then quarterly. Crew and passenger training |
| **Medium cruise ships** (100 to 499) | Similar, with a **1 nm** trigger |
| **Large ferries** | Carrying **100 tons** of vehicles or more: deck water through an oily water separator. Authorized for **250 passengers** or more: graywater rules |
| **Barges** | Scuppers plugged during cargo or fueling. Broom-clean before washing. Visual sheen test |
| **Tankers and chemical carriers** | Inert gas scrubber effluent and deck seal water authorized. Scuppers plugged during loading. Sheen tests |
| **Research, emergency and rescue vessels** | Limited discharges for research, training and tests |

### State conditions (Part 6)
Each State's water quality certification adds conditions that become enforceable. Examples: **California** bans large passenger vessel graywater in State waters and sets oil and grease at **15 mg/L** or less. **Connecticut** bans bilgewater, graywater and scrubber washwater discharge in certain waters. **Arkansas** needs phone notice within **24 hours** and written notice in **3 days**.

## VIDA national standards in detail

### Three general standards (all vessels)
| Standard | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Operation and maintenance** (40 CFR 139.4) | Minimize discharges with available and practicable measures. No additives except for treatment. Contain cargo. Store hazardous materials properly. Discharge while underway and as far from shore as possible |
| **Biofouling management** (139.5) | A **biofouling management plan** for anti-fouling systems and practices, with hull and niche area cleaning rules. In-water cleaning is restricted. **Annual inspections** and drydock reports |
| **Oil management** (139.6) | Used or spent oil is not discharged. **Environmentally acceptable lubricants** at oil-to-sea interfaces unless technically infeasible |

### Twenty specific discharges
- **Ballast tanks, bilges, boilers, cathodic protection, chain lockers, decks, desalination and purification systems, elevator pits, exhaust gas emission control systems, fire protection equipment, gas turbines, graywater systems, hulls and niche areas, inert gas systems, motor gasoline and compensating systems, non-oily machinery, pools and spas, refrigeration and air conditioning, seawater piping, sonar domes**
- **13 are substantially unchanged** from the VGP, **2 slightly changed** (bilges, desalination), and **5 changed most**: ballast tanks, exhaust gas emission control systems, graywater systems, hulls and niche areas, seawater piping
- **Federally protected waters:** slightly changed rules for chain lockers, decks, hulls, pools and seawater piping. Extra rules for the Great Lakes and the Pacific region

### Key numbers
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Ballast discharge limit** (139.10) | Same numbers as the VGP: fewer than **10 per m³** (50 µm or larger), **10 per mL** (10 to 50 µm). *V. cholerae* under **1 cfu per 100 mL**, *E. coli* under **250**, enterococci under **100**. Instantaneous maximums |
| **Biocide limits** | Chlorine dioxide **200 µg/L**, chlorine or ozone **100 µg/L**, peracetic acid **500 µg/L**, hydrogen peroxide **1,000 µg/L** |
| **Ballast exchange** | **200 nm** from shore for certain voyages. **50 nm** for voyages starting in the US or Canadian EEZ |
| **Ballast exclusions** | Flow-through systems, vessels using only Safe Drinking Water Act water, permanent sealed ballast, discharge only to a reception facility, National Defense Reserve Fleet vessels for disposal. Coastwise crude oil tankers are **not** exempt |
| **New Great Lakes ships** | Of **3,000 GT or more**, operating only in the Great Lakes and St. Lawrence River, built after the Coast Guard rule: a USCG type-approved system. **Existing Lakers** get no equipment standard |
| **Bilges** (139.11) | Vessels **400 GT and above**: discharge underway, at least **1 nm** from shore where feasible, at **15 ppm** oil or less |
| **Chain lockers** (139.14) | Rinse chain and anchor of organisms and sediment on retrieval. No discharge in port |
| **Exhaust gas emission control** (139.18) | Limits on pH, PAHs, turbidity, suspended particles, nitrates plus nitrites. No sludge or residue discharge |
| **Records** | Measures to minimize discharges are written in shipboard logs and plans. Inspectors can check records, sample discharges and verify equipment |
