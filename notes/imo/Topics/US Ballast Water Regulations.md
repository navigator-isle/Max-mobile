# US Ballast Water Regulations — 33 CFR 151 subpart D

## At a glance
| | |
|---|---|
| **Rule** | **33 CFR 151 subpart D**, from the Coast Guard final rule of **23 Mar 2012** (77 FR 17254), effective **21 Jun 2012**. Great Lakes and Hudson River rules are in **subpart C** |
| **Who** | **Non-recreational** US and foreign vessels **with ballast tanks** operating in US waters |
| **Not a party** | The US is **not party to the IMO BWM Convention**. It has its own rules, so they differ from the Convention (for example, the US standard counts **living** organisms, the Convention counts **viable** ones) |
| **Standard** | The **ballast water discharge standard**, met with a **Coast Guard type-approved BWMS** (46 CFR 162.060) |
| **Reporting** | **National Ballast Information Clearinghouse (NBIC)** |
| **Interim rule** | Until VIDA is in force, subpart D stays. The EPA's own ballast rules in the VGP apply together with it |

> **Key Point:** The Coast Guard has no "grandfather" rule. After a ship's compliance date, ballast water exchange no longer counts as compliance. A system not type-approved by the Coast Guard cannot be used to meet the standard, except as a temporary **Alternate Management System (AMS)**.

## Who is covered, who is exempt (151.2010, 151.2015)
| Vessel | Exempt from |
|---|---|
| **Armed Forces** and Coast Guard vessels, and foreign warships and government vessels on non-commercial service | **Everything** in subpart D (foreign government ships should still act consistently so far as reasonable) |
| **Coastwise crude oil tankers** | Management (151.2025), reporting (151.2060) and recordkeeping (151.2070) |
| Vessels that sail **only between ports in one Captain of the Port (COTP) zone** | The same three |
| **Seagoing vessels in more than one COTP zone** that **never leave the EEZ** and are **1,600 GRT or less**, or **3,000 GT or less** (1969 Tonnage Convention) | Management only. They still follow 151.2050, 151.2060 and 151.2070 |
| **Non-seagoing vessels** (for example inland towing) | Management only |
| Vessels in more than one COTP zone that **take up and discharge ballast within one zone** | Management only. Taking ballast up outside that zone ends the exemption until tanks are cleaned, sediment removed |

- **Recreational vessels** of any size are not covered. A large yacht that is chartered for hire is commercial and must comply
- **Unmanned deck barges** with ballast tanks are covered in US waters. **FPSOs** only within **12 nm** of the US baseline. **MODUs** are exempt when entirely beyond **12 nm**. A jack-up's pre-load water counts as ballast water

## Methods of management (151.2025)
One of five methods:
| Method | Rule |
|---|---|
| **1. Approved BWMS** | A system approved under **46 CFR 162**, installed by the schedule in 151.2035(b), maintained to the maker's specification, meeting the standard at every discharge |
| **2. US public water** | Only water from a US public water system that meets 40 CFR 141 and 143. Keep the source records and a **receipt or invoice** (a log entry alone is not enough). Tanks cleaned of sediment with no later ambient water, or never having held ambient water |
| **3. Exchange** | A **complete exchange at least 200 nm from any shore**, only until the ship's compliance date (or extended date) |
| **4. No discharge** | No ballast discharged into US waters |
| **5. Transfer** | To an onshore facility or another vessel for treatment. Piping to the last manifold or valve must stop untreated ballast reaching US waters |

- A vessel with a BWMS in independent laboratory testing is deemed compliant with method 1 (151.2025(g))
- A vessel that discharges **no ballast within 12 nm** of the US need not install a BWMS
- The Coast Guard **cannot waive** compliance (a COTP cannot excuse it)

## Discharge standard (151.2030)
| Measure | Limit |
|---|---|
| Organisms **50 µm or larger** | Fewer than **10 per m³** |
| Organisms **10 to under 50 µm** | Fewer than **10 per mL** |
| *Vibrio cholerae* (O1 and O139) | Less than **1 cfu per 100 mL** |
| *Escherichia coli* | Fewer than **250 cfu per 100 mL** |
| Intestinal enterococci | Fewer than **100 cfu per 100 mL** |

## When a type-approved system is required (151.2035)
| Ballast capacity | Built | Compliance |
|---|---|---|
| All | On or after **1 Dec 2013** | On delivery |
| Under 1,500 m³ | Before 1 Dec 2013 | First scheduled drydocking after **1 Jan 2016** |
| 1,500 to 5,000 m³ | Before 1 Dec 2013 | First scheduled drydocking after **1 Jan 2014** |
| Over 5,000 m³ | Before 1 Dec 2013 | First scheduled drydocking after **1 Jan 2016** |

An **in-water survey (UWILD)** is **not** a "first scheduled drydocking" (NVIC 01-18 and MSIB 13-15).

## Extensions (151.2036)
- **Who asks:** the master, owner, operator, agent or person in charge
- **Grounds:** compliance with 151.2025 is **not possible despite all efforts**. Typical grounds: installing a BWMS already acquired, or one under review for type approval
- **When:** at least **12 months before** the compliance date. In writing, by email to environmental_standards@uscg.mil
- **Length:** no longer than the minimum time the Coast Guard decides the ship needs. In practice, generally a specific date **no more than 12 months** past the compliance date
- **Public:** summaries of decisions, with vessel and owner names, are posted
- **Policy of 6 Mar 2017:** the Coast Guard decides the length after reviewing the application, and considers whether a type-approved system is available. For **compliance dates on or after 1 Jan 2021**, supplemental extensions "should not be anticipated"
- **Without an extension** a ship can use: an AMS (temporary), US public water, a reception facility, or no unmanaged discharge inside 12 nm

## Alternate Management System, AMS (151.2026)
- A BWMS **approved by a foreign administration under the 2004 BWM Convention** can be recognised by the Coast Guard as an AMS. The maker applies to the Marine Safety Center with the approval certificate, the test authority's details, the final test results and quality records, any changes after testing, and a US type approval application (46 CFR 162.060-12)
- A ship that **installed an AMS before its compliance date** can use it for up to **5 years after** the date it would otherwise have to meet the standard, maintained to its specification
- **Once the Coast Guard type-approves systems** for a class, type or specific vessel, those ships can **no longer install an AMS** instead
- An AMS determination can be suspended, withdrawn or ended (46 CFR 162.060-18)

## Type approval (46 CFR 162.060)
| Test | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Land-based** | Three salinity ranges: fresh **under 1 PSU**, brackish **10 to 20 PSU**, marine **28 to 36 PSU** |
| **Operation and maintenance** | At least **50 hours**, equal to **10,000 m³** for a 200 m³/h system |
| **Shipboard** | **Five valid trials** over at least **6 months**, across at least **two Large Marine Ecosystems**, at least 24 hours apart at one location |
| **Changes** | A change to an approved system needs Coast Guard review first, or the approval is void |
| **Certificate** | Covers all units of the model made while it is valid. Hold times: at least 24 hours in testing, with the maker's limits on the certificate |

## Practices and plan (151.2050)
- Avoid taking up or discharging ballast in or affecting **marine sanctuaries, preserves, parks or coral reefs**
- Minimize or avoid uptake where **harmful organisms** such as toxic algal blooms are known, near **sewage outfalls** and **dredging**, where tidal flushing is poor or the water is turbid, **at night** when bottom organisms may rise, where propellers may stir sediment, and near whale pods, convergence zones or major current boundaries
- **Clean tanks** regularly of sediment. Dispose of sediment as far from shore as practicable and always outside **12 nm**
- Discharge only the **minimum** ballast needed in US waters
- **Rinse anchors and chains** when retrieved. Remove fouling from hull, piping and tanks
- **Train** the master, operator and crew
- Use only reception facilities that hold an **NPDES permit** to receive ballast water

**Ballast water management plan (BWM plan)** (151.2050(g))
- Safety procedures
- How the mandatory requirements and practices are carried out
- Fouling maintenance and sediment removal procedures
- How to coordinate with Coast Guard authorities
- The **officer(s) responsible**
- Reporting requirements and procedures for US ports
- A translation into **English, French or Spanish** if the working language is different
- The Coast Guard does **not formally approve** the plan. It reviews it during port state control exams. It may reference other on-board documents, which must be available for examination

## Reporting (151.2060)
- **Who:** the master, owner, operator, agent or person in charge, for ships bound for US ports or places
- **To:** the **NBIC**, electronically
**Deadlines**
| Voyage | Deadline |
|---|---|
| **Great Lakes**, from outside the EEZ | At least **24 hours** before arriving at Montreal, Quebec. Non-US, non-Canadian ships may use the St. Lawrence Seaway form |
| **Hudson River** north of the George Washington Bridge, from outside the EEZ | At least **24 hours** before entering New York |
| **Other US-bound ships** with ballast tanks | **No later than 6 hours after arrival**, or before departing, whichever is earlier |


- **Contents:** vessel details; voyage details with last and next ports; total ballast capacity, number of tanks, volume on board, tanks in ballast and the management method; for **each tank discharged**: tank, source of water (dates, locations, volumes), management, and discharge dates, locations and volumes; and a **certification** (name and title) that the activities followed the BWM plan, with an explanation of any deviation
- **Amendment:** before leaving US waters, or within **24 hours** after leaving the port, whichever is earlier. A corrected form is marked "Corrected Form"
- A vessel using **only US public water** must still report
- **Alternative reporting** needs Coast Guard approval within **30 days**, and it must be at least as effective

## Records (151.2070)
- Keep a **signed copy** of the report information, plus **sediment** details (the name and location of the disposal facility if sediment was discharged in US jurisdiction), on board for **2 years**
- The signer is the master, owner, operator, agent or person in charge. Printed name and title are required. For an electronic report, affirming the certification can serve as the signature
- Monitoring records under 46 CFR 162.060-20(b) are kept **2 years** as well
- Records may be written or digital, and must be **readily viewable** by the Coast Guard
- US-flag ships in foreign ports follow that port State's law

## If something fails (151.2040)
- If a BWMS fails or a method becomes unavailable, report to the **nearest COTP or District Commander** as soon as practicable. An NBIC report does not replace this
- A ship **that cannot practicably comply** may discharge **outside the Great Lakes and the Hudson River north of the George Washington Bridge** if its voyage does not take it **200 nm** or more from any shore for long enough while it still holds ballast, or if the master identifies **safety or stability concerns**. This is **not** allowed for a ship that already must have an approved BWMS
- Discharge is limited to the amount **operationally necessary** for the safety of the vessel for cargo operations. Records are available to the COTP on request
- A ship past its compliance date arriving to load cargo may discharge beyond 12 nm before entering port, but not unmanaged ballast inside 12 nm. If loading cannot finish without discharging, loading must stop

## Great Lakes and Hudson River (subpart C)
- Ships discharging ballast into US waters meet the discharge standard in **151.1511(a)** using a Coast Guard-approved BWMS, use an AMS, or ballast only with **US public water** (151.1512(a))
- The compliance dates in **151.1512(b)** are the same as the table above
- **New "Lakers"** of 3,000 GT or more operating only in the Great Lakes and St. Lawrence River need a type-approved system under the EPA rule in [[VGP and VIDA]]. Existing Lakers have no equipment standard

## Enforcement
- The Coast Guard checks documents, crew knowledge, system installation, maintenance and operation in **port state control exams**, and can **sample** ballast water. Ballast rules are in the **Foreign Tank Vessel Exam Book**
- Penalties are in **33 CFR 151.1518 and 151.2080**. Ballast water violations can also be Clean Water Act violations under the VGP
